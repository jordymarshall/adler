### New must fix

1. **§0 Facts that bound the method; §1 commitment 1** — The method's foundational commitment — "The daily tap is the intervention; the coach is the witness (P1)" — quotes Harkin's d = 0.40 and the reported-to-someone d+ = 0.47 but omits the moderator in the same analysis that most threatens it: objective monitoring tools g = 0.40 against subjective logs −0.02. Adler's tap is a subjective log. §0 lists four bounding facts and honestly names the claims ceiling, the population gap and the idiographic ceiling, but silently drops the one moderator that could make the base ingredient null in exactly the form Adler ships it. Every downstream rule that treats adherence as "the intervention itself" (§11 primary outcome, §5.4 tap integrity, §7 signals) inherits the omission.
   - Fix: Add a fifth bounding fact in §0: "Monitoring's base effect is moderated by instrument. In Harkin's own analysis objective recording tools gave g = 0.40 while subjective logs gave −0.02. Adler's tap is a subjective log reported to a machine. The direction is claimed, not the magnitude, and §11 test 2 (tap-only arm) is the experiment that decides whether the tap or the coach carries anything at all." Cross-reference it from §1 commitment 1 and from §11's outcome layer.
   - Basis: Synthesis P1 (Harkin, 138 RCTs, N = 19,951: objective tools g = 0.40 vs subjective logs −0.02; public cell k = 3, CI [−0.16, 1.26]); NICE ESF Standard 14 requires effectiveness claims to rest on evidence in the deployed form; §0.1's own rule that no literature effect is claimed for the product.

2. **§8.9 Pessimism flag; §5.1 step 2** — The flag fires on "more con than pro reasons at formation, collected in 5.1 step 2". §5.1 step 2 collects the importance ruler, one pro-only elicitation ("What makes it a [n] and not lower?") and the two concordance items. It collects no con reasons and no pros-and-cons move — BCTTv1 9.2 was correctly dropped from §5.2 in this draft. So the first limb of a rule that routes users to WHO-5 and the referral script is uncomputable, and v0.2 now asserts as fact a collection point that does not exist in its own script. This is worse than v0.1, which merely left the code dangling.
   - Fix: Delete the pro/con limb rather than scripting it — eliciting con reasons is fishing for sustain talk, which P11 and Magill 2018 make an active harm. Restate §8.9 as: "Pessimism flag: guilt exceeding value by 2 or more on the §5.1 step 2 concordance items across two or more goals; or confidence < 5 on every goal; or goals that neither move nor park for three weeks." Both remaining limbs are already stored (`goals.importance`, the concordance margin, `goals.status`).
   - Basis: BCTTv1 9.2 definition (pros and cons) requires an actual elicitation of both sides; synthesis §4.9 states the same trigger but the method must supply the datum; P11/Magill 2018 (MI-inconsistent elicitation raises sustain talk, r = .19 with worse outcomes); NICE PH49 rec 14 requires intervention components to be identifiable from the record.

3. **§5.5 move 6 (disagreement check); §5.7 Script; §11 rubric** — §5.5 requires, when the coach has agreed with everything, "one honest line... 'I'd hold rather than grow this week. The rubric therefore has a state in which the review fails whichever way the coach behaves, and the eval cannot be run to a pass on a review where the coach genuinely agrees with the user.
   - Fix: Wrap the disagreement move in a permission turn (rewrite 5) and add one sentence to §11: "A coach-stated position that contains no proposal and no directive is scored under the disagreement item, not as Persuade; the moment it carries a proposed change it requires a preceding permission turn." Apply the same wrapper to the §5.7 hold script.
   - Basis: MITI 4.2.1 Persuade counts toward MI-non-adherent, which §11 sets at 0; method §2 constraint 7 and §10 Mechanics ("No unwrapped proposal"); P12 (Cheng 2026) requires the disagreement, so the two requirements must be reconciled explicitly rather than left to the coder.

### Rewrites

1. **§9, Friend question, third turn**
   - Current: Do you want that as this week's action? When and where?
   - Proposed: Do you want that as this week's action?  →  [reflection]  →  When and where?
   - Why: A single scripted string with two question marks. §11's automatic-fail list includes 'any script turn with two questions' and §1 states that 'a lint on the prompt corpus rejects any scripted string with more than one question mark' — so the method ships a line that fails both its own lint and its own eval. It is also the turn that converts a wisdom move into a committed action, which is the last one to rush.

2. **§5.8, Self-management transfer**
   - Current: What would you want me to keep doing, and what can you do without?
   - Proposed: What would you want me to keep doing?  →  [reflection]  →  And what can you do without?
   - Why: Two questions in one turn joined by 'and'. It carries one question mark, so the §1 lint cannot catch it, which is precisely why the split has to be written into the script rather than left to the linter. Asking both at once also lets the user answer only the first, and the second is the one that governs the dose drop.

3. **§5.2, Balanced course, habit-shaped variant**
   - Current: Something small and daily usually takes a couple of months to feel routine, with a wide spread either side. The first two weeks are the wobbly part.
   - Proposed: Something small and daily takes longer to feel routine than most people expect, and the spread is wide. The first two weeks are the wobbly part.
   - Why: 'A couple of months' is a single days-to-habit number in words, which §5.2's own rule forbids ('Never quote one days-to-habit number; the range is selected by route'), §10 forbids ('no single days-to-habit number') and §15.3 bans from public copy ('any days-to-habit number'). The facility-based variant in the same bullet correctly gives a range ('four to seven months'), so the rule is understood and simply not applied here. This also restores the round-one rewrite that v0.1's 'about two months' was failed for.

4. **§5.4, After Yes, reserve tap**
   - Current: Could you have done more without it costing you tomorrow?
   - Proposed: Could you have kept going today?  ·  a lot more  ·  a bit more  ·  no
   - Why: Ten words with an embedded conditional and an embedded negative, asked daily, immediately after a Yes, on a phone. The tell that the wording is wrong is that the stored options do not grammatically answer it: 'a lot · a bit · none' answers 'how much more', not 'could you'. Five words and matching option labels carry the same reserve reading. Basis: landing research §2, 'read every sentence aloud (HIG)'; §1 voice, 'direct, plain, warm, short'.

5. **§5.5, Monthly personal reference class**
   - Current: Last four weeks you planned 6 and did 4 on average. Four weeks is thin, so call that 4 or 5 on a normal week. Keep planning 6, or plan 5 and treat 5 as the week working?
   - Proposed: Last four weeks you planned six and did four. That's thin, so call it four or five on a normal week. Keep planning six, or plan five?
   - Why: Thirty-nine words to twenty-six on a monthly card. 'Treat 5 as the week working' is not sentence English read aloud, and mid-sentence digits under ten break with the rest of the user-facing copy ('Four of six this week'). The proposal keeps the hedge, the exposure count and the two doors, which are the load-bearing parts.
