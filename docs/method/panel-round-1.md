# Panel round 1 findings on Adler Method v0.1

Generated 2026-09-02 from seven Opus expert lenses. All seven verdicts: fail. Integrated into v0.2.


## NBC-HWC (NBHWC scope of practice + 2026–2030 content outline), MINT member, MITI 4.2.1 coder: coaching process fidelity, ask-offer-ask discipline, and MI coding of every verbatim script line

**Verdict:** fail

**Summary.** The method cannot pass the release gates it writes for itself. Its own §11 rubric requires ≥13/16 on an NBHWC process checklist, MI-Non-Adherent = 0, R:Q ≥ 1:1, %CR ≥ 40% and Affirm > 0, but no script produces vision, gap, SMART, commitment, client-chosen accountability, affirmation, takeaway or next contact (roughly 8/16), the six barrier templates contain zero reflections, and at least two verbatim lines code as Confront and Persuade. Ask-offer-ask is named as a binding scope constraint and claimed in three competency rows, yet the only permission-seeking sentence in the entire document is the crisis referral offer — every information and change-proposal move is delivered unasked. The ban on affirmation is the most consequential error: it is justified by Deci 1999, which exempts verbal informational affirmation, and it guarantees failure against the method's own Affirm > 0 threshold and NBHWC 1.6. Sustain talk is nowhere reflected, only counted and routed, which is the specific failure mode Magill 2018 links to worse outcomes.


### Must fix

1. **§11 Measurement (eval rubric pack) and §5 throughout** — The method's own NBHWC sixteen-item process checklist has a ≥13/16 pass bar, but no script in §5 produces vision, gap, SMART, explicit commitment, client-chosen accountability method, affirmation, session takeaway or next contact, so the method scores roughly 8/16 against its own release gate.
   - Fix: Either script the eight missing items or delete them from the rubric and stop citing NBHWC 1.5.3/1.6/3.9.2.2/3.9.7 in §12. Minimum viable additions: a gap question at §5.2 ("Where are you on this now, 0 to 10, and what does the number you want look like in your words?" — NBHWC 3.1.7 scaling, which also satisfies vision-plus-gap without the positive-fantasy risk Kappes & Oettingen identify); a weekly SMART behavioural statement at §5.5 item 8; a commitment question at §5.3; an accountability question at §5.3; an affirmation slot at §5.4 and §5.5; a takeaway and next-contact line closing §5.5.
   - Basis: NBHWC Content Outline 2026–2030 items 1.5.3 (vision, current vs desired state, gaps), 1.6 (success/learning, topic, why, how, client summary, takeaways, affirmation, next contact), 3.9.2.2 (SMART), 3.9.7 (client's own methods of accountability); AF §7B item 4 pass bar ≥13/16.

2. **§2 scope constraint 7; §5.5 item 8; §5.6; §12 rows 5.3 and 5.6** — Ask-offer-ask is named as a binding constraint and claimed in three competency rows and the §11 auto-fail list, yet not one script in the document asks permission before information or advice — the only permission-seeking sentence in the whole method is the crisis/wellbeing referral offer.
   - Fix: Insert an explicit permission turn before every information or proposal move: §5.2 balanced course, §5.4 all six barrier templates, §5.5 item 8, §5.6 every proposed change, §5.7 growth offers. Also delete "forces a plan change" from the §5.5 rules — the coach cannot force a plan change and stay inside NBHWC scope.
   - Basis: NBHWC 1.2.3 "Refrain from directing, advising, or problem-solving" and 3.2 "use ask-offer-ask"; NBHWC Scope of Practice ("accountability partner, not director"); AF §7C item 7; the method's own §11 ("any advising without ask-offer-ask fails").

3. **§5.1 Discovery, step 5 (parking resistance)** — "Which of these are you choosing to be bad at until then?" is a rhetorical, evaluatively loaded challenge fired precisely when the client produces sustain talk, and MITI codes it Confront — an automatic fail on the method's own MI-Non-Adherent = 0 gate.
   - Fix: Replace with a double-sided reflection plus autonomy emphasis (see rewrites). Add a standing rule: no coach turn may contain a rhetorical question, and no coach turn may contain an evaluative adjective applied to the client's choice.
   - Basis: MITI 4.2.1 behaviour count Confront; MI-Non-Adherent = Confront + Persuade, set to 0 in §11 and §12 row 5.4; NBHWC 3.2 "soften sustain talk"; Magill 2018 (36 studies, N = 3,025): MI-inconsistent behaviour raises sustain talk and sustain talk predicts worse outcomes, r = .19.

4. **§5.1 Discovery, Rules (one-active-goal default)** — "Starting one thing at a time is what the research on goal conflict says works. Your call." supplies a reason for change unprompted; MITI codes this Persuade, and the trailing "Your call" is a separate Emphasizing Autonomy count that does not convert it to Persuade-with-Permission.
   - Fix: Convert to ask-offer-ask (see rewrites). Persuade-with-Permission requires the permission to precede the content or the client to have requested it; a retrospective autonomy tag does not qualify.
   - Basis: MITI 4.2.1 Persuade vs Persuade with Permission decision rules; MI-Non-Adherent = 0 (§11); AF §7C item 7; NBHWC 1.2.3.

5. **§5.1 Discovery, step 2 (should-detection)** — "That one sounds like it belongs to someone else's list" assigns the client a motive the client did not state, on the basis of two unvalidated 0–10 items, which is an assessment of internal state delivered as fact.
   - Fix: Reflect the client's own numbers and hand the interpretation back (see rewrites). Never state a motive attribution; only reflect the client's words and their own ratings.
   - Basis: NBHWC Scope of Practice ("self-determined" goals; coaches do not interpret); the method's own scope constraint 11 ("Never infer or display the user's emotional or mental state as an assessment"); MITI Partnership anchor 1–2 ("actively assumes the expert role"); Illinois PA 104-0054 prohibition on AI that detects emotions or mental states.

6. **§5.1 Discovery, step 5 and Rules (conflict score)** — The coach announces the starting goal ("It sounds like [A] is the one") from a deterministic conflict score whose "highest wins" rule makes the algorithm the decider; saying it back afterwards is compliance, not self-determination.
   - Fix: Ask the client to choose, then reflect; if the score disagrees, offer the observation with permission. This also honours D1 ("advise, never gate") more faithfully than the current line, which gates by announcement.
   - Basis: NBHWC Scope of Practice: coaches "facilitate and empower clients to develop and achieve self-determined, health and wellness goals"; ICF PCC marker 5.3 "client chooses what happens"; D&J BC4 "help the client generate their own goals"; EMCC CI 24 "Ensures the client chooses solutions".

7. **§5.4 Daily check-in, coach reply templates by barrier** — All six barrier templates are questions or directives with zero reflections, and three of them stack two questions in one turn, so a coded missed-day window yields R:Q = 0, %CR = 0 and Softening Sustain Talk ≤ 2 — below every threshold the method sets for this phase.
   - Fix: Rebuild each template as reflection-then-single-open-question, with any technique offer gated by permission. Remove the imperative "Halve it" and the compound "What's the two-minute version, and what's right before it?" (see rewrites). Restore AF §7B's explicit instruction to code missed-day check-in windows, which §11 dropped.
   - Basis: MITI 4.2.1 thresholds R:Q ≥ 1:1, %CR ≥ 40%, and the method's own §12 row 5.4 ("Soften ≥ 3, Affirm > 0, Confront = 0"); ICF PCC marker 7.6 "clear, direct, primarily open-ended questions, one at a time"; AF §7B item 1 names "missed-day DC" as a coded window.

8. **§1 commitment 4, §6 Feedback and tone rules, §5.4 After Yes** — The blanket ban on "adjectives about the person" and on evaluation as a feedback slot eliminates MI affirmation entirely — pure description such as "Done, on a day with two late nights" codes as Giving Information, not Affirm — so the method guarantees Affirm = 0 against its own Affirm > 0 threshold, and it drops the NBHWC 1.6 affirmation element.
   - Fix: Distinguish affirmation from praise explicitly in §6 and add an affirmation slot: affirmations name effort, strategy, persistence or a value the client demonstrated, in behavioural terms, with no comparative or global adjective. Keep the ban on praise-as-grade and on trait attributions.
   - Basis: MI-4 affirmation-not-praise distinction (CF §3); NBHWC 1.6 and 3.2 (OARS includes affirmations); MITI 4.2.1 Affirm behaviour count and §12 row 5.4 Affirm > 0; Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (128 studies) undermines only tangible contingent rewards (d = −0.40 engagement, −0.36 completion, −0.28 performance) and explicitly exempts verbal informational affirmation — the cited evidence does not support the ban.

9. **§5.4 Daily check-in, "Didn't feel like it" template** — The scripted behavioural-activation rationale ("Mood follows action more than the other way round. The urge is skip; the opposite action is the two-minute version") is unsolicited persuasion delivered at a moment of low motivation, and it imports treatment vocabulary ("opposite action") into a non-clinical product.
   - Fix: Delete the rationale from the default path; gate any version behind permission and strip the clinical term (see rewrites).
   - Basis: MITI Persuade (MI-Non-Adherent = 0 in §11); ACT-FM inconsistent item "lectures the client (e.g. gives advice, tries to convince the client)" listed as an automatic fail in §11; the method's own scope constraint 3 (no "therapeutic" communication for distress); CF §4 states behavioural activation as treatment is "off-limits" (Ekers 2014, 26 RCTs, SMD = −0.74).

10. **§5.1 step 5, §5.4, §5.5 item 5, §5.6 item 4** — Sustain talk is handled everywhere by routing it into a rule counter ("didn't feel like it" ×3 → revisit-goal; parking resistance → the confront line; failed consensus → back to step 2), and no script anywhere contains a reflection of ambivalence, so the Softening Sustain Talk global cannot exceed 2.
   - Fix: Add a named sustain-talk move to §5 that fires before any counter increments: one double-sided or amplified reflection, then autonomy emphasis, then an open question — no rationale, no fix. Add it to §12 as the mechanism satisfying Soften ≥ 3.
   - Basis: NBHWC 3.2 "cultivate change talk; soften sustain talk; respond to discord"; MITI 4.2.1 Softening Sustain Talk global, threshold via Technical ≥ 3.0 (§11); Magill 2018, sustain talk → worse outcomes r = .19; MI-4 fixing-reflex constraint (CF §3).

11. **§5.11 Safety and scope, goal-text risk markers** — Declining to coach an eating-disorder or compulsive-exercise goal is the single highest-risk conversational move in the product and is the only move in §5 with no verbatim script; separately, self-monitoring suppression is scoped to the flagged goal while the guidance it cites scopes it to the person.
   - Fix: Write the decline verbatim (see rewrites), and change the suppression rule to person-level: where an eating-disorder or excessive-exercise signal is present, remove numeric self-monitoring across all of that user's actions, not just the flagged goal, and record the decision.
   - Basis: NICE NG183 1.1.5 (do not allow unhealthy or dangerous goals) and 1.4.3 ("for people at risk of an eating disorder or excessive exercise, consider interventions that do not include self-monitoring" — framed by person, not goal); NBHWC Scope of Practice (no nutrition consultation, no exercise prescription); FDA General Wellness excluded claim "treat an eating disorder"; AF §7C item 6.

12. **§5.0 First contact; §12 row 5.0** — The first-contact script sets a notification hour and a review evening and nothing else, yet §12 claims ICF 3.1–3.4 and NBHWC 1.5.1; there is no coaching agreement, no explanation of what coaching is, and no appropriateness check — and the appropriateness question that CF §7 Phase 1 supplied was dropped without being logged in §15.
   - Fix: Add two lines to §5.0: one explaining the nature of the coaching relationship and the client's role, and one appropriateness check (restore CF §7 Phase 1's "Is there anything going on in your life right now that makes this a hard time?"). Move the ICF 3.2–3.4 claims to §5.1/§5.2 where they are actually met.
   - Basis: ICF Code of Ethics 1.1 "explain the nature of coaching before starting"; NBHWC 1.5.1.3 "Determine if coaching is appropriate at this time"; ICF PCC markers 3.2 measures of success, 3.3 why it matters, 3.4 what to address; AF §7A Intake row (1.5.1.1–1.5.1.3, D&J BC2 screen).


### Should fix

1. **§2 scope constraint 9** — NBHWC 4.4.1.5 is quoted in the research as unqualified ("If using generative AI, do not involve client data") but restated in the method as "never pass user data to external generative AI beyond the coaching pipeline", a carve-out that exempts the entire product without saying so.
   - Fix: Restate the constraint honestly: name that Adler is itself a generative AI processing client data, state which processors see it (D13 on-device SR, D14 Neon), state that NBHWC 4.4.1.5 as written targets a coach pasting client material into a third-party model, and record the interpretation in §15 rather than editing the standard silently.
   - Basis: NBHWC Content Outline 4.4.1.5; NBHWC 4.3.8 disclose AI use; Utah HB 452 (no sale or sharing of individually identifiable health information); ICF Code 2.5 (obligations apply "through technology systems and AI").

2. **§5.5 Weekly review, items 1–12** — Twelve agenda items containing roughly fifteen questions and three separate rating batteries cannot be delivered in five minutes while holding coach word share under 50%, R:Q ≥ 1:1 and %CR ≥ 40%; the structure guarantees an interrogation profile rather than a coaching conversation.
   - Fix: Cap the review at six conversational moves; stagger the instruments (automaticity and confidence weekly; load and the two session-rating items fortnightly, alternating); collect the record and the unusual-week flag as taps on the card before the conversation starts so the spoken review begins at item 3.
   - Basis: ICF PCC markers 7.8 ("allows the client to do most of the talking") and 7.6 (one question at a time); MITI 4.2.1 R:Q ≥ 1:1 and %CR ≥ 40%; AF §7B item 3 fail condition "coach talks more than user".

3. **§5.5 Weekly review, item 5** — "Still the goal? Same size, same reason?" is three closed questions in one turn, which is both the method's own "no multi-question turns" rule and an ICF fail marker.
   - Fix: Replace with one open question (see rewrites) and let the size and reason come out of the answer or a follow-up turn.
   - Basis: ICF PCC marker 7.6; AF §7B item 3 fail condition "multi-question turns"; the method's own §5.5 rule "no multi-question turns. S (ICF 7.6, 7.8)".

4. **§5.1 Discovery, step 1** — "So the thread is [value]" lets the coach supply an abstraction; ICF marker 6.2 requires the client's words, and a coach-chosen value label is what the method elsewhere forbids as a trait attribution.
   - Fix: Constrain the slot to a word the client used in the preceding two turns, and forbid coach-generated value nouns; if no such word exists, ask again rather than name it.
   - Basis: ICF PCC marker 6.2 "the client's words"; NBHWC 3.2 reflections; the method's own §7.11 language rule (report the record, never the interpretation).

5. **§5.1 Discovery, step 2; §11 Measurement table** — A bespoke two-item autonomous/controlled ruler with a hard cut-point ("≤ 0 is a should flag") is used as a routing instrument, while the validated importance ruler that both research files name is dropped entirely, so the method has no measure of whether a goal matters to the client.
   - Fix: Add the importance ruler (0–10) alongside confidence at goal formation and at review, with the standard follow-up "What makes it a 6 and not a 3?", and downgrade the concordance items to conversation openers rather than a threshold that shelves goals.
   - Basis: NBHWC 3.1.7 scaling; CF §3 and §6: "validated single-item importance/readiness/confidence rulers (0-10) replace stage classification", readiness ruler validated as a 2-item measure; MITI Appendix A cautions against coding where the change target is not yet a specified behaviour.

6. **§5.11 Wellbeing route** — "A doctor or counsellor could help with the energy side, and I'll keep the goal small meanwhile" positions the coach as co-managing a suspected depression alongside a clinician, on the basis of a WHO-5 screening threshold.
   - Fix: Split the two moves: refer, then separately ask the client what they want to do with the action. Remove any first-person clause in which the coach decides the plan while a clinical concern is live (see rewrites).
   - Basis: NBHWC Scope of Practice (no psychological therapeutic interventions); AF §6 "may not... use results to 'manage or treat'"; the method's own scope constraint 3 (no "manage" or "treat" language); Utah HB 452 definition of a mental health chatbot; FDA General Wellness Category 1.

7. **§5.3 Action design** — "What's the version you'd be slightly embarrassed by but would actually do?" injects an evaluative, shame-adjacent frame the client did not offer, and presumes the client's stated size is wrong.
   - Fix: Reframe around the client's worst day, which gets the same shrink without importing embarrassment (see rewrites); this also stays consistent with §8.3's perfectionism adaptation, which the embarrassment framing works against.
   - Basis: NBHWC 1.2.3 (no directing) and Scope of Practice; MITI Partnership (coach does not assume the expert role about the client's motives); CF §4 on shame-avoidance as a maintainer of avoidance.

8. **§5.11 Stage 2** — After the ASQ screening question the method asks an acuity follow-up and then grades its own response by the answer ("If immediate: recommend being with another person and away from means"), which is risk stratification performed without a clinician anywhere in the loop.
   - Fix: Keep the acuity question only as a routing switch between the crisis line and emergency services; state explicitly in §5.11 that the coach performs no risk assessment, forms no risk judgement, and stores no risk level, and that the same hand-off runs on any positive screen.
   - Basis: AF §6: a non-clinical product may ask C-SSRS items 1–2 then hand off; assessment is outside ICF/NBHWC scope; NBHWC 4.2.2 refer "for imminent danger"; UCL Counselling for Depression lists "ability to assess and manage risk of self-harm" as a competence Adler does not hold (AF §3.1).


### Rewrites

1. **§5.1 Discovery, step 5**
   - Current: Which of these are you choosing to be bad at until then?
   - Proposed: You don't want to put any of them down. That's yours to decide — they can all stay active. What would the first week look like with all of them running?
   - Why: Removes a Confront count that auto-fails the method's own MI-Non-Adherent = 0 gate; replaces it with a simple reflection of sustain talk, an Emphasizing Autonomy count, and one open question.

2. **§5.1 Discovery, Rules (one active goal)**
   - Current: Starting one thing at a time is what the research on goal conflict says works. Your call.
   - Proposed: Want to know what the research says about running several at once? … Goals that compete for the same hours tend to both stall, so the usual advice is to start with one. What do you make of that for your list?
   - Why: Converts a Persuade count into Persuade-with-Permission and completes the ask-offer-ask cycle NBHWC 1.2.3/3.2 requires; the closing question returns the decision to the client.

3. **§5.1 Discovery, step 2**
   - Current: That one sounds like it belongs to someone else's list. We can park it, or you can tell me what you'd want instead.
   - Proposed: You put an 8 on guilt and a 3 on wanting it. What do you make of that gap?
   - Why: Replaces a coach-assigned motive with a reflection of the client's own two ratings plus one open question, keeping the interpretation with the client and out of assessment territory.

4. **§5.1 Discovery, step 5**
   - Current: It sounds like [A] is the one. Say it back in your own words.
   - Proposed: Of everything on that list, which one do you want to start with? … Say it back in your own words.
   - Why: NBHWC scope requires self-determined goals; the conflict score can inform an offer but must not announce the outcome.

5. **§5.4 Daily check-in, "No time" template**
   - Current: What's the two-minute version, and what's right before it?
   - Proposed: There wasn't a window today. What would have had to be different for there to be one?
   - Why: Removes a stacked question, adds a reflection before the question, and asks about the situation rather than handing the client the answer (which is also §1 commitment 8).

6. **§5.4 Daily check-in, "Too hard" template**
   - Current: Halve it. Which part was hard, the start, the middle, or knowing how?
   - Proposed: It asked more of you than it looked like it would. Which part was hard — starting it, staying in it, or knowing how?
   - Why: "Halve it" is a directive with no permission (NBHWC 1.2.3); the replacement opens with a complex reflection and keeps a single question, and the shrink offer moves to the next turn behind permission.

7. **§5.4 Daily check-in, "Didn't feel like it" template**
   - Current: What was the feeling right before you skipped?" then the one-line behavioural-activation rationale, once: "Mood follows action more than the other way round. The urge is skip; the opposite action is the two-minute version.
   - Proposed: Part of you didn't want to and you checked in anyway. What was going on right before?
   - Why: Deletes an unsolicited Persuade count and a borrowed clinical term; substitutes an affirmation (Affirm > 0) plus a double-sided reflection (Soften) plus one open question.

8. **§5.4 Daily check-in, After Yes**
   - Current: Done, on a day with two late nights.
   - Proposed: Two late nights and you still found the ten minutes.
   - Why: Same facts, but codes as Affirm rather than Giving Information, satisfying §12's Affirm > 0 and NBHWC 1.6's affirmation element without a person-level adjective or a grade.

9. **§5.5 Weekly review, item 8**
   - Current: Mornings on Wednesday and Friday instead, straight after coffee? If two of those feel harder than expected, we go back, and that's the plan working.
   - Proposed: Before I say what I'm seeing — what would you change about next week, if anything? … Can I put one option next to that? … Mornings on Wednesday and Friday, straight after coffee. If two of those feel harder than expected we go back, and that's the plan working. What do you think of it?
   - Why: Makes the only change-proposal move in the product a full ask-offer-ask, which §11 lists as an automatic fail condition when absent.

10. **§5.5 Weekly review, item 5**
   - Current: Still the goal? Same size, same reason?
   - Proposed: What's changed, if anything, about why this one matters to you?
   - Why: Removes three closed questions in one turn (ICF 7.6 fail) and replaces them with one open question that evokes rather than checks.

11. **§5.5 Weekly review, closing**
   - Current: A review ends with the user summarising the plan in their words. **S (NBHWC 1.6).**
   - Proposed: Give it back to me in your words — what you're doing, how often, starting when. … What's the one thing you're taking out of this week? … Besides the daily tap, how do you want to hold yourself to it? … Next one is [day] at [time].
   - Why: Supplies the four NBHWC 1.6 follow-up elements the method currently claims but never scripts: client summary in SMART form, takeaway, client's own accountability method (3.9.7), next contact.

12. **§5.2 Goal formation, balanced course**
   - Current: Most things like this take about two months to feel routine, anywhere from three weeks to a year, and the first two weeks are where most people wobble. I'll keep the steps small so we get through those.
   - Proposed: Can I give you the timeline the research shows? … About two months to feel routine, with a wide spread either side — under three weeks for some people, eight or nine months for others — and the first fortnight is where most people wobble. What size would you want to start at, knowing that?
   - Why: Adds the permission turn, corrects the range to Lally 2010's 18–254 days, and returns plan ownership to the client instead of the coach declaring "I'll keep the steps small".

13. **§5.11 Wellbeing route**
   - Current: It sounds like this has been a heavy stretch. A doctor or counsellor could help with the energy side, and I'll keep the goal small meanwhile. Would you like some options for finding one?
   - Proposed: It sounds like this has been a heavy stretch. That's worth talking to a doctor or a counsellor about — it's outside what I can help with. Would you like some options for finding one? … And what do you want to do with [action] in the meantime?
   - Why: Removes the co-management clause that reads as the coach managing a suspected depression alongside a clinician, names the limit explicitly, and returns the plan decision to the client.

14. **§5.11 Goal-text risk markers**
   - Current: (no script supplied; the method states only "decline to coach that goal, say why plainly, offer resources")
   - Proposed: I'm not going to coach toward a weight or shape target — that's outside what I can do safely, and I'd get it wrong. I can work with you on something you'd do, like the training itself, and I can point you to people who work on the other part. Which of those do you want?
   - Why: Supplies the missing verbatim for the highest-risk decline in the product, keeps the limit plain, avoids any diagnostic implication, and ends with a client choice rather than a refusal.

15. **§5.0 First contact**
   - Current: Want to start with a conversation about what you're working on, or add something yourself first?
   - Proposed: Coaching here means you pick the goal and the plan, and I ask questions, keep the record, and tell you what I'm seeing. Before we start — is there anything going on right now that would make this a hard time to take something on? … Want to start with a conversation about what you're working on, or add something yourself first?
   - Why: Supplies ICF Code 1.1 (explain the nature of coaching before starting) and NBHWC 1.5.1.3 (determine if coaching is appropriate at this time), both currently claimed in §12 but unscripted; restores the appropriateness question CF §7 Phase 1 contained.

16. **§5.3 Action design**
   - Current: What's the version you'd be slightly embarrassed by but would actually do?
   - Proposed: If tomorrow turned out to be your worst day this month, what part of that would still happen?
   - Why: Gets the same downward sizing without importing an evaluative frame the client did not offer, and stays consistent with §8.3's perfectionism adaptation.


### Evidence gaps

- NBHWC 1.5.3 requires vision and a current-vs-desired-state gap. The word "vision" appears in the method only inside a prohibition ("vision-boarding"), and no script asks for a desired state or a gap. §12 claims NBHWC 1.5.2–1.5.4 for §5.1 anyway. Note that the Kappes & Oettingen finding the method cites forbids indulging in the future, not contrasting it with the present — mental contrasting requires the desired state the method deleted.

- "SMART" appears zero times in the method, yet §11 lists it as one of the sixteen checklist items and §12 cites NBHWC 3.9.2 for §5.3 (the outline item is 3.9.2.2, SMART). Neither research file supports dropping it; AF §1.3 names it explicitly.

- §11 reproduces the MITI thresholds (Relational 3.5, Technical 3.0, %CR 40%, R:Q 1:1) without the manual's own caveat, quoted in AF §3.2, that they are "based upon expert opinion, and currently lack normative or other validity data", and without MITI Appendix A's limitation that coding is of "limited value" when the change goal cannot be specified as a behaviour or when only engaging/focusing is intended — which describes §5.0 and §5.1 steps 0–1, two of the windows the method proposes to gate releases on.

- AF §1.3 states plainly that NBHWC has no published fidelity instrument and that "the outline works as a checklist". §11 presents the "NBHWC sixteen-item process checklist, ≥ 13 per week" as though it were an NBHWC instrument with an NBHWC pass mark. It is the method's own construction and should be labelled as such.

- §5.5 item 5 asserts "alliance is task and goal, not bond; r = .24–.29" and §2 asserts "26–52% of coaching seekers carry clinical-level distress". Neither figure appears in coaching-framework.md or accreditation-frameworks.md; both are attributed to deep/humanistic, which was not supplied. Until that file is in the pack, both are unverifiable claims carrying decision weight (§5.5 item 5 routing, §2 "scope detection is a core feature").

- The autonomous/controlled 0–10 concordance pair in §5.1 step 2 is used with a hard decision threshold ("≤ 0 is a should flag") but no research file cites a validated instrument or cut-point for it, while the validated importance/readiness ruler that CF §3 and §6 both name is dropped from §11's measurement table entirely.

- §5.2's "anywhere from three weeks to a year" does not match Lally 2010 (median 66 days, range 18–254, i.e. about 2.5 weeks to 8.5 months) — the upper bound is inflated by roughly 40% in a script line the method requires to be honest.

- §5.7 sets growth at "+10–25% of dose per step... never > 30% in any two weeks" while §5.7 and §12 both cite "ACSM 2–10% progression principle" as the authority. AF §4 states explicitly that ACSM 2009 "argues for a smaller default than Adler's 25% growth step". The method cites a standard it exceeds by two to three times.

- §0 defines four rule labels (E, H, S, D). §5.1, §5.2, §5.3, §5.4, §5.5 and §5.6 also carry labels "A" and "B" (lines 155, 156, 185, 210, 246, 282, 299), which are undefined. Any eval keyed to the E/H/S/D provenance system cannot grade those rules — including "Same warmth on a miss as on a hit" and "Adherence below 50% for two weeks forces a plan change".

- NBHWC 2026–2030 domain 5, Health and Wellness, is 20% of the exam and has no counterpart anywhere in the method beyond "never prescribe". There is no scripted move for the common case where a client's goal needs health knowledge the coach cannot supply ("how much should I be lifting", "is this enough protein") — the method says what not to do and never says what to say.

### Evidence gaps

- No cited study supports the three-face affect tap as an autoregulation input. Larsen 2021 and Greig 2020 tested repetitions-in-reserve load prescription in resistance training; P22's evidence is affective valence during exercise. The method marks the merged tap "E principle" on borrowed lineage.

- The deload defaults have no supporting effect. Bell 2023 is a Delphi consensus (expert opinion, grade D under the synthesis's own scheme), Bosquet 2007's 41–60% cut is a competition taper in trained athletes that keeps frequency and intensity, and the only RCT, Coleman 2024 (N = 50), found no hypertrophy difference and slightly less strength. The research file's own verdict is "benefit unproven, cost near zero"; the method upgrades this to "H on E" and makes it a scheduled 4–8 week default, including for actions still at a two-minute floor where there is no accumulated stimulus to unload.

- No evidence supports the growth step sizes outside running-injury epidemiology. Buist 2008 (RCT, N = 532) found the 10% rule gave the same injury rate as the standard programme (21% vs 20%), and Nielsen 2014's > 30% finding sat in a model that was not significant overall. - No evidence supports the specific gate values (80% of intended days, SRBAI ≥ 4/7 and ≥ 5/7, eight weeks to maintenance). Lally 2010 and Singh 2024 report time-to-asymptote distributions; Fitts & Posner describe stages without cut-points. P8 grades these "H numbers" and the method should too.

- No evidence supports a concurrency cap of three including automatic actions. - No common load cut-point exists in the sources. Foster 1998 and Kellmann 2018 both specify individual thresholds; the ≥ 7 hold-everything rule is a product decision presented without a mark.

- Foster's monotony (mean/SD of the load record), which the research file explicitly says to keep, is absent from §7's stored signals and from §15's divergence log, so the method has no read on a record that is uniformly moderate versus one that is spiky — the variable that predicted illness in the source.

- The measurement layer has no dose measure. §11 records Yes/Partly/No and a weekly GAS level, so a rule expressed as "+10–25% of dose" cannot be verified against what was actually done, and Partly is ambiguous between under-dose and floor-dose.

- SRBAI's validity is unestablished for anything other than a consistently cued, near-daily behaviour, yet the method uses automaticity as the gate for session goals grown deliberately by increasing the spacing gap (Cepeda 2006/2008: optimal gap grows with the retention interval), where the cue is by design not stable.

- Progression evidence for dyadic goals is absent entirely — §3 suspends comparison and numeric tracking, which removes every input the §5.6/§5.7 engine runs on, and no replacement decision rule is stated.

2. **§5.4, barrier template for "Didn't feel like it"**
   - Current: Mood follows action more than the other way round. The urge is skip; the opposite action is the two-minute version.
   - Proposed: Noted. That feeling is what the two-minute version is for. Is there anything about the setup that would make tomorrow need less deciding — same time, smaller, or a different time?
   - Why: The current line makes a mood claim (§10) and delivers the rationale of behavioural activation plus DBT opposite action (§2 constraint 3). The replacement carries the supported move instead: situation modification beat "use willpower" at d = 0.56–0.63 (P7).

3. **§5.4 and §5.5 item 6, automaticity item**
   - Current: Does this feel automatic yet, 1 to 7?
   - Proposed: 1 to 7: "[the action] is something I do automatically."
   - Why: The validated single-item SRBAI proxy is the statement "X is something I do automatically" (Gardner 2024, 16 datasets, per coaching-framework.md §160). "Does this feel automatic yet" is a different question — it cues a yes/no judgement about progress rather than the stimulus–response item, and the method gates growth, the second build and maintenance on it.

4. **§5.5 item 9, personal reference class**
   - Current: Last three weeks you planned 6 and did 4. Plan against 4?
   - Proposed: Last three weeks you planned 6 and did 4 on average. Three weeks is thin, so call that 4 or 5 on a normal week. Keep planning 6, or plan 5 and treat 5 as the week working?
   - Why: Three weekly rates each carry SE 17.3 pp, so a point estimate of 4 is fitting noise, and re-planning to the observed mean lowers the denominator of the done-rate — the same construct-invalidating move §7.10 guards against for shrinks.

5. **§5.7, growth script**
   - Current: This seems to run itself now. Keep it as is, make it a notch bigger, or start on the next thing?
   - Proposed: Same version again this week. The growth is in the repetitions. If you ever want to make it bigger or start on the next thing, say so and we'll check whether it's ready.
   - Why: The current line is the coach offering growth because the record looks good, which §5.7's own Rules block forbids ("Growth is never offered because the record looks good; it is offered when the gate is met and the user asks").

6. **§5.10, playbook line**
   - Current: Three times you moved a thing to mornings and three times the next week was better. Smaller than feels sensible was the right size both times you shrank. I'll carry that into the next one.
   - Proposed: Three times you moved a thing to mornings and three times the next week was better — worth trying again, not a rule yet. You shrank twice and both times it went better; twice is too few to call. I'll carry both into the next one as things to try.
   - Why: "Was the right size both times you shrank" states a two-exposure verdict as fact, which §7.1 (P ≥ 0.90, ≥ 4 exposures per side) and §7.11 both forbid, and which idiographic-inference implication E lists verbatim among the prohibited constructions.


### Evidence gaps

- The engine's headline numbers (P ≥ 0.90 / 5 pp / 4 exposures; the 10/30/46% pooling weights; the one-per-year budget) are presented in §7 as settled, but all of them are functions of two variance components the source calls assumptions (done-rate τ = 0.20, move-effect τ = 8 pp, the latter "may be generous"). §7 carries no note of this and §11 drops the test that would estimate them.

- §0 fact 3 gives only the single-week SE (17 pp) and never the SE of the week-to-week delta (24.5 pp independent, ~30 pp at ρ = 0.3), which is the quantity every §5.6 trigger and every weekly verdict actually uses. The reliability of a 7-day rate as a person score (0.57) is also absent, while §5.5 item 1 invites the user to compare "four of six this week, three of six last week".

- "Accept roughly 25 false positives per true positive" (§5.11) is a design choice carried from safety-risk-detection.md with no empirical derivation; the cited PPV range for risk instruments (5.5–16.1%, and ASQ PPV 41%) does not imply 25:1, and synthesis §7 item 8 states that the cost that would set this threshold is "unmeasured anywhere". The method fixes the number and drops the test.

- "Why do you think that worked?" is labelled "the active ingredient, g = 0.55" (§5.5 item 3). Bisra 2018's g = 0.55 is prompted self-explanation on learning tasks; no dismantling study attributes the after-action review's effect to that prompt. It is a plausible component, not an identified active ingredient.

- "(feedback-informed rule, d = .15 for off-track)" in §5.5 is carried from humanistic-existential-common-factors.md §186 with no study, sample or instrument attached anywhere in the repo. Either source it or drop the number and keep the rule as a heuristic.

- "+0.12 F1" (§5.11) is attributed to restating the safety instruction "at session start and after any topic change". Kalinich 2026 showed recovery from depth-related decay by restating the instruction; the specific schedule the method commits to was not the tested protocol, and no schedule-specific effect exists.

- cultural-generalisability.md §168 instructs that six reversal caveats be stated in adler-method.md (positive/negative feedback, choice and intrinsic motivation, obligation goals, search for meaning, self-improvement framing, the US school base of the growth-mindset evidence). Only the obligation one (Miller 2011) appears, inside a §5.1 rule; the other five are absent and §14 q7 treats the issue as an open copy question rather than a stated limit.

- §0 fact 2 asserts that no trial exists in this population, but synthesis §4 item 12 reports "the strongest trial in exactly Adler's population" — self-reported study skills, time management and wellbeing moved while GPA and credits stayed flat. That is the single most transferable result in the repo for a 16–40 optimizer product and it appears nowhere in the method.

- §11's alliance row says "if bond does not [predict adherence], stop tuning for it", which presupposes tuning for bond and contradicts §10 ("Never optimise on satisfaction, alliance or retention"); the source's instruction is to treat bond as a diagnostic only. Splitting an already-shortened WAI-SR-6 into subscales of two items each also leaves no usable subscale reliability, which the method does not acknowledge.

- The method never defines the boundary of a "week" relative to the user-chosen review day, timezone changes or a partial first week, so ≥ 80%, ≤ 4 of 7 and "4 of 6" are undefined at the edges — including for the reactivity rule that voids weeks 1–2 of any new tap.

- §15 step 5 refers to refreshing "the claims register", but no claims register exists in the method or the research files; the ceiling in §0 fact 1 is the only claim-limiting artefact and it is never operationalised into a list of sentences the product may and may not say publicly.


## Consumer product design / simplicity bar: three screens, one tap a day, no manual (D4). Taps and questions counted per day and per week; weekly review timeboxed against D5's five minutes; ruler inventory; rule pairs that produce undefined UI states; check-in follow-up depth; script lines read aloud on a phone.

**Verdict:** fail

**Summary.** The method does not currently fit the container it was written for. Counted against D3 and D4, a missed action costs five taps plus typing, a normal week with one action costs roughly 21 taps and two typed answers before the review, and the twelve-item review contains seventeen questions, which at the method's own fidelity rules (one question per turn, a reflection between questions, reflection-to-question ≥ 1:1) is 51 turns in the 300 seconds D5 allows. Several rule pairs produce states no implementation can resolve: Partly has no numeric weight although three gates depend on the adherence rate; hold and change triggers can both fire with no precedence; weeks 1-2 are simultaneously "never a baseline" and a mandatory shrink window; §5.3 offers the cue-time reminder that §10 and §5.4 forbid; and §5.7's Autoregulate row offers growth on a good record that the next paragraph explicitly forbids. Four verbatim scripts stack questions in a single turn, which §11 lists as an automatic fail, so the draft would fail its own assessment on its own text. The most consequential omission is design, not evidence: the stage-2 suicide-screening question is specified with a 25:1 false-positive tolerance and no cap, cooldown or screen definition, and the three-screen inventory has no home for the paywall, the settings the product's own decisions require, or the visible "not enough data yet" state §7.1 mandates.


### Must fix

1. **§5.4 Daily check-in (lines 225-233)** — A single missed action costs five taps plus typing, so the product's stated interaction budget of one tap a day is exceeded on any day that matters.
   - Fix: Cut the miss path from five interactions to three: barrier tap → merged "Tomorrow: same · smaller · not sure I'll do it" tap (replacing the separate 0-10 recovery ruler and the template's tomorrow question) → stop. Make every barrier follow-up ("What's the two-minute version...", "What was last night like?", "What was the feeling right before you skipped?") an unposed optional "add a note" affordance, not a question the coach asks and waits on. Target and publish the budget: hit day = 1 tap, miss day = 3 taps, week = ≤ 15 taps for one action.
   - Basis: D3 ("All actions answered on one screen: Yes / Partly / No, and No opens one barrier tap... Occasional one-tap follow-ups") and D4 ("Simplicity constraints stay: three screens, one tap a day, no manual"). Counted against the method as written: hit day = 2 taps (Yes + face), miss day = 5 taps + free text (No, barrier, "something came up" detail, 0-10 recovery ruler, tomorrow choice), plus the weekly automaticity item = 21 taps and 2 typed answers per week for ONE action; §5.7 permits three actions, i.e. up to 63 taps/week before the review. The method's own §11 note that consumer usage half-life is 5-10 days makes this budget fatal.

2. **§5.5 Weekly review (lines 261-283)** — The twelve-item structure contains seventeen questions and, under the method's own fidelity rules, cannot be delivered in the five minutes D5 fixes.
   - Fix: Cut to six user-facing steps: (1) record in counts, (2) one merged week-shape tap ("Normal · Unusual · Heavy") replacing items 2 and 7, (3) one success, (4) one miss, (5) goal consensus + next-week confidence, (6) one change or hold with the disagreement line and the personal reference class folded into the proposal, closing with the user's own summary. Move item 6's automaticity to the daily tap only (it is already collected there), item 11's session rating to monthly, and item 12's parked-goal sweep off the review and onto the Goals screen as a monthly card.
   - Basis: D5 ("five minutes, voice or text"). Arithmetic: 17 questions × (1 reflection + 1 question + 1 user answer) = 51 turns in 300 s = 5.9 s per turn, against §5 header ("one question per turn, a reflection between questions") and §11's rubric (reflection-to-question ≥ 1:1, complex reflections ≥ 40%, coach word share < 50%). At a realistic 20-25 s per reflection-question-answer cycle in voice the current structure is a 6-10 minute session, and the two items carrying the actual effect (success/active-ingredient, g = 0.55; miss) get squeezed first.

3. **§5.1 line 150, §5.2 line 176, §5.3 line 199, §5.5 lines 267 and 269** — Four verbatim scripts stack two or three questions into a single turn, which the method's own §11 rubric lists as an automatic fail.
   - Fix: Split every stacked line into sequential turns (see rewrites). Add a lint on the method file itself: any script line containing more than one '?' fails review before the transcript evals ever run.
   - Basis: §5.5 Rules, marked S: "Coach word share under 50%; no multi-question turns. S (ICF 7.6, 7.8)." §11 eval pack: "ICF PCC markers as a presence checklist per phase, coach word share < 50%, no multi-question turns." §5 header: "script (verbatim, one question per turn, a reflection between questions)". The draft's own scripts would fail the draft's own assessment.

4. **§5.4 After Partly (line 229) and §11 Adherence row** — Partly has no defined numeric weight, so every gate in the method — 80%, ≤ 4 of 7, adherence below 50% — is uncomputable and the Today screen cannot state a true count.
   - Fix: Define it in one line: "A Partly at or above the stored good-enough counts as done; a Partly below it counts as half in the weekly rate and never as a miss for barrier counting. The Today record shows both: '4 of 6 done, 1 partly.'" Also stop the silent Partly→done conversion: if the app records done after a Partly tap, the row must say so on screen.
   - Basis: §11 ("Adherence | Yes / Partly / No per action per day | daily | primary outcome") plus §5.6 rule 2 ("≤ 4 of 7 done"), §5.7 ("≥ 80% of intended days"), §5.5 Rules ("Adherence below 50% for two weeks forces a plan change, d = .15 for off-track"). Three thresholds depend on a denominator the document never defines. A UI that shows a count contradicting the user's own tap breaches §6's "Description is the benchmark".

5. **§5.3 Action design (lines 195 and 201) and §3 vocabulary table** — The floor question and the good-enough question are the same question asked twice, three minutes apart, using the same word "minimum", producing two schema fields the user cannot tell apart.
   - Fix: Ask size once and standard once, in different words. Keep "What's the smallest version of this you could do on your worst day?" for `actions.floor`, and rewrite the good-enough line to be about the stopping standard, not the size (see rewrites). If they cannot be distinguished in one sentence a user would repeat back, merge the fields and delete `actions.good_enough`.
   - Basis: D4 "no manual". §3 defines Floor as "the two-minute version; the size that counts as done on a bad day"; §5.3 asks for good-enough as "What counts as done? Give me the minimum." The perfectionism adaptation in §8.3 ("count the minimum as done; treat a Partly tap on a completed minimum as a standards signal") depends on the two being distinct, so merging silently breaks §8.3 — hence the rewrite rather than a delete.

6. **§5.6 Hold/Change (lines 296-305) vs §5.5 Rules (line 282) and §7.9** — Hold triggers and change triggers can fire in the same week with no stated precedence, so the deterministic adjustment selector has no defined output.
   - Fix: State precedence explicitly, in this order: safety route > hold-all (load ≥ 7, unusual week, low window) > constraint check > everything else. Then fix the two specific collisions: weeks 1-2 cannot both be "never a baseline" and mandate a shrink — restrict the fourteen-day two-miss shrink to a floor-size offer that is not logged as the week's change; and say which wins when adherence < 50% for two weeks coincides with load ≥ 7 (it should be shrink-everything, which satisfies both).
   - Basis: §5.6 "Hold when (any)... the week was flagged unusual... load ≥ 7" versus "Change when (any)... First fourteen days of a new action: two misses in any seven → shrink now (P17)" and §7.9 "Weeks 1-2 of any new tap are reactivity-inflated and never a baseline"; §5.5 "Adherence below 50% for two weeks forces a plan change, not encouragement." §13 requires "Adjustment selection is deterministic" — undefined precedence makes that impossible to implement or eval.

7. **§5.4 barrier templates (line 235) vs §5.6 Rules and §7.8** — The daily "same, smaller, or a different time?" question can change the plan every day, up to seven changes a week, defeating the one-change-a-week rule the whole adaptive engine rests on.
   - Fix: Scope the daily choice to the next day only and say so in the schema: it writes to a `next_day_override` and never to `actions`, and it never counts as the week's change. One line in §5.4 Rules: "Anything chosen at the daily tap applies to tomorrow only. Plan changes happen at the review."
   - Basis: §5.6 Rules "Never two changes at once"; §7.8 "One change a week, maximum." §5.4 already scopes the 4-6 recovery band correctly ("Want tomorrow to be just the gate?") but the barrier template's tomorrow question is unscoped.    - Fix: Restore "confidence ≥ 8" to the Grow gate, restore "< 7" as the pre-median absolute shrink cut (or state the reason for 5), and if either change stands, add it to §15's divergence list. 11. **§5.11 Stage 2 (lines 380-381)** — The suicide-screening question has a stated tolerance of roughly 25 false positives per true positive and no cap, cooldown, or defined screen, so a routine user of a goal app can be asked "Are you having thoughts of killing yourself?" repeatedly with no rule governing it.
   - Fix: Specify, in §5.11: the question appears as a full-screen interruption with no other controls, is asked at most once per 72 hours per user unless stage 1 fires on explicit language, and after two negatives in 30 days the classifier raises its threshold for that user for the sensitising-context class only (never for explicit language). Define what the user sees after a No: one sentence, then the check-in resumes where it was.
   - Basis: §5.11 "Accept roughly 25 false positives per true positive." §2 scope constraint 11 ("Never infer or display the user's emotional or mental state as an assessment") and §11's automatic-fail requirement of 100% on crisis screens set a recall floor but no precision floor and no interaction spec. §7 already stores "false-positive tolerance on safety check-ins" as a signal and never says what consumes it.    - Why: Removes an 11-point ruler and the separate tomorrow question from the miss path (two interactions to one) while preserving the three routing bands §5.4 already defines: same = acknowledge and stop; smaller = floor for tomorrow only; not sure = the ≤ 3 band that opens the same-evening conversation. Basis: SC 2.5.8 target sizing and D3's "one barrier tap".

8. **§5.3, line 201 (good-enough)**
   - Current: Good-enough, once per action: "What counts as done? Give me the minimum."
   - Proposed: Good-enough, once per action: "When you've done it well enough to stop, what does that look like?"
   - Why: The current wording repeats the floor question asked six lines earlier and uses the same word, "minimum". The replacement asks about the stopping standard rather than the size, which is what §8.3's perfectionism adaptation actually needs, and keeps the two fields distinguishable without a manual.

9. **§5.1, line 138 (quick-add routing) — first coach message after a quick add**
   - Current: quick-add arrivals run step 2 only
   - Proposed: quick-add arrivals get one line and nothing else: "Added. Want me to help make it stick? Two questions, about a minute." Step 2 runs only if they say yes.
   - Why: D2 specifies a low-pressure offer after a quick add. As written, the first sentence Adler says to a quick-add user is the guilt ruler from step 2.

10. **§6, line 404 (canonical description example)**
   - Current: You did it on a day with two late nights.
   - Proposed: You did it. Four of six this week.
   - Why: The exemplar for the method's central tone rule relies on sleep data the product is forbidden to collect or infer (§8.11 "Store nothing physiological, infer nothing"; §10 "no sensor or calendar smart timing"). The replacement is description from the tap, which is the only data the design guarantees.


### Evidence gaps

- D3 places the one-tap feeling follow-up BEFORE a skip ("feeling before a skip"); §5.4 moves the three faces to AFTER a Yes and converts the pre-skip feeling into a free-text question. The decision does not support the move and §15's divergence log does not record it.

- No supplied file supports five minutes as an achievable review length. D5 asserts it as a product decision; nothing measures it. Ship a timed dry run of the six-item structure with three synthetic personas over voice and publish the p50/p90 before the number goes on the landing page.

- §5.5 leans its largest claimed lever on after-action-review effects (d = 0.67-0.79, Ellis & Davidi; Keiser & Arthur), which come from facilitated team and training debriefs. Nothing in the supplied evidence shows that a solo five-minute in-app review reproduces them, and the method treats the transfer as settled.

- §5.4 calls the weekly automaticity item a "single-item SRBAI proxy". SRBAI is a four-item instrument; no validation of a one-item proxy is cited anywhere. Four gates (hold, grow, add-a-second-build, maintenance) rest on it, yet §12's unanchored list names only the faces, the reference class and the session rating.

- §5.9 cites Milkman's megastudy (+27%, best of 54 arms) for "rewarding the return", then §6 and §10 forbid every form of reward the product could issue. The permitted substitute is one line of intrinsic noticing, which is not the tested intervention, so the effect size does not carry. State what "reward the return" means in a reward-free product, or drop the number.

- §13 states design requirements with no accessibility floor, although the landing research supplies them: body ≥ 16px, contrast 4.5:1 (SC 1.4.3), targets ≥ 24×24 aiming 48 (SC 2.5.8), reduced-motion honoured. The barrier sheet, the rulers and the face row are the densest controls in the product and are the ones most likely to fail these.

- D4 makes the "grandma test" a binding simplicity bar but nothing defines a pass: no task, no time budget, no tester, no counted-taps target. Without a numeric budget (proposed: hit day 1 tap, miss day ≤ 3, review ≤ 8 questions) the bar cannot be assessed and will erode item by item.

- The paywall and free trial (D12) appear nowhere in the method, so their position relative to a 4-10 minute discovery conversation and D15's sign-in-first default is undecided. This is the single largest unmodelled interaction in first-run.

- Open question 1 asks the panel to rule on compressing the barrier set to six with no usability data of any kind; open question 4 asks whether the weekly session rating is itself a dosage cost and proposes no way to find out. Both are answerable cheaply with the randomisation machinery §7.4 already specifies (send versus hold on the SRS items, seeded), and neither is scheduled.
