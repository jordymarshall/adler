# Individual differences: conscientiousness, ADHD, procrastination, perfectionism, chronotype, mood, autism

Research date: 2026-09-02. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `goal-discovery-frameworks.md` (GDF §n), `deep/motivation-science.md` (MS §n), `deep/self-regulation-theory.md` (SRT §n), `deep/humanistic-existential-common-factors.md` (HE §n), `deep/bct-effectiveness.md` (BCT §n). Established there and not repeated: Steel 2007 correlates (MS §5); van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018 and Rozental 2018 headline effects (MS §5, BCT §4); grit is conscientiousness relabelled (MS §6); the basics of CBT for perfectionism (HE §5); the Curran & Hill and CDC one-liners (GDF §0); "externalise cues, never label" and behavioural activation (CF §4); avoidance goals (SRT §1); the no-diagnosis, no-medication rule (CF §5).

The narrower question here: **which stable differences between users change what works, how a coach detects them from behaviour rather than from a label, and what it does differently.**

Evidence levels: **A** meta-analysis or multi-site RCT; **B** single RCT or lab experiment; **C** correlational, longitudinal or cohort; **D** qualitative or theory. Effects are d/g/r/OR as reported.

---

## 0. Frame: tailor on behaviour, not on labels

**Tailoring buys little.** Noar, Benac & Harris 2007 (**A**; 57 studies, N = 58,454): tailored print messages r = .074 on behaviour. Lustria et al. 2013 (**A**; 40 studies, N = 20,180): web-tailored d = .139 post, .158 at follow-up. The one large trait-matching result is persuasion, not coaching: Matz et al. 2017 (**B**; three Facebook field experiments, >3.5 M people) got up to 40% more clicks and 50% more purchases from ads matched to extraversion or openness — which is precisely the exploitation EU AI Act Art. 5 forbids a coach (CF §5).

**Labels are not homogeneous groups.** Sonuga-Barke, Bitsakou & Thompson 2010 (**C**; 71 ADHD probands, 71 siblings, 50 controls, nine tasks): temporal-processing, inhibitory and delay-related deficits formed *independent* components, their co-occurrence within diagnosed children was no greater than chance, and substantial subgroups showed only one. Two people with the same diagnosis can need opposite adjustments. This, not privacy alone, is the argument for storing behavioural parameters instead of categories.

**Base rates, 16–40.** Adult ADHD 2.58% persistent, 6.76% symptomatic (Song et al. 2021, **A**) against 6.0% self-report (GDF §0). Autism in adults ≈1% (Brugha et al. 2011, **C**), with ADHD in 28%, anxiety 20%, depression 11% (Lai et al. 2019, **A**, 96 studies). Past-year anxiety disorder ≈22% at 18–44; past-year major depressive episode 18.6% at 18–25 (NIMH/NSDUH). Self-labelling is common and often wrong: 52% of the 100 most-viewed TikTok ADHD videos were misleading, 21% useful (Yeung et al. 2022, **C**). A user saying "my ADHD" may be right, may mean procrastination, may mean depression. Adler's behaviour should be identical in all three cases.

## 1. Conscientiousness

**Prediction (A).** Bogg & Roberts 2004 (194 studies): related in the healthy direction to all eight risky and all beneficial health behaviours. Wilmot & Ones 2019 (92 meta-analyses, 175 variables, N > 1.1 M): grand mean validity ρ = .20, desirable direction for 98% of variables. Poropat 2009 (N > 70,000): at tertiary level it predicts academic performance about as strongly as intelligence, and largely independently of it. Roberts et al. 2007 (review of meta-analyses): personality effects on mortality, divorce and occupational attainment are indistinguishable in size from SES and cognitive ability. Steel 2007: strongest correlate of procrastination, r = −.62 (MS §5).

**But it predicts adherence only weakly.** Molloy et al. 2014 (**A**; 16 studies, N = 3,476, medication adherence): r = .15, rising to r = .26 in younger samples. A high-C user is a modestly better bet, not a different species; low C is not a prognosis.

**Plans substitute for the trait (B/C).** Webb, Christian & Armitage 2007 (*Learning and Individual Differences*; class attendance): attendance was predicted by conscientiousness, by goal intentions and by the implementation-intention manipulation, and **the intervention worked best for students lowest in conscientiousness** — verified, contra the hedge in the previous draft. Conner, Rodgers & Murray 2007 (N = 146, exercise): conscientiousness strengthened the intention–behaviour link only when the behaviour had to be performed in an unusual context. Same conclusion from both directions: the if-then plan does for a low-C user what the trait does for a high-C user, and the gap appears on disrupted days.

**Traits move, and behaviour is the lever (A/B).** Roberts et al. 2017 (207 studies): interventions shift trait measures d = .37 over ~24 weeks, persisting; conscientiousness moves less than emotional stability. Stieger et al. 2021 (**B**; RCT, N = 1,523, PEACH app, 3 months): d = 0.52 self-reported and 0.35 observer-rated on chosen traits, holding 3 months later. Hudson et al. 2019 (**C**; 15-week intensive longitudinal): *accepting* behavioural challenges predicted nothing; only *completing* trait-typical behaviours predicted trait change. "Become disciplined first" is backwards. The daily action is the trait change.

## 2. ADHD

**If-then plans (B, children only).** Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008 (two studies): children with ADHD who furnished a suppression goal with an if-then plan inhibited at the level of children without ADHD; plan plus stimulant was best. Gawrilow, Gollwitzer & Oettingen 2011 (two papers): if-then plans reduced perseverative errors on set-shifting, improved maths under distraction, and improved delay of gratification; Gawrilow et al. 2013 extended to MCII in at-risk schoolchildren. One lab, small samples, lab tasks, children. **No trial of implementation intentions in adults with ADHD was located** (PubMed, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, September 2026). The mechanism — the plan delegates control to the cue and bypasses the executive step — is consistent with Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (CF §1) and with what the effective adult packages contain, but the adult effect size is unknown.

**Delay and reward (A).** Marx et al. 2018 (37 comparisons, N = 3,763): small-to-medium preference for smaller-sooner over larger-later rewards; *real* rather than hypothetical rewards nearly doubled the odds ratio. Amlung et al. 2019 (*JAMA Psychiatry*): ADHD delay discounting g = 0.37. Delay aversion is real; the folk "interest-based nervous system" is untested clinical lore and should not be cited.

**Time (A, children).** Zheng et al. 2022 (27 studies; 1,620 ADHD, 1,249 controls): less accurate (g > 0.40) and less precise (g = 0.66) time perception with a tendency to overestimate elapsed time; adult data sparse. This is a measurable estimation deficit, not "blindness". The general remedy is not ADHD-specific: unpacking a task into its parts lengthens and de-biases duration estimates (Kruger & Evans 2004, **B**; Forsyth & Burt 2008, **B**, segmentation effect), which is the debiasing move only when the error is underestimation.

**Procrastination link (C).** Suriano 2026 (systematic review, 11 studies, N = 2,788): inattention is the most robust correlate. Niermann & Scheres 2014 (N = 54): inattention, not hyperactivity-impulsivity. Netzer Turgeman & Pollak 2026 (N = 640 adults): the path ran through delay sensitivity, low perceived task value and flexible goal adjustment.

**Treatment, stated carefully (A/B).** Cortese et al. 2018 (51 adult trials, N = 8,131): amphetamines SMD −0.79, methylphenidate −0.49, atomoxetine −0.45, all with worse tolerability (OR 2.3–4.0). Knouse, Teller & Brooks 2017 (32 studies): CBT for adult ADHD g = 0.65 vs control, smaller against active controls, and **medication status did not moderate**. Lopez et al. 2018 (Cochrane, 14 RCTs, N = 700): SMD −0.84 vs waitlist (moderate quality) but −0.16, ns, vs supportive therapy (low quality). Solanto et al. 2010 (**B**; N = 88): metacognitive therapy — planning, organisation, time management, external reminders — OR 5.41 for response. D'Amelio et al. 2026 (**B**; pragmatic RCT, N = 337): fully self-guided digital CBT+mindfulness, ASRS d = 0.85 at 3 months, stable at 6, consistent across medication use. The active content is external structure. Medication is not Adler's business (CF §5); if a user raises it, keep the plan compatible with what they and their prescriber agreed.

**Coaching, the closest analogue to Adler (B/C, weak field).** Field et al. 2013 (**B**; randomised, N = 127 college students with ADHD; 30-minute weekly phone coaching for 24 weeks plus between-session check-ins) found significantly higher LASSI totals, all three clusters (Skill, Will, Self-Regulation) and wellbeing in the coached group; the headline effects usually quoted (total d = 1.02, Self-Regulation 1.10) are within-group pre-post values, and the study evaluates the coaching provider's own programme. Eddy et al. 2021 (**B**; ACCESS, multi-site RCT, N = 250 college students with ADHD aged 18–30, two semesters vs delayed treatment) is the strongest trial in this space and the most sobering: moderate improvements on **self-reported** study skills, time management, daily functioning and wellbeing, maintained or increased into the second semester — but no significant effect on interpersonal functioning and **no change in objective academic records (GPA, credits earned)** in either arm. A coaching-shaped intervention in exactly Adler's population moved how students reported functioning without moving the registrar's data. Against that, Van der Oord et al. 2020 (**B**; RCT, N = 58 Flemish students, six-session CBT planning intervention delivered inside real counselling services) found a specific effect on **one** outcome, inattention, called it modest, and concluded "the treatment needs adaptation". Anderson & Marino 2024 (**C**; systematic review, 17 studies 2009–2021) reports consistently positive outcomes — GPA, time management, organisation, self-esteem — but only two RCTs, small samples, and 35% of studies not reporting demographics. Direction favourable, quality poor, effectiveness under ordinary conditions the weakest link.

**Body doubling (D).** Eagle, Baltaxe-Admony & Ringland 2023 (survey N = 220): community-coined, widely used before it was named, motivations are momentum and staying on task, and "no academic exploration exists". Nearest evidence is social facilitation (Bond & Titus 1983, 241 studies): presence of others speeds simple or well-learned tasks and slightly impairs complex ones, small effects — consistent with Harkin 2016's "report to someone" moderator (CF §1). Adler can name it as an option for *initiation*; it should not present its own presence as one.

## 3. Procrastination as emotion regulation

**Who (C).** Steel & Ferrari 2013 (N = 16,413, global): procrastinators are younger, male, single, less educated. Adler's audience sits in the peak band.

**Mechanism (T → B).** Sirois & Pychyl 2013: procrastination is short-term mood repair, the cost landing on a future self experienced as a stranger. Tested by Eckert et al. 2016 (three studies): cross-sectionally the emotion-regulation link ran through *tolerating* aversive emotion; cross-lagged, the ability to *modify* aversive emotion reduced later procrastination while procrastination eroded later tolerance; a two-arm RCT of ER-skills training reduced procrastination (no effect size reported). Sirois 2014 (**C**; four samples, N = 768): self-compassion mediated the procrastination–stress link in all four — the mechanistic basis for treating self-criticism, not laziness, as the target. Sirois & Monaghan 2026 (**A**; 36 samples, N = 8,603): trait procrastination and poor self-rated health r = .21, partly via stress.

**Consequences (C, prospective).** Johansson et al. 2023 (*JAMA Netw Open*; 3,525 Swedish students, 9 months): per SD, depression β = .13, anxiety .08, stress .11; RR 1.27 disabling upper-limb pain, 1.15 economic difficulty, 1.09 poor sleep. Small per unit, broad in scope.

**What works (A).** van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018 and Rozental 2018 (MS §5, BCT §4): CBT strongest, duration irrelevant, time-management advice the weak arm. Across CBT, ER training and the ADHD packages the ingredients are identical: shrink the aversive first step, tolerate the feeling for the two minutes it lasts, put the start on an external cue.

## 4. Perfectionism

**The rise (A, cross-temporal).** Curran & Hill 2019 (164 samples, N = 41,641, 1989–2016): self-oriented +0.12 SD, other-oriented +0.19, socially prescribed +0.40 (32% more students above the 1989 mean). Soenens & Vansteenkiste 2019 (commentary, *Psych Bull*) accept the trend but reject the authors' parental-control explanation: it conflates control-as-structure with control-as-pressure, and the historical parenting data do not show a rise in pressure. Take the trend, not the causal story.

**Two dimensions (A).** Hill & Curran 2016 (43 studies, N = 9,838): perfectionistic *concerns* relate medium-to-large to burnout; *strivings* small negative or null, clearly protective once concerns are partialled; concerns are more harmful in work than in sport or education. Madigan 2019 (37 studies, N = 8,901, 156 effects): strivings–achievement r = .24, concerns r = −.08. Limburg et al. 2017 (284 studies): both dimensions relate transdiagnostically to psychopathology. Smith et al. 2021 (67 longitudinal studies, N = 20,583): concerns and depression are reciprocal, while strivings predict later depression *unidirectionally* — "adaptive perfectionism" is not free. Smith et al. 2018 (45 studies, N = 11,747): perfectionism, socially prescribed above all, relates to suicidal ideation, placing this section inside the safety net (CF §5).

**Goal pursuit specifically (B, one lab).** Powers, Koestner & Topciu 2005 (*PSPB*; two studies, New Year's resolutions): forming implementation intentions **reduced** goal progress for people high in socially prescribed perfectionism relative to a control exercise, and raised negative affect; the authors call planning "contra-indicated for individuals with self-critical tendencies". Powers et al. 2011 (five studies): self-criticism consistently predicted worse progress while self-oriented perfectionism predicted better progress once self-criticism was controlled. Powers et al. 2021: self-critical perfectionism → poor progress and depressive symptoms via low goal autonomy. No direct replication located in 21 years; it targets Adler's rank-1 technique and its core user, so it must be handled as a live hypothesis rather than dismissed.

**This is the one profile with a real treatment (A).** Galloway et al. 2022 (15 RCTs, N = 912, **mean age 23**): CBT for perfectionism produced concern over mistakes g = 0.89, clinical perfectionism g = 0.87, personal standards g = 0.57, depression g = 0.60, anxiety g = 0.42, with no publication bias; the limitation is few active-treatment comparisons. Suh et al. 2019 (10 RCTs, 65 perfectionism effects): medium effects, and **delivery modality did not moderate** — online matched face-to-face. Adler is not a therapist and must not deliver CBT-P as treatment (CF §5), but the coachable content — define good enough in advance, run the behavioural experiment, separate standards from worth — has the best evidence-to-audience fit in this entire file.

## 5. Chronotype and task timing: weaker than it looks

**Correlates (A).** Tsaousis 2010 (35 samples, N = 8,589): morningness–conscientiousness r = .29, the largest personality correlate of chronotype. Preckel et al. 2011 (**A**): morningness–achievement r = .16, eveningness–achievement r = −.14, eveningness–cognitive ability r = .08, morningness–cognitive ability r = −.04. Owls are slightly smarter and get slightly worse grades, which mostly indexes school timing. Digdon & Howell 2008 (N = 308): eveningness correlates with low self-control and procrastination, direction unknown. Au & Reece 2017 (36 studies, N = 15,734): eveningness–depression z = −.20. Daghlas et al. 2021 (*JAMA Psychiatry*; Mendelian randomisation, ~698k, 170,756 MDD cases): each hour earlier in genetically proxied sleep midpoint, OR 0.77 for depression — the only causal signal here, and it is about mood, not output.

**The synchrony effect is inconsistent in this age band (A).** Chauhan et al. 2025 (*Chronobiology International*; PRISMA systematic review, 65 studies): **>80% found no main effect of chronotype on cognition**, and a synchrony effect (better performance at optimal time of day) appeared in only **29 of 64 studies (45%) of adults aged 18–45**, mostly in attention, inhibition and memory — against **10 of 12 (83%) in older adults**. May, Hasher & Healey 2023 (*Perspectives on Psychological Science*, narrative review) explains why: benefits concentrate in people with *strong* morning or evening tendencies and in effortful, inhibition-heavy tasks, while roughly 50% of young adults are neutral types for whom time of day matters little (~40% evening-leaning, <8% morning). For a 16–40 audience, chronotype-based scheduling is a coin flip unless the user has a strong preference.

**Reverse synchrony (B, unreplicated).** Wieth & Zacks 2011 (*Thinking & Reasoning*): insight problems were solved *better* at the non-optimal time; analytic problems showed no consistent effect. Cited approvingly by May et al. 2023 and consistent with the reduced-inhibition account, but no direct replication located. Hypothesis, not rule.

**Modifiable (B, small).** Facer-Childs et al. 2019 (RCT in late chronotypes, small n): timed light, fixed meals, caffeine and exercise timing advanced sleep/wake ~2 h in three weeks without shortening sleep, with better self-reported depression and stress and better morning reaction time and grip. Becoming a morning person is a goal with a protocol, not a default to impose.

## 6. Depression and anxiety in goal pursuit

**Depression (C/A).** Dickson, Moberly & Kinderman 2011 (23 vs 26): no difference in number or importance of approach/avoidance goals, but depressed participants rated approach outcomes less likely, avoidance more likely, control lower. Dickson et al. 2016 (42 vs 51): fewer approach goals generated under time pressure, plus difficulty disengaging from unattainable goals *and* re-engaging with new ones. Halahakoon et al. 2020 (**A**; 48 case-control studies, 1,767 MDD): reward-processing impairment SMD 0.345 (reward bias 0.64), effort-based response vigour ns. Depressed users are not short of goals or of willingness to expend effort; they discount likelihood and value, and they can neither drop nor start. In Adler's data that reads as low confidence on *every* goal, more con than pro reasons, and goals that neither move nor get parked.

**Anxiety (C).** Dickson & MacLeod 2006 (28 vs 28, ages 16–18): more avoidance goals, fewer approach goals, reasons for failure rather than success, lower perceived control. Oertig et al. 2013 (N = 283 over a month; N = 132): avoidance-goal pursuit depleted self-regulatory resources, and depletion mediated lower wellbeing independent of neuroticism. Anxiety shows up as avoidance framing, over-planning as reassurance, and perfectionistic concerns (§4).

## 7. Autism, routines and inertia

Demetriou et al. 2018 (**A**; 235 studies, N = 14,081): broad executive dysfunction g = 0.48, stable across age, few measures clinically sensitive. Jenkinson, Milne & Thompson 2020 (**A**; 12 studies): intolerance of uncertainty tied to anxiety in 9 of 10 studies, at roughly non-autistic strength. Uljarević et al. 2017 (**C**; N = 71): insistence on sameness ↔ anxiety r = .45, ↔ effortful control r = −.39. Buckle et al. 2021 (**D**; 32 autistic adults): difficulty starting, stopping and switching "not within their conscious control", improved by prompting and a compatible environment, worsened by stress. Raymaker et al. 2020 (**D**): autistic burnout as ≥3 months of exhaustion, skill loss and reduced stimulus tolerance arising when expectations exceed supports, recovering through reduced expectations and unmasking. Monotropism (Murray et al. 2005) is theory only. Routine is both a strength (a fixed cue is a habit engine, CF §1 rank 6) and a vulnerability (change costs more). Supported: predictability, literal wording, one announced change at a time, prompts over motivation, and reading a global adherence collapse as load, never willpower.

## Implications for Adler

**Questions to ask (intake, optional, behavioural, none is a screen).**
- "When you write out a plan in detail, do you feel calmer or more pressure?" — planning-backfire flag (Powers 2005).
- "How much do you rely on lists and reminders for things you mean to do?" — externalisation need.
- "Is it easier to start when someone else is around or expecting you?" — social initiation.
- "How much does a sudden change to your plan throw you off?" — change tolerance.
- "Do you have a strong best time of day, or does it vary?" — **only** if the answer is strong morning or strong evening does §5 justify acting on it.
- "For a task you dislike, what is the feeling right before you put it off?" — the emotion-regulation diagnostic (already CF Phase 4).
- At goal formation, count reasons: more reasons it will fail than succeed → pessimism flag (Dickson 2011).
Reused, not re-asked: single-item procrastination and energy (MS), confidence rulers (SRT), barrier taps (CF §6), WHO-5 (HE).

**Rules and thresholds (hypotheses on evidence, tested per user).**
1. **No labels, ever.** Store parameters — peak window, externalisation need, change tolerance, planning response, delay sensitivity, estimation ratio, pessimism flag — never "ADHD-like" or "perfectionist". Sonuga-Barke 2010 says the label would not tell you which parameter is off anyway. If a user self-labels, accept it as their information, neither confirm nor question, change nothing except through parameters. Never advise on medication (CF §5).
2. **Externalise by default, more for those who need it.** High "forgot" rate → physical object at the cue, one fixed slot, one list, a visible next step (Solanto's package). Still no cue-time push notifications (D3).
3. **Planning-backfire detector.** If planning "feels like pressure", or language carries *should / they expect / doesn't count unless*, or affect drops after a detailed plan: keep the cue, loosen the standard. Replace "7:00 in the kitchen" with a loose anchor plus an explicit good-enough version ("after coffee, the two-minute version counts"), then compare affect and 7-day adherence. Never remove the cue.
4. **Perfectionism is the highest-value adaptation.** Galloway 2022 (g = 0.89 on concerns, mean age 23) and Suh 2019 (online = in person) say the mechanism transfers to a digital medium in this exact age band. Coachable version: define good enough per action *at creation*; count the minimum version as done; treat a "partly" tap on a completed minimum as a standards signal, not a shortfall; separate standards from worth (HE §5). Target concerns, keep strivings, never sell "healthy perfectionism" (Smith 2021). Suicidal-ideation link (Smith 2018) keeps this inside the safety net.
5. **Delay-aversion profile.** "Didn't feel like it" dominant plus tasks framed as pointless until the deadline → shorten the horizon to a daily unit with a visible immediate result, make progress real rather than symbolic (Marx 2018: real rewards nearly double the effect), offer temptation bundling (CF rank 10). Do not argue long-term benefits; that is the discounted quantity.
6. **Time-estimation guard.** Ask "how long?", log actual, and after three dated steps with >50% underestimation apply the user's own multiplier ("you tend to run about 1.6× your estimate"). Before estimating anything longer than an hour, ask for the parts and sum them (Kruger & Evans 2004). Never say "time blindness".
7. **Schedule to peak only for strong types.** Chauhan 2025 and May 2023 put ~50% of this audience in the neutral band where time of day barely matters. Rule: if the user reports a strong preference, place the most inhibition-heavy action within ~2 h of their stated peak; otherwise place it where the routine cue is most reliable and let adherence data find the window. Creative or open-ended work may go off-peak (Wieth & Zacks, hypothesis, low confidence). Never default to early mornings. Becoming a morning person is a goal with a protocol (Facer-Childs), never an imposed norm.
8. **Mood signatures.** Confidence < 5 on every goal, more con than pro reasons, or goals that neither move nor get parked for three weeks → WHO-5 and the referral script (HE), while continuing to coach the smallest action (behavioural activation, CF §4). Avoidance framing in more than half of goals → reframe to approach (SRT §1).
9. **Sameness-consistent defaults.** High change-sensitivity → announce any coach-proposed change a day ahead, one at a time, literal wording, no surprise UI or content variation, no novelty gamification. Global adherence collapse across all domains plus reported exhaustion is a load signature: shrink everything, propose rest, never push (Raymaker 2020).
10. **Identity without labels.** "The habit is the trait" (Hudson 2019, Stieger 2021) is the only permitted identity framing: describe what the user did, not who they are.
11. **Self-criticism is the procrastination target.** Sirois 2014 puts self-compassion on the causal path from procrastination to stress. After a miss, the response is the plan, not the person — matching the lapse rules already written (`lapse-relapse-recovery.md`).
12. **Do not accept self-report as the outcome.** Eddy 2021 moved self-reported study skills, time management and wellbeing in this exact population while GPA and credits stayed flat. Adler's primary metric must stay behavioural (actions completed, adherence over 14 days, automaticity), with self-reported confidence and wellbeing as secondary. If confidence rises while completion does not, that is a warning, not a success (CF §6).

**What to avoid.** Diagnostic language; "interest-based nervous system", "dopamine", "time blindness" as absolutes; treating a self-reported diagnosis as a diagnosis; personality quizzes as routing engines; moralising about mornings; prescribing grit or discipline (MS §6); "just plan it in detail" for self-critical users; presenting the coach's presence as body doubling; delivering CBT-P as therapy.

**Adapting to one person over time.** Every rule is a logged hypothesis (D3), and all seven parameters are estimable from the existing tap stream with no new instrument: cue-dependence (forgot rate before vs after externalisation), initiation cost by hour (didn't-feel-like-it rate by time of day, which also estimates the peak window empirically and beats self-report given §5), planning response (adherence and affect in the seven days after a detailed vs loose plan), delay sensitivity (adherence on dated vs open-ended actions), estimation ratio (estimated vs actual minutes), change tolerance (adherence dip in the week after any adjustment), load sensitivity (adherence across all goals vs reported demand days). Confirmed values enter the user's playbook; contradicted ones are dropped. Over months this produces the behavioural profile a label only gestures at — and unlike a label, it updates.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Implementation intentions universal (d = 0.65, CF §1) vs backfire for socially prescribed perfectionists (Powers 2005).** Two studies from one lab, unreplicated in 21 years, against a 94-test meta-analysis. Evidence favours if-then plans as the default with a detector and a loosened variant. Adler's own logs are the cheapest available replication.
- **Strivings adaptive (Hill & Curran 2016; Madigan 2019, r = .24) vs strivings → depression (Smith 2021).** Not a contradiction: strivings help performance and protect against burnout while still raising depression risk over time. Keep the standard, target the concerns.
- **Chronotype scheduling.** Popular productivity advice ("work with your chronotype") outruns the evidence: Chauhan 2025 found a synchrony effect in fewer than half of studies of 18–45s, and May 2023 puts half of young adults in the neutral band. Evidence favours cue reliability over clock alignment for most users. This is a change from the previous draft, which over-weighted Wieth & Zacks.
- **ADHD if-then evidence is child-only and laboratory.** The adult packages (Knouse, Solanto, D'Amelio) contain the same ingredients but cannot isolate them. Extrapolation, flagged.
- **ADHD coaching: self-report vs objective outcome.** Field 2013 (d ≈ 1.0 pre-post, provider-run) and Eddy 2021 (moderate self-report gains, null on GPA and credits) and Van der Oord 2020 (one modest outcome in real services) diverge in enthusiasm but agree on the pattern: people report functioning better, records do not move. Evidence favours the pessimistic reading. This is a measurement problem for Adler as much as an efficacy one — see rule 12.
- **Curran & Hill's trend vs its explanation.** Soenens & Vansteenkiste 2019 accept the rise and reject the parental-control account. Adler should cite the trend and never the cause.
- **Depression: no goal deficit (Dickson 2011) vs fewer approach goals (Dickson 2016).** Method difference (rating listed goals vs generating under time pressure). Both show pessimism and low control, which is what the coach can actually observe.
- **Body doubling** is ubiquitous and untested; social facilitation predicts help on easy tasks, mild harm on hard ones. Offer for initiation, not for hard thinking.
- **Prevalence: 6.0% self-report vs 2.58% persistent / 6.76% symptomatic (Song 2021).** Adler's users skew self-identified; the coach's behaviour must not depend on which figure is right.
- **No RCT of individual-difference-tailored coaching exists in this population**, and the general tailoring effect is r ≈ .07. The honest claim is that per-user adaptation is a design bet supported by mechanism, not by outcome evidence. The per-user hypothesis log is the study.
- **Autism guidance is qualitative**; the sameness defaults are reasoned from correlational anxiety data, not trials.
