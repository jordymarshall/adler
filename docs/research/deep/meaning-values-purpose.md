# Meaning, purpose, values and the good life: what survives scrutiny

Scope: the evidence behind "why does this goal matter to you." Extends, does not repeat: self-concordance, the aspiration index headline, Ikigai, Brooks and Burkeman as *frameworks* are in goal-discovery-frameworks.md §§1–10; Frankl, Yalom and meaning-centred therapy in humanistic-existential-common-factors.md §3; PPI effect sizes, self-affirmation, gratitude, anticipated regret and hedonic adaptation in emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md §§2, 3, 5, 9; want-to vs have-to motivation in motivation-science.md §4.

Grades: **A** = meta-analysis or large replicated body; **B** = single strong RCT or large prospective cohort; **C** = correlational, cross-sectional or theory.

## 1. Presence versus search: the measurement fact that changes the product

Steger, Frazier, Oishi & Kaler 2006 (*J Counseling Psychology* 53:80–93; three studies, US undergraduates) built the Meaning in Life Questionnaire as two five-item scales — **Presence** ("I have a good sense of what makes my life meaningful") and **Search** ("I am looking for something that makes my life feel meaningful"). Unlike the older Purpose in Life Test, no item overlaps with distress measures; the two-factor structure holds across languages. The subscales behave very differently. **Li, Dou & Liang 2021** (*J Happiness Studies* 22:467–489; three-level meta-analysis, k = 147 studies, 726 effect sizes, N = 92,169): presence–subjective wellbeing r ≈ **.42**; search–wellbeing is small, sometimes negative, and moderated by culture — **search is associated with *more* wellbeing in more collectivistic samples**. **A**. Consistent picture in **Boreham & Schutte 2023** (*J Clinical Psychology* 79:2736–2767; k = 99, N = 66,468): purpose–depression r = **−.49** [−.52, −.45], purpose–anxiety r = **−.36** [−.40, −.32], stronger in clinical samples. **A**, all cross-sectional.

The corrective: **Heintzelman & King 2014** (*American Psychologist* 69:561–574). Reviewing epidemiological data and every published PIL and MLQ mean they could find: 95% of a Health and Retirement Study subsample (n ≈ 1,062) said yes to "did you feel that your life has meaning"; in Gallup polls of 132 nations (N = 137,678, Oishi & Diener 2013) **91%** said their life has an important purpose or meaning; sample means sit roughly 1.2–1.5 SD above scale midpoints. Meaning is the human default, not a scarce achievement. **A** for the descriptive claim.

Product consequence: a coach that opens with "what's your purpose?" solves a problem most users do not have, and pushes them from presence into search — the subscale associated with slightly *worse* wellbeing in individualistic samples.

## 2. Purpose and hard outcomes: the strongest correlational finding in this literature

- **Cohen, Bavishi & Rozanski 2016** (*Psychosomatic Medicine* 78:122–133): 10 prospective studies, N = 136,265. Adjusted pooled RR **0.83** [0.75–0.91] for all-cause mortality and **0.83** [0.75–0.92] for cardiovascular events. Purpose measures pooled Ryff's subscale, "usefulness to others", life engagement and *ikigai*. **A**.
- **Sutin et al. 2026** (*Psychological Medicine* 56:e214), the current best estimate: 8 cohorts of individual-participant data + 17 published samples = 25 samples, **N = 488,765**, 48,928 deaths, up to 32 years. HR = **0.76** [0.70, 0.83] per SD, i.e. ~30% lower mortality risk. Heterogeneity is enormous (I² = 98.5%), Egger's test is significant (p = .004) but the bias-corrected estimate is unchanged (0.75), and leave-one-out is stable. Controlling behavioural and clinical risk factors attenuates to **0.85** [0.82, 0.89]; controlling depression attenuates to **0.91** [0.88, 0.94]. Association was **slightly stronger in younger participants** (HR_interaction = 1.04, p = .003). **A**.
- **Hill & Turiano 2014** (*Psychological Science* 25:1482–1486): MIDUS, N = 6,163, 14 years, 569 deaths; purpose predicted survival controlling for other wellbeing facets and affect, with **no moderation by age** — the effect is not an old-age phenomenon. **B**.
- **Martela, Laitinen & Hakulinen 2024** (*Psychology and Aging* 39:589–598): MIDUS, N = 5,993, 23 years, 1,857 deaths. Purpose HR 0.75 [0.72, 0.78] unadjusted → 0.93 [0.89, 0.97] fully adjusted; life satisfaction went marginal once purpose and self-rated health were in the model, and the two correlate only **r = .24** — not the same evaluation. **B**.
- **Alimujiang et al. 2019** (*JAMA Network Open* 2:e194270): HRS, N = 6,985; lowest vs highest purpose category HR = **2.43** [1.57, 3.75].

Caution: all observational. No trial has raised purpose and measured survival, and Sutin's 62% attenuation through depression means much of "purpose extends life" may be "not being depressed extends life". Adler may say purpose *tracks* better outcomes; it may not promise health benefits.

## 3. Eudaimonic vs hedonic: a distinction that fails as a measurement claim and survives as a motive claim

**Disabato, Goodman, Kashdan, Short & Jarden 2016** (*Psychological Assessment* 28:471–482, international sample): latent hedonic and eudaimonic wellbeing correlated **r = .96** — negligible discriminant validity between Diener's SWB and Ryff's PWB. **A**. **Kashdan, Biswas-Diener & King 2008** (*J Positive Psychology* 3:219–233) had already argued the split does not survive operationalisation and inflates researcher degrees of freedom.

The biology claim is worse: **Fredrickson et al. 2013** (*PNAS* 110:13684; N = 80) reported eudaimonic wellbeing predicting lower CTRA inflammatory gene expression and hedonic wellbeing predicting higher; **Brown, MacDonald, Samanta, Friedman & Coyne 2014** (*PNAS* 111:12705) showed the analysis was fatally flawed. Evidence favours the critics — do not cite gene-expression benefits of meaning.

What survives: **Huta & Ryan 2010** (*J Happiness Studies* 11:735–762) measured hedonic and eudaimonic *motives for activities* (HEMA) rather than two outcomes. Hedonic motives predict positive affect more strongly; eudaimonic motives predict meaning and elevation. **C/B**. The usable version: ask *why this activity*, not *which kind of happiness do you have*.

## 4. Relationships: the popular claim and the defensible one

The Harvard Study of Adult Development (Grant + Glueck cohorts, 1938–, 724 men, now ~2,500 with descendants; directed by Robert Waldinger) is the most-quoted source for "relationships predict a good life better than anything else". Evidence quality: an uncontrolled, all-male, initially near-all-white cohort with era-bound measures; "relationship satisfaction at 50 beat cholesterol as a predictor of health at 80" is within-sample prediction, not a designed comparison of risk factors. The published granular work is narrower — **Waldinger & Schulz 2010** (*Psychology and Aging* 25:422–431), 47 octogenarian couples, 8-day diaries: marital satisfaction buffered the daily link between health stressors and mood; time-with-others did not. **C**.

The claim survives on other evidence. **Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010** (*PLoS Medicine* 7:e1000316; 148 studies, N = 308,849): OR **1.50** [1.42, 1.59] for survival with stronger social relationships, invariant across age, sex, baseline health and cause of death. **A**. And asked in their own words what makes life meaningful — **Pew Research Center 2021**, 17 advanced economies, ~19,000 adults — family is the top source in **14 of 17** publics, occupation second. Cite Holt-Lunstad and Pew; use Waldinger as illustration only.

## 5. Aspirations, materialism, and the "if I got it, would I still want it" question

Beyond Kasser & Ryan 1996 and Bradshaw 2023 (goal-discovery-frameworks.md §10): **Dittmar, Bond, Hurst & Kasser 2014** (*JPSP* 107:879–924), 753 effect sizes from 259 independent samples: materialistic values–wellbeing r = **−.19** (ρ = −.24), robust across gender, age and culture but modest. **A**. **Kasser et al. 2014** (*Motivation and Emotion* 38:1–22) adds the only causal thread: three longitudinal studies (12 years, 2 years, 6 months) in which *decreases* in materialism tracked *increases* in wellbeing, plus a small RCT of a three-session financial-values programme that lowered materialism and raised self-esteem in the initially most materialistic adolescents. **B**, small and unreplicated.

For a 16–40 optimizer audience: extrinsic content (money, image, status) is a small negative signal at r ≈ −.2, not a diagnosis, and these users often have legitimate financial goals. A "why" question, not a veto (consistent with goal-discovery-frameworks.md §10).

## 6. Time affluence: the field's own replication problem

- **Whillans, Dunn, Smeets, Bekkers & Norton 2017** (*PNAS* 114:8523): correlational samples across four countries (n = 6,271; 4,469 pooled), buying time → life satisfaction **d = 0.24** [0.18, 0.31], unmoderated by income or wealth; plus a within-subjects field experiment of only **N = 60** (positive affect 4.00 vs 3.71, t(59) = 2.57). **B/C** — the causal leg is underpowered.
- **Whillans & West 2022** (*Scientific Reports* 12:719): preregistered six-week field experiment with working mothers in Kenya. Time-saving vouchers, equivalent cash, and neutral control produced **statistically similar** gains in wellbeing, stress and relationship conflict; the preregistered hypothesis failed. **B**, and the better-designed study.
- **Sharif, Mogilner & Hershfield 2021** (*JPSP* 121:933–947): 35,375 Americans (ATUS + a second dataset) plus two experiments. Discretionary time shows a **negative quadratic** relation with wellbeing: below ~2 hours a day, stress rises; above ~5 hours, wellbeing falls again unless the time is spent on something productive or social. **A/B**.
- **Giurge, Whillans & West 2020** (*Nature Human Behaviour* 4:993–1003) is a perspective piece; the quoted "time poverty hurts wellbeing more than unemployment" figure is their reanalysis, not a new experiment. **C**.

Net: *feeling* time-poor is a real correlate of low wellbeing; *buying* time is weakly supported; *more* free time is not monotonically better. Target the felt scarcity and the contents of the hour, not the hour count.

## 7. Finitude: keep the logic, drop the mortality lever

Burkeman's finitude argument (goal-discovery-frameworks.md §7) has no trials, and the nearest experimental literature has collapsed: **Klein et al. 2022, Many Labs 4** (*Collabra: Psychology* 8:35271; 21 labs, N ≈ 2,200, original-author-approved protocols included) found mortality salience → worldview defence at **d ≈ 0.03** versus d ≈ 0.5 in the early literature. Priming death does not reliably do anything. Use finitude as an arithmetic constraint ("only one of these can move this season"), never as an emotional prompt.

## 8. Values-affirmation and written goal-setting: weaker than the reputation

Self-affirmation before threat is small and fragile (emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md §3). The goal-setting-plus-values *writing* intervention has a clean failure: **Dobronyi, Oreopoulos & Petronijevic 2019** (*J Research on Educational Effectiveness* 12:38–66; NBER WP 23738) randomised ~1,400 first-year undergraduates to one of two online goal-setting exercises or control, half also getting follow-up reminders: **no effect on GPA, credits or persistence**, precise enough to rule out a 0.07 SD effect. Schippers et al. 2015/2023 report gains on credits and retention in Dutch samples; the difference is unexplained. **A** for "do not assume transfer": a written values exercise is scaffolding for the conversation, not an intervention with an expected outcome effect.

## 9. "Should" goals, introjection, and self-criticism

Extending motivation-science.md §4: **Powers, Koestner, Zuroff, Milyavskaya & Gorin 2011** (*PSPB* 37:964–975; five prospective studies across academic, health and athletic domains) split what is usually merged as perfectionism — self-critical perfectionism predicts **worse** goal progress via lower autonomous motivation, personal-standards perfectionism predicts **better** progress once self-criticism is partialled out. A demanding user is not the problem; a self-attacking one is (individual-differences.md §4).

**Davidai & Gilovich 2018** (*Emotion* 18:439–452; six studies) sharpen the "should" question from the other end: enduring regrets come from failing the **ideal self** (hopes, aspirations), not the **ought self** (duties) — ought-failures get apologised for and closed, ideal-failures stay open. With introjected regulation predicting effort but not persistence (Howard et al. 2021, motivation-science.md §4): obligation goals cost effort and produce little, while aspiration goals produce progress *and* are the ones whose abandonment still stings in ten years.

## 10. Which wellbeing interventions actually work

- **Manco & Hamby 2021** (*American J Health Promotion* 35:866–873): 33 RCTs of interventions targeting meaning in life, mostly in illness or hardship populations. SMD **0.85** vs passive controls, **0.32** vs active controls; mindfulness largest vs passive (1.57), narrative largest vs active (0.61). The 0.32 is the honest number. **A**.
- **van Agteren et al. 2021** (*Nature Human Behaviour* 5:631–652): 419 RCTs, N = 53,288; multi-component PPI and mindfulness best, effects "moderate at best", quality low-to-moderate, intensity the key moderator. **A**.
- **2025 network meta-analysis** (*Nature Human Behaviour*, preregistered; 183 RCTs, N = 22,811, non-clinical adults): **movement combined with a psychological component**, and yoga, rank highest; single-component PPIs perform about as well as plain physical activity. **A**.
- Contrast Carr 2021 vs White 2019 vs Choi 2025 (emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md §5) and Vos et al. 2015, d = .65 in illness populations (humanistic-existential-common-factors.md §3).

Read together: purpose-flavoured self-help content has an active-control effect around g = 0.2–0.35, and structured behaviour plus a psychological frame beats either alone — precisely Adler's shape.

## 11. What actually predicts regret

- **Roese & Summerville 2005** (*PSPB* 31:1273–1285): meta-analysis of 11 regret-ranking studies. Top domains: **education, career, romance, parenting, self, leisure**. The **opportunity principle** — regret is most intense where perceived opportunity for change remains open, not where loss was largest. **A** for the ranking, **B** for the mechanism.
- "Inaction regrets dominate in the long run" (Gilovich & Medvec 1994, *JPSP* 67:357, N = 32) is shakier than its reputation. **Yeung & Feldman 2022** (*Collabra* 8:37122, N = 988): replicated in 3 of 4 designs, weaker. **Richardson & Gilovich 2023** (*Royal Society Open Science* 10:221574, N = 2,600): short-term 58% action regrets, long-term a **51/49 split** — the interaction survived (w = 0.065 vs 0.15) but the headline did not.
- With Brewer et al. 2016 (anticipated inaction regret r = .29 with behaviour; emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md §2): anticipated regret is a real lever, but *retrospective* inaction dominance is weaker than the folk version. Never say "you'll regret it forever."

## Implications for Adler

**Questions to ask (intake and the weekly review).** All of these are presence-framed, single-sentence, and answerable by someone who is not in an existential crisis.
1. "What in your life already feels worth the effort?" (presence, not search; Li 2021.)
2. "Who would notice if this went well?" (relationships as the top real source of meaning; Pew 2021, Holt-Lunstad 2010.)
3. "Is this a hope or an obligation?" — one binary tap. Hope → ideal-self, worth protecting; obligation → check it is genuinely someone's request and not introjected (Davidai & Gilovich 2018; Howard 2021).
4. "If you never told anyone you'd done it, would you still want it?" (extrinsic screen; Dittmar 2014, kept as a yellow flag per goal-discovery-frameworks.md §10.)
5. "In a year, which of these would bother you most to be in the same place on?" (opportunity-framed regret, Roese & Summerville 2005 — and only in the user's own words.)
6. Time question: **not** "do you have enough time?" but "which hour of this week already feels like yours?" (Sharif 2021: the optimum is a shape, not a maximum.)

**Rules and thresholds.**
- Never run a "find your purpose" module. Run a **why-line per goal**: one sentence, user's words, stored on the goal, surfaced at review and after a lapse.
- Store the hope/obligation tag per goal. **Threshold:** if a goal is tagged obligation *and* has ≤ 4 of 7 completion for two consecutive weeks *and* the top barrier is "didn't feel like it", the review proposes shelving rather than shrinking (shelving beats abandoning, Mayer & Freund; shrink rule stays as in decisions.md D2's correction list for the other barrier types).
- **Purpose language is descriptive, never promissory.** Permitted: "people who can say why a goal matters tend to stick with it." Prohibited: any health, longevity or gene-expression claim. All of §2 is observational and 62% attenuates through depression.
- **Wellbeing add-ons must clear the active-control bar (g ≈ 0.3).** Meaning-in-life journaling, gratitude modules and values-card decks do not clear it as standalone features (Manco & Hamby 2021; emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md §5). What does clear it is what Adler already is — a psychological frame wrapped around scheduled behaviour, and specifically around movement (2025 network meta-analysis).
- Written values/goal exercises are **conversation scaffolding, not an intervention**: no claim that writing them improves outcomes (Dobronyi 2019).
- Domain coverage (D11's five wealth types) informs a *question*, never a nudge. An empty Social domain is the strongest signal in this literature — but it is asked about, not scored.

**What to avoid.**
- The word "purpose" as a noun the user must produce. It reliably shifts users into search.
- Death, "wasted years", "you only get 4,000 weeks" as motivational copy: no replicated effect (Many Labs 4) and it violates the no-guilt rule in coaching-framework.md's don'ts.
- "You'll regret not doing this." Long-run inaction dominance is a 51/49 split, not a law (Richardson & Gilovich 2023).
- Two-kinds-of-happiness copy ("chase meaning, not pleasure"). Latent r = .96; the defensible version is about *motives for an activity*, not types of person.
- Treating a money or status goal as pathology. r = −.19.

**How this lets the coach adapt to one person over time.** Three stored variables, each cheap to collect and each with a rule attached. (a) **Why-line per goal** — if the user rewrites it unprompted, that is the highest-value signal Adler gets; re-open the goal's shape. (b) **Hope/obligation tag** — the ratio across a user's active goals is the personalisation dial: an all-obligation portfolio predicts effort without progress (Milyavskaya 2015; Howard 2021), so the coach's move is subtraction, not planning. (c) **Self-criticism marker** — detected from the user's own language after a miss ("I'm useless at this"), it flips the response template from problem-solving to self-compassion first (Powers 2011; individual-differences.md §4), because for these users the barrier is motivation quality, not plan quality. Over months, the coach should be able to say back to the user which goals kept their why-line and which quietly became obligations — that summary is the product's differentiator and costs one tap a week.

## Conflicts and open questions

1. **Whillans 2017 vs Whillans & West 2022.** Buying-time happiness is correlational plus a 60-person within-subject study; the same author's preregistered field experiment found time-saving vouchers no better than cash or control. Favour the null; do not sell "outsource your chores".
2. **Sutin 2026's heterogeneity.** I² = 98.5% with a significant Egger's test but a stable bias-corrected estimate. Quote the pooled HR as a direction, not a dose.
3. **Purpose as cause or symptom.** 62% attenuation through depression means much of the mortality association may be depression's shadow, and no trial resolves it. If Adler ever measures purpose, a low score is a safety-pathway prompt, not a coaching target.
4. **Schippers vs Dobronyi.** Same intervention family, opposite results, both large; moderators (course integration, incentives, country) unexplained. Evidence favours the null in a self-serve digital context, which is Adler's.
5. **Search for meaning cross-culturally.** Li 2021 finds search is *positive* in collectivistic samples, so a blanket "never ask about search" rule may be wrong for part of a global audience. Testable.
6. **Ideal-self regrets vs shelving.** Davidai & Gilovich imply shelving an aspirational goal is what will be regretted — in tension with the parked-goals design (goal-discovery-frameworks.md §11). Proposed resolution: parked goals stay *visible*, preserving perceived opportunity; deleted ones do not. Worth an A/B.
7. **Is meaning movable in a healthy adult?** Manco & Hamby's trials are in illness and hardship populations. Whether a functioning 25-year-old optimizer can raise presence-of-meaning by app-deliverable means is untested.
