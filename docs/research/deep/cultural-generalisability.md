# Cultural generalisability: do Adler's feedback, praise and autonomy rules survive outside North America and Europe?

Research date: 2026-09-02. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `deep/feedback-and-progress.md` (FP §n), `deep/motivation-science.md` (MS §n), `deep/social-accountability.md` (SA §n), `deep/individual-differences.md` (ID §n), `deep/meaning-values-purpose.md` (MVP §n). Established there and **not repeated**: FIT and the praise policy (FP §1–2, §8B); the AAR spec (FP §8C); to-date/to-go (MS §2, SR §1); want-to vs have-to (MS §4); the hope/obligation tag and its shelving threshold (MVP §"Implications" rule 3 and the stored-variable list); Harkin's witness cell and the no-audience rule (SA §1, §"Rules" 1); "store parameters, not labels" (ID §"Rules" 1).

This file asks one question the repo has never asked: **the method's tone rules were estimated on a sample that is 96% Western. Which of them transfer?**

Evidence levels: **A** meta-analysis or multi-site RCT; **B** single experiment or field trial; **C** correlational or cross-sectional; **D** theory or commentary.

---

## 1. How WEIRD is the base

Arnett 2008 (*American Psychologist* 63:602–614; audit of six premier APA journals 2003–2007; **C**) and Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan 2010 (*BBS* 33:61–83; **D/A** review) give the numbers Adler must state once and then live with: **68% of subjects came from the United States and 96% from Western industrialised countries, which hold 12% of the world's population**; 73% of first authors were at US universities and 99% at Western ones; **67% of the American samples were psychology undergraduates**, so "a randomly selected American undergraduate is more than 4,000 times more likely to be a research participant than is a randomly selected person from outside of the West". Thalmayer, Toscanelli & Arnett 2021 (*American Psychologist* 76:116–129; same six journals, 2014–2018) is the follow-up: the US share fell to a little over 60%, but the gain came from **other English-speaking and Western European countries**, so roughly 11% of humanity is now represented and 89% is not; majority-world authors and samples remain at 4–5%. Rad, Martingano & Ginges 2018 (*PNAS* 115:11401–11405) find the same in *Psychological Science*, where most papers do not report sample nationality at all.

The counterweight matters as much as the indictment. **Klein et al. 2018, Many Labs 2** (*AMPPS* 1:443–490; 28 preregistered replications, 125 samples, **N = 15,305, 36 countries**; **A**) found that "variability in the observed effect sizes was attributable more to the effect being studied than to the sample or setting in which it was studied", with **little heterogeneity between high- and low-WEIRDness cultures**. WEIRDness is not a uniform tax: it concentrates in the domains Henrich et al.'s review names — self-concept and self-related motivation, personal choice, fairness, analytic reasoning. **Those are exactly the domains Adler's tone rules sit in**, which is why this file exists and why it does not conclude that the whole method is parochial.

**Caveat sentence for the method preamble** (one sentence, quantified, to be pasted into `adler-method.md`):

> *Every rule below was estimated on a literature in which 68% of samples were American and 96% Western — countries holding 12% of the world's population — and two-thirds of the American samples were psychology undergraduates (Arnett 2008; Henrich et al. 2010; still ~60% US and ~11% of humanity in 2014–18, Thalmayer et al. 2021); the rules most exposed by that bias are the ones governing praise, self-evaluation and personal choice, which are precisely the rules that set this coach's tone.*

Gap worth stating: **no equivalent audit exists for health-psychology or digital-health trials.** The nearest evidence is Spanhel et al. 2021 (§4).

## 2. Feedback, praise and self-improvement: a live dispute with a product consequence

**The finding that would invert Adler's phase rules.** Heine, Kitayama, Lehman, Takata et al. 2001 (*JPSP* 81:599–615; four studies; **B**): North Americans who **failed** persisted **less** on a follow-up task than those who succeeded; **Japanese who failed persisted more**. Japanese failers also raised the task's rated importance and diagnosticity; North Americans lowered it. Study 2 showed task-specificity, Study 3 traced it to divergent lay theories about the utility of effort, Study 4 removed the Likert-scale artefact. Heine & Raineri 2009 (*JCCP* 40:158–163) extends self-improving motivation to Chileans, so this is not Japan-only.
**The meta-analytic backbone.** Heine & Hamamura 2007 (*PSPR* 11:4–27; **91 comparisons, 30 methods**; **A**): mean cross-cultural difference **d = 0.84**; within cultures, Westerners self-enhance **d = 0.87**, East Asians **d = −0.01**, Asian Americans **d = 0.52**. The exception is implicit self-esteem. The intermediate Asian-American value is the number that matters commercially: an English-language App Store audience is largely bicultural, not "East Asian" or "Western".

**The counter-case, at full strength.** Sedikides, Gaertner & Toguchi 2003 (*JPSP* 84:60–79) and Sedikides, Gaertner & Vevea 2005 (*JPSP* 89:539–551) argue self-enhancement is pancultural but tactical: Westerners self-enhance on individualistic attributes, East Asians on collectivistic ones, and the difference vanishes once modesty norms are controlled; Hepper, Sedikides & Cai 2013 (*JCCP* 44:5–23) show Chinese participants use the same self-enhancement *strategies*; Yamaguchi, Greenwald, Banaji et al. 2007 (*Psych Science* 18:498–500) found positive implicit self-esteem in China, Japan and the US. Heine 2005 (*JPSP* 89:531–538) and Heine, Kitayama & Hamamura 2007 (*AJSP* 10:198–200) reply that the tactician analysis rests on the better-than-average method, compromised by the "everyone is better than their group's average" artefact (Sedikides et al. 2007, *AJSP* 10:201–203, reply again), and Falk & Heine 2015 (*PSPR* 19:177–198) argue the standard implicit measure lacks validity, so it cannot arbitrate. Gaertner, Sedikides, Cai & Brown 2010 (commentary on Henrich, *BBS*) restate the whole thing as levels of analysis: one universal *motive*, different *behaviours*.

**Where the evidence lands, for Adler's purposes.** The dispute is about the *motive*, not the *behaviour*. Both camps accept that in East Asian and East-Asian-heritage samples people take negative self-relevant feedback more readily, report less positive explicit self-evaluation, and — critically — **increase effort after failure rather than after success**. Adler acts on behaviour, so the behavioural consensus is operative and the theoretical dispute is noise for product purposes. The developmental channel is visible too: Ng, Pomerantz & Lam 2007 (*Developmental Psychology* 43:1239–1255; Study 1 N = 421, Study 2 N = 128 observed mother–child dyads): Chinese parents de-emphasised success and emphasised failure, American parents the reverse, and this partly accounted for children responding less positively to success and more negatively to failure.

**Verdict on the AAR spec (FP §8C).** The rule "review a success *and* a miss every week" (Ellis & Davidi 2005) **does not need a cultural variant — it needs a variable order**. It survives best cross-culturally precisely because it does not presuppose which side of the record motivates. What is WEIRD-bound is the *implicit weighting*: the spec opens on the best day. For a user whose record shows a self-improving pattern, opening on the miss is the informative move and opening on the success may be the disengaging one. FP §8H already logs "success-review response" as a per-user parameter; this section supplies the reason to expect real variance in it.

**Verdict on to-date/to-go.** The switch is driven by commitment level (MS rule 1), and no cross-cultural test of it exists. The theoretical exposure is real: to-date framing works by supplying evidence of accomplishment, which is a self-enhancement operation. **Unknown, not safe.** Do not change the default; do log the parameter.

## 3. Autonomy and obligation

**Autonomy support as delivery style: transferable.** Slemp, Kern, Patrick & Ryan 2018 (*Motivation and Emotion* 42:706–724; **754 correlations, 72 studies, 83 samples, N = 32,870**; **A**) found leader autonomy support positively associated with autonomous motivation, need satisfaction, wellbeing and positive work behaviours, and **correlations were not moderated by country of sample**. Chirkov, Ryan, Kim & Kaplan 2003 (*JPSP* 84:97–110; N = 559, South Korea/Russia/Turkey/US; **C**) is the theoretical anchor: cultures differ in *what* is internalised, but *relative autonomy* predicts wellbeing in all four, and horizontal practices internalise more readily than vertical ones. Cheon, Reeve & Moon 2012 (*JSEP* 34:365–396; **B**, RCT, 19 Seoul PE teachers randomised, 1,158 students) is the experimental proof outside the West: autonomy-support training improved all six student outcomes via need satisfaction; Vansteenkiste, Zhou, Lens & Soenens 2005 (*JEP* 97:468–483) agree in Chinese learners. Yu, Levesque-Bristol & Maeda 2018 (*J Happiness Studies* 19:1863–1882) meta-analyse autonomy and wellbeing across US and East Asian studies; **its effect sizes were not verifiable from an accessible abstract — cite the design, not a number.**

**Choice provision: not transferable.** Iyengar & Lepper 1999 (*JPSP* 76:349–366; **B**): Anglo-American children preferred, persisted longer at and performed better on a task when they had made superficial choices themselves; **Asian-American children were equally motivated when a trusted other made the same choices**. Savani, Markus & Conner 2008 (*JPSP* 95:861–876): Indians were slower to choose, chose less consistently with personal preference, and construed fewer actions as choices. Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008 (*Psych Bulletin* 134:270–300, 41 studies) establishes the mean choice effect but never tests cultural moderation. **Autonomy ≠ choice count.**

**The decisive study for the hope/obligation rule.** Miller, Das & Chakravarthy 2011 (*JPSP* 101:46–61; three studies, US and Indian adults; **B/C**): expectations to help family and friends were positively correlated with **satisfaction and perceived choice among Indians but not Americans**; Americans reported *less* satisfaction and choice for strongly versus weakly socially expected helping, whereas **Indians reported equal satisfaction and choice for both**. The authors' methodological conclusion is that SDT scales need items tapping the internalised meaning of role obligations — the standard instrument mis-scores exactly the case Adler's tag is trying to detect.

**Verdict on the shelving rule (MVP rule 3 / threshold).** As written — obligation tag + ≤4/7 for two weeks + "didn't feel like it" → propose shelving — the rule **is not culturally safe**, because "obligation" is being read as *controlled* regulation when for a substantial minority of users it is *identified* regulation with a family referent. The fix requires knowing nothing about the user's background: **do not shelve on the tag; shelve on the behavioural signature.** MS §4 (Milyavskaya 2015) supplies the discriminator — have-to motivation produces more effort, more obstacles, no progress and worse control facing temptation; want-to produces progress via subjective ease. An obligation goal showing the want-to signature is self-endorsed and must not be shelved. Add one volition probe before any shelving proposal (§7.3).

## 4. Does adapting an intervention to culture help, and which kind of adaptation?

| Synthesis | Scope | Comparator | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Griner & Smith 2006, *Psychotherapy* 43:531–548 (**A**, 76 studies) | culturally adapted mental-health interventions | heterogeneous controls | **d = 0.45**; single-culture-targeted 4× more effective than mixed-group; native-language 2× English |
| Hall, Ibaraki, Huang, Marti & Stice 2016, *Behavior Therapy* 47:993–1014 (**A**, 78 studies, N = 13,998, 95% non-European-American) | as above | mixed controls | **g = 0.67**; **vs the unadapted version of the same intervention g = 0.52**; treatment 0.76 vs prevention 0.25; remission OR 4.68 |
| Benish, Quintana & Wampold 2011, *J Counseling Psych* 58:279–289 (**A**, direct-comparison MA) | adapted vs unadapted *bona fide* therapy | strict | **d = 0.32**; the **sole** significant moderator was adaptation of the illness myth (explanatory model), **d = 0.21** |
| Soto, Smith, Griner, Domenech Rodríguez & Bernal 2018, *J Clin Psych* 74:1907–1923 (**A**, 99 studies) | adapted interventions | mixed | **d = 0.50; 0.35 after publication-bias correction**. Second MA: client-rated therapist cultural competence r = .38 with outcome; therapist self-rated r = .06 |
| Harper Shehadeh, Heim, Chowdhary, Maercker & Albanese 2016, *JMIR Mental Health* 3:e44 (**A**, 8 RCTs) | minimally guided / e-mental-health | controls | pooled SMD **−0.81** (printed CI −0.10 to −0.62 is internally inconsistent — treat magnitude as unreliable); **higher adaptation score → larger effect, p = .04** |
| Spanhel et al. 2021, *npj Digital Medicine* 4 (**A**, 55 articles) | internet/mobile interventions | — | taxonomy of **17** adaptation components; adherence and effectiveness "seemed similar to the original", but **no included study made a direct comparison** |
| Fu, Burger, Arjadi & Bockting 2020, *Lancet Psychiatry* 7:851–864 (**A**, 22 RCTs, N = 4,104, mean age 20–35) | digital psychological interventions in LMICs | controls | **g = 0.60 [0.45, 0.75]** |

**Reading.** The honest ordering: **against nothing, adaptation looks large (d = 0.45–0.67); against the same intervention unadapted, d = 0.32–0.52; after publication-bias correction, ~0.35.** The only identified active ingredient is **deep** — Benish's illness myth, the explanatory model of what is wrong and why the method should work. Surface adaptation has one solid quantitative claim behind it, Griner's **native-language interventions were twice as effective**, which for an English-language product is a warning about the ceiling rather than a feature request. Rathod et al. 2018 (*Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment* 14:165–178) reaches the same "promising but methodologically weak" verdict. Fu 2020 shows the *delivery medium* travels at full strength in Adler's exact age band.

**Product translation.** Deep adaptation for Adler is not new content: it is **the one-sentence rationale attached to each rule** ("we review both a good day and a bad day because the pattern is in the difference"). That sentence is Adler's illness myth, it already exists, and it is the highest-leverage thing to vary. Surface adaptation worth building is narrow: **observance-aware scheduling**, and examples that are not exclusively gym-and-startup.

## 5. Religion and family as goal context

**Religious practice is already a self-regulation technology.** McCullough & Willoughby 2009 (*Psych Bulletin* 135:69–93; **A**) organise the evidence around six propositions: religion promotes self-control, shapes goal selection and organisation, **facilitates self-monitoring**, builds self-regulatory strength, and prescribes proficiency in self-regulatory behaviours. A user with a five-times-daily prayer schedule already holds the strongest habit cue Adler could design — fixed, externally timed, socially reinforced (CF §1 rank 6, Wood & Neal).

**Observance changes the behaviour, measurably.** Farooq et al. 2021 (*BMC Public Health* 21; pedometer cohort, 2013–2019, N = 209 surveyed; **C**): during Ramadan, fasting participants' steps fell **−385/day (SE 158, p = .046)** while non-fasting participants' rose **+731/day (p = .010)**; fasting participants preferred activity **before sunset (33.8%) or evening (39.7%)**, non-fasters early morning (34.5%). That is a month-long, predictable shift in the exact metric the shrink rule keys on: as written (≤4/7 + barrier) Adler would propose halving a goal in response to a calendar event.

**Family is a legitimate accountability structure, including in the US.** Fuligni, Tseng & Lam 1999 (*Child Development* 70:1030–1044; **N > 800** US 10th and 12th graders; **C**): adolescents from Filipino, Chinese, Mexican and Central/South American backgrounds held substantially stronger family-obligation values than European-background peers, and the differences were **large and consistent across generation, gender, family composition and SES**. Second-generation status did not wash it out. This is the empirical reason Adler cannot treat "Western country of residence" as a proxy.

**Does the ban on person-level affirmation read as coldness?** No direct evidence exists. The nearest is Sanchez-Burks, Lee, Choi, Nisbett et al. 2003 (*JPSP* 85:363–372; four experiments): East–West differences in attention to indirect meaning are **larger in work than nonwork contexts**, because Americans specifically suppress relational attention at work. A coaching app sits between those registers, so a purely task-focused voice is likelier to be read by a high-context user as a category error than as hostility. Neff, Pisitsungkagarn & Hsieh 2008 (*JCCP* 39:267–285) is the corrective against country stereotyping: self-compassion was **highest in Thailand, lowest in Taiwan, US in between** — no East–West gradient — but **associated with wellbeing in all three**. The mechanism transfers; the level does not follow the label.

## 7. Implications for Adler

### 7.1 Transfer verdict per rule

| # | Rule (source in repo) | Verdict | Basis / what changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No person-level evaluative praise (FP §8B) | **Transferable** | Evidence is *stronger* outside the West: person praise is a meta-task intervention everywhere, and self-effacing norms make it additionally aversive. The method's single most robust cross-cultural asset. |
| 2 | Message schema: record → process → one how-to (FP §8A) | **Transferable** | Information content is the moderator; Fong's "how-to neutralises the cost of negative information" is what makes miss feedback safe in every sample tested. |
| 3 | No normative comparison, leaderboards or percentiles (SA rule 1) | **Transferable** | Comparison *harms* travel; comparison *benefits* are the ones that need a culture. |
| 4 | Autonomy-supportive delivery — elicit the user's reasons, never supply them (CF §1 rank 5) | **Transferable** | Slemp 2018: country did not moderate, 83 samples, N = 32,870. Strongest transfer evidence in this file. |
| 5 | Self-compassion after a miss; answer the plan, not the person (ID rule 11) | **Transferable** | Neff 2008: link to wellbeing in all three cultures, levels not on an East–West gradient. |
| 6 | "A miss is information" register (FP §8G) | **Transferable** | Native to a self-improving orientation (Heine 2001). |
| 7 | AAR opens on the best day (FP §8C) | **Conditional** | Keep "a success *and* a miss"; make the **opening side** a logged parameter. Detector: 7-day adherence after success-opened vs miss-opened reviews. |
| 8 | Informational positive feedback ("you did it after two late nights") | **Conditional** | Deci's d = 0.33 assumes a self-enhancement baseline. Extend FP §8H(a) "evaluative sensitivity" to non-evaluative positives. |
| 9 | Hope/obligation tag → propose shelving (MVP rule 3) | **Change the rule** | Miller 2011: strongly expected helping carries *equal* satisfaction and choice for Indians. Shelve on the have-to behavioural signature plus one volition probe, never on the tag. See §7.3. |
| 10 | Witness, never audience (SA rule 1) | **Conditional** | The no-leaderboard half transfers. The assumption that the recipient is a designated individual *outside the family* is a Western default: if a user names a parent or sibling, that is the strongest witness available, not a fallback (Fuligni 1999). |
| 11 | One voice: direct, plain, short, no exclamation marks (D9) | **Conditional** | Sanchez-Burks 2003 predicts a purely task-focused register reads differently in high-context settings. Test a one-line relational opener (T3). |
| 12 | Presence of meaning only; never ask about search (MVP) | **Conditional** | Li, Dou & Liang 2021 already contradicts this for collectivistic samples (MVP open question 5) — now flagged as cultural, not idiosyncratic. |
| 13 | To-date/to-go phase switch (MS rule 1) | **Unknown** | No cross-cultural test. To-date framing supplies evidence of accomplishment, which is a self-enhancement operation — theoretically exposed. Log the frame per message; do not change the default. |
| 14 | No streak counts (D2) | **Unknown** | The streak literature is Western except Aulagnon's Peruvian RCT (FP §4), which is *pro*-streak. Unresolved on both axes. |
| 15 | Growth-mindset register (FP §8G) | **Unknown** | The one large positive RCT is US school-based; expected effect ≈ 0 anyway, so the risk is cosmetic. |
| 16 | Mechanical core: if-then plans, self-monitoring, confidence rulers, shrink rule, barrier taps | **Unknown, low risk** | Untested by culture, but Many Labs 2 and Fu 2020 (g = 0.60 in LMICs, ages 20–35) make broad transfer the reasonable prior. |

### 7.2 What Adler asks at intake: nothing about culture

**Ask no question about nationality, ethnicity, religion, language background or values orientation.** Four reasons: (a) Fuligni 1999 — the parameter that matters, family-obligation orientation, varies hugely *within* a US-resident cohort and is invariant across generation and SES, so a label estimates it poorly; (b) Neff 2008 — the level of the relevant variable does not follow the East/West label at all; (c) Heine & Hamamura's Asian-American d = 0.52 sits midway between the poles, which is where most of Adler's non-Western users sit; (d) it is ID rule 1's argument for diagnoses, plus GDPR Art. 9 special-category exposure. **Store parameters, not labels** applies verbatim.

Four parameters, all estimable from the existing tap stream and weekly review, no new instrument:

- **`failure_response_gain`** — adherence in the 7 days after a miss-opened review minus adherence after a success-opened review. Sets AAR order; detects §2 without naming it.
- **`obligation_volition`** per goal — the want-to/have-to signature (reported effort, obstacle rate, progress) for obligation-tagged goals. Gates the shelving proposal.
- **`observance_windows`** — recurring weekday/time clusters in "something came up" and in check-in latency, plus multi-week regime shifts in baseline adherence. Catches Friday midday, Saturday, Sunday morning and Ramadan without asking. Threshold: the same weekday slot 3 weeks running, or a ≥20% adherence shift sustained 7+ days across *all* goals → propose a schedule change, never a shrink.
- **`relational_register`** — whether the user's own turns open with a greeting before content, and whether they ask for fewer or more words (already a D9 affordance). Sets whether the weekly review opens with a relational line or the record.

One optional behavioural question at goal formation, in the family SA already asks: **"Is there anyone this goal is partly for?"** It is about the goal, not the person, anyone can answer it, and it recovers most of what a culture question would.

### 7.3 The shelving rule, rewritten

> Current (MVP): obligation tag + ≤4/7 for two consecutive weeks + top barrier "didn't feel like it" → propose shelving.
> **Replacement:** the same three conditions **plus** the have-to signature (reported effort not falling, obstacle rate elevated, progress flat) **plus** one volition probe before any proposal: *"Some things we do for other people we'd still choose. Is this one of those, or is it one you'd put down if you could?"* Shelve only on "put it down". If the user endorses it, the coach's move is the same as for any hope goal — shrink, re-cue, or fix the time — and the tag is updated to `endorsed_obligation`.

Basis: Miller 2011 (equal satisfaction and choice for strongly expected helping among Indians) and Chirkov 2003 (relative autonomy predicts wellbeing regardless of *what* is internalised). Cost: one question, fired rarely. Benefit: it stops the coach telling a substantial minority of users to drop the goal that carries their strongest identified regulation.

### 7.4 Copy variants and the A/B tests worth running

Three tests, all runnable on Adler's own cohort with no culture data, each with a behavioural primary outcome.

**T1 — AAR opening side.** A (current): review opens on the best day. B: opens on the miss — *"Thursday and Friday didn't happen. What was different about those days?"* — success reviewed second. Primary: **adherence over the following 14 days**; secondary: review completion. Run as a within-user crossover (alternate weeks, ≥6 weeks) so each user yields their own `failure_response_gain`. §2 predicts a non-trivial subgroup with a positive gain that is invisible to any between-arm mean: **analyse the variance, not just the average**.

**T2 — Obligation goals: shelve vs endorse.** A (current): shelving proposal on tag + threshold. B: volition probe first, shelve only on non-endorsement (§7.3). Primary: **goal-level adherence at 8 weeks and goal survival**; secondary: 30-day retention and whether the user rewrites the why-line (MVP's highest-value signal). This decides whether the want-to/have-to distinction is a universal or a US undergraduate finding.

**T3 — Relational opener (the coldness test).** A (current): the review opens with the record. B: one fixed line first — *"Thanks for checking in this week. Here's what it looked like."* No praise, no evaluation, no adjective about the person, so FP §8B holds. Primary: **review completion rate and 30-day retention**; secondary: words per user turn. A null here retires a real worry cheaply.

Optional fourth: **observance-aware scheduling** — when `observance_windows` fires, offer a schedule change instead of the shrink rule, and measure whether adherence recovers without the goal shrinking.

### 7.5 Claims in the method that are known to be WEIRD-bound

State these in `adler-method.md` rather than leaving them implicit: (1) *positive feedback raises persistence, negative feedback lowers it* — reversed in Japanese samples (Heine 2001); (2) *choice raises intrinsic motivation* — reversed in Asian-American children (Iyengar & Lepper 1999), weak in Indian adults (Savani 2008); (3) *obligation goals cost effort and produce little* — contradicted for socially expected helping in India (Miller 2011); (4) *search for meaning is unrelated to wellbeing* — positive in collectivistic samples (Li 2021, MVP); (5) *self-improvement framing presumes a self-enhancement baseline* — absent in East Asian samples (Heine & Hamamura 2007, d = −0.01); (6) the growth-mindset base, including its one large positive RCT, is US school-based.

**What to avoid.** Any intake question about culture, religion, ethnicity or values orientation; any country- or locale-based branching of tone (the App Store locale is not the user's cultural parameter); translated-only "localisation" sold as cultural adaptation; holiday or festival greetings, which are surface adaptation with no evidence and high error cost; assuming a family-named witness is second-best; firing the shrink rule on an observance-driven adherence dip; and citing "collectivism" as an explanation for anything in copy.

## 8. Conflicts and open questions

1. **Self-enhancement: universal motive or Western artefact?** Heine & Hamamura's 91 comparisons (d = 0.84) vs the tactician model and the implicit-measure evidence. Evidence favours **Heine on behaviour, Sedikides on motive**; Falk & Heine 2015 shows implicit measures cannot arbitrate. Moot for Adler, which acts on behaviour, and both sides agree the behaviour differs.
2. **Henrich vs Many Labs 2.** Large variation in specific domains vs little WEIRD/non-WEIRD heterogeneity across 28 effects. Not a contradiction: the domains Henrich names (self, choice) are underrepresented among the 28. Favours **domain-specific** rather than blanket caution — hence the per-rule marking.
3. **How big is adaptation's real effect?** 0.45–0.67 against weak comparators, 0.32–0.52 against the same intervention unadapted, ~0.35 after bias correction. Favours the conservative number and **deep over surface**, on Benish's single-moderator finding.
4. **Digital adaptation is essentially untested.** Spanhel 2021: 55 papers, **zero** direct adapted-vs-unadapted comparisons. Harper Shehadeh's dose–response (p = .04) rests on 8 RCTs with a misprinted CI. T1–T3 would be among the first controlled tests of tone adaptation in a digital coach.
5. **Bicultural users are the modal case and the least-studied.** Asian Americans sit at d = 0.52 between the poles; Fuligni's second-generation adolescents keep family-obligation values at full strength. No literature says how to weight the two.
6. **No cross-cultural test exists** of implementation intentions, to-date/to-go, streak framing, the confidence-ruler threshold or the shrink rule. Broad transfer is a reasonable prior (Many Labs 2, Fu 2020), not a fact.
7. **The coldness question is unanswered.** Sanchez-Burks 2003 is about indirect meaning at work, not warmth in coaching. T3 is the only way Adler learns it.
8. **No audit exists** of sample diversity in health-psychology or digital-health behaviour-change trials. Every effect size in `bct-effectiveness.md`, `feedback-and-progress.md` and `maintenance-long-term.md` inherits an unmeasured provenance bias.
