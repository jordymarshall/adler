# Physical foundations: sleep, exercise, light, nutrition as substrate for self-regulation

Research date: 2026-09-02. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `atomic-habits-and-training-science.md` (AH), `deep/self-regulation-theory.md` (SR), `deep/emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md` (EW), `deep/individual-differences.md` (ID), `deep/lapse-relapse-recovery.md` (LR), `deep/habit-science-deep.md` (HS). Already established there and not repeated: chronotype, the synchrony effect and Facer-Childs' timed-light/meal/caffeine advance (ID §5); affective response *during* exercise as the predictor of maintenance and the ventilatory-threshold dose (EW §2); recovery, detachment and Ebert's digital insomnia trial (EW §6); ego depletion's collapse (SR §3); progressive overload, deloads and maintenance volume (AH); the "no energy" barrier route and WHO-5 escalation (CF §6, §7); Dr K's dopamine framing (CF §3).

Evidence levels: **A** meta-analysis or multi-site RCT; **B** single RCT or large field experiment; **C** prospective, EMA or correlational; **D** theory, narrative review or protocol claim.

## 1. Sleep is the largest modifiable input to the coach's raw material

**Sleep predicts self-control within the same person (A).** Guarana, Ryu, O'Boyle, Lee & Barnes 2021 (*Sleep Med Rev*; 61 studies): sleep quality → self-control **within-individual ρ = 0.35** [0.24, 0.45], between-individual 0.26; duration 0.20 / 0.14. Within-person exceeding between-person is the product-relevant part: a *state* lever, not a trait sort. Mostly correlational.

**Emotion is hit before cognition (A).** Palmer et al. 2024 (*Psychol Bull*; preregistered; k = 154, 1,338 effects, N = 5,717): total deprivation, partial restriction and fragmentation all reduced **positive affect SMD −0.27 to −1.14** and raised **anxiety 0.57 to 0.63**; arousal to emotional stimuli was **blunted −0.20 to −0.53**; negative affect and depressive symptoms varied by type; dose–response non-linear. Losing positive affect, not gaining misery, is the signature — precisely what erodes wanting to do the thing. Lim & Dinges 2010 (*Psychol Bull*; 70 articles, 147 tests) show the cognitive gradient: lapses in simple attention **g = −0.78**, reasoning accuracy only **−0.13 (ns)**. Users experience this as "I couldn't focus", so they discount it.

**People cannot self-report their own impairment (B, landmark).** Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington & Dinges 2003 (*Sleep*; n = 48; 14 nights at 4/6/8 h vs 3 nights total deprivation): 6 h or less produced cumulative deficits equal to up to two nights of total deprivation while **subjective sleepiness plateaued**. The most useful sleep fact for a coach: never take "I'm fine on five hours" as data.

**Sleep loss withdraws helping (B/C).** Ben Simon, Vallat, Rossi & Walker 2022 (*PLoS Biol* 20(8):e3001733), three studies: one night of loss cut willingness to help, night-to-night reductions predicted next-day helping in daily life, and the one-hour daylight-saving loss reduced real giving across >3 million donations.

**Improving sleep improves mental health, causally (A).** Scott, Webb, Martyn-St James, Rowse & Weich 2021 (*Sleep Med Rev*; 65 RCTs, N = 8,608): composite mental health **g = −0.53**, depression −0.63, anxiety −0.51, rumination −0.49, stress −0.42, with a **dose–response**. The strongest warrant for treating sleep as upstream rather than as another goal.

**But the treatment that works is CBT-I, not tips (A/B).** van Straten et al. 2018 (*Sleep Med Rev*; 87 RCTs, 3,724 treated / 2,579 controls): insomnia severity **g = 0.98**, sleep efficiency 0.71, onset latency 0.57, **total sleep time only 0.16** — CBT-I fixes distress and consolidation, not hours. Espie et al. 2019 (*JAMA Psychiatry*; N = 1,711): digital CBT-I beat **sleep hygiene education** on functional health, wellbeing and sleep-related quality of life, mediated by insomnia improvement. Sleep hygiene education was the control arm that lost.

**Regularity beats duration (C, large).** Windred et al. 2024 (*Sleep*; 60,977 UK Biobank participants, >10M hours of accelerometry, 1,859 deaths): the top four Sleep Regularity Index quintiles had **20–48% lower all-cause mortality** than the least regular, and regularity outperformed duration in matched models. Duration guidance remains the AASM/SRS consensus **≥7 h** (Watson et al. 2015).

**The audience's failure mode is bedtime procrastination (A, correlational).** Azeem, Sulaiman & Haidong 2026 (*Front Psychol*; 18 studies, N = 35,097, students): r = .34 with distress, .38 with perceived stress, .28 depression. Consistent with the emotion-regulation account of procrastination (EW §1, LR): a self-regulation event at 11pm, not a knowledge deficit.

## 2. Exercise: the strongest mood effect and the weakest cognition effect in this file

**Treatment of depression (A, low confidence).** Noetel et al. 2024 (*BMJ* 384:e075847; network meta-analysis, 218 studies, 495 arms, **N = 14,170**) vs active controls: walking or jogging **g = −0.62** [−0.80, −0.45] (κ = 51), yoga −0.55, strength training −0.49, mixed aerobic −0.43, tai chi/qigong −0.42. Effects were **proportional to prescribed intensity**; yoga and strength training best tolerated; robust to publication bias, but **only one study met Cochrane low-risk-of-bias criteria**, so CINeMA confidence was *low* for walking/jogging and *very low* elsewhere. A May 2024 correction restandardised to baseline SD without material change.

**Exercise also improves sleep (A).** Kredlow et al. 2015 (66 studies): regular exercise gives small gains in total sleep time and efficiency, small-to-medium on onset latency, moderate on subjective sleep quality — the cheapest link between §1 and §2.

**Converging, smaller, low-bias estimate (A).** Heissel et al. 2023 (*BJSM*; 41 RCTs, N = 2,264): SMD **−0.95** overall, **−0.67** [−0.99, −0.34] in the 12 low-risk-of-bias trials. Singh et al. 2023 (*BJSM* umbrella; 97 reviews, 1,039 trials, **128,119 participants**): median d = −0.43 depression, −0.42 anxiety, −0.60 distress; higher intensity better; **effects diminished with longer interventions** — adherence decays. Stubbs et al. 2016 (40 RCTs, 1,720 depressed adults): dropout 18.1%, **lower than controls** (OR 0.64).

**Prevention, with a dose curve (A, prospective).** Pearce et al. 2022 (*JAMA Psychiatry*; 15 cohorts, 191,130 adults, 2.1M person-years, 28,806 cases): versus no activity, **half the guideline (4.4 mMET-h/wk) RR 0.82** [0.77, 0.87]; full guideline (8.8) RR 0.75; double (17.5) RR 0.72. The curve is steepest at the bottom (population impact fraction 11.5% at the guideline).

**Cognition: near zero after bias correction (A, contested).** Ciria, Román-Caballero, Vadillo et al. 2023 (*Nat Hum Behav*; umbrella review, 24 meta-analyses of RCTs in healthy people): **d = 0.22** raw → **0.13** adjusting for active control and baseline differences → **0.05** [−0.09, 0.14] after publication-bias correction. Ludyga et al. 2020 (*Nat Hum Behav*; 80 RCTs): a small domain-general effect moderated by exercise type and sex. A 2024 *Nat Hum Behav* reply disputes Ciria on methodological grounds. **Verdict:** exercise is a mood, energy and sleep intervention; it is not an established nootropic.

## 3. Minimum effective doses

- **WHO 2020 (Bull et al., *BJSM* 54:1451–62).** Adults: **150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous** per week, **muscle-strengthening ≥2 days/week**, limit sedentary time; the **10-minute bout minimum was removed**; "some physical activity is better than doing none" (strong recommendations, moderate-certainty evidence). Cross-sectionally, more is not always better: Chekroud et al. 2018 (*Lancet Psychiatry*; 1.2M US adults, matched) found the fewest poor-mental-health days at **45 min, 3–5×/week**, with higher volumes worse.
- **Steps (A, prospective).** Paluch et al. 2022 (*Lancet Public Health*; 15 cohorts, 47,471 adults, 3,013 deaths): vs the lowest quartile (~3,553 steps/day), HRs **0.60 / 0.55 / 0.47** at ~5,800 / 7,800 / 10,900; inflection **6,000–8,000 (age ≥60)** and **8,000–10,000 (<60)**; stepping rate added nothing. Ding et al. 2025 (*Lancet Public Health*; 57 studies, 35 cohorts): **7,000 vs 2,000 steps/day** → all-cause HR **0.53**, dementia 0.62, CVD incidence 0.75, **depressive symptoms 0.78**, falls 0.72; inflections ~5,000–7,000; moderate certainty. Saint-Maurice et al. 2020 (*JAMA*; 4,840 adults): 8,000 vs 4,000 steps HR 0.49; **intensity ns after adjusting for volume**.
- **Strength (A).** Momma et al. 2022 (*BJSM*; 16 cohorts): J-shaped, with maximum risk reduction (10–20%) at **30–60 min/week** of muscle-strengthening activity.
- **Pattern matters less than volume (C).** O'Donovan et al. 2017 (*JAMA Intern Med*; 63,591 adults): "weekend warriors" (1–2 sessions/week) all-cause HR 0.70, indistinguishable from regularly active (0.65). Stamatakis et al. 2022 (*Nat Med*; 25,241 non-exercisers): **three 1–2 minute bursts of vigorous incidental activity per day** associated with 38–40% lower all-cause and cancer mortality, 48–49% lower CVD mortality.

The honest floor is far below what fitness culture implies: ~75 min/week, or 7,000 steps, or two sessions, or a few one-minute bursts.

## 4. Light and circadian timing

- **Evening light is potent at ordinary indoor levels, and sensitivity varies 60-fold (B).** Zeitzer et al. 2000 (*J Physiol*; n = 23): phase delay and melatonin suppression follow a logistic dose–response, with **~100 lx (dim room light) producing half the maximal delay of ~9,000 lx**. Phillips et al. 2019 (*PNAS*): group ED50 **24.6 lx**, individual ED50 **6 to 350 lx**; 10/30/50 lx delayed melatonin onset by 22/77/109 min. A universal lux instruction is therefore wrong for most people.
- **Daytime light entrains, and helps late types most (B).** Wright et al. 2013 (*Curr Biol*): a week camping under natural light advanced the clock to solar time, with **larger advances in later chronotypes**. The ingredient is the whole day–night contrast, not a sunrise ritual (cf. ID §5, Facer-Childs 2019).
- **Night light is a health exposure (C, large).** Windred et al. 2025 (*JAMA Netw Open*; 88,905 people, ~13M hours of wrist light data, 9.5 years): brightest-night decile vs darkest half — coronary artery disease aHR 1.32, myocardial infarction 1.47, heart failure 1.56, atrial fibrillation 1.32, stroke 1.28, adjusted for activity, smoking, alcohol, diet, sleep duration, SES and polygenic risk.
- **Light therapy is a clinical tool, not a coaching one (A, mixed).** Population-specific syntheses are null (Messak et al. 2026, Parkinson's: depression SMD −0.13, ns). No light boxes.

## 5. Alcohol and caffeine

- **Alcohol (A review + C real-world).** Ebrahim et al. 2013: at all doses alcohol shortens sleep-onset latency, consolidates the first half, **disrupts the second half**, delays and reduces REM. Pietilä et al. 2018 (*JMIR Ment Health*; **4,098 employees**, within-subject, nights with vs without): dose-dependent sympathetic dominance and impaired recovery in the first three hours of sleep, **present even at ≤0.25 g/kg**, and **not protected against by regular physical activity or young age**. Gunn et al. 2018 (*Addiction*): hangover impairs short-term memory **g = 0.64**, psychomotor speed 0.66, long-term memory 0.59, sustained attention 0.47.
- **Caffeine (A + B).** Gardiner et al. 2023 (*Sleep Med Rev*; 24 studies): total sleep time **−45 min**, efficiency −7%, onset +9 min, wake-after-onset +12 min, N1 up and N3/N4 down; **107 mg (a standard coffee) needs 8.8 h before bed, 217.5 mg needs 13.2 h**. Drake et al. 2013 (*JCSM*): 400 mg **6 h before bed** still disrupted sleep objectively. Burke et al. 2015 (*Sci Transl Med*): 200 mg 3 h before bed **delayed melatonin ~40 min**, about half the delay from 3 h of 3,000 lux.

## 6. Nutrition: the weakest link

Firth et al. 2019 (*Psychosom Med*; 16 RCTs, outcome data for 45,826): dietary interventions reduced depressive symptoms **g = 0.162** [0.055, 0.269] after two errata (originally larger); **anxiety null** (0.085, ns). Molero, Molendijk et al. 2025 (*J Affect Disord*; 21 RCTs + 92 cohorts, >700,000): treatment RCTs d = −0.80 but **low internal validity**; **prevention RCTs strongly null**; effects appeared for self-reported but **not diagnosed** depression; overall certainty "very low". The most-cited trial, Jacka et al. 2017 (SMILES, n = 67, unblinded), is one small study. Supplements: the VITAL sub-cohort (Vyas et al. 2023, n = 720) was **null** for vitamin D3 and omega-3 on depression prevention.

Two adjacent myths: **glucose does not restore willpower** — Orquin & Kurzban 2016 (*Psychol Bull*; 42 studies): no uniform effect, only domain-specific food-related ones (consistent with ego depletion's collapse, SR §3); and **hydration effects are small** — Wittbrodt & Millard-Stafford 2018 (33 studies, 413 subjects): ES −0.21 overall, −0.28 only **above 2% body-mass loss**, not a normal desk day.

## 7. Audit: Huberman-style protocols

Protocol text quoted from the Huberman Lab "Toolkit for Sleep" newsletter.

| Protocol | Claim | Evidence | Verdict / Adler's use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning sunlight | "within 30–60 minutes of waking"; 10 min bright / 20 cloudy / 30–60 overcast | Day–night contrast supported (Zeitzer 2000, Phillips 2019, Wright 2013, B); ED50 varies 6–350 lx; **no RCT of this ritual** | Direction right, numbers invented. Say "get outside early-ish, dim the evening" — no minutes, no lux |
| Caffeine cutoff 8–10 h | "Avoid caffeine within 8–10 hours of bedtime" | Gardiner 2023 (8.8 h for 107 mg), Drake 2013, Burke 2015 (A/B) | **Supported** — the strongest protocol in the set. Keep as one number tied to their bedtime |
| Delay caffeine 90–120 min after waking | adenosine/cortisol rationale | No trial located (D) | Unsupported story. Do not offer |
| Deliberate cold, ≈11 min/week | mood, focus, "dopamine +250%" | Šrámek 2000 (1 h at 14 °C: noradrenaline +530%, dopamine +250%, small n); Cain 2025 (11 RCTs, N = 3,177): stress reduced **only at 12 h** (SMD −1.00), null at 0/1/24/48 h, inflammation **up** immediately (1.03), immunity null, **mood null**, sickness absence −29%; the "11 min" traces to Søberg 2021 winter-swimmer physiology (n = 8 men) | Safe for most, mood claims weak. Never prescribe; if the user wants it, fine |
| Cold after lifting | recovery | Roberts 2015 (*J Physiol*, 12 wk, n = 21): CWI **blunted strength and hypertrophy** vs active recovery; Yu 2026 NMA (87 studies, 2,313) ranks passive recovery best for resistance strength | **Harmful to a strength or hypertrophy goal.** Flag as a goal conflict (AH) |
| NSDR / yoga nidra | for sleep and recovery | Singh 2026 meta-analysis: 5 unique studies; PSQI SMD −1.04 **ns**, ISI −1.00 **ns**, very low certainty. Dutheil 2021 (11 studies, N = 381): a short nap improves cognition 0.18, alertness 0.29 | Plausible, unproven; a nap has better evidence. Offer as the user's choice, never as protocol |
| Dopamine "detox" | receptor "reset", tolerance | No mechanistic evidence; Desai 2024 is narrative; Wu 2016 (14 RCTs): screen-time interventions cut screen time 4.63 h/wk with weak downstream effects; Sullivan 2026 (pilot RCT, n = 82): tentative gains **gone at 2 months** | Neurobiology wrong; friction is the real ingredient. Use environment restructuring (BCT 12.1/12.3), never the word "dopamine" (CF §3) |
| "Don't exercise in the evening" | sleep harm | Stutz 2019 (23 studies): evening exercise **did not harm sleep** (slow-wave +1.3 pp); impairment only when **vigorous exercise ends ≤1 h before bed** | Myth. Never move a workout out of the only slot the user has |
| Sleep hygiene tips | as an intervention | Espie 2019: sleep hygiene education was the **control arm** dCBT-I beat | Inert. Do not ship a tips list |

**How to reference any of these:** name the behaviour and its observable consequence, never the mechanism; give a number only where one is evidenced (caffeine hours, steps, weekly minutes); attribute nothing to a podcast; treat a protocol the user brings as *their* experiment, with the coach supplying the measurement (Ask–Offer–Ask, D3's hypothesis log).

## Implications for Adler

**Questions to ask.** Three, behavioural, none a screen, all reusable. (1) Weekly, one integer: *"How many nights this week did you get to bed within about an hour of your usual time?"* — the construct with the best evidence (Windred 2024) and no moralising. (2) After "no energy" twice in a week: *"Was it the night before, or the day itself?"* — routes to sleep or to load. (3) For any physical goal: *"What time of day is this actually going to happen?"* — placement, not chronotype (ID §5). Reused, not re-asked: the six barrier taps, weekly automaticity, confidence rulers, WHO-5.

**Rules and thresholds** (each a logged hypothesis under D3, checked against the next seven days).
1. **Foundations are a diagnosis before they are a goal.** When "no energy" or "didn't feel like it" dominates, shrink the existing action and ask about the night before; do not add a sleep or exercise action. Adding foundations to an over-committed person is the failure mode EW warns about.
2. **Regularity before duration.** Coach a consistent wake time, never an hours target. Never say a user needs eight hours; never say five is fine.
3. **Never accept self-rated alertness as evidence of sufficiency** (Van Dongen 2003) — and never argue. Show the pattern: bedtime variance against completion and barrier mix over four weeks (SR: mastery feedback moves confidence; coach persuasion is the weakest source).
4. **Movement floor, stated honestly:** ~75 min/week moderate, or 7,000 steps, or two sessions, or three 1–2 minute bursts a day. **Not** 150 minutes, **not** 10,000 steps. Half the guideline already carries RR 0.82 for depression (Pearce 2022).
5. **Intensity is a conflict, resolved by sequence.** Depression effects scale with prescribed intensity (Noetel, Singh), but during-exercise affect predicts maintenance and turns unpleasant above the ventilatory threshold (EW §2). Default to a talkable pace; raise intensity only after four weeks at ≥80% adherence, ≤25% per step (AH).
6. **Do not relocate an evening workout.** Only vigorous exercise ending within an hour of bed is a problem (Stutz 2019).
7. **One caffeine number:** last coffee ~9 h before target bedtime, ~13 h for pre-workout doses. Nothing about mornings.
8. **Alcohol is data, never morality.** "It gets you to sleep faster and wrecks the second half; it shows up the next day." Offer a two-week within-person comparison on nights before a target action (Pietilä's design is the template).
9. **Light: one sentence, no numbers.** "Get outside sometime in the first few hours you're up; dim the room in the last hour." Sensitivity varies 60-fold (Phillips 2019), so lux and minute prescriptions are unjustifiable. No light boxes.
10. **Nutrition is coached as behaviour, never as diet.** Regular meals; water only against a specific complaint. No macros, no supplements, no "eat this for focus" (Orquin & Kurzban 2016). Restriction or compulsive-exercise language routes to CF §5/§7 immediately.
11. **Refer rather than treat.** Trouble sleeping ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months → name CBT-I as first-line (g ≈ 0.98), say where to get it, keep coaching the small action meanwhile. Never ship sleep-hygiene tips as an intervention (Espie 2019).
12. **Protocol requests are honoured as experiments.** If a user brings cold plunges, NSDR or dopamine fasting, do not debunk: ask what they expect to change, define the readout, and flag only the documented conflict (cold within hours after strength training).

**What to avoid.** Neuroscience vocabulary in user-facing copy. Numeric protocols the evidence does not carry (10 minutes of sunrise light, 11 minutes of cold, 90-minute caffeine delays). Wearable scores as truth. Morning-person moralising (ID §5). Stacking a "foundations routine" on an existing goal set. Framing exercise as cognitive enhancement (Ciria 2023). Any claim that sleep or diet fixes a mood disorder.

**Adapting to one person over time.** Every parameter here is estimable from taps Adler already collects plus one weekly integer (bedtime consistency). Derivable per user: whether "no energy" clusters after irregular nights; whether completion drops the day after drinking; whether the exercise action predicts *other* goals' adherence next day (the per-user test of the keystone-habit claim, which the literature does not support in general); which time-of-day placement survives four weeks; whether a caffeine cutoff moved the consistency count. Confirmed relationships enter the user's playbook as their own findings — the only persuasion that works in a domain where everyone already knows the advice and disbelieves it applies to them.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Noetel's g = −0.62 vs Heissel's low-bias −0.67 vs Ciria's ~0.** Different outcomes, same bias profile (unblinded, small, self-report), corrected in Ciria and not in Noetel. Evidence favours "real and worthwhile for mood, unestablished for cognition". Quote the mood claim only.
- **Intensity helps depression but hurts adherence.** Noetel and Singh find larger effects at higher prescribed intensity; EW §2 finds unpleasant during-exercise affect predicts dropout. Unresolved; rule 5 is a testable guess.
- **Duration vs regularity.** Watson 2015 gives ≥7 h; Windred 2024 shows regularity predicts mortality better and duration adds little once regularity is modelled. Both observational for duration; regularity is also the more actionable target.
- **Diet and mood.** Firth 2019 (g = 0.16) versus Molero/Molendijk 2025 ("very low" certainty, prevention RCTs null, self-report-only effects). Evidence favours the sceptical side: make no dietary claims.
- **Cold immersion's stress effect appears at 12 hours and nowhere else** among five time points (Cain 2025) — more consistent with multiplicity than a real delayed effect. The harm to resistance-training adaptation, by contrast, is clean and replicated.
- **"Exercise is a keystone habit" has no good evidence.** The classic citation (Oaten & Cheng 2006) is small and belongs to the self-regulatory-strength literature that failed to replicate (SR §3). Test spillover per user; do not assert it.
- **Untested for this product:** whether embedding foundation work inside a goal coach helps or simply adds load. Ebert 2015 (EW) is the closest positive precedent and it targeted perseverative cognition, not habits. No trial exists of a coach that *withholds* foundation advice until a barrier pattern justifies it — which is exactly what rule 1 proposes.
