# Lapse, relapse, recovery: what a miss does, and what brings people back

Research date: 2026-09-01. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `atomic-habits-and-training-science.md` (AH), `deep/humanistic-existential-common-factors.md` (HE) and `decisions.md`. Already established there and not repeated: one miss is harmless (Lally; CF §1); streaks turn a miss into a quit (Silverman & Barasch; CF §1); self-compassion and compassionate self-correction after a miss (CF §1 rank 7; HE); fresh start as a restart timer (Dai 2014; CF §1 rank 11); the six-barrier post-miss script (CF Phase 4); lapse tiers by gap length (AH); re-engage after 3 silent days, max two messages, no "I missed you" (CF Phase 9; decisions). This file goes underneath: what a lapse does to a person, what predicts the slide to quitting, and what actually brings people back.

Evidence levels: **A** meta-analysis or multi-site RCT; **B** single RCT, micro-randomised trial (MRT) or large field experiment; **C** prospective EMA/correlational; **D** theory or descriptive.

---

## 1. Marlatt's model and the lapse/relapse distinction

**Model (D).** Marlatt & Gordon 1985: a *lapse* is one return to the old behaviour; a *relapse* is a return to the old pattern. The response decides which: coping and intact self-efficacy end it; the *abstinence violation effect* (AVE: internal, stable, global attribution plus guilt) drives it on. Their taxonomy of 311 episodes put negative emotional states (about a third), social pressure (a fifth) and interpersonal conflict (a sixth) first.

**Status.** The taxonomy failed replication: the multi-site Relapse Replication and Extension Project (Lowman, Allen & Stout 1996, *Addiction*) found reliability "variable both within and across" sites and "little predictive validity". Witkiewitz & Marlatt 2004 (*American Psychologist*) replaced it with a dynamic model: *tonic* background risk sets the threshold, *phasic* states (mood, craving, momentary self-efficacy) set the timing. The treatment package works modestly: Irvin et al. 1999 (JCCP; 26 studies, N = 9,504): r = .14 on substance use, .48 on psychosocial functioning, weakest for smoking. Bowen et al. 2014 (JAMA Psychiatry; N = 286): standard RP beat mindfulness-based RP on time to first use at 6 months; MBRP beat RP and usual care on use-days at 12 months. A for the package; mechanism claims C.

**Lapse → relapse (C).** Kenford et al. 1994 (JAMA; N = 88 and 112): of patch users who smoked at all in week two, 83% and 97% had relapsed by six months; 74% of all six-month failures began in weeks one or two. Brandon et al. 1990 (N = 129, 92 lapsed) modelled lapse-to-daily latency from confidence and affect. The transferable point: the *early window* is where a miss is most diagnostic, and the response to the miss, not the miss, is the lever.

## 2. The abstinence violation effect: theory strong, data weak

- **Curry, Marlatt & Gordon 1987** (JCCP): attributional AVE validated in quitters. D/C, founding study.
- **Grilo & Shiffman 1994** (JCCP; 50 bingers, phone follow-up): more internal, global, uncontrollable attributions → sooner next binge; *within-person* AVE variation across binges predicted time to the next. C, supportive.
- **Shiffman et al. 1996** (JCCP; N = 133, palmtop EMA within minutes of the first lapse): AVE indicators "generally failed to predict progression to relapse". What did: dependence, stress-triggered lapses (faster), and one item, *feeling like giving up* (faster second lapse). Restorative coping slowed same-day progression. C, disconfirming.
- **Gwaltney et al. 2005** (J Abnormal Psych; N = 305, EMA): daily self-efficacy predicted the first lapse next day; after a lapse, daily self-efficacy predicted relapse onset controlling for concurrent smoking. C.
- **Kirchner, Shiffman & Wileyto 2012** (J Abnormal Psych; N = 203, 1,001 lapses, recurrent-event survival): self-efficacy drops accelerated the next lapse (HR 1.09); *internal blame was protective* (HR 0.98); guilt accelerated on active patch and protected on placebo; responses to the *first* lapse did not predict, responses to later lapses did. C, disconfirming for the affective AVE.

**Verdict.** The robust piece is *recovery self-efficacy* measured after the lapse. Guilt is not reliably harmful; taking responsibility is not harmful; the danger signal is "I feel like giving up". Coaching that removes blame is unsupported; coaching that protects the belief that the next attempt will work is what the data reward.

## 3. What-the-hell and false hope

**What-the-hell (C, contested).** Herman & Mack 1975: restrained eaters ate about 66% more after a milkshake preload; unrestrained ate 47% less. Polivy & Herman: once the daily rule is broken the goal is abandoned for the day. Replication is uneven: Sin & Vartanian 2012 (N = 118) found counter-regulation but not via motivated overeating; Nederkoorn & Jansen 2002 (N = 46), Dritschel et al. 1993, Timko et al. 2012 (N = 76) and Martins et al. 2008 (review) found none; Jansen 2025 argues reverse causation. The effect is real in some restrained-eater samples and is **not a general law of goal pursuit**. What generalises is narrower: *all-or-nothing daily rules create a violation state; graded targets do not*.

**False hope syndrome (D).** Polivy & Herman 2002 (American Psychologist): self-change is launched on unrealistic beliefs about speed, ease, amount and payoff; the commitment pays off in self-image immediately; failure is blamed on the method, so the cycle repeats (the same resolution about ten times in a lifetime). This is the optimiser's failure mode and the reason for CF's modest first step and AH's floor. Against the pessimism, Oscarsson et al. 2020 (PLOS One; N = 1,066 randomised to no/some/extended support; B): 55% self-rated success at one year; approach goals 58.9% vs avoidance 47.1%; *some* support beat both control and *extended* support.

## 4. Planning for the lapse: coping planning

- **Sniehotta, Scholz & Schwarzer 2006** (BJHP; N = 211 cardiac patients, three arms): only action + coping planning raised exercise at two months. B.
- **Kwasnicka, Presseau, White & Sniehotta 2013** (HPR; 11 RCTs): coping planning "appears to be efficacious when participants are supported in the process of forming coping plans"; combined "seems to be more efficacious than action plans only". A, narrative.
- **Carraro & Gaudreau 2013** (Psych Sport Exerc; 23 correlational + 21 experimental studies): both spontaneous and induced action and coping planning raise activity, small-to-moderate, experimental effects smaller. A. (Exact coefficients are paywalled and were not re-verified.)
- **RCT-only meta-analysis 2022** (IJERPH; 35 RCTs): planning SMD 0.35 (0.25–0.44); action planning alone 0.41; action + coping 0.30. A. Adding coping planning did **not** beat action planning alone.
- **Zhang, Zhang, Schwarzer & Hagger 2019** (Health Psychology; HAPA MASEM, 108 samples, N = 32,231): zero-order r with behaviour: action planning .305, coping planning .294, maintenance self-efficacy .283, **recovery self-efficacy .248**, intention .304, past behaviour .447. Paths: coping planning β = .099, action planning .087, maintenance self-efficacy .144, **recovery self-efficacy .064 (ns)**; intention fell from .18 to .10 once past behaviour entered. A.
- A two-week study of 85 adults (2022): action planning predicted change, coping planning added nothing. C.

**Verdict.** Coping planning is modest, works when a coach helps build it, and can dilute the action plan as a second form. The HAPA authors note recovery self-efficacy only matters "where individuals experience a setback", which surveys rarely sample; the EMA studies in §2, which do, find it is the predictor that counts. Kwasnicka et al. 2016 (HPR; 100 theories) sort maintenance into five themes: motives, self-regulation, resources, habit, environment/social. A lapse is a failure in one of these and the diagnosis should say which.

## 5. Coming back: returns, resets, representation

- **Milkman et al. 2021** (Nature megastudy; N = 61,293 gym members, 54 four-week programmes; B): the best of 54 arms was a **micro-reward (about $0.09) for returning after a missed workout**, +27% weekly visits (effective arms 9–27%); 45% of arms beat control; only 8% showed effects after the programme ended. Rewarding the *return* beat planning prompts, reminders and larger flat incentives.
- **Dai 2018** (OBHDP; three experiments plus 40 years of MLB data): an unanticipated reset raises self-efficacy and performance after *weak* prior performance and lowers both after *strong*. B/C.
- **Silverman & Barasch 2023** (JCR; 7 studies): a broken streak hurts more when the break is attributed to the self, less when the streak can be *repaired*; the effect depends "solely on how that behavior is represented within the log". B.
- **Eskreis-Winkler & Fishbach 2019** (Psych Science; 5 studies, N = 1,674): people learn less from failure than success feedback because failure is ego-threatening. Spiller 2025 shows an answer-consistency artefact can produce the pattern. B, contested. Learn from exceptions (CF Phase 5), not from post-mortems.
- **Ego depletion** (Hagger et al. 2016, 23 labs, N = 2,141, d ≈ 0.04; Vohs et al. 2021, 36 labs, N = 3,531, d = 0.06): "willpower ran out" is not an evidence-based account of a lapse. A.

## 6. Fresh starts, deeper

- **Dai, Milkman & Riis 2015** (Psych Science; 5 experiments): a salient landmark raises *intentions* to initiate, via disassociation from the "past, imperfect self". B.
- **Beshears, Dai, Milkman & Benartzi 2021** (OBHDP; N = 6,082 employees, mailed field experiment): fresh-start framing of a future contribution date raised future take-up without lowering immediate take-up, and raised contributions over eight months. B, the strongest behavioural evidence.
- **Koo, Dai, Mai & Song 2020** (OBHDP; archive + 4 experiments): making an *upcoming* landmark salient reduces effort now, because the post-landmark self is expected to carry it, with no catch-up afterwards; mitigated by reminding people of everyday things they already do for the goal. B.
- Hennecke & Converse 2017 (SPPS) report the same initiation preference for boundaries (not fetched; direction only).

**Status.** One research group, no independent preregistered replication found, effects on initiation not persistence. CF's rule (restart on a landmark) stands. The new constraint is its inverse: never make an upcoming landmark salient mid-pursuit, and never reset a counter on someone doing well.

## 7. Re-engagement messages: what prompts buy

- **Alkhaldi et al. 2016** (JMIR; 14 studies, N = 8,774): prompts vs none RR 1.27 (1.01–1.60), heterogeneous; one study: prompts announcing *new content*, sent *early*, worked best. A, weak.
- **Bidargaddi et al. 2018** (JMIR mHealth; MRT, N = 1,255, 89 days): a tailored push raised next-24-h self-monitoring by 7.4% (weekday 12:30) and 11.8% (weekend 12:30 and 19:30). B.
- **Drink Less MRT 2023** (JMIR mHealth; N = 350, 30 days): a notification raised next-hour app opening 3.5× (2.9–4.3); wording made no difference; no decay over 30 days; **no difference in time to disengagement**. B. A push buys a session, not a user.
- **Klasnja et al. 2019** (Annals Behav Med; HeartSteps MRT, N = 37): a contextual suggestion added 167 steps to a 253-step half-hour on day one, decayed about 2% a day, zero by day 28. B.
- **Wang & Miller 2020** (33 JITAI studies): g = 1.65 vs waitlist, 0.89 vs non-JITAI. A, but implausible against the MRTs; treat as inflated.
- **Jakob et al. 2022** (JMIR; 99 studies): adherence 56% of intended use; drivers: personalised content, *individualised* reminders, stable usable app, human support alongside. A, narrative.
- **Torous et al. 2020** (J Affect Disord): pooled dropout 26.2%, 47.8% bias-adjusted; lower with human feedback and in-app monitoring. A. **Linardon et al. 2025** (13 studies): engagement–outcome r = .16. A. Engagement is a weak proxy for outcome.

## 8. Dropout: base rates and prediction

- **Baumel et al. 2019** (JMIR; 93 apps): median 15-day retention 3.9%, 30-day 3.3%. C.
- **Pratap et al. 2020** (npj Digital Medicine; 8 studies, >100,000 people): median retention 5.5 days; clinician referral +40 days, payment +22, having the condition +7, older age +4. C. A person who referred you is the largest retention factor measured.
- **Buyalskaya et al. 2023** (PNAS; 12 million gym check-ins): gym habits take months to become predictable; *predictable gym-goers were less responsive to an incentive*. C. Nudges work on the not-yet-habitual.
- **Goldstein et al. 2017** (N = 12, EMA): a lapse-prediction tree hit 72% accuracy and needed about four weeks of a person's data. **Forman et al. 2019** (N = 181 RCT): WW + OnTrack lost 4.7% vs 2.6% body weight where the algorithm worked. B, small.

## Implications for Adler

**No violation state to begin with.** Adler tracks days done, not abstinence, and "Partly" counts (CF Phase 4). Graded targets remove the all-or-nothing state the what-the-hell literature, where it replicates, depends on. Keep it that way.

**Measure the one predictor: recovery self-efficacy, after the miss.** After any "No", one tap: *"Tomorrow, 0–10?"* Log it against the barrier.
- ≥ 7: one line, no analysis (first-miss responses carry little information: Lally, Kirchner).
- 4–6: offer the two-minute version *for tomorrow only*; do not re-plan the goal.
- ≤ 3, or free text like "giving up / what's the point": Shiffman's one predictive item. Open a Talk offer the same evening: "Sounds like this one has stopped feeling possible. Shrink it, pause it with a return date, or drop it? All three are fine." Do not wait for the weekly review.

**Protect efficacy, not innocence.** Kirchner: blame was protective, the efficacy drop was the risk. Never "it wasn't your fault", never "you failed". Specific and forward (HE): "Tuesday didn't happen. What's the smallest version of Thursday?" When a user checks in after a gap, affirm the *return* itself, informationally ("Back on. That counts more than the gap"), never the record (Milkman 2021).

**Coping plans: one line, coach-built, for the barrier that has already happened.** At action design ask for the single most likely obstacle and one if-then; never a bank of contingencies. Rebuild only when the same barrier repeats (CF Phase 6). Kwasnicka: works when supported; IJERPH 2022: as a form it dilutes.

**Phase, not just gap length.** AH's tiers (≤ 3 / 4–13 / ≥ 14 days) are keyed to detraining. Kenford and Buyalskaya add a second axis: a miss in the first 14 days of a new action is far more predictive than the same miss at week eight, and once automaticity is ≥ 5/7 nudges stop mattering. Rule: *first 14 days*, two misses in any 7 → shrink now, not at the review; *established action (SRBAI ≥ 5)*, three misses in 7 → ask what changed in the context before touching the dose.

**Counters.** Show the weekly count (decisions) *and* a cumulative total done. A weekly reset after a bad week is a fresh start (Dai 2018); after a strong week it is a loss; the cumulative total is the repairable representation Silverman found protective. Never show consecutive days.

**Landmarks for restarts only.** Time restarts and parked-goal returns to a landmark (CF; goal-discovery). Never say "new month on Monday, good time to push" to an active user (Koo 2020). If the user proposes "properly next month", reflect it, ask what they already do daily for the goal (Koo's mitigation), then offer the two-minute version now.

**Re-engagement: a changed plan, not a reminder.** A push earns a session, nothing more. Within the two-message cap: message one is CF's script; message two, only past a landmark, carries something *new* (Alkhaldi): the coach's one proposed change, accept in one tap (D5). Send at 12:30 or weekend evening unless the user's check-in time is known. No third message: no evidence, documented cost.

**Early-window dropout model.** Days 1–14 are the risk window (Kenford; Pratap's median 5.5 days). Log from day one: days since last check-in, week number, misses in last 7, barrier types, confidence trend, post-miss recovery score, "giving up" flag, and whether a person suggested Adler (Pratap's +40 days; ask at intake). Group rules until about four weeks of a user's own data exist (Goldstein), then per-user thresholds. The weekly review is Adler's stand-in for the human feedback that halves dropout (Torous): skippable by the user only (D5).

**Adapting to one person.** Grilo & Shiffman: the post-lapse reaction varies *within* a person and predicts the next lapse. Keep a per-user lapse profile: typical barrier, typical recovery score, and which move (shrink / re-cue / pause / drop) was followed by a return within 7 days. Confirmed moves go to the playbook (decisions, "How the coach learns"). Lapses clustering with "no energy" and low mood route to the CF wellbeing check (stress-triggered lapses progress fastest: Shiffman 1996).

**Avoid.** "Willpower" or "discipline" explanations; "you broke the chain"; guilt hooks; a "what went wrong" ritual (Eskreis-Winkler); extended support after a miss (Oscarsson: some > more); resetting anything on a user doing well; more than one coping plan per action; just-in-time suggestions as a daily feature (Klasnja: gone by day 28).

## Conflicts and open questions

1. **AVE theory vs EMA data.** Marlatt predicts blame and guilt drive relapse; Shiffman 1996 and Kirchner 2012 find blame protective, guilt context-dependent, self-efficacy the consistent predictor. Evidence favours the data. All of it is smoking and binge eating; no EMA study of goal lapses in non-clinical optimisers exists.
2. **Coping planning: helpful or dilutive?** Sniehotta 2006 and Kwasnicka 2013 say combined beats action planning; the 2022 RCT-only meta-analysis and the HAPA SEM (β = .10) say the increment is small or nil. Favour the RCT pool: a small, conditional add-on, valuable as one coach-built if-then.
3. **Fresh starts.** Initiation benefits (Dai 2014, 2015; Beshears 2021) vs anticipatory slacking (Koo 2020) and reset harm after strong performance (Dai 2018). Same laboratory, no independent preregistered replication, persistence untested. Use for restarts; treat as B.
4. **What-the-hell.** Replicates in some restrained-eater preload studies, fails in others, never tested on generic goal behaviour. A hypothesis about all-or-nothing rules, not a fact about people.
5. **Prompts.** JITAI meta-analysis (g = 0.89–1.65) vs MRTs (large proximal effects, no retention effect, habituation within a month). Favour the MRTs.
6. **"Never miss twice."** Kirchner (later-lapse responses predict, first-lapse responses do not) and Kenford (early lapses predict) give oblique support for attending to the *second* miss in a new action, from smoking only. Keep it a heuristic (decisions), now with a stated reason.
7. **Gap length vs phase.** AH's tiers assume the gap is the variable; Kenford and Buyalskaya suggest the phase is. The two-axis rule above is a hypothesis for Adler's own data (CF measurement: retention after first miss).
8. **Recovery self-efficacy as one item.** HAPA's construct is multi-item and rarely measured after a real setback; Gwaltney and Kirchner used daily single items. The "Tomorrow, 0–10?" tap rests on the EMA work. Validate against 7-day return in Adler's cohort.
9. **Failure-learning** is under methodological challenge (Spiller 2025); the design choice (learn from exceptions) is cheap either way.
10. **Open:** no study of re-engagement copy tone or plan-change content in a coaching app exists; the two-message cap and "changed plan" second message are inferences from Alkhaldi and the Drink Less null, and are A/B hypotheses.
