# Humanistic, existential, third-wave and common-factors research: what a goal coach can borrow

Status: complete, 2026-09-01. Extends `docs/research/coaching-framework.md` sections 3-5 (MI, GROW, SF basics, Theeboom/Jones/Burt & Talati headline effects, ICF definition and referral criteria, Adlerian encouragement, TTM, Dr K table, ACT summary, distortion list, BA headline, safety hand-off). Those are referenced, not repeated. Depth psychology (Freud, Jung, Adler, Horney, attachment, schema, IFS, TA) is in `depth-psychology.md`.

Evidence levels: **A** = several meta-analyses or RCTs pointing the same way; **B** = one good RCT, or a consistent correlational meta-analysis; **C** = theory, expert consensus, or trials too thin to rely on.

## 1. Common factors and the alliance

**Core ideas.** Wampold's contextual model (*The Great Psychotherapy Debate*, 2nd ed. 2015): treatments work through a real relationship, through expectations created by a credible explanation and ritual, and through specific ingredients that get the client doing something healthy. The "dodo bird" claim (Wampold 1997): bona fide treatments differ little; effect sizes for head-to-head comparisons sat homogeneously around zero, upper bound d = 0.20. Marcus 2014 re-ran it and found small, non-zero, heterogeneous differences. Cuijpers, Reijnders & Huibers 2019 is the fair summary: comparative meta-analyses have methodological problems, common-factor evidence is correlational, and the alliance-outcome link may partly reflect early improvement producing a better alliance.

**Evidence.**

| Factor | Study | k / N | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alliance | Flückiger, Del Re, Wampold & Horvath 2018 | 295 studies, >30,000 | r = .278 (about 8% of variance); holds for internet-delivered therapy and after controlling early symptom change |
| Empathy | Elliott, Bohart, Watson & Murphy 2018 | 82 samples, N = 6,138 | r = .28 (d = .58); client-rated predicts best, therapist-rated worst |
| Positive regard | Farber, Suzuki & Lynch 2018 | 64 studies | g = .28 (.36 multilevel) |
| Congruence | Kolden et al. 2018 | update of 2011 | r about .23 |
| Goal consensus | Tryon, Birch & Verkuilen 2018 | 54 studies, N = 7,278 | r = .24 (d = .49) |
| Collaboration | Tryon 2018 | 53 studies, N = 5,286 | r = .29 (d = .61) |
| Outcome expectation | Constantino, Vîslă, Coyne & Boswell 2018 | 81 samples, N = 12,722 | r = .18 (d = .36); stronger with manualised treatment; causality unestablished |
| Therapist effects | Baldwin & Imel 2013; Johns et al. 2019 | review | about 5% of variance overall, 8% in RCTs, range 0.2-29%; larger with sicker patients |
| Feedback / ROM | de Jong et al. 2021 | 58 studies, N = 21,699 | d = .15 overall, .17 for not-on-track clients; lowers drop-out; best with OQ-45/ORS, expected-recovery curves and clinical support tools |

Goal consensus and collaboration (the "task" and "goal" parts of the alliance) predict as well as the emotional bond, and in coaching they predict *better* (de Haan 2016, Section 7). Feedback-informed treatment (Lambert's OQ-45; Miller and Duncan's ORS/SRS) only helps when it changes what the practitioner does for off-track clients: measurement without a decision rule is inert.

**Alliance with software.** Darcy et al. 2021 (Woebot, >36,000 users): WAI-SR bond within 5 days comparable to outpatient CBT norms, sustained to 8 weeks. Beatty et al. 2022 (Wysa, n = 1,205, PHQ-4 positive): WAI-SR rose over the first days to face-to-face norms. Both are retrospective, survivor-biased and unlinked to outcomes. The 2025 JMIR Mental Health integrative review ("Does the digital therapeutic alliance exist?") proposes goal alignment, task agreement, bond and engagement as the construct and finds no robust evidence yet that it predicts outcome. Terblanche et al. 2024 is the warning: generative coaching bots produced *stronger* alliance and eagerness than scripted ones, with *no difference* in goal attainment. Bond ratings for an AI can inflate without buying anything. Moore et al. 2025 (framework doc) argue a therapeutic alliance requires identity and stakes.

**Borrow (A for constructs, C for transfer to AI).** Make goal consensus explicit and revisit it; make collaboration visible (the user edits the plan); use the user's rating as the criterion (coach self-ratings do not predict); attach a decision rule to every feedback measure.

## 2. Rogers and person-centred therapy

**Core ideas.** Rogers 1957: six "necessary and sufficient" conditions, three canonical: accurate empathy, unconditional positive regard (prizing the person independent of behaviour), congruence (genuine, not a role). The actualising tendency: given these, people move toward growth on their own. "Non-directive" (Rogers 1942) meant no steering of content, no interpretation, no advice.

**Evidence.** The three conditions are the relationship rows in Section 1 (r = .28, .28, .23). "Sufficient" is not supported: structured, directional treatments beat supportive counselling for defined problems (supportive-expressive d = .20 vs .47-.65 for structured meaning work, Vos 2015). "Necessary-ish" as a base holds. Bohart's client-as-active-self-healer fits the coaching finding that baseline self-efficacy and hope predict outcome (de Haan 2016; de Haan, Terblanche & Nowack 2026).

**How this underpins MI.** Miller trained in the Rogerian tradition; MI was first defined as "client-centred, directive", later softened to "guiding", a style between directing and following. Magill 2018's finding (framework doc) that MI-inconsistent behaviour, not change talk, carries the effect is a Rogerian result: the harm is in violating regard and autonomy.

**What "non-directive" means for a coach that proposes changes.** Non-directive about *what matters* (values, which goal, what "good" looks like); directive about *process and structure* (cue, size, review cadence); propose with permission (Ask-Offer-Ask) and hand the decision back. Two points matter more for an AI than a human:

- Unconditional regard is tested at the miss. Regard contingent on adherence is praise (Deci 1999). The post-miss message is where "unconditional" is proved or broken.
- Congruence is the antidote to sycophancy. UPR is regard for the person, not agreement with their statements. A congruent coach says "I don't think that plan survives a bad week" while keeping regard. The APA 2025 sycophancy warning is, in Rogers' terms, a congruence failure.

Level A for the conditions as correlates; B for autonomy-supportive style improving adherence (SDT); C for any specific phrasing.

## 3. Existential approaches

**Frankl** (*Man's Search for Meaning*, 1946; *The Doctor and the Soul*): the primary motive is a will to meaning; meaning comes through a deed or work, through experiencing something or someone (love), and through one's attitude to unavoidable suffering. Techniques: **paradoxical intention** (intend the feared outcome to break anticipatory anxiety: "try to stay awake") and **dereflection** (shift attention from self-monitoring to the task or other person). "Noö-dynamics": a healthy person needs tension between what they are and what they ought to become, not homeostasis; the existential version of Locke & Latham's difficulty principle.

**Yalom** (*Existential Psychotherapy*, 1980): four givens (death, freedom/responsibility, isolation, meaninglessness); will as wishing, choosing, acting; responsibility assumption as the core task. **May** (*Love and Will*, 1969): intentionality; normal anxiety as the price of growth.

**Evidence.**
- Paradoxical intention for insomnia: Jansson-Fröjmark et al. 2022 meta-analysis: moderate-to-large improvements, large reductions in sleep effort and sleep-performance anxiety. Shoham-Salomon & Rosenthal 1987: paradoxical interventions comparable to other treatments, especially at follow-up. Level B, only for anxiety-maintained loops.
- Dereflection: no independent trials; indirect support from the sleep-effort construct and self-focus/choking research. Level C.
- Meaning-centred work: Vos, Craig & Cooper 2015 (JCCP): 21 eligible RCTs, 15 with unique data, N = 1,792. Meaning-centred therapies (6 studies, mostly physically ill patients): meaning in life d = .65 post, .57 follow-up; psychopathology d = .47; self-efficacy d = .48. Supportive-expressive therapy d = .20. Experiential-existential and cognitive-existential: no significant effects. Meta-regression: gains in meaning predicted falls in distress (β = −.56). Vos & Vitali 2018: g about 1.0 in a broader set, with heterogeneity and small-study concerns. Level A for *structured* meaning work in illness populations; transfer to healthy optimisers is inference.
- Existential coaching (van Deurzen & Hanaway 2012; Spinelli): a 2024 qualitative study in *Coaching* found the field defined by phenomenological method, existential theory and goal focus, with "limited empirical research". Level C.

**Borrow.** One meaning question at goal formation ("Who or what is this for?") mapped to Frankl's three sources; a one-line value tag per action; dereflection for the over-monitoring optimiser ("track the task this week, not yourself"); Yalom's wish-choose-act as the skeleton of a planning turn. **Do not:** confront death or finitude; process isolation; use paradoxical intention beyond low-stakes loops (sleep, blank page); treat "what's the point" as a meaning question (it is a safety cue).

## 4. Third-wave approaches

### ACT (beyond the framework doc)
**Core.** Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson (1999; 2012). Hexaflex: acceptance, defusion, present-moment contact, self-as-context, values, committed action; target is psychological flexibility. Values are chosen directions ("being a reliable friend"); goals are instances; the claim is that value-linked goals persist under discomfort.

**Evidence.** A-Tjak et al. 2015 (39 RCTs, N = 1,821): g = .57 vs controls (.82 vs waitlist, .51 vs psychological placebo, .64 vs TAU), not superior to established treatments; Gloster 2020 agrees. Level A. **Values work specifically:** Chase et al. 2013 randomised psychology majors to online goal-setting training, values plus goal-setting, or waitlist; values plus goals raised next-semester GPA; **goal setting alone did nothing** versus waitlist or non-responders. Level B, and the most directly relevant single finding here for a goal app. **Procrastination:** Malouff & Schutte 2019 (12 RCTs, N = 646) g = 1.18 for interventions overall; van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018 (24 studies, N = 1,173): large reductions, stable at follow-up, CBT stronger than self-regulation or strengths approaches, duration not a moderator. ACT specifically is mixed: Wang et al. 2017 found ACT held better than CBT at follow-up; a trial in Rozental et al.'s 2018 review found ACT group g = .05 vs CBT g = .61. Level B. **Health behaviour:** smoking RR 1.7-1.8 to 6 months; weight BMI WMD −0.50 (2023 meta-analyses). Level B, small-to-moderate.

**Borrow:** values before goals (Chase); defusion phrasing; "willingness" as "make room for two boring minutes"; committed action as the daily unit. **Do not:** run acceptance exercises on the user's distress; sell "psychological flexibility" as an outcome.

### DBT skills
**Core.** Linehan 1993 (skills manual 2nd ed. 2015). Mindfulness ("wise mind" between emotion mind and reasonable mind); distress tolerance (STOP, TIPP, radical acceptance); emotion regulation ("check the facts"; "opposite action": act against the action urge when the emotion does not fit the facts or acting on it is ineffective); interpersonal effectiveness (DEAR MAN).

**Evidence.** Linehan et al. 2015 (JAMA Psychiatry, n = 99): the two skills-inclusive conditions beat individual therapy without skills on suicide attempts and self-injury, so skills are an active ingredient. Neacsiu et al. 2014 (n = 44 anxious/depressed adults with high emotion dysregulation, no BPD, 16 weeks skills-only vs activities group): emotion dysregulation d = 1.86, skills use d = 1.02, anxiety d = 1.37, depression ns; skills use mediated change. Valentine et al. 2015 (17 trials): preliminary, methodologically limited. Level B for skills-only in non-clinical emotion regulation.

**Borrow:** opposite action is the cleanest one-line answer to "didn't feel like it": "the urge is to skip; the opposite action is the two-minute version"; "check the facts" as light restructuring; STOP as the pause before abandoning a goal. **Do not:** distress tolerance for self-harm urges; chain analysis; "radical acceptance" of situations the user should leave.

### Compassion-Focused Therapy
**Core.** Gilbert 2009/2010. Three affect-regulation systems: threat (protection; anxiety, shame), drive (resource-seeking; achievement), soothing/affiliative (safeness). Self-criticism keeps threat on; many high achievers run drive to escape threat, so achievement never soothes. *Compassionate self-correction* (specific, forward-looking, wants improvement) vs *shame-based self-attacking* (global, past-focused, punitive).

**Evidence.** Craig, Hiskey & Spector 2020 and Millard et al. 2023 (15 RCTs and randomised pilots): small-to-moderate effects on compassion outcomes and symptoms vs waitlist; a 2023 meta-analysis reports self-compassion g = .23-4.14 and self-criticism g = .29-1.56 (extreme heterogeneity, small samples). Level B. Adjacent and more relevant: Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett 2010 (n = 119, two midterms): among heavy procrastinators, self-forgiveness for procrastinating on exam 1 predicted less procrastination before exam 2, partly via reduced negative affect. Level B.

**Borrow:** the post-miss script *is* compassionate self-correction: specific ("Tuesday didn't happen"), forward ("what would make Thursday easier?"), non-global (never "you always"). Name the three systems in one sentence when achievement never satisfies. **Do not:** compassionate imagery; pathologise drive; use "self-compassion" as cover for silently shrinking the goal.

### Mentalization and epistemic trust
**Core.** Fonagy & Allison 2014: mentalizing is reading behaviour in terms of mental states; **epistemic trust** (Csibra & Gergely) is willingness to treat communicated knowledge as relevant and generalisable. Why any therapy works: (1) feeling accurately mentalized is an "ostensive cue" that lowers epistemic vigilance; (2) social learning reopens; (3) trust generalises to learning from others in life. Campbell et al. 2021 add epistemic *mistrust* and *credulity* (uncritical acceptance) as separate failure modes.

**Evidence.** MBT has RCT support for BPD; the epistemic-trust account of common factors is theoretical with correlational support (2024 path analyses). Level C as mechanism, but it is the best available account of *why people accept guidance*.

**Borrow:** a marked, specific reflection before every suggestion ("so mornings fail because the bag is never packed, not because you don't care"): understood first, then offered. Invite the user to test suggestions. **Do not:** exploit credulity; a fluent, agreeable AI can manufacture it.

### Behavioural Activation
**Core and evidence.** Jacobson et al. 1996 (n = 150): BA alone, BA plus automatic-thought work, and full cognitive therapy were equivalent at post-treatment, 6 months and (Gortner 1998) 2 years; all three reduced negative thinking, so changing thoughts did not require targeting them. Martell, Addis & Jacobson 2001: work "outside-in", act on plan and goal rather than mood; TRAP/TRAC (Trigger, Response, Avoidance Pattern to Alternative Coping). Richards et al. 2016 (COBRA, n = 440): BA from junior workers non-inferior to CBT from experienced therapists at 12 months, about 20% cheaper. Ekers 2014 in framework doc. Level A.

**Borrow:** "action before motivation" as the stated rationale for tiny actions; TRAP/TRAC as barrier-tag logic. **Do not:** mood monitoring as treatment; frame BA as a depression intervention.

## 5. CBT specifics beyond the framework doc

**Socratic questioning.** Braun, Strunk, Sasso & Cooper 2015 (n = 55, cognitive therapy for depression): observer-rated Socratic questioning in sessions 1-3 predicted next-session improvement, controlling for alliance. Level B. Padesky's 1993 four stages (informational questions, empathic listening, summary, synthesising question) are the usable structure: ask, reflect, summarise, then one question that puts the pieces together. Guided discovery, not persuasion.

**Behavioural experiments vs thought records.** Bennett-Levy 2003 (trainees) rated experiments more powerful for belief change; McManus, Van Doorn & Yiend 2012 (non-clinical, single session, randomised): both beat control, experiments at least as strong. Level B. Consistent with Jacobson: doing beats disputing. "Test it" is safer than "challenge it": turn "I can't do mornings" into a three-day experiment with a written prediction.

**Perfectionism (Shafran, Egan, Wade).** Clinical perfectionism = self-worth contingent on striving to meet self-imposed standards (Shafran, Cooper & Fairburn 2002). Galloway et al. 2022 (15 trials): personal standards g = .57, concern over mistakes g = .89, clinical perfectionism g = .87, medium effects on depression and eating symptoms; Lloyd et al. 2015 similar. Level A. Sirois, Molnar & Hirsch 2017 meta-analysis: procrastination correlates with perfectionistic *concerns* (r = .23) and negatively with perfectionistic *strivings* (r = −.22); high standards are not the problem, fear of falling short is. Borrow: separate standards from worth; "good enough" experiments; catch "it doesn't count unless". Do not: deliver CBT-P; label the user.

**Problem-solving therapy (D'Zurilla, Nezu).** Problem orientation (positive vs negative) plus rational style (define, generate, decide, implement and verify) vs impulsive or avoidant styles. Malouff, Thorsteinsson & Schutte 2007 (31 studies, N = 2,895): d = 1.37 vs no treatment, .54 vs TAU, .54 vs attention placebo, .22 (ns) vs other bona fide treatments; moderators: **orientation training included** and **homework assigned**. Bell & D'Zurilla 2009 (21 samples): equal to other therapies and medication; better with orientation plus all four skills. Level A. The transferable finding: how the person frames the problem (solvable, normal, approachable) is an active ingredient, not just the steps. Adler's barrier tag plus "one thing to try" is PST-lite; the coach's language should model positive orientation.

**Cognitive distortions: construct status.** Measures built on the Beck/Burns taxonomy (CD-Quest; Cognitive Distortions Scale, Covin et al. 2011) consistently show a **single factor**: the categories are not empirically distinct, though total scores correlate with depression and anxiety. Cognitive mediation is contested (Longmore & Worrell 2007). New "distortions" are proposed without validation (a 2023 paper adds the "forever fallacy"). Level A that negative interpretive bias exists and matters; C that the named categories are real kinds. Naming a pattern ("that's an all-or-nothing read of one missed day") is legitimate psychoeducation; treating the label as a finding about the user is not.

## 6. Solution-focused brief therapy, deeper

**Core.** de Shazer & Berg (Milwaukee, 1980s; *Words Were Originally Magic*, 1994): the client already has exceptions; amplify them. Tools: miracle question, exceptions, scaling, coping questions, compliments, "what's better?" openings.

**Evidence.**
- Kim 2008: d = .13-.26, only internalising significant. Zhang et al. 2018 (medical RCTs): psychosocial d = .34, behavioural d = .28 (p = .06). Franklin et al. 2024 (community RCTs, 28 studies): g = .65. Franklin and Kim are proponents; Iranian trials inflate estimates (Froerer 2025).
- Grant 2012 and its partial 2022 replication (framework doc). Grant & Gerrard 2020 (n = 80) added a combined problem-then-solution condition: for participants low in dysfunctional attitudes, all three question sets reduced negative affect equally; for those *high* in dysfunctional attitudes, problem-focused and even combined questions were less effective, and only solution-focused questions were protective.
- Evidence isolating scaling or exception questions as single techniques is thin; process research (Franklin et al. 2017) shows solution-talk shifts in-session affect. Level B for the question set on affect, self-efficacy and goal approach; C for plan quality.

**Borrow:** "what's better since last week?" as the review opener; exceptions after a partial week ("Tuesday happened; what was different?"); scaling for confidence with "what makes it a 6, not a 5?". With self-critical users, acknowledge the problem in one sentence and move to exceptions; dwelling costs them (Grant & Gerrard). **Do not:** substitute the miracle question for barrier diagnosis.

## 7. Coaching psychology outcomes, including AI coaching

Headline meta-analyses are in the framework doc. Additions:

- Sonesh et al. 2015: coaching moved relationship outcomes (g = .32) more than goal attainment (g = .11); behavioural outcomes g = .19; students g = 1.0 vs executives g = .10. Relationships are easy to move, behaviour is hard.
- Wang, Lai, Xu & McDowall 2022 (psychologically informed coaching, 20 studies, N = 957): goal attainment g = 1.29, self-efficacy g = .59. de Haan & Nilsson 2023 (RCT-only): medium effects.
- **Dose.** Theeboom 2014: number of sessions did not moderate outcome. Jones 2016: no moderation by format (face-to-face vs blended) or duration; multisource feedback *reduced* effects. Terblanche 2022: within-user dose-response with chatbot usage. Reinhold 2018 (expressive writing): more sessions, larger effects. No evidence that longer sessions help; some that more frequent contact does.
- **Mediators.** Bozer & Jones 2018 (117 studies): self-efficacy, coaching motivation, goal orientation, trust, interpersonal attraction, feedback, supervisory support. de Haan, Grant, Burger & Eriksson 2016 (1,895 pairs, 366 coaches): effectiveness predicted by alliance and client self-efficacy, unrelated to personality or personality match; alliance mediated self-efficacy; **task and goal aspects of the alliance predicted more than bond**. Graßmann, Schölmerich & Schermuly 2020 (27 samples, N = 3,563): alliance-outcome r = .41 (.32-.64 by outcome type), negatively related to unintended negative effects (r = −.29). Level A.

**Digital and AI coaching trials, 2020-2026.**
- Terblanche, Molyn, de Haan & Nilsson 2022 (PLOS ONE): Vici chatbot, n = 75 vs 94, six months' use, eight measurement points. Goal attainment rose significantly (about 55% vs 24%); resilience, wellbeing and stress unchanged; dose-response; gains held three months after. Against the human-coach study it replicated, humans beat Vici on wellbeing, resilience and stress and matched it on goal attainment.
- Terblanche et al. 2024: generative vs scripted bots: equal goal attainment; generative better adoption and alliance.
- de Haan, Terblanche & Nowack 2026 (*HRDI*): 114 coachees randomised to accredited human or AI coaches; mid-to-high effects on goals, wellbeing and self-efficacy **only for human coaching**; baseline self-efficacy and hope predicted success; the authors propose "co-regulation" as the human advantage.
- Passmore, Tee & Rutschmann 2025: 43 managers coached by a generative agent, sessions rated by ICF assessors with the ICF BARS: met ACC and many PCC competencies; short on human connection, client-perceived empathy, personal experience, metaphor, humour.

A narrow AI coach can move goal attainment when design is tight and usage frequent; it does not move wellbeing; head-to-head under better control it lost. Adler's honest claim is adherence and goal attainment.

## 8. Narrative and identity

**Narrative therapy (White & Epston 1990).** Externalising ("the problem is the problem"), unique outcomes, re-authoring. Systematic reviews (eating disorders 2022; depression in somatic illness 2024; mood disorders 2023) find small samples, poor specification, inconsistent outcomes. Level C. Borrow the language only: "the skipping showed up Tuesday", not "you're a skipper".

**McAdams: narrative identity.** Three levels of personality (traits, adaptations, life story). McAdams et al. 2001: **redemption sequences** (bad to good) correlate with well-being and generativity; contamination sequences with lower well-being. Adler 2012 (n = 47 in therapy, 12 waves): rises in *agency* themes preceded mental-health gains. Adler et al. 2015 (4-year longitudinal): agency, redemption and contamination predicted mental-health trajectories. Adler, Lodi-Smith, Philippe & Houle 2016: incremental validity over traits. Level A correlational; C that inducing themes causes change; McAdams notes the redemptive self is a culturally American script. Borrow: a review question inviting honest agency ("what did *you* do that changed the week?"); never force redemption on a bad week.

**Expressive writing (Pennebaker & Beall 1986).** Frattaroli 2006 (146 studies): r = .075 (d about .15). Reinhold, Bürkner & Holling 2018 (39 RCTs, healthy adults): no significant long-term effect on depressive symptoms; larger with more sessions and a more *specific* topic. Level A that the effect is small. Best-possible-self writing (King 2001; reviews by Loveday 2018, Carrillo 2019): small effects on optimism and positive affect. Borrow: two-sentence structured reflection at the weekly review; never pitch journaling as therapy.

**Possible selves and identity-based motivation.** Markus & Nurius 1986: hoped-for, expected and feared selves link self-concept to motivation. Oyserman's identity-based motivation: identity congruence, action readiness, and **interpretation of difficulty** (as "this matters" vs "this isn't for me"). Oyserman, Terry & Bybee 2002 (School-to-Jobs, n = 62 vs 146): more school bonding, balanced possible selves paired with strategies, better attendance. Oyserman, Bybee & Terry 2006 (randomised, two-school-year follow-up): IBM changes mediated better core grades and fewer course failures. Horowitz et al. 2018: teacher-delivered version scaled. Oyserman et al. 2018 (randomised): a difficulty-as-importance mindset raised academic identity certainty and task performance vs difficulty-as-impossibility. Level A in adolescents; transfer to adults is inference. Borrow: pair a hoped-for self with a feared self *and* a strategy (fantasy alone fails; compare mental contrasting, framework doc); at friction, "hard because it matters" rather than "hard because it isn't you".

## 9. Cross-cutting: shared ingredients and the line a coach must not cross

**What effective approaches share.**
1. A credible rationale and ritual (expectation r = .18; open-label placebo g = .72 across 11 clinical trials, von Wernsdorff 2021: expectancy survives full disclosure, so honesty and expectancy are compatible).
2. Goal consensus and collaboration (r = .24-.29 in therapy; alliance r = .41 in coaching, task and goal leading).
3. Action between sessions. Mausbach et al. 2010 (23 studies, N = 2,183): compliance to outcome r = .26. Kazantzis et al. 2016 (17 studies, N = 2,312): *quantity and quality* of homework both predict outcome. Kazantzis, Whittington & Dattilio 2010 (46 studies): treatments with homework vs without, d = .48. Homework was a PST moderator (Malouff 2007).
4. Feedback that changes the plan (de Jong 2021).
5. Values or meaning as fuel (Chase 2013; Vos 2015).
6. Positive problem orientation (Malouff 2007; Bell & D'Zurilla 2009).
7. Behaviour first, cognition second (Jacobson 1996; COBRA; behavioural experiments).

**Expectancy used honestly.** Say what the evidence supports and for whom ("people who review weekly and change one thing keep going longer"); give a rationale for each move; make predictions the user can check; promise nothing. Open-label placebo data say this loses little; the APA advisory says implied promises and retention hooks are manipulation.

**Risk of drifting into therapy.** Green, Oades & Grant 2006: 52% of life-coaching seekers had clinically significant distress (BSI); Spence & Grant 2007: 26%. Cavanagh 2005: mental health is a major, rarely assessed moderator of coaching. Bachkirova & Baker 2019: coaches overestimate their ability to handle therapeutic material outside crises; the boundary is defined by accountability, goal type, time orientation and focus, not topic. ICF referral criteria are in the framework doc; the EMCC Global Code of Ethics (2021) adds an explicit duty to recognise limits of competence and refer. Techniques that look like coaching but are treatment: exposure hierarchies, chain analysis, schema or core-belief work, trauma narrative, formal cognitive restructuring, distress tolerance for self-harm. Drift signals for Adler: past-cause exploration, reassurance-seeking instead of planning, mood talk exceeding goal talk across two sessions, WHO-5 below threshold.

## Proposed alliance-measurement plan for an AI coach

**Instruments.**
- **WAI-SR** (Hatcher & Gillaspy 2006): 12 items, goal/task/bond subscales, 5-point, alpha about .95; 6-item form validated (Falkenström et al. 2015). Adapt "therapist" to "coach"; keep goal and task items intact; keep two bond items ("I feel the coach understands me", "I believe the coach cares how I do") because feeling understood is the epistemic-trust cue, but analyse bond separately.
- **Session Rating Scale** (Duncan et al. 2003): four visual-analogue items (relationship, goals and topics, approach, overall). Use two items after each weekly review: "we worked on what matters to me" (goal) and "the way we worked fit me" (task).

**Cadence.** Two-item SRS after every weekly review. WAI-SR-6 at day 7 (chatbot alliance forms by day 5), week 4, week 12, then quarterly. Full 12-item only in cohort studies.

**Behavioural proxies, logged continuously.** Proposal acceptance and edit rates (collaboration), check-in completion, reply latency, unprompted barrier disclosures, opt-outs of suggestions. These are the coaching analogue of homework quality.

**Decision rules (the part de Jong 2021 says matters).**
- SRS goal or task item below 7/10: next turn asks "what should we change about how this works?"; the change is logged.
- WAI-SR-6 drop of more than 1 SD between waves: re-run goal consensus before anything else.
- Never use a low alliance score to increase message frequency or emotional appeals (APA 2025).

**Validate before optimising.** Test whether day-7 goal/task scores predict 4-week adherence and 12-week retention, and separately whether bond does. If bond does not predict adherence, stop tuning for it (Terblanche 2024). Expected from de Haan 2016: goal and task predict, bond adds little.

**Quality audit.** Quarterly transcript sample rated against the ICF BARS by an external assessor (Passmore 2025), tracking the known AI weak spots: perceived empathy, connection, over-agreement.

## Implications for Adler

1. Chase 2013 is the strongest single finding for a goal app: goal setting without values did nothing; values plus goals moved grades. Discovery must produce a "why" before a "what".
2. Build a task-and-goal alliance, not a bond: agree the goal, revisit it, let the user edit every plan. That is what predicts coaching outcomes and what an AI can honestly offer.
3. Feedback works only with a rule that forces a change. The weekly "adjust one thing" is the FIT mechanism; make it mandatory when adherence is low.
4. The post-miss message carries Rogers (unconditional), Gilbert (compassionate correction) and Wohl (self-forgiveness) at once: specific, forward, non-global, identical in warmth to the success message.
5. "Didn't feel like it" has a one-line evidence-based answer: BA rationale, DBT opposite action, IBM difficulty-as-importance.
6. Experiments and problem orientation beat disputation. When a belief blocks a goal, test it; when a barrier appears, model "solvable".
7. Congruence is the anti-sycophancy principle: the coach must be able to disagree.
8. Claim adherence and goal attainment, not wellbeing; the AI trials support the first and the 2026 head-to-head favoured humans on the rest.
9. A quarter to a half of coaching seekers carry clinical-level distress. Scope detection is a core feature.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **AI alliance vs outcome.** Woebot and Wysa show therapy-level WAI-SR; Terblanche 2024 shows alliance rising without outcome gain; de Haan 2026 shows humans winning. Whether an AI alliance is causal, an engagement proxy, or noise is unresolved. Measure it and test it.
- **Common vs specific factors.** Cuijpers 2019 is right that correlational data cannot settle it. Practical answer: do both, relationship variables plus behavioural ingredients.
- **Dose.** No session-count moderation in coaching meta-analyses; dose-response within chatbot users and in expressive writing. Frequent short contact is the best guess; no direct evidence on check-in cadence.
- **Transfer.** Meaning-centred effects come from cancer patients; possible-selves RCTs from 13-year-olds; DBT skills from clinical dysregulation; PST from depression. Every borrow here is an inference to healthy 16-40-year-old optimisers.
- **Allegiance.** SFBT (Franklin, Kim), CFT (Gilbert's group), PST (developer involvement was a Malouff moderator) and ACT estimates come partly from proponents.
- **Distortion categories** are pedagogically useful and psychometrically unreal. Whether naming a pattern helps more than proposing a test is untested.
- **Self-forgiveness and self-compassion** can become cover for silently shrinking goals. The user decides the size.
