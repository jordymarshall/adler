# Wisdom, self-distancing and decision hygiene: frame-shifting as a coach move

Research date: 2026-09-02. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `deep/self-regulation-theory.md` (SRT §n), `deep/emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md` (EW §n), `deep/individual-differences.md` (ID §n), `deep/lapse-relapse-recovery.md` (LR §n). Established there and not repeated: perspective-taking is the best-supported emotion-regulation family (Webb 2012, d = 0.45; EW §1); "what would you tell a friend?" as an inhibition tactic and distancing's place in Duckworth's self-deployed cognitive quadrant (SRT §4, §6); self-compassion's friend framing (CF §1 rank 7; EW §6); WOOP's obstacle step (CF §1 rank 4, Phase 2); detailed plans backfire for socially prescribed perfectionists (Powers 2005; ID §4); the post-miss script (CF Phase 4; LR §5). This file prices the frame-shifting moves themselves: the friend question, the year question, third-person self-talk, the pre-mortem, unpacking, and the outside view.

Evidence levels: **A** meta-analysis or multi-site RCT; **B** single RCT, lab or field experiment; **C** correlational, diary or archival; **D** theory or practitioner description. Effect sizes as reported (d/g/ηp²/r).

---

## 1. Wise reasoning is a state, not a trait

Grossmann's construct — intellectual humility, recognition of change and multiple outcomes, others' perspectives, integration/compromise — behaves like a situation, not a personality. In a two-week daily diary (Grossmann, Gerlach & Denissen 2016, *SPPS*, N = 152; **C**): day-to-day r = .20 for wise reasoning (perspectives/compromise .06), odd-vs-even-day aggregates r = .48, **within-person SD exceeded between-person SD on every facet**, no individual-difference measure predicted it (all |t| < 1.49), reasoning was wiser in social than non-social situations (B = .045 humility, p = .0002), and keeping the diary itself raised self-transcendence (B = .030, p = .029). Brienza et al. 2018 (*JPSP*, N = 4,463; **C**) agrees from psychometrics: the situated SWIS correlated r = .48 across waves, was unrelated to self-deception/impression management and predicted balancing of self-protective and cooperative interests, whereas *global* wisdom self-reports were contaminated by socially desirable responding and were unrelated or **negatively** related to balance. Dong, Weststrate & Fournier 2023 (*Perspectives on Psych Science*; **A**) confirms the split: self-report and performance-rated wisdom share only openness and well-being correlates.

Consequence: never assess wisdom as a trait or ask about it abstractly; ask about one concrete situation, and expect the same person to reason well on Tuesday and badly on Thursday.

**Situational boosts with experimental support (all B).** Reasoning about someone else's version of the problem (Grossmann & Kross 2014); an observer vantage or third-person language (Kross & Grossmann 2012, 2 experiments — raised dialecticism, humility, attitude assimilation, willingness to join a bipartisan group); being asked what people whose opinion you value would say (Staudinger & Baltes 1996); diary practice. Strongest single result: **Grossmann et al. 2021** (*Psychological Science*, two preregistered longitudinal experiments, total N = 555): a month of daily third-person diary reflection raised wise reasoning about interpersonal challenges relative to first-person diarying (distanced narratives M = .16 [.12, .19] vs control .06 [.01, .10]; B = 0.10, SE = 0.03), mediated by a broader self-focus; a one-week experiment replicated it against both first-person and no-pronoun controls. This is the only wisdom "training" with a preregistered, replicated design.

## 2. Self-distancing: real, small, and easy to administer wrong

Kross & Ayduk (2017 *Adv. Exp. Soc. Psych.*; 2023 *Handbook of Emotion Regulation* ch. 63; **D**) define distancing as shifting vantage point while keeping attention on the same experience — not avoidance, broader than reappraisal, narrower than mindfulness. Taxonomy: linguistic (distanced self-talk, generic "you", foreign language, expressive writing), conceptual (temporal distancing, big-picture appraisals, observer perspective, detached reappraisal), behavioural (rituals, awe). Mechanism: reconstruing rather than recounting.

Effect sizes depend heavily on what is pooled. **Moran & Eyal 2022** (*PSPR*, k = 230 distance manipulations; **A**): g = 0.52 for attenuating emotional experience, but 0.64 for basic emotions and only 0.29 for self-conscious ones — the family that goal lapses actually produce (shame, guilt, embarrassment). **Murdoch et al. 2023** (*Stress and Health*, 25 experiments, N = 2,397, 68 effects; **A**), restricted to self-distanced vs self-immersed reflection on a real autobiographical stressor: **g = 0.19 [0.05, 0.33]**, I² = 66%, 95% prediction interval **−0.42 to 0.80**; the only meaningful moderator was the target — reflections keyed to one's emotional state or the event's emotional significance reached g = 0.44 [0.27, 0.62]. No funnel asymmetry, but 14/25 studies were high risk of bias, 24/25 undergraduate samples, GRADE certainty **low**. The original Kross et al. 2014 effects (*JPSP*, 7 studies, N = 585; **B**) sit at ηp² = .046–.112 (d ≈ 0.45–0.70) in cells of 25–60 — the familiar small-sample inflation pattern.

Two hard negatives. **Riddell et al. 2023** (*Stress and Health*, preregistered RCT, N = 257; **B**): one guided distanced reflection produced no benefit over immersed reflection on sleep duration or well-being across the following work week, and *worse* self-reported sleep quality (−3.87%, p = .024). **Giovanetti et al. 2019** (*J. Soc. Clin. Psych.*; **B**): repeated third-person expressive writing raised depressive symptoms relative to ordinary expressive writing and to no writing. Decisive for product design: in Riddell, **51 of 158 people assigned to the distanced condition wrote it incorrectly** (32%), and Murdoch found adherence checked in only 11 of 19 write-based experiments.

What survives well: distancing is *cheap* and *targetable*. Webster, Berg, Kross & Moser 2022 (*Int. J. Psychophysiology*, N = 67; **B**) directly replicated that distanced self-talk lowers the late positive potential without raising the stimulus-preceding negativity (no extra anticipatory effort), while detached reappraisal showed neither. Kross et al. 2017 (*Applied Psych: Health & Well-Being*, N = 1,257, 2014 Ebola scare; **B**): third-person writing produced more fact-based reasons and lower worry **only among people high in baseline worry**.

## 3. Solomon's paradox and the friend question

Grossmann & Kross 2014 (*Psych Science*, 3 experiments, N = 693; **B**): people reason more wisely about a friend's identical problem than their own (Study 1 ηp² = .25, N = 104), and instructed self-distancing closes the gap (Study 2 self-distanced vs self-immersed ηp² = .06, N = 120; Study 3 ηp² = .015, N = 469, with no age differences — against "with age comes wisdom" for personal problems). Note the monotone shrinkage with sample size. Independent replication: Xu, Zhang & Wang 2022 (*Frontiers in Psychology*, N = 399; **B**), asymmetry at ηp² = .03, mediated by positive affect then self-transcendence.

The caveat for a goal coach: **advice is systematically more idealistic than choice** (Danziger, Montal & Barkan 2012, *JPSP*, 6 experiments; **B**) — advisers construe at a higher level and weight ideals, choosers weight pragmatics. The friend question produces the values-consistent answer and under-weights Tuesday-night constraints. It is a prioritisation instrument, not a scheduling one.

## 4. Temporal distancing: "what will this look like in a year"

Bruehlman-Senecal & Ayduk 2015 (*JPSP*, 7 studies; **B**): adopting a distant-future perspective on a recent stressor reduced distress relative to near-future and control framings (Studies 1a/1b, N ≈ 216, ηp² = .11), mediated by **impermanence focus** rather than avoidance or ideal-future focus. Study 3 is the useful one: the benefit appeared only for students who did badly on the midterm, not for those who did well. Benkley, Willroth, Ayduk, John & Mauss 2023 (*Emotion*, 8-day diary, N = 155, 837 observations; **C**) found within-person temporal distancing tracked lower negative and higher positive emotion in daily life.

Its cognitive side effect is real but smaller than advertised: Soderberg et al. 2015 (*Psych Bulletin*, 125 studies, 310 effects; **A**) put distance → abstraction at g = 0.475 [0.405, 0.545] (trim-and-fill 0.299, I² = 68%); the preregistered high-powered test of the canonical "one day vs one year" manipulation (Sánchez, Coleman & Ledgerwood 2021, *Social Cognition*; **B**) found **d = 0.276 [0.097, 0.455]**. The direction is *away* from concrete action representation — which is why a year question must be followed by a concrete step.

## 5. Planning fallacy: the finding is narrower than the folklore

Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994 (*JPSP*; **B**): thesis students predicted 33.9 days, took 55.5, and only ~30% finished by their own prediction. **Halkjelsvik & Jørgensen 2012** (*Psych Bulletin*, integrative review; **A**) is the corrective: underestimation dominates engineering/management data but *not* psychology lab studies, where there is no general bias; the "small tasks overestimated, large underestimated" pattern is partly a random-error artifact; and *performance time* (hours of work) differs from *completion time* (delivery date), which most studies conflate. Defensible claim: underestimation is robust for **completion dates of self-relevant, multi-step, interruptible projects** — Adler's exact case — not for duration judgments generally, and no universal multiplier is justified.

Correctives with direct evidence:
- **Unpacking/segmentation.** Kruger & Evans 2004 (*JESP*; **B**): listing subcomponents raised predictions and reduced underestimation. Forsyth & Burt 2008 (*Memory & Cognition*, 3 experiments; **B**): summed subtask allocations reliably exceed whole-task allocations, not explained by rounding. Replication is thin — the one indexed independent attempt (Huang 2021, MTurk) failed, but it is weak enough not to outweigh the originals.
- **Backward planning.** Wiese, Buehler & Griffin 2016 (*JDM*, 4 studies; **B**): planning in reverse from the deadline produced longer and (Study 4, against actuals) less biased predictions. Park, Lu & Hedgcock 2017 (*Psych Science*; **B**): it also raised motivation, goal expectancy and goal-relevant performance while lowering felt time pressure — the rare debiasing move that does not cost motivation.

## 6. Pre-mortem and prospective hindsight

Mitchell, Russo & Pennington 1989 (*JBDM*; **B**) is the root finding: describing a future event as though it had already happened increased correctly identified reasons by ~30%. Klein 2007 (*HBR*; **D**) packaged it as the pre-mortem. Tests: Veinott, Klein & Wiggins 2010 (ISCRAM, N = 178; **B**) — the pre-mortem cut plan confidence more than critique or pros-and-cons; Keysor, Wojtyna & Veinott 2020 (SJDM; **B**) replicated on a new scenario (Exp 1, N = 53, F(2,51) = 4.02, p = .024, **d = 0.81**), with no individual-vs-group difference (Exp 2, N = 43); Gallop, Willy & Bischoff 2016 (*J. Enterprise Transformation*, 101 program managers/engineers; **B**) — pre-mortem teams generated more high-quality risks, plan changes and "black swan" risks than brainstorming teams.

What none of this shows is improved *outcomes* or *calibration*: every measured outcome is confidence, reason count or risk count. Confidence reduction is a gain only if confidence was too high, and self-efficacy predicts goal progress (SRT §2), so a pre-mortem run on a shaky user after a lapse can subtract something valuable.

## 7. The outside view and decision hygiene

Kahneman & Tversky's outside view, operationalised as reference-class forecasting (Flyvbjerg 2006; **C/D**), is mandated by several transport ministries but its evidence is archival. Cantarelli, Davis, Pinto & Turner 2025 (*Production Planning & Control*, systematic review 2001–2025; **A** of **C** studies) report mean overruns of 1–238% across 25 project classes and conclude RCF's advantage is *conditional*: it wins at high risk levels, loses to conventional methods at moderate confidence levels, and fails where the reference class is thin, the technology routine, or overruns stem from late scope change rather than bias (Ika, Love & Pinto 2022). Strongest quasi-experimental support: Lovallo, Clarke & Camerer 2012 (*SMJ*; **B/C**), where a similarity-weighted reference class beat both a few familiar analogies (private equity) and regression models (film revenue).

The underlying principle — structure beats intuition — is solid: Grove et al. 2000 (*Psychological Assessment*, meta-analysis; **A**), mechanical prediction ~10% more accurate, substantially better in 33–47% of studies, worse in only 6–16%. And bias training transfers further than the 2011 consensus assumed: Morewedge et al. 2015 (*Policy Insights*, 2 longitudinal experiments; **B**) found medium-to-large debiasing from one session persisting ≥2 months, and Sellier, Scopelliti & Morewedge 2019 (*Psych Science*, N = 290; **B**) found trained students **29% less likely** to pick the confirmation-biased solution in an unannounced case.

## Implications for Adler

**Verbatim questions (use as written; each is a licensed move, not a habit).**
1. *Friend question* — for "which goal do I drop / should I quit this": "Someone you respect has your exact situation — same goal, same week, same constraints. What do you tell them to do next?" then "What part of that are you not doing?" then, mandatory, "Do you want that as this week's action? When and where?" (Grossmann & Kross 2014; Danziger 2012 forces the third turn.)
2. *Year question* — only after a bad week or a visible setback: "Put yourself a year from now, looking back at this week. What will you say about it?" then "What of this actually changes the thing you're building?" Never phrase it as "it doesn't matter" — the mechanism is impermanence, not dismissal (Bruehlman-Senecal & Ayduk 2015).
3. *Name/third-person* — for high-arousal rumination only: "Answer this one as if you were talking to yourself by name — why is [name] taking this so hard, and what does [name] do next?" (Kross et al. 2014; Kross et al. 2017.)
4. *Pre-mortem* — at plan formation for a dated project goal ≥ 4 weeks out: "It's [deadline]. It didn't happen. Write the three reasons why." then "Which one can you make impossible this week?" (Mitchell 1989; Klein 2007; Veinott 2010.)
5. *Backward plan + unpack* — for any time estimate: "List the steps end to end. Now start at the deadline and work backwards — what has to be true the week before?" (Wiese 2016; Park 2017; Forsyth & Burt 2008.)
6. *Personal reference class* — Adler's own advantage: "Last three weeks you planned N sessions and did M. Do you want to plan against that number instead of the ideal one?" (outside view with the user's own base rate, which sidesteps every RCF criticism about thin or mismatched classes.)

**Rules and thresholds.**
- Fire a distancing move only when one of these is true: self-reported distress high (≥ 7/10) or emotion-laden language present; the same obstacle logged ≥ 3 times (aligns with the 3-in-14 revisit rule in `deep/motivation-science.md`); the user asks whether to quit or drop a goal. In neutral or positive states, stay immersed — distancing dampens positive affect too.
- One distancing move per conversation. Never stack the friend question and the year question.
- **Verify the move happened.** If the reply to a third-person prompt comes back entirely in "I", or the year answer is written in present tense, re-prompt once with a worked example, then abandon and switch to problem-solving. 32% of assigned participants executed it wrongly in the one preregistered trial (Riddell 2023); an unchecked distancing prompt has an expected effect of roughly zero.
- Cap third-person written reflection at ~1/week; do not build a daily third-person journal for a low-mood user (Giovanetti 2019). Grossmann's month-long daily diary produced wisdom gains in a general sample — that is a growth intervention, not a distress intervention, and should be offered as an opt-in n-of-1 experiment (`deep/adaptive-interventions.md` §6), not a default.
- Always close a distancing move with one concrete if-then. Distancing raises construal level (g ≈ 0.30–0.48) and advice runs idealistic; without the concrete close, the output is a wise sentence and no behaviour.
- Never run a pre-mortem on someone whose confidence is already low, or within 7 days of a lapse (LR §5), or on socially prescribed perfectionists flagged by the ID §4 rule. Require ≥ 3 reasons; convert ≥ 1 into an if-then; then re-check plan confidence. If confidence falls below ~40%, respond by shrinking scope (CF Phase 6), never by dropping the goal in the same session.
- No generic planning multiplier ("×1.5"). Halkjelsvik & Jørgensen shows no universal bias; use unpacking + backward planning, and quote the user's own ratio only once ≥ 3 comparable predicted-vs-actual pairs exist.
- Measure situations, not the person: if wisdom-ish state is logged at all, use 3–5 SWIS-style items about *this* situation. Never ask "how self-aware are you", never store a "wise user" trait — global wisdom self-reports measure impression management (Brienza 2018).

**What to avoid.** "Why do you feel that way?" asked cold — analysing feelings from an immersed stance is the rumination-producing version of the same operation (Kross et al. 2012 in MDD adults); if you ask why, prefix the distanced frame. The "Will I?" interrogative self-talk trick (Senay et al. 2010) failed independent replication and should not be used. Detached reappraisal ("view it neutrally, as a scientist") showed no ERP effect where distanced self-talk did (Webster 2022) — prefer name/you framing. Do not present the pre-mortem as a prediction, and do not use imagined-failure language for identity goals.

**How this helps the coach adapt to one person.** Three per-user statistics fall out: (a) *planning ratio* per goal type (predicted ÷ actual) — a personal reference class that replaces generic advice about optimism once ≥ 3 pairs exist; (b) *distancing responder status* — log which frame-shift preceded a return to action within 7 days and stop deploying the ones that never do (extends the EW implications rule); Riddell found ~8% greater residual variance in the distanced arm, i.e. real responders and real non-responders; (c) *situational profile* — wise reasoning is state-like and higher in social contexts, so note which domains (relationships, money, career) produce this user's worst reasoning and route the friend question there first.

## Conflicts and open questions

1. **How big is self-distancing?** Moran & Eyal's g = 0.52 pools every distance manipulation and mostly basic emotions; Murdoch's g = 0.19 with a prediction interval spanning zero is the estimate that matches Adler's use case (real autobiographical stressor, brief written reflection). Riddell's preregistered null and Giovanetti's harm signal are the same construct. The evidence favours **small, heterogeneous, condition-dependent** — treat it as a targeted tool for high-arousal moments (g ≈ 0.44 when the reflection is keyed to the emotional meaning), not a routine.
2. **Effect-size decay with rigour.** Solomon's paradox: ηp² = .25 (N = 104) → .015 (N = 469) → .03 in independent replication. Temporal distance → abstraction: g = .475 meta-analytic → d = .276 preregistered. Direction robust; magnitude in a product should be assumed at the preregistered end.
3. **Distancing makes reasoning wiser but plans vaguer.** Higher construal is the documented consequence and advisers weight ideals over pragmatics; nobody has tested the sequence a coach would actually use — distance → decide → re-immerse → schedule. Worth an internal A/B once Adler has volume.
4. **Does any of this change behaviour?** The distancing evidence is emotional and cognitive; the wisdom evidence is reasoning quality. There is no adult RCT of self-distancing on goal adherence; the nearest positives are child perseverance studies and Park 2017's backward-planning performance effect. Treat behavioural claims as unproven.
5. **Pre-mortem measures confidence, not calibration**, and confidence reduction trades against self-efficacy. Contrast mental contrasting, which has outcome evidence (CF §1 rank 4): if only one obstacle move can be spent, WOOP has the better warrant and the pre-mortem is a plan-level supplement.
6. **Outside view vs error.** Flyvbjerg's bias account and Ika/Love/Pinto's error-and-scope-change account are both endorsed by the 2025 review. Matters less for an individual (their own history is a clean reference class), but the coach should not moralise about optimism.
7. **Unpacking's replication record is thin** and padded estimates may cost motivation; backward planning is the safer default because it improved motivation as well as accuracy.
8. **Dose.** Grossmann's wisdom gains came from ~4 weeks of daily diarying while the one-shot literature is null-ish; whether a conversational coach delivers that dose without the diary is untested.
