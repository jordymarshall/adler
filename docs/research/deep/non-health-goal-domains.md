# Non-health goal domains: money, learning, career, creative work, relationships

Research date: 2026-09-02. Extends `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `atomic-habits-and-training-science.md` (AH), `deep/habit-science-deep.md` (HS), `deep/bct-effectiveness.md` (BCT), `deep/lapse-relapse-recovery.md` (LR), `deep/social-accountability.md` (SA), `deep/feedback-and-progress.md` (FP). Not repeated: implementation intentions, monitoring, WOOP, streaks (CF §1); Lally, cue type, the four-bouts-a-week figure (HS §4); the progression table (AH Part C); deliberate practice (AH Part B); partner-support nulls (SA §2); Theobald 2021 (BCT §4, de-orphaned below).

Grades: **A** meta-analysis or large replicated quasi-experiment; **B** single RCT or field experiment; **C** uncontrolled programme report; **D** anecdote or theory only. Everything in this file is weaker than the health corpus Adler's mechanics were built on. Section 9 says how much weaker.

## 1. Money

**The two big syntheses agree the education route is near-worthless and disagree only about how near.** Fernandes, Lynch & Netemeyer 2014 (*Management Science*; 168 papers, 201 studies; **A**): interventions to improve financial literacy explain **0.1% of the variance** in financial behaviour, weaker in low-income samples, negligible beyond 20 months. Kaiser, Lusardi, Menkhoff & Urban 2022 (*J Financial Economics*; **76 RCTs, N > 160,000**; **A**) re-estimate the same construct upward: unweighted mean effect on behaviour **0.090 SD** (knowledge 0.187 SD), publication-bias-corrected **0.065 SD [0.043, 0.089]** against Fernandes's re-computed 0.018 SD [−0.004, 0.022]. Mean dose 11.7 hours of instruction. Decay: 0.099 SD under six months, 0.071 SD after six, 0.057 SD after two years (differences beyond six months not significant). Kaiser & Menkhoff 2020 (*Economics of Education Review*; 37 school experiments; **A**): knowledge +0.25 SD, behaviour **+0.05 SD**. Verdict: eleven hours of teaching buys about a tenth of a standard deviation. Adler must never position itself as financial education.

**What moves money is structural.** Madrian & Shea 2001 (*QJE*; single-firm natural experiment; **A**, replicated): 401(k) participation ~86% under automatic enrolment versus about half of the cohort hired just before, at matched tenure — and most stayed at both the default rate and the default fund. Chetty et al. 2014 (*QJE*; 41 million Danish observations; **A**): ~85% of people are "passive savers"; $1 of subsidy raises total saving by **1 cent**, while automatic contributions raise wealth substantially. Beshears et al. 2022 (*J Finance*): auto-enrolment raised cumulative contributions by **4.1% of salary over four years** with no rise in financial distress. The correction: Choukhmane 2025 (*AER*; 100+ US plans plus a UK panel; **A**) replicates the 12-month effect but shows it **attenuates by 36 months**. Defaults are the strongest lever here and still smaller than the folklore.

**Commitment and reminders, the two repeatable moves.** Ashraf, Karlan & Yin 2006 (*QJE*; **B**): baseline N = 1,777, 710 offered a SEED commitment account, **202 (28.4%) took it**; savings balances **+81 percentage points at 12 months** (ITT). Karlan et al. 2016 (*Management Science*; three field experiments, Philippines/Peru/Bolivia; **B**): a **monthly** reminder raised total saved **6%** (p = .065–.079) and goal attainment by **3 percentage points**; reminders naming the client's specific goal raised savings **16%**, reminders that did not had **no effect**. Theodos, Stacy & Daniels 2018 (*JEBO*; **B**) is the only RCT of financial *coaching*: real effects on money management, debt, savings and well-being, but the two sites produced different outcomes. Thaler & Benartzi 2004 (*JPE*) pre-commits future pay rises to saving; design verified, headline figures not independently checked here.

**Action-design rules (money).**
1. The first action for a money goal is a **one-off structural change with a date**, not a daily habit: automate a transfer, raise the pension rate, cancel N subscriptions, move to a higher-rate account.
2. The only recurring artefact is a **monthly review that names the specific goal** (Karlan: naming the expenditure is what carried the effect; unnamed reminders did nothing).
3. Never teach. If the user asks a factual money question, answer in one sentence and return to the structural change.
4. Ask about commitment *once*, framed as opt-in: 28% took it up in Ashraf; the other 72% must not be pushed.
5. Verify persistence at 3, 6 and 12 months (Choukhmane): "is the transfer still running at the amount we set?"

**Transfer.** Implementation intentions transfer (a structural change still needs when/where/how). Monitoring transfers, at monthly cadence. **Automaticity, the days-per-week floor, ≤25% growth and the lapse ladder do not transfer at all** — there is no stimulus–response link in a standing order.

## 2. Learning and skills

**The strategy is the intervention.** Dunlosky et al. 2013 (*PSPI* 14(1); criteria-based synthesis, **A** for breadth): high utility = practice testing and distributed practice; low utility = rereading, highlighting, summarisation, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text. Effect sizes since: Rowland 2014 (*Psych Bulletin*; 159 effects) testing vs restudy **g = 0.50 [0.42, 0.58]**; Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan 2017 (*Rev Educ Res*) **g = 0.61 [0.58, 0.65]** against all comparison conditions (figure as reported by Latimier et al. 2021; the original is paywalled and I could not open it); Yang et al. 2021 (*Psych Bulletin*; **222 classroom studies, 48,478 students**) **g = 0.499**, moderated by corrective feedback and number of repetitions; Donoghue & Hattie 2021 (*Frontiers in Education*; 242 studies, 1,619 effects, 169,179 participants) overall **0.56**, distributed practice and practice testing highest, underlining and summarisation lowest — with the authors' own caveat that most outcomes are **surface or factual**, and effects are much larger for lower-ability learners.

**Two findings that contradict product folklore.** Latimier, Peyre & Ramus 2021 (*Educ Psych Rev*; 29 studies): spaced retrieval beats massed retrieval **g = 0.74**, but **expanding versus uniform spacing schedules g = 0.032, ns** — the expanding-interval algorithm at the heart of every SRS app is not supported over simply spacing evenly. Brunmair & Richter 2019 (*Psych Bulletin*; 59 studies, 238 effects): interleaving **g = 0.42** overall, but paintings 0.67, maths 0.34, expository texts and tastes non-significant, and **words g = −0.39** (blocking wins). Interleaving is a conditional prescription, not a rule.

**Teaching the strategies is worth about half of the strategies.** Theobald 2021 (*Contemporary Educational Psychology*; 49 studies, N = 5,786; BCT §4): self-regulated-learning training **g = 0.38**, metacognitive strategies 0.40. Bisra et al. 2018 self-explanation g = 0.55 (FP §5) is the same family. Macnamara & Maitra 2019 (*R Soc Open Sci*) failed to replicate Ericsson's core deliberate-practice finding and found teacher-designed practice no better than practice alone (AH Part B).

**Action-design rules (learning).**
1. The action names a **strategy plus a duration**, never a duration alone: "20 minutes of closed-book recall on chapter 4", not "20 minutes of study".
2. The check-in has **two taps**: did the session happen, and did you retrieve before looking. The second tap is the one that carries g ≈ 0.5.
3. Default schedule: **2–4 spaced sessions a week**, spacing increasing across weeks. Do not push toward daily; spacing is the effect (0.74), frequency is not.
4. Offer interleaving **only** for similar-but-confusable material (languages, maths problem types, visual discrimination). Explicitly do not interleave vocabulary lists or prose.
5. Do not sell expanding intervals as optimal; uniform spacing is statistically indistinguishable.
6. Growth is in **material difficulty and strategy fidelity**, not minutes.

**Transfer.** Implementation intentions and monitoring transfer. **The automaticity gate does not**: a study session that has become automatic tells you nothing about whether retrieval happened, and the SRBAI item is undefined for a cognitively effortful session. The four-days-a-week floor actively contradicts the spacing evidence.

## 3. Career and job search

**Interventions work; the effective ones combine two ingredients.** Liu, Huang & Wang 2014 (*Psych Bulletin*; 47 experimentally or quasi-experimentally evaluated interventions; **A**): odds of obtaining employment **OR = 2.67**. Components that mattered: job-search skills, self-presentation, self-efficacy, proactivity, goal setting, social support — and interventions promoted employment **only when both skill development and motivation enhancement were present**. Larger effects for younger and older (vs middle-aged) and for the short-term unemployed. Vinokur, Price & Schul 1995 (*Am J Community Psych*; JOBS II randomised field experiment, **N = 1,801**, 671 treated; **B**) names the mechanism Adler needs: mastery, problem-solving, and **inoculation against setbacks**; benefits concentrated in those at high risk for depression.

**But effort barely predicts outcome.** van Hooft et al. 2021 (*JAP*; **378 samples, N = 165,933**; **A**): job-search intensity → interviews **rc = .23**, offers **rc = .14**, employment status **rc = .19**, and intensity **failed to predict employment quality** at all — applying to more jobs explains roughly 2% of the variance in offers. Job-search *self-regulation* and *quality* predicted both. Wanberg, Zhu, Kanfer & Zhang 2012 (*AMJ*; 20 weekly waves with newly unemployed; **B**): intensity and mental health co-move with transient self-regulatory states (motivation control, self-defeating cognition), not traits; Wanberg et al. 2012 (*Personnel Psychology*; qualitative) name the mechanisms as managing mood, feedback seeking and self-reflection.

**Action-design rules (career).**
1. **The check-in unit is the week, not the day.** The outcome (offer) is binary and lags months; the weekly count of *targeted* applications, conversations and follow-ups is the only thing a check-in can honestly hold.
2. Track **quality, not volume**: one tap for "targeted or mass?" Volume has rc = .14 on offers and zero on job quality.
3. **Inoculate before the first rejection** (Vinokur): state the base rate at goal formation — "most applications end in silence; that is the process, not a signal about you." Do this once, at setup.
4. **A rejection is not a lapse.** Never route it into the miss/shrink/re-cue machinery (CF Phase 6). The correct move is a self-regulation prompt (Wanberg: mood management, feedback seeking, one strategy change), not a smaller action.
5. Both ingredients or neither (Liu): every career goal carries a skill action *and* a motivation/self-efficacy element. Skills alone did not work.

**Transfer.** Weekly monitoring transfers. Specific-challenging goals transfer for the *input*. **The 4-days floor, automaticity and the lapse ladder do not.** The shrink rule (CF §2) transfers only when the barrier is "no time", never when the barrier is a rejection.

## 4. Creative and knowledge work

**This is the weakest domain in the file.** Boice 1989 (*Behav Res Ther*): the abstract describes two inquiries, the second "tested an intervention for procrastination (helping new faculty find brief, daily sessions in which to write) that proved effective and relatively unaversive". No randomisation, no sample size and no effect size appear in the abstract. **Grade C.** The widely-repeated figures for contingency-managed writers come from Boice's own programme reports. Sword 2016 (*Int J Academic Development*, "'Write every day!': a mantra dismantled") is the standing counter-argument; I verified the citation, not its numbers. Crossref searches surfaced **no RCT of an adult writing-productivity intervention**.

**What controlled evidence exists is about incentives, not schedules.** Byron & Khazanchi 2012 (*Psych Bulletin*; 60 studies, 69 samples; **A**): creativity-contingent rewards raise creative performance, especially with positive, task-focused feedback and choice; **performance-contingent and completion-contingent rewards have a slight negative effect**. Ordóñez et al. 2009 (*Acad Manag Perspectives*; review, contested) documents goal-induced narrow focus, distorted risk preference and reduced intrinsic motivation. Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (CF §1) put completion-contingent rewards at d = −0.36 on intrinsic motivation. Together these are the honest basis for "a word-count target can displace quality" — the mechanism is *rewarding completion*, not *recording it*.

**The counter-evidence is real.** Simonton 1997 (*Psych Review*) — the equal-odds rule: across careers, high-impact works are a roughly constant proportion of total output, so quantity is not the enemy of quality at career scale (**archival**). And no study shows that *tracking* creative sessions harms output. The deep-work claims should be audited, not repeated: the "~23 minutes to resume" figure traces to Mark, Gonzalez & Harris 2005 (*CHI*), an in-situ observation of 24 information workers, and the most-cited experiment is titled "The cost of interrupted work: **more speed** and stress" (Mark, Gudith & Klocke 2008, *CHI*) — interrupted work was faster; the cost was stress. Leroy 2009 (*OBHDP*) supplies "attention residue" from lab studies. That is the whole evidential base for a bestselling doctrine.

**Action-design rules (creative).**
1. Track **session occurrence** (input) and never a word/output count as the goal. Recording is fine; rewarding completion is not (Byron & Khazanchi).
2. **No praise, no celebration, no badge on a creative session.** FP §2 already bans praise; here it has an independent justification.
3. Let the user choose the rhythm. Daily is a defensible default *only* as an anti-procrastination floor, and must be labelled C-grade in the method: "this is the weakest evidence we have."
4. Ask for a **quality note in the user's own words** at the weekly review ("what was the best thing you wrote?"), never a rating the coach scores.
5. Never quote 37×, deep-work neuroscience, or the ceramics-class anecdote (AH "claims Adler must not repeat").

**Transfer.** The tiny-first-step floor transfers as a heuristic. Monitoring transfers. **Growth rules do not** — there is no dose-response evidence for creative output. **Streak and reward machinery is actively contraindicated here** more strongly than anywhere else in the corpus.

## 5. Relationships and social goals

Hawkins et al. 2008 (*JCCP*; 86 reports, 117 studies, >500 effect sizes; **A**): relationship education, experimental studies, relationship quality **d = .30–.36**, communication skills **d = .43–.45**; moderate dosage beat low dosage. Hawkins & Erickson 2015 (*J Family Psych*; 38 studies) for lower-income participants: control-group **d = .061** against one-group pre-post **d = .352** — the design gradient again. Wood et al. 2012 (*JPAM*; Building Strong Families, 8 sites, **>4,700 couples randomised**; **A**): **no overall effect** on relationship quality, staying together or marrying; one site positive, one site with numerous negative effects. Against that, Doss et al. 2016 (*JCCP*; **300 couples, N = 600**, randomised vs waitlist; **B**): an 8-hour online programme plus four 15-minute calls produced relationship satisfaction **d = 0.69**, confidence 0.47, depressive symptoms 0.71, anxiety 0.94 — **with both partners participating**.

**Prediction claims.** Heyman & Slep 2001 (*J Marriage and Family*): divorce-prediction accuracy of the kind reported in Gottman et al. 1998 **drops precipitously under cross-validation**; results without cross-validation should be read with extreme caution. Adler must never quote a percentage accuracy for predicting a relationship's future.

**Loneliness.** Masi et al. 2011 (*PSPR*; 50 interventions; **A**): single-group pre-post d = −0.367, non-randomised −0.459, **randomised −0.198 [−0.32, −0.08]**; the best randomised results came from addressing **maladaptive social cognition**, not from arranging more contact. Hickin et al. 2021 (*Clin Psych Review*; **28 RCTs, N = 3,039**): psychological interventions **g = 0.43**, mostly CBT, high heterogeneity. Faseru et al. 2018 (Cochrane, SA §2): enhancing partner support did not aid quitting and mostly failed to raise support at all.

**Action-design rules (relationships).**
1. The action is always **unilateral and observable by the user alone**: "text my sister on Sunday", "ask one open question at dinner". Never an action whose completion depends on the other person's response.
2. **Refuse**: scoring the other person, predicting the relationship, coaching the user to change a partner, or running couple work with one partner present. Point to couples work (which is what produced d = 0.69) instead.
3. For loneliness, prefer the **social-cognition** move (test the expectation of rejection) over "meet more people" — that is where the randomised evidence sits.
4. **Do not shrink the dose** when a relational action fails; ask whether the other person was available. Barrier taps need a seventh option here: *"the other person wasn't up for it."*
5. **Screen for abuse before accepting a relationship goal.** `safety-risk-detection.md` has no IPV content; this is a gap that must be closed before any relationship goal ships.

**Transfer.** Implementation intentions transfer. Monitoring transfers weakly (the user grades a subjective experience). **Shrink-the-dose, automaticity and the growth rule do not transfer.**

## 6. Taxonomy of goal types

| Type | Repeatable | Verifiable in one tap | Outcome lag | Needs others | Adler rules that apply | Rules suspended |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Habit-shaped** (train, sleep, read, drill) | daily | yes | days–weeks | no | Everything: CF Phases 3–7, AH Part C in full | none |
| **Structural** (automate transfer, cancel, switch plan) | no | once, then persistence | months | no | Implementation intention; dated step; monthly verification | automaticity, days-floor, growth, lapse ladder |
| **Session-with-content** (study, practise, write) | 2–4×/week | occurrence yes, quality no | weeks–months | no | Implementation intention; two-tap check-in; spacing; strategy prescription | 4-days floor, SRBAI gate, ≤25% growth |
| **Campaign** (job search, submissions, pitching) | weekly input, binary lagged output | input yes, outcome no | months | yes (gatekeepers) | Weekly unit; quality tap; rejection inoculation; skill + motivation pair | lapse ladder, shrink-on-miss, automaticity |
| **Dyadic** (relationship, family, friendship) | irregular | no | variable | yes (a specific person) | Unilateral action only; social-cognition work; abuse screen | shrink-the-dose, automaticity, growth, streak logic |

## 7. Health-derived rules that must NOT be exported

| Rule | Origin | Why it breaks | Replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SRBAI automaticity gate** (CF §6) | Gardner 2015; S–R definition (HS §1) | Undefined without a repeated cue–response pair; a standing order has no cue, an automatic study session says nothing about strategy | Structural: *persistence check* ("still running?"). Session: *strategy-fidelity tap*. Campaign: *quality tap*. Dyadic: nothing. |
| **4 days a week floor** | Kaushal & Rhodes 2015, new gym members (HS §4) | Contradicted by spacing (Latimier g = 0.74 spaced > massed); irrelevant to one-off and dyadic goals | Learning: 2–4 spaced sessions, gaps widening. Money: monthly. Career: weekly. Creative: user's choice. |
| **≤ 25% growth per step** (AH Part C) | Nielsen 2014 running-injury data | No tissue-tolerance analogue in cognitive or financial work | Grow *difficulty of material* or *specificity of strategy*; money grows by a single decision, not a ramp |
| **Lapse ladder ≤3 / 4–13 / ≥14 days** (AH, LR) | Cue decay, Bosquet detraining | A rejection or a quiet month is not cue decay | Structural: persistence check. Campaign: rejection protocol. Dyadic: no lapse concept. |
| **One-tap yes/no check-in** (D3) | Health behaviours are self-contained | Loses the variable carrying the effect (which strategy, what quality) | Two taps for session and campaign goals: *happened* + one content tag |
| **Shrink on miss** (CF §2) | Too-hard / no-energy barriers | Wrong when the blocker is another person or a gatekeeper | Seventh barrier option: "the other person wasn't up for it"; for campaigns, change strategy not size |
| **"Project goals get a repeating work-on-it action"** (D7) | Untested extrapolation | Correct for session-type goals, wrong for structural money goals | Route by taxonomy row, not by "habit vs project" |

## 8. Implications for Adler

1. **Route by taxonomy, not by "habit vs project".** At goal formation ask four questions in this order: *Would you do this again and again, or is it one decision?* *Could you tell me in one tap whether it happened?* *When would you know it worked?* *Does anyone else have to agree?* The answers pick the row in §6, which picks the check-in unit, the growth rule and the lapse policy. This replaces D7's untested "project goals get a repeating work-on-it action".
2. **Per-row defaults** (detail in §§1–5): money — one dated structural step, monthly goal-naming review, persistence checks at 3/6/12 months, no daily tap. Learning — the action string must contain a verb from {recall, self-test, explain aloud, work problems from blank}, 2–4 spaced sessions weekly, four-days nudge suppressed. Campaign — weekly count of *targeted* actions, quality tap, one-time inoculation at setup, no dose reduction on rejection. Creative — session tap, no praise, no output target. Dyadic — unilateral actions only, seventh barrier option, abuse screen.
3. **How this helps the coach adapt over one person.** The taxonomy gives the adjustment engine a *reason* for each rule it suspends, so the weekly hypothesis log (D3) records "suspended growth rule — session goal" rather than silently skipping it. Over months the coach learns which row each of a user's goals sits in and can name the pattern back: "your money goals move when we change one setting; your learning goals move when we change what happens in the hour."
4. **Honesty constraint.** When the coach explains a non-health rule it says "this is what we're trying", not "the research says". The research says it only inside health.

## 9. Conflicts and open questions

- **Fernandes (0.018 SD) vs Kaiser (0.065–0.090 SD).** Kaiser's is the larger, newer, RCT-only sample and directly re-runs Fernandes's decay model, finding it under-powered at 29 observations. Evidence favours Kaiser on magnitude and on decay. Both are small enough that the practical conclusion is identical: do not coach money by teaching.
- **Defaults: Madrian/Chetty (large) vs Choukhmane (attenuates by 36 months).** Choukhmane replicates the short run and is the only study with a medium-run horizon; evidence favours "large at 12 months, modest at 36". Implication: the persistence check is not optional.
- **Masi (RCT d = −0.20) vs Hickin (g = 0.43).** Different populations and content: Hickin is psychological/CBT interventions only, Masi includes social-contact and support programmes. Evidence favours "cognitive work beats arranging contact", which both agree on internally.
- **Hawkins (d = .30–.45) vs Wood/BSF (null, one site harmful).** BSF is far larger and randomised at scale on a disadvantaged unmarried population; Hawkins's samples are more advantaged and self-selected. Evidence favours: relationship education works for people who choose it and can attend together, and does not generalise.
- **Boice (daily beats bingeing) vs Sword (mantra dismantled) vs Simonton (quantity is fine).** No controlled trial exists on either side. Evidence favours agnosticism; Adler should offer a rhythm and let the user's own record decide.
- **Does session-count tracking corrupt creative goals?** Unresolved. The mechanism evidence (Byron & Khazanchi, Deci) is about *rewards*, not records; FP §4's streak evidence is about health behaviours. Testable in-product: randomise showing versus hiding the session count for creative goals and compare self-rated quality at 8 weeks.
- **Unstudied entirely:** app-delivered coaching for money, learning, career or creative goals in employed 16–40-year-olds. Every rule above is an extrapolation from workshops, classrooms, banks or laboratories.
- **Weakest link:** the two-tap check-in. Nothing in the literature tests whether adding a strategy-fidelity tap to a habit tracker changes strategy use. It is derived from the *size* of the strategy effect, not from evidence that asking about it works.
