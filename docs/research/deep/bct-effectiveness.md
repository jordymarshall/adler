# Behaviour change techniques: what actually moves adherence

Scope: the BCT meta-regression and mechanism literature, 2009-2026, and what does not replicate. Extends coaching-framework.md §1 (experimental evidence for implementation intentions, progress monitoring, goal setting, MCII) and §2 (COM-B to BCT mapping); points made there are cited, not repeated. Codes are BCTTv1.

## 1. How to read this literature

Nearly every "which BCTs work" claim comes from meta-regression: coders tick which of 26 (Abraham & Michie 2008) or 93 (BCTTv1) techniques a trial report describes, then regress trial effect size on presence. That is correlational across trials, with specific flaws:

- Techniques are not randomised and co-occur (goal setting and self-monitoring travel together). Peters, de Bruin & Crutzen 2015 (Health Psychology Review): no BCT meta-analysis controlled for co-occurrence, and 22.7% of techniques were also present in control arms, so the regression often cannot say what was tested.
- Coding is from published descriptions. Hennessy et al. 2020 (meta-review, 30 reviews, N = 409,185): only 3 of 30 reviews requested protocols (35% success); mean AMSTAR-2 quality 37%.
- Presence is binary; dose, fidelity and tone are invisible. "Feedback" once by letter and daily from a coach are the same tick.
- Hennessy's verdict: "no BCT that was examined in more than two reviews was consistently related to improved outcomes in all reviews"; the design is a "Type 3 error".

So: treat associations as hypotheses ranked by how many independent reviews agree, anchored on experiments where they exist (Harkin 2016 d = 0.40 for monitoring; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 d = 0.65 for implementation intentions; Epton 2017 d = 0.34 for goal setting; coaching-framework.md §1).

## 2. The founding result and its replication record

Michie, Abraham, Whittington, McAteer & Gupta 2009 (Health Psychology): 122 evaluations, N = 44,747, 26-technique taxonomy, pooled d = 0.31 (0.26-0.36). "Prompt self-monitoring of behaviour" explained the most between-study heterogeneity (13%): d = 0.42 (0.30-0.54, n = 10,572) with it vs 0.26 (0.21-0.30) without. Interventions that "combined self-monitoring with at least one other technique derived from control theory" (goal setting, prompting intention formation, reviewing goals, feedback) were significantly more effective. DARE's appraisal: quality was not assessed, "conclusions may be not reliable". No technique-count effect is reported.

**Replicates or extends**
- Knittle et al. 2018 (Health Psychology Review; 89 studies, k = 200, N = 19,212): self-monitoring plus any other control-theory BCT, goal setting (behaviour), and behavioural practice each raised intention, stage and autonomous motivation, d = 0.12-0.46. Gains in intention and stage, not autonomous motivation, predicted PA change.
- Samdal et al. 2017 (IJBNPA; 48 studies, overweight/obese adults): d = 0.37 at ≤ 6 months, 0.24 at ≥ 12. Goal setting (behaviour) and self-monitoring predicted both horizons; long-term effects also predicted by goal setting (outcome), feedback on outcome, graded tasks, adding objects to the environment, and autonomy-supportive counselling.
- Vetrovsky et al. 2022 (BJSM; 85 studies, 75 pooled, N = 12,057): over step-counter self-monitoring alone, added components gave +926 steps/day (651-1,201) post-intervention and +413 (210-615) at follow-up. Prescribed goals added ~600; phone/video counselling ~1,129. Websites, apps, texts, email or in-person counselling and financial incentives added nothing significant.
- Compernolle et al. 2019 (IJBNPA; 19 studies, N = 2,800): self-monitoring cut sitting g = 0.32 (~34 min/day); objective tools g = 0.40, subjective logs g = -0.02.
- Bull et al. 2018 (Int J Behav Med; 35 RCTs, ~17,000 low-income adults): diet interventions with self-monitoring d = 0.48 vs 0.22 overall.
- Preuhs et al. 2026 (Obesity Reviews; 65 RCTs, ~10,000): long-term weight g = -0.19; goal setting, feedback, self-monitoring (behaviour and outcome), instruction, demonstration, conserving mental resources and incentives associated with better long-term weight; no maintenance effect after the intervention ends.
- Dalgetty, Gray & Ogden 2019 (BJHP, review of reviews): theory-based interventions no more effective in general, except control theory, MI and SDT-based ones for PA and diet. Hennessy 2020: self-monitoring the most consistent BCT for weight (3 of 4 reviews).

**Fails to replicate**
- Hennessy 2020: for physical activity, "evidence that BCTs have efficacy ... was largely absent"; goal setting, self-monitoring, barrier identification, action planning and feedback showed no consistent benefit.
- McDermott et al. 2016 (BJHP; 25 interventions): behaviour d = 0.41; feedback on performance negatively associated with behaviour, relapse prevention negatively with intention.
- Carraça et al. 2021 (Obesity Reviews; 62 RCTs, N ≈ 12,854): digital d = 0.42, face-to-face 0.78. In digital arms goal setting (behaviour) β = 0.89, goal setting (outcome) 0.76, graded tasks 0.87, social incentive 2.37; self-monitoring of behaviour β = -1.04, "the most striking and unexpected" result. Face-to-face: only behavioural practice/rehearsal (β = 1.83).
- Olander et al. 2013 (IJBNPA; 61 comparisons, obese adults): behaviour d = 0.50; largest effects for "teach to use prompts/cues", "prompt practice", "rewards contingent on effort"; self-efficacy change uncorrelated with behaviour change (ρ = -0.18).
- Pegado et al. 2023 (J Sleep Res): "not a direct link between behaviour-change techniques and the success of the intervention".

Reading: the experiment (Harkin) says recording progress causes attainment. Meta-regressions agree wherever monitoring is objective, actually done, and the comparator does not also monitor. The association goes null or negative when both arms wear a tracker (Vetrovsky's design), monitoring is subjective (Compernolle), or an app "includes" a log users do not sustain (Carraça). The variable is monitoring performed, not monitoring offered.

## 3. Digital delivery

- Webb, Joseph, Yardley & Michie 2010 (JMIR; 85 RCTs, N = 43,236): d = 0.16 (0.09-0.23). Per-technique d+: stress management 0.50, communication skills 0.49, modelling 0.35, relapse prevention/coping planning 0.32 (k = 14), goal setting 0.27 (k = 25), action planning 0.25, feedback 0.22, barrier identification 0.20 (k = 26), instruction 0.20, information on consequences 0.14 (k = 29), emotion control 0.09, others' approval 0.06. More BCTs, larger effects (β = 0.36, p < .001). Web plus SMS d+ = 0.81 (k = 4). TPB-based d+ = 0.36.
- van Genugten, Dusseldorp, Webb & van Empelen 2016 (JMIR meta-CART; 52 interventions): the first tree split was time to understand and use; interventions that "take little time" were more effective. Barrier identification combined with rewards d = 0.23 (0.08-0.44) vs 0.43 (0.27-0.59) otherwise. No synergy between modes of delivery and BCTs.
- Direito et al. 2017 (Annals of Behavioral Medicine; 21 RCTs, N = 1,701): sedentary behaviour SMD -0.26 (-0.53 to -0.00); total PA 0.14, MVPA 0.37, walking 0.14, all non-significant. Intervention arms used 6.9 BCTs vs 3.1 in comparators; only 31 of 93 BCTs appeared.
- Mönninghoff et al. 2021 (JMIR; 117 RCTs, N = 21,118): walking 0.46, MVPA 0.28, total PA 0.34 at end; beyond 6 months 0.25 and 0.19. Scalable and non-scalable designs equivalent. Lee et al. 2025 (npj Digital Medicine; 18 RCTs, standalone digital): PA SMD 0.32, low certainty.
- Mair et al. 2023 (Annals umbrella; 85 reviews, > 865,000 participants): strong evidence for credible source, social support, prompts/cues, graded tasks, goals and planning, feedback and monitoring, human coaching, personalisation. Some reviews found ≥ 5 BCTs better; weight-loss apps showed no association with number or type of features. "No universal dose-response".
- Garnett et al. 2018 (Annals; 41 RCTs, digital alcohol): -23.6 g/week overall; behaviour substitution -95 g (-163 to -27), problem solving -46, credible source -32. Mean 9.2 BCTs (1-22); count not associated with effect.
- Krukowski et al. 2024 (IJBNPA; 19 studies, N = 3,261): PA interventions with feedback on self-monitoring d = 0.73 (0.09-1.37); human vs algorithmic and frequency comparisons "mixed".
- Yardley, Spring, Riper & Morrison 2016 (AJPM, expert consensus): "effective engagement" is "sufficient engagement with the intervention to achieve intended outcomes", to be promoted "rather than simply more engagement".
- JITAIs: Wang & Miller 2019 (Health Communication; 33 studies): g = 1.65 vs waitlist (k = 9), 0.89 vs non-adaptive (k = 21); tailoring on both past patterns and current state was additive, as was combining human and algorithmic decisions. Hardeman et al. 2019 (IJBNPA; 14 JITAIs, 6 randomised): "mixed evidence ... no study was sufficiently powered". Treat the large g as inflated by small waitlist-controlled trials.

## 4. Domain-specific combinations

**Physical activity.** Dusseldorp et al. 2014 (Health Psychology meta-CART; 122 interventions): behaviour-health-link information plus prompt intention formation d = 0.46; link information plus consequences plus follow-up prompts 0.44; feedback without instruction 0.05. Information alone is inert; information plus a commitment step, or feedback plus how-to, are the effective pairs. Howlett, Trivedi, Troop & Chater 2019 (TBM; 26 RCTs of healthy inactive adults, 16 pooled, n = 2,346): d = 0.32 (0.16-0.48) post, 0.21 (0.12-0.30) at ≥ 6 months. Post-intervention: biofeedback, demonstration, behavioural practice, graded tasks. Maintenance: action planning, instruction, prompts/cues, practice, graded tasks, self-reward. Digital PA (§2): goals, graded tasks, social incentive and human counselling add; more app features do not.

**Diet and weight.** Self-monitoring is the most replicated ingredient (Michie 2009, Samdal, Bull, Hennessy, Preuhs). Bull 2018 found feedback on behaviour, prompts/cues and information about emotional consequences associated with smaller diet effects; Hennessy reports feedback "discouraging for very sedentary older adults". Maintenance is where every review is weakest.

**Sleep.** Pegado 2023 (18-29 year olds; 20 studies, 8 pooled): sleep quality g = -0.26 (-0.42 to -0.09), I² = 21%; 6 of 20 theory-based; no BCT-outcome link. Lu et al. 2025 (JMIR; 13 RCTs, N = 5,251, digital): sleep quality g = -1.25 (-1.83 to -0.66), follow-up -0.53; insomnia severity g = -4.08, not credible as a standardised effect; therapist guidance, type and duration moderated. A 2025 Scientific Reports review (6 RCTs, N = 969, middle-aged): CBT-based programs, 28 of 93 BCTs used. The active ingredients are CBT-I components (stimulus control, sleep restriction, fixed rise time): 12.1 restructuring the environment, 8.3 habit formation, 8.2 substitution, 7.1 cues. Sleep-hygiene education (4.1, 5.1) is the weak arm.

**Productivity, procrastination, learning.** No BCTTv1-coded meta-analysis exists. Rozental et al. 2018 (Frontiers in Psychology; 12 RCTs, N = 718): g = 0.34 (0.11-0.56), I² = 61%; CBT subset g = 0.55 (0.32-0.77); follow-up too sparse to pool. van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018 (Educational Research Review; 24 studies, k = 44, N = 1,173): large reductions, stable at follow-up; CBT strongest, self-regulation training weaker. Theobald 2021 (Contemporary Educational Psychology; 49 studies, N = 5,786): self-regulated-learning training g = 0.38; academic performance 0.37; metacognitive strategies 0.40. Parés-Salomón et al. 2024 (workplace digital sedentary; 11 studies, N = 3,529): -30 min per 8-hour day (-45 to -15); prompts, automated feedback and self-monitoring, strongest with environmental change. The experimental core for productivity goals remains specific goals plus implementation intentions (coaching-framework.md §1 ranks 1 and 3).

## 5. Mechanisms: Carey, Connell, Johnston and the Theory & Techniques Tool

- Carey et al. 2019 (Annals): 277 intervention reports, 77 BCTs, 26 MoAs, 2,636 author-stated links (9.56 per article); most-linked MoAs were beliefs about capabilities and intention. Claims, not tests.
- Connell et al. 2019 (Annals): 105 experts, 61 BCTs × 26 MoAs = 1,586 possible links; 90 "definite" (5.7%), 464 "definitely not", 1,032 unsure.
- Johnston et al. 2021 (TBM): triangulation gave 92 links (37 concordant, 55 reconciled) covering 51 of 56 BCTs and 22 of 26 MoAs; published as the interactive Theory & Techniques Tool heat map.
- Links relevant to Adler (re-check in the heat map before freezing in the method): 2.3 self-monitoring → behavioural regulation; 2.2 feedback → behavioural regulation, beliefs about capabilities; 1.1 goal setting and 1.4 action planning → intention, goals, behavioural regulation; 1.2 problem solving → behavioural regulation, beliefs about capabilities; 8.7 graded tasks → beliefs about capabilities, skills; 7.1 prompts/cues → memory/attention, environmental context; 8.3 habit formation → behavioural regulation; 3.x social support → social influences; 9.1 credible source and 5.1 health consequences → beliefs about consequences.
- Caveat: mediation is rarely tested. Olander 2013: self-efficacy change uncorrelated with behaviour change. Knittle 2018: autonomous-motivation gains did not predict PA change. The tool is a hypothesis map, which is what an adaptive coach needs: a prior on which lever to pull for which barrier, updated per user.

## 6. Dose-response

Technique count: positive in Webb 2010 (β = 0.36), Samdal 2017 and some reviews in Mair 2023; null in Garnett 2018, Direito 2017, Michie 2009 (not reported) and weight-loss apps (Mair). The positive findings are confounded with intensity and human contact: van Genugten's tree puts time-to-use first, and Vetrovsky shows human counselling adds where apps and texts do not. Contact dose: coaching meta-analyses find no session-count moderation (humanistic-existential-common-factors.md, dose note); feedback on monitoring d = 0.73 (Krukowski). Decay: effects roughly halve after 6 months (Mönninghoff; Howlett 0.32 → 0.21; Samdal 0.37 → 0.24; Vetrovsky +926 → +413). Engagement: effective, not maximal (Yardley).

## 8. Implications for Adler

**Rules and thresholds**
1. Monitoring performed is the base ingredient; the one-tap check-in (D3) is the intervention. If check-ins land on fewer than 4 of 7 days for two consecutive weeks, the coach targets the check-in itself before any other technique ("What makes the tap hard to do?"), then moves or shrinks it. Prefer objective sources (HealthKit steps, sleep) where the goal allows; subjective sitting logs did nothing.
2. Default stack per action is the control-theory cluster in coaching-framework.md §2: 1.1 goal + 2.3 monitoring + 2.2 feedback + 1.5/1.7 review. It is the only combination with both experimental and meta-regression support.
3. Feedback is descriptive, paired with a how-to, never evaluative: "4 of 6 this week" plus one concrete adjustment. Withhold outcome-level feedback (weight, minutes, word counts) for the first 14 days.
4. Graded tasks are the first move for "too hard" and "no energy"; the grow rule stays at ≤ 25%. Graded tasks are in every maintenance list; big jumps are in none.
5. Every "stop" goal gets a substitute behaviour at goal formation: "What will you do instead, in that same moment?"
6. Any "why" conversation ends in an intention step (consequences information is inert alone, 0.46 with intention formation). Never close a Talk on insight only.
7. Never pair barrier talk with a reward or incentive frame. Adler issues no points, badges or coach rewards; a self-reward a user proposes is theirs and small.
8. Cues are taught and user-built, not pushed. D3's no-notification default matches the evidence.
9. Time-to-use predicts effectiveness ahead of any technique. Three screens, one tap (D4) is a BCT decision.
10. The weekly review is where counselling lives (+1,129 steps for phone/video counselling; "human coaching" in Mair). D5's non-optional review matches. Keep the milestone (D7) visible weekly, not daily; outcome goals help at 12 months.
11. Expect decay. Build Grow/Close around the maintenance list: disruption if-thens, taught cues, practice, small self-reward.

**Domain defaults.** PA: session or step goal + objective tracker + graded tasks + weekly counselling. Sleep: CBT-I components (fixed rise time, bed only for sleep, get up if awake), not hygiene tips; escalate persistent insomnia (coaching-framework.md §5). Productivity: treat procrastination as avoidance (name the feeling, shrink the start) plus plan-monitor-reflect scaffolding plus implementation intentions; CBT-style beats generic self-regulation in both meta-analyses. Diet: self-monitoring first; no emotional-consequence or antecedent lectures.

**How the coach adapts to one person**
- The Theory & Techniques links are the prior: barrier → likely mechanism → candidate BCT (coaching-framework.md §2). Each application is logged as (barrier, BCT, adherence over next 7 days) per §6. After two trials of a technique for the same user, that user's hit rate outranks the population prior. This is the only defensible route through Hennessy's inconsistency: the population answer is "it depends"; the per-user answer is measurable.
- Tailor on both past pattern and current state (the additive pair in Wang & Miller); not on stage of change (coaching-framework.md §1).
- Log monitoring performed, not offered; a user who stops tapping has lost the base ingredient and every downstream technique is untested for them.
- Discriminating questions: "Did you forget, or remember and not start?" (memory vs motivation); "Hard to do, or hard to start?" (graded task vs cue); "If a friend had asked, would you have done it?" (social lever).

**Avoid.** Feature accretion (each technique costs time-to-use and buys nothing on average); streaks, points and coach-issued rewards; self-efficacy as a weekly outcome metric (ρ = -0.18 with behaviour change in Olander; confidence stays a shrink trigger); consequence and emotional-consequence messaging as motivation; claiming JITAI-scale effects for adaptive timing.

## 9. Conflicts and open questions

1. **Self-monitoring, positive vs negative.** Michie, Harkin, Samdal, Bull, Preuhs vs Carraça (digital β = -1.04) and Hennessy (PA absent). Evidence favours the causal finding with the §2 design explanation. Adler's design (the tap is the outcome; no tracker-vs-tracker comparison) sidesteps the confound.
2. **Technique count.** Webb and Samdal positive; Garnett, Direito, Mair null. Favours no dose-response for count; the confound is human contact. Fewer, better-delivered techniques.
3. **Theory use.** Webb 2010 (TPB d+ = 0.36) vs Prestwich 2014 and Dalgetty 2019 (no general advantage). Favour the later reviews; the surviving exception (control theory, MI, SDT) is the cluster Adler uses.
4. **Feedback.** Krukowski 0.73 and Webb 0.22 vs McDermott and Bull negative. Favour a conditional reading: descriptive plus how-to helps; evaluative or discouraging harms. No trial has manipulated tone.
5. **Autonomous motivation.** coaching-framework.md §1 rank 5 (Ng 2012) vs Knittle 2018 (autonomous-motivation gains did not predict PA change) and Dalgetty (SDT-based interventions more effective). Favour autonomy support as delivery style (Samdal: it predicts long-term effects), not as a weekly mechanism metric.
6. **JITAIs.** g = 1.65 vs "no study sufficiently powered". Favour Hardeman; adaptive timing is a hypothesis for Adler's own logs.
7. **Sleep effect sizes.** Pegado g = -0.26 vs Lu -1.25 and -4.08. Favour Pegado.
8. **Systematisation.** Ogden 2016: the coding-to-behaviour gap and practitioner variability make BCT prescription "neither feasible nor desirable"; Peters & Kok and Michie's group reply that no alternative exists. Adler's position: code techniques to learn from logs, not to script the coach turn by turn.

Open: whether an AI coach as witness reproduces Harkin's "reported to someone" boost; the minimum effective feedback frequency; whether rankings from inactive or obese samples transfer to already-motivated optimizers (ceiling and self-selection differ); whether social incentive can be added without a social feed.
