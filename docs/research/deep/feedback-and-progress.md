# Feedback, progress framing and reflection: what to say back to a person about their own record

Research date: 2026-09-02. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF), `deep/self-regulation-theory.md` (SR), `deep/motivation-science.md` (MS), `deep/bct-effectiveness.md` (BCT), `deep/lapse-relapse-recovery.md` (LR), `deep/maintenance-long-term.md` (ML). Established there and **not repeated**: monitoring d = 0.40 (CF §1 rank 2); Kluger & DeNisi's headline (SR §1); to-date vs to-go by commitment level (SR §1, MS §2, MS rule 1); Deci 1999 undermining (CF §1); Silverman & Barasch's broken streaks (CF §1, LR); Eskreis-Winkler (LR); Krukowski d = 0.73 and the BCT 2.2 contradiction set (BCT §7, §9.4); automated feedback fails in maintenance (ML §3). This file goes underneath: the *internals* of feedback intervention theory, what message content actually moves people, whether praise and mindset language earn their place, the streak evidence that cuts against our decision, and the strongest single result here — the structured after-action review.

Evidence levels: **A** meta-analysis or several RCTs; **B** field or lab experiment; **C** correlational, diary or theory.

## 1. Feedback intervention theory, opened up

**Kluger & DeNisi 1996** (*Psychological Bulletin* 119:254–284; 131 papers, 607 effect sizes, 23,663 observations; **A**). Weighted mean d = 0.41 with **38% of effects negative**. FIT posits three ordered loci of attention — *task learning* (how to do it), *task motivation* (the task), *meta-task* (includes the self); the higher attention goes, the worse the intervention, because resources go to affect regulation instead of the discrepancy. Effects were more positive with correct-solution information or goal setting, negative for praise, discouraging messages, and person-level or normative comparison. Their own audit: over two-thirds of the prior literature lacked controls or used confounded treatments. Refined since, not overturned.

**Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020** (*Frontiers in Psychology* 10:3087; 435 studies, k = 994, N > 61,000; **A**). Overall d = 0.48 [0.44, 0.51], I² = 83.4%, **17% of effects negative**. The moderator is the payload: **high-information feedback** (task + process + self-regulation content together) d = 0.99 (k = 42); corrective 0.46 (k = 238); **reinforcement or punishment 0.24** (k = 39). Cognitive outcomes 0.51, motor 0.63, motivational 0.33, behavioural 0.48. Funnel asymmetry was severe (Egger's z = 9.75), so treat magnitudes as inflated and the *ordering* as the finding: information beats evaluation roughly 4:1.

**Negative feedback.** Fong, Patall, Vasquez & Stautberg 2019 (*Educational Psychology Review*; 78 studies; **A**): negative feedback had **no effect** on intrinsic motivation versus neutral or no feedback, but decreased it versus positive feedback. Demotivation was attenuated when the message contained **instructions on how to improve**, used **criterion-based rather than normative standards**, and was delivered in person. FIT restated for motivation: negative information is safe once it is instrumental.

**Frequency.** Lam, DeRue, Karam & Hollenbeck 2011 (*OBHDP* 116:217–228; **B**): frequency has an **inverted-U** relation with performance, mediated by task effort — frequent feedback consumes the resources the task needs. Small lab studies, no meta-analysis, but the only quantitative evidence against "more is better".

**Normative comparison.** The feedback type FIT predicts should harm is the one consumer products default to. Schultz et al. 2007: below-average energy users *increased* consumption after descriptive-norm feedback, a boomerang removed by an injunctive cue. Allcott 2011 (*J Public Economics*; ~600,000 Opower households; **B/A**): 1.4–3.3% reductions, concentrated in the top usage decile, ~0 in the bottom, no increase among favourable comparers — Opower shipped the injunctive smiley face to suppress exactly that. Normative feedback works on one tail only and needs a corrective to be safe; it has no place in a one-person product (SR §2: persuasion by others ≈ 1% of self-efficacy variance).

## 2. Praise: the evidence is thinner and more negative than the folklore

**Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999** (128 studies; **A**) is cited in CF §1 for undermining. Its other half matters here: **positive verbal feedback increased free-choice persistence d = 0.33 and self-reported interest d = 0.31**. Informational positive feedback is a live ingredient, not a neutral one.

**Person vs process praise.** Mueller & Dweck 1998 (*JPSP*, six studies with fifth-graders; **B**): intelligence praise produced worse persistence, enjoyment and post-failure performance than effort praise. Since then the *harm* side has replicated better than the *benefit* side. Brummelman et al. 2014 (*JEP:General*; N = 357 adults, N = 313 children; **B**): adults give person praise preferentially to low-self-esteem children, and person praise predisposes those children to shame after failure. Pomerantz & Kempner 2013 (*Developmental Psychology*; N = 120 dyads, 10-day diary; **C**): mothers' **person** praise predicted entity theory and lower challenge-seeking; process praise predicted nothing. Directly relevant to Adler's age band, **Skipper & Douglas 2012** (*BJEP*; N = 145 children, **N = 114 university students**; **B**, vignette) added the missing control — objective outcome feedback, no praise. Person praise was worst on every post-failure measure; **process praise did not differ from the no-praise control**. A 2019 vignette study in vocational students (*Educational Psychology*) is reported as a failed replication of person-praise effects; effect sizes unverified.

**Verdict.** The defensible claim is asymmetric: *person praise harms*; *process praise is not demonstrably better than plain description*. No meta-analysis of person-versus-process praise exists, no adult behavioural (as opposed to vignette) test was found, and the mechanism is FIT's. CF's "affirm effort and strategy, never grade the person" survives, but the upgrade path is toward description, not better-worded praise.

## 3. Growth mindset: use the tone, do not buy the intervention

- **Sisk et al. 2018** (*Psychological Science*; MA1 k = 273, N = 365,915 correlational; MA2 k = 43, N = 57,155 interventions; **A**): weak overall in both; some support for low-SES and at-risk students benefiting.
- **Yeager et al. 2019** (*Nature*; National Study of Learning Mindsets, >12,000 students, 65 US schools, preregistered; **A/B**): a ~50-minute module raised core-course GPA **0.10 points** for below-median students, ~0 for high achievers, and only where **peer norms** supported challenge-seeking.
- **Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023** (*Psychological Bulletin*; 63 studies, N = 97,672; **A**): overall **d̄ = 0.05 [0.02, 0.09]**, non-significant after publication-bias correction; studies with a successful manipulation check (13 studies, N = 18,355) **d̄ = 0.04 [−0.01, 0.10]**; the highest-quality subset (6 studies, N = 13,571) **d̄ = 0.02 [−0.06, 0.10]**. Authors with a financial incentive reported larger effects.
- **Burnette et al. 2023** (*Psychological Bulletin*; 53 independent samples; **A**): academic achievement **d = 0.14 [0.06, 0.22]**; mental-health outcomes in targeted subgroups delivered with high fidelity **d = 0.32 [0.10, 0.54]**.
- **Tipton et al. 2023** (*Psychological Bulletin* commentary): both meta-analyses read the same literature; the dispute is whether to average across heterogeneous contexts or model the heterogeneity.
- **Yeager et al. 2022** (*Nature*; six double-blind RCTs, n = 118–2,717): a ~30-minute "synergistic mindsets" module moved stress appraisals, cardiovascular reactivity, cortisol and anxiety. Same lab.

**Verdict.** For an unselected, already-motivated adult population the expected effect of mindset messaging on behaviour is indistinguishable from zero. What survives is the *framing register* — difficulty is expected, ability responds to practice, a miss is information — which FIT already entails and which costs nothing.

## 4. Progress framing and display: the streak evidence cuts against us

MS §2 and SR §1 cover to-date/to-go and the small-area hypothesis. Three additions.

**Manufactured progress works, which is why it is a trap.** Nunes & Drèze 2006 (*JCR*; car-wash field experiment, ~200 cards; **B**): an 8-stamp card was completed by **19%**, a 10-stamp card pre-stamped twice — identical real effort — by **34%**. Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006 (*JMR*; café loyalty field data plus experiments; **B**): purchases accelerate near a reward, and illusory progress (12-stamp card, 2 free stamps) cut median completion from 15 days to 10. Both work by making the *remaining* portion small — the small-area mechanism. Neither licenses fabricating progress; both license *counting an existing baseline as progress*.

**Streaks: a large field RCT now argues the other way.** **Aulagnon, Cristia, Cueto & Malamud 2025** (NBER WP 34173; *Economics of Education Review*; **~60,000 students**, grades 4–6, Peru; arms: streak highlighting, personalised reminders with positive reinforcement, generic reminders, control; **A/B**): streak-highlighting messages significantly increased use of an online maths platform versus generic reminders and control, streaks stronger on the **intensive margin**, personalised reminders on the extensive margin; among the 1,500 students who sat an endline test, streak-highlighted students scored **0.13–0.17 SD** higher than control. Larger effects for girls, grade 4, urban students and high prior users. The paper reports nothing about behaviour after a break — the exact quantity Silverman & Barasch isolate. A direct adjudication is run and unpublished: **University of Pennsylvania, NCT07220044**, streak counter vs cumulative tally in a 30-day digital health challenge, randomised, **N = 7,004**, primary outcome total lessons completed, completed January 2026, results not posted.

**Gamified wrappers.** Mazéas et al. 2022 (*JMIR* 24:e26779; 16 RCTs, N = 2,407; **A**): g = 0.42 [0.14, 0.69] on physical activity, **0.23 [0.05, 0.41] against an active non-gamified comparator**, 0.15 [0.07, 0.23] at ~14 weeks. Game layers add a little over an equivalent non-gamified intervention and do not wholly decay.

## 5. Reflection and the after-action review: the largest effect in this file

- **Keiser & Arthur 2021** (*JAP* 106:1007–1032; 61 studies, 107 effect sizes, 915 teams and 3,499 individuals; **A**): AAR/debrief **d = 0.79**, larger than most training-method effects. Two characteristics contributed consistently: **alignment** to the individual or team reviewed, and **objective performance-review media**; a **self-led** facilitation approach was in the most effective combinations. Bare-bones meta-analysis (sampling-error correction only).
- **Tannenbaum & Cerasoli 2013** (*Human Factors* 55:231–245; 46 samples, N = 2,136; **A**): **d = 0.67**, ~25% improvement; similar for individuals and teams, simulated and real settings, medical and non-medical.
- **Ellis & Davidi 2005** (*JAP* 90:857–871; quasi-field experiment, soldiers on successive navigation exercises; **B**): reviewing **successes and failures** beat reviewing failures only; mental models of failed events started richer than of successful ones and the gap closed. Ellis et al. 2010 (*JAP*; N = 112): personal and filmed AERs both beat no review, **mediated by self-efficacy** — linking AAR to SR §2's mastery readout.
- **Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano & Staats** ("Learning by Thinking"; call-centre field experiment plus lab studies, reported N ≈ 4,340 across ten studies; **B**): 15 minutes of end-of-day written reflection produced **~23%** better performance at day 10 than equivalent extra practice. Caveat: a co-author is subject to the 2023 Harvard data-fabrication findings on *other* papers; this one is not among those flagged and has no independent replication.
- **Bisra et al. 2018** (*Educational Psychology Review*; 69 effects, 64 reports; **A**): prompted **self-explanation** g = 0.55. The active ingredient is a "why did that work" prompt, not free journaling. BCT §4 has Theobald 2021 (SRL training g = 0.38; metacognitive strategies 0.40).

## 6. Feedback inside digital health products

Krukowski, Denton & König 2024 (*IJBNPA*; 19 studies, N = 3,261; **A**) is the only review isolating feedback: 10 studies compared feedback with no feedback, pooling to **d = 0.73 [0.09, 1.37]** for physical activity; human-vs-algorithm (5 studies) and presentation-format (4 studies) comparisons could not be pooled and were **mixed**; risk of bias high. Laranjo et al. 2021 (*BJSM*; apps/trackers with automated continuous self-monitoring and feedback; **A**): SMD **0.350 [0.236, 0.465]** at a mean 13 weeks. Against these, **Jakicic et al. 2016** (*JAMA* 316:1161; IDEA RCT, N = 470, 24 months; **A**): adding a wearable with a web feedback interface to a standard behavioural programme produced **less** weight loss — 3.5 kg vs 5.9 kg, difference −2.4 kg [−3.7, −1.0], p = .002. Automated feedback can substitute for the behaviour it was meant to support (ML §3: NULevel's tailored-SMS arm matched a newsletter).

## 8. Implications for Adler

**A. The message schema.** Every feedback utterance has three slots and no fourth: (1) **record** — counts, no adjectives ("4 of 6; both misses were Thursday and Friday"); (2) **process** — the condition that explains it ("both were days you train after work"); (3) **one how-to** ("move Friday to the morning, or make it the two-minute version"). A fourth slot evaluating the person is prohibited. Basis: Wisniewski's 0.99 vs 0.24, and Fong's finding that instructional content neutralises the cost of negative information. Tightens BCT §8 rule 3.

**B. Praise policy, sharpened.** Default: **no evaluative praise**. Plain description is the control that process praise failed to beat (Skipper & Douglas). Permitted: informational positive feedback tied to a specific act and condition ("you did it on a day with two late nights") — Deci's d = 0.33, SR §6's mastery readout. Banned in prompts and copy: "you're crushing it", "amazing", "proud of you", "you're the kind of person who…", any percentile or comparison with other users, any cumulative deficit ("you've missed 9 sessions this month"), any inflated modifier before a small number.

**C. The weekly review is an after-action review; build it to the AAR spec.** D5 fixes the ritual; the literature fixes its shape (d = 0.67–0.79, the largest effect here): (i) **aligned** to this person's record, never to a cohort; (ii) **objective review media** — open with the tap record on screen, not a question about feelings; (iii) **self-led** — the user reviews, Adler asks and summarises (also ICF-compliant, accreditation-frameworks.md); (iv) **both sides** — a success *and* a miss every week (Ellis & Davidi), never a failures-only post-mortem, which is also LR's answer to Eskreis-Winkler. Four-question core: "Which day went best, and what was different about it?"; "Which day didn't, and what got in the way?"; "Why do you think that worked?" (self-explanation, g = 0.55); "What is the one change for next week?" Note the deviation: the tested reflection dose is 15 minutes, Adler's review is five.

**D. Frequency ceiling.** One daily tap plus one weekly review is the entire feedback budget (D3, D5). No unsolicited mid-week summaries, no "halfway to your weekly goal" pings. Basis: Lam's inverted U, Kluger's resource argument, message fatigue (adaptive-interventions.md).

**E. Progress display, extending MS rule 1.** Keep the stateful to-date/to-go switch. Add: (i) **count the baseline as progress** at goal formation — if the user already walks three days a week and the target is four, the first display is "3 of 4", not "0 of 4" (the honest version of endowed progress); (ii) never fabricate progress; (iii) no progress bars toward distant, known-distance outcomes (Amir & Ariely, MS §2) — bars for the week, numbers for the goal.

**F. Streaks: hold the decision, run the test.** D2's "no streak counts anywhere" rests on Silverman & Barasch alone. Aulagnon's 60,000-student RCT is the larger field result on the other side, and a 7,004-person streak-vs-tally trial reports imminently. Interim position: **default stays weekly count plus cumulative total** (LR §5: the cumulative total is the repairable representation), because this audience is over-indexed on all-or-nothing rules and the harm lands at the self-attributed break — the moment Adler exists to handle. Any "show consecutive days" opt-in goes behind an A/B with retention-after-first-miss as the primary outcome. Re-read when NCT07220044 posts.

**G. Growth-mindset language without the mindset claim.** Permit the register (difficulty is expected, skill responds to practice, a miss is data). Prohibit a mindset module, a mindset measure at intake, any outcome claim in copy, any citation of Dweck as evidence. Basis: best-evidence d = 0.02 [−0.06, 0.10].

**H. What the coach learns about one person.** Log per feedback message: frame (to-date / to-go), slot-3 how-to present or absent, valence, and the next seven days' adherence. Four person-level parameters beyond MS §"Adapting to one person": (a) **evaluative sensitivity** — does adherence dip in the 72 hours after any message containing an evaluative adjective? If yes, hard-lock to description; (b) **review depth fit** — does the "why do you think that worked" prompt beat a plain summary over the next 14 days? (c) **frequency fit** — does one extra mid-week summary help or hurt (tested once per user, never default); (d) **success-review response** — if the user disengages when a good week is discussed, keep that step to one sentence and move to the plan. Each is a hypothesis in the existing intervention log (D3), not a new instrument.

**I. No feedback surface that can substitute for the behaviour.** IDEA's wearable arm and NULevel's SMS arm both lost to plainer comparators. The tap is the record, the coach is the interpretation; never ship a dashboard whose passive consumption could stand in for the action.

## 9. Conflicts and open questions

1. **Streaks.** Silverman & Barasch 2023 (7 consumer studies; break-driven abandonment) vs Aulagnon 2025 (~60,000-student field RCT; +0.13–0.17 SD). Evidence favours a reconciliation, not a winner: streak salience raises intensive-margin effort while intact; the cost lands at a self-attributed break. Neither study measures the other's outcome. Adler's audience, horizon (years, not 30 days) and lapse-handling role tilt toward counts, but that is judgement. NCT07220044 (N = 7,004) is the adjudicating test.
2. **Self-level feedback vs coaching affirmation.** MI-4 affirmations and alliance-building are person-directed by design (CF §3, humanistic-existential-common-factors.md). Resolution: specific and informational ("you did X under Y") is task-level; global and evaluative ("you're disciplined") is meta-task. Untested in a coaching app; the highest-value tone A/B Adler could run.
3. **Growth mindset.** Macnamara & Burgoyne (d̄ = 0.02–0.05, ns) vs Burnette (0.14 academic, 0.32 targeted mental health) vs Tipton's heterogeneity commentary. Evidence favours "≈ 0 on average, plausibly non-zero for at-risk subgroups in supportive contexts". Adler's audience is neither at-risk nor school-aged: expected effect ≈ 0.
4. **Digital feedback.** Krukowski d = 0.73 (CI down to 0.09, k = 10) and Laranjo 0.35 against IDEA's −2.4 kg and NULevel's null. Favour the conditional reading: interpretive feedback plus a plan helps, ambient device feedback can displace effort. No trial has manipulated feedback *tone* (BCT §9.4 agrees).
5. **Process praise.** No meta-analysis, no adult behavioural test, and the one adult study with the right control found process praise no better than description. Adler's "affirm effort" rule may add nothing over the count. Cheap to test.
6. **Reflection dose and transfer.** The tested doses are 15 minutes daily (Di Stefano) or a structured team debrief (Keiser); Adler ships five minutes weekly. Every AAR effect size comes from military, medical, aviation or simulated training with objective media; transfer to personal-life goals with self-reported media is untested. Di Stefano's provenance is compromised by co-authorship, not by retraction.
7. **Endowed progress.** Field effects are large (34% vs 19%) but every demonstration involves a firm giving something away. Whether re-describing a genuine baseline as progress carries any of that effect is untested.
8. **Open for Adler's own data.** Does slot 3 (how-to) beat slots 1–2 alone over the next seven days? Does the "review a success" step raise next week's adherence or only satisfaction (ML)? Does evaluative sensitivity cluster with any intake variable (individual-differences.md)?
