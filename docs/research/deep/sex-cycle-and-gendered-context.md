# Sex, menstrual cycle and gendered life context

Research date: 2026-09-02. Companion to `coaching-framework.md` (CF §n), `deep/individual-differences.md` (ID §n), `deep/physical-foundations.md` (PF §n), `deep/adaptive-interventions.md` (ADI §n), `deep/maintenance-long-term.md` (ML §n), `deep/safety-risk-detection.md` (SR §n), `deep/emotion-wellbeing-foundations.md` (EW §n). Not repeated: the no-diagnosis, no-medication rule and the referral script (CF §5–6); tailoring buys little, tailor on behaviour not labels (ID §0); the shrink / revisit / grow thresholds (`decisions.md`); autoregulation and deloads (`atomic-habits-and-training-science.md`); SCOFF's operating characteristics and the goal-text park rules (SR §5, §D).

The narrower question: **does a recurring multi-day pattern in energy and mood exist strongly enough to be a personalisation parameter, what may Adler store, and which of its numeric thresholds were estimated in samples that do not look like its users.**

Levels: **A** meta-analysis / multi-site RCT; **B** RCT or controlled experiment; **C** correlational, cohort or validation; **D** consensus, audit or arithmetic.

---

## 1. Performance and cognition: real physiology, near-null behaviour

**Exercise performance (A, low-quality evidence).** McNulty, Elliott-Sale, Dolan, Swinton, Ansdell, Goodall, Thomas & Hicks 2020 (*Sports Medicine*; 78 studies, 51 pooled pairwise, 73 in a network meta-analysis, Bayesian multilevel models): early follicular ES₀.₅ = **−0.06 [95% CrI −0.16, 0.04]**, the largest contrast anywhere (early- vs late-follicular) −0.14 [−0.26, −0.03], SUCRA 30% for early follicular against 53–55% for every other phase, evidence graded **"low" (42%)**. Their conclusion is the product decision: "general guidelines on exercise performance across the MC cannot be formed; rather... a personalised approach should be taken based on each individual's response."

**Oral contraceptives (A).** Elliott-Sale et al. 2020 (*Sports Medicine*; 42 studies, 590 participants, 83% moderate-to-very-low quality): probability of even a small effect (d ≥ 0.2) favouring OCP users **effectively zero**; favouring naturally menstruating women p = 0.40 and 0.19 across two phase-matching methods; **within the pill cycle, p ≤ 0.001 in either direction** — flat. Between-study variance was large (I²₀.₅ = 0.16, 0.22).

**Cognition (A, a null).** Jang, Zhang & Elfenbein 2025 (*PLoS ONE*; **102 articles, N = 3,943, 730 comparisons**) across attention, creativity, executive function, intelligence, motor, spatial and verbal ability: "no systematic robust evidence for significant cycle shifts in performance", no publication bias, and the two nominal spatial results vanishing where phase was robustly confirmed. This replicates Sundström-Poromaa 2018: differences on sexually dimorphic tasks "are small and difficult to replicate", while emotion-processing differences are more consistent. Pletzer et al. 2019 (n = 36) is the cleanest illustration — hippocampal and fronto-striatal activation shifted with phase, **behavioural performance did not**.

**Physiology moves; the outputs a coach observes do not.** Schmalenberger 2019 finds cardiac vagal activity falling follicular → luteal (d = −0.39), and Alzueta 2024 (N = 116, Oura plus LH testing across a full cycle) finds oscillation in finger temperature and heart rate, physical symptoms at menses, slightly higher mood positivity near ovulation — and **no significant change in sleep efficiency, duration, WASO, onset latency or quality**. Zheng 2015 (SWAN actigraphy, n = 163) did find ~5% lower premenstrual sleep efficiency, at mean age 51.5, outside Adler's band. Nor is the cycle a clock: Bull 2019's 612,613 cycles give a mean of 29.3 days with wide within- and between-person variance, so any "day 14" model is wrong for most users most months.

**Reading.** Group-level effects on what Adler measures are trivial-to-absent and rest on poor-quality studies; the replicated finding is **large between-person variation** — an argument for a per-user parameter and against any population rule. Elliott-Sale et al. 2021 (*Sports Medicine* 51:843–861; extended by Part II, 2026) reach the same place methodologically: most sport-science evidence comes from men, and female-specific work is under-powered and inconsistently designed.

## 2. Mood: no population pattern; a rare, severe clinical one

**Prospective daily ratings do not show the folk pattern (A/C).** Romans, Clarkson, Einstein, Petrovic & Stewart 2012 (*Gender Medicine* 9:361–384; **47 prospective daily-mood studies** in non-help-seeking samples): **18 (38.3%) found no association of mood with any cycle phase**; 18 found premenstrual plus another phase; **only 7 (14.9%)** found the classic premenstrual-only pattern. The literature "failed to provide clear evidence in support of the existence of a specific premenstrual negative mood syndrome in the general population." Romans et al. 2013 (*Psychother Psychosom*; **395 complete cycles**, daily data): only half the mood items showed any phase association, and **physical health, perceived stress and social support were far stronger predictors than phase (p < 0.0001 each)**. Their sleep paper (2015, n = 76 over 24 weeks) found premenstrual sleep quality worse by 1.32 units — an effect that lost significance once stress and support entered the model.

**PMDD is real, rare, and clinical (A).** Reilly et al. 2024 (44 studies, 48 samples, **N = 50,659**): **3.2% [1.7–5.9]** with confirmed prospective diagnosis, inside DSM-5's 1.8–5.8%; prospective community estimates run 1.3% (Gehlert 2009) to 5.8% (Wittchen 2002), while retrospective screening inflates severalfold (29.6% of adolescents on the PSST-A). Impairment approaches MDD (Halbreich 2003) and suicidality is elevated (Prasad 2021, Yan 2021). Diagnosis **requires two cycles of prospective daily rating** (Pearlstein & Steiner 2008) — a clinician's job, not a coach's.

**Functional impact at population scale is nonetheless large (C).** Schoep et al. 2019 (**N = 32,748** Dutch women aged 15–45): 13.8% reported any menstrual absenteeism (mean 1.3 days/year), but **80.7% reported presenteeism — 23.2 days a year at ~33% reduced productivity** — and **67.7% wanted more flexibility in tasks and hours** rather than time off. Self-report, cross-sectional, social-media recruited, and the only number of its kind; it says the product response is *flexibility*, not a diagnosis.

**Reading.** Cyclicity is a **minority, individually variable** parameter, and where it is severe it is a clinical condition to route, not model. That settles the question against building it into the default personalisation model.

## 3. What the market does, and what regulators did about it

**"Cycle syncing" runs far ahead of the data (C, audit).** Pfender, Wanzer, Mikkers & Bleakley 2025 (*Perspect Sex Reprod Health*; **100 #cyclesyncing TikToks**): about one-third of creators gave credentials, **very few cited scientific evidence**, and more than half prescribed phase-specific diet and exercise. Their focus-group companion (*Qual Health Res*, n = 39) shows young women adopting the practice on the strength of inconclusive literature. Against §1, phase-based training periodisation has no evidential warrant at all.

**Cycle apps are poor at both jobs (C).** Nielsen-Tehranchian, Strotbaum & Pobiruchin 2023 (*Stud Health Technol Inform*; **18 apps**, DiGA/MARS-derived scoring): mean data-privacy score **0.40**, mean medical score **0.11**, only 6 apps scored anything on medical criteria, **89% requested permissions unnecessary for the app type**.

**Enforcement is established, not theoretical (D, legal).** FTC v. Flo Health (matter 192-3133, order finalised 2021) concerned a fertility-tracking app that, per the FTC's own headline, "shared sensitive health data with Facebook, Google, and others" despite explicit privacy promises. FTC v. GoodRx (2023) was the first enforcement of the **Health Breach Notification Rule**, amended in 2024 to state explicitly that health apps and connected devices are covered. Washington's **My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373)** defines consumer health data to include reproductive and sexual health data and is enforceable by individuals through the Consumer Protection Act. In the EU/UK, cycle data is **GDPR Art. 9 special-category data**, in practice requiring explicit consent plus a DPIA. Storing it buys an evidentially trivial signal at the price of the strictest data class in two regimes, a private right of action, and the company of the worst-behaved category in consumer health.

**Answer to the audit question.** Only one numeric threshold rests on a male-dominated sample — **Voils 2017's maintenance/satisfaction package**, 85% male, mean age 62. Everything else is female-majority (Harkin, Milkman, HeartSteps, NoHoW, NWCR) or balanced (Drink Less). The recurring external-validity problem is **age**, not sex (48, 62, 48 against a 16–40 audience). The genuine sex gap is that most BCT syntheses **do not report composition at all**: the honest statement there is "unknown", not "male".

## 5. Gendered context: time, self-criticism, and sex-specific risk base rates

**Unpaid work (C, national statistics).** ONS *Time use in the UK*, 23 Sept–1 Oct 2023: women averaged **3 h 37 min/day** of unpaid work (housework, care, volunteering) against men's **2 h 43 min** — a **54-minute** gap, narrowed from 1 h 12 min in 2020. A constraint input, not an identity input: a nontrivial fraction of users have an hour a day already spoken for, distributed unevenly across the week.

**Dispositional differences are real and small (A).** Salk, Hyde & Abramson 2017 (N ≈ 3.6M across 160 national datasets): depression OR 1.95, d = 0.27, peaking at OR 3.02 for ages 13–15 — Adler's youngest users sit at the peak. Johnson & Whisman 2013 (59 studies, N = 14,321): rumination d = 0.24. Perfectionism differences are smaller still: Sand et al. 2021 (N = 10,217 Norwegian 16–19s) found near-identical means (socially prescribed d ≈ 0.05) but **more girls in the top decile**. None of this justifies sex-based routing; all of it justifies the detectors Adler already has (ID §4, rule 3).

**Disordered eating differs by sex in *shape*, not just rate (A/C).** Galmiche 2019: lifetime ED **8.4% women vs 2.2% men**. But Nagata 2019 (Add Health, N = 14,891, prospective to ages 18–24) found **22% of males vs 5% of females** reporting muscularity-oriented disordered eating — eating to bulk, supplements, anabolic steroids. SCOFF is thinness-oriented, loses sensitivity as samples include more men (Kutz 2020, SR §5) and does not ask about this pattern at all (Murray 2019, MOET). **The male failure mode is invisible to the screen Adler currently specifies.**

## Implications for Adler

**A. Schema decision (proposed D17): store nothing physiological; store one user-named recurring window.** Adler collects **no** menstrual, contraceptive, pregnancy, postpartum or hormonal field, infers none, and never derives a cycle-phase estimate from tap patterns. It offers one optional, neutral, non-medical parameter:

```
recurring_low_window(user_id, label TEXT,        -- the user's own words
                     typical_days INT,           -- default 3, max 7
                     start_signal ENUM('user_marks_it','fixed_interval'),
                     interval_days INT NULL,     -- only if the user set one
                     active BOOL, created_at, ended_at)
```

The privacy argument, written out: cycle data is Art. 9 special-category data in the EU/UK, consumer health data under RCW 19.373 with a private right of action, and squarely inside the FTC's HBNR after the 2024 amendments — while §1–2 say the signal it buys is trivial on average and absent for most users. A field the user names themselves ("heavy week", "custody weekend", "night-shift block", "month-end", "migraine cluster") is **not health data on its face**, serves shift workers, carers and people with chronic conditions equally, and cannot be re-identified as a cycle unless Adler's own copy makes it one — which rule C forbids. *Infer nothing, ask nothing, let the user name a window.* The stronger option (consented cycle storage with phase modelling) fails on evidence before it fails on law; the weaker option (store nothing) is rejected only because it leaves the shrink machinery firing on a predictable low week.

**B. The suppression rule (prevents shrink/revisit firing on a predictable low week).** While today falls inside an active window:
1. *No energy*, *too hard* and *no time* taps **do not increment** the shrink counter (≤ 4 of 7); *didn't feel like it* does not increment the revisit-goal counter (3 in 14 days).
2. Adler offers the **pre-agreed minimum version** and holds the plan — the deload logic from `atomic-habits-and-training-science.md` on a user-named schedule rather than a physiological one.
3. Caps, so the window cannot silently disable the safety valve: max 7 days per occurrence, no more than one per 21 days; if windows cover more than ~a third of days, stop treating it as a window and read it as a **load signature** (ID rule 9, PF rule 1) — shrink everything, propose rest.
4. Outside the window, existing thresholds apply unchanged. The window suppresses *inference*, never *safety*: SR §A–C detection is stateless and runs regardless.

**C. Copy and attribution rules.**
- **Never attribute a dip inside a named window to motivation, commitment, discipline or wanting it enough.** Inside the window the only permitted framing is scheduling: "This is the week you flagged. The two-minute version counts. We'll look at the plan after."
- **Never volunteer a physiological explanation the user has not offered.** The words *hormones, cycle, period, luteal, follicular, PMS, PMDD, ovulation* never originate from Adler.
- If the user uses those words, **mirror them once, in their words, and stop** — no elaboration, no prediction, no phase advice, no "cycle syncing" training plan (§3), no citation of §1.
- **Never predict the next window** unless the user set a fixed interval themselves; never say "you're due". Deleting the window deletes it everywhere (D14).
- Escalation is unchanged in mechanism: severe, recurring, functionally impairing low mood routes to the existing WHO-5 / referral script (CF §6, EW). Adler may say a clinician will want **two cycles of daily ratings** (Pearlstein & Steiner 2008) and that notes help; it may not name a condition (CF §5). Prasad 2021 raises the priority of the *existing* risk route (SR §B), it does not create a new one.

**D. Questions to ask (optional, behavioural, none sex-linked).**
- "Is there a stretch of days that comes round regularly when everything is harder? You don't have to say why." → creates the window, in the user's words.
- "When it lands, what's the version of this you could still do?" → the pre-agreed minimum, defined *outside* the window.
- "In a normal week, how many hours are already committed to things other people depend on you for — and which days?" → the constraint check that uses the ONS asymmetry without inferring it from sex; actions are placed off those days, and a Tuesday miss on a care day is never read as motivation.

**E. Safety-file amendments (SR §D, §E).** The kcal park rule is sex-split (1,200 women / 1,500 men) and so requires knowing sex: **drop the split and fire the conversation below ~1,500 for everyone** — it already triggers on goal *text*, and one threshold removes a stored attribute while being more sensitive, not less. **Add muscularity-oriented goal text** to the park rules: anabolic steroid or SARM mention, "eat as much as possible", supplement stacks to gain, body-fat targets below ~10%, training through injury to protect a bulk. Do **not** apply SCOFF differently by sex; add the muscularity items and keep every screen an opener, never a score.

**F. Caveats to attach to existing thresholds.** Tag the maintenance/satisfaction numbers (ML §2, Voils 2017) **85% male, mean age 62** — the furthest of any anchor from Adler's user and the first to test in-app. Tag monitoring (Harkin, d = 0.40) 66% female with sex tested and null: transportable on sex, untested on age. Record "sex composition not reported" against Michie 2009 and the BCT rank table rather than assuming balance.

**G. Adapting to one person over time.** **Detect but do not announce.** One tap a day gives barely three repetitions of a ~28-day period in a quarter — not enough to support a claim (ADI, Implications 5: pool first, personalise slowly). The detector's only job is to *raise confidence in a window the user already named*: after ≥ 3 recurrences at consistent phase, the coach may offer "the week you flagged seems to land around here; want to move it?" It never proposes an unnamed window, never says what causes it, never predicts. Three further parameters fall out of taps Adler already collects: adherence inside vs outside the window, whether the pre-agreed minimum protects the following week's adherence, and whether the window's true length differs from the user's estimate.

**What to avoid.** Cycle-phase training plans or nutrition advice (§1, §3). Any inferred cycle field, including one derived from taps. Asking sex or contraception at intake. "Hormonal", "that time of the month", or any tone that treats a low week as a female phenomenon. Population-level claims about women's energy or cognition (§1 says there aren't any). Naming PMS or PMDD. Treating a named window as a diagnosis, an excuse, or a permanent exemption.

## Conflicts and open questions

1. **Trivial average effect (McNulty, Jang) vs 23.2 presenteeism days a year (Schoep 2019).** Not a contradiction: controlled tests of maximal performance in small samples measure something different from self-reported functioning in 32,748 people, and a mean of −0.06 is compatible with a minority experiencing a large effect. **Evidence favours: no population rule, and a per-user window that costs nothing when it is wrong.**
2. **Romans 2012's null vs the ubiquity of the belief.** The prospective literature is the stronger design (retrospective PMDD screening inflates prevalence ~10-fold against confirmed diagnosis, §2). Evidence favours Romans. But Adler must not *tell* users their pattern is imaginary; rule C's silence is the correct posture in both directions.
3. **Physiology moves, behaviour does not** (Schmalenberger HRV d = −0.39 vs Alzueta's sleep null; Bull 2019's cycle-length variance). Wearable vendors surface the former as if it implied the latter, and calendar prediction is unreliable in either case. Do not ingest "readiness" scores as evidence about capacity (PF); handle timing by asking, not modelling.
4. **The suppression rule is untested.** No trial exists of a coach that withholds its shrink rule during a user-declared low window. Failure modes: the window becomes an avoidance device, or suppression delays a genuinely needed dose reduction. The caps in rule B3 are guesses; the per-user log is the study.
5. **Unknown sex composition of the BCT rank table.** Michie 2009 and most syntheses do not report it. State the gap; do not fill it.
6. **The age gap is larger than the sex gap** across every anchor (Harkin 48, Voils 62, NWCR 48, NoHoW 46 vs a 16–40 audience) — a bigger external-validity problem than the one this file was commissioned to check.
7. **Hormonal contraception.** A substantial minority of the band uses it, which flattens the cycle entirely (Elliott-Sale 2020: performance is flat across the pill cycle). Adler cannot know and will not ask — another reason the parameter must be a named window, not a cycle model.
8. **Unmeasured:** whether the copy rules read as respect or obtuseness to a user who wants the cycle named. Testable in transcripts; the safer default is silence.
