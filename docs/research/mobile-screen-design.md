# Mobile screens that make Adler understandable

Status: this records the earlier design review. The user’s subsequent approved update replaces breakfast/email with phone distraction and the hero proof card with original abstract scroll-controlled artwork. See [current landing implementation](../landing-assets.md) and [art direction](landing-hero-art-direction.md).

Research brief · 10 September 2026 · Sources, design hypotheses and the accepted direction recorded below

The strongest direction is to make the next useful decision obvious, using familiar controls, while showing how one goal develops through action, feedback and revision. Adler's distinction can live in that continuity and the quality of its guidance. Novel navigation, additional metrics and animation are not evidence of innovation or effectiveness.

## What the sources support

All ten sources are first-party and were accessed on 10 September 2026. **Practitioner advice** and **platform guidance** below are not experimental evidence that an exact Adler layout will work. NN/G supplies usability heuristics and practitioner research reporting; this brief does not turn those into quantified effect estimates. **H** labels below identify proposed product heuristics requiring validation.

## Adler constraints that govern the application

Read with [product principles](../product-principles.md), the [current method](../method/adler-method.md), [engineering design](../method/coaching-engineering.md) §§2–8, and [domain language](../../CONTEXT.md). The latest user instructions govern the design; historical screens, fixed intake scripts and numerical defaults are not requirements to restore.

- **Goal before complete plan.** Preserve the person's desired outcome and horizon. A saved draft can remain unplanned; do not invent work, metrics or milestones to make the UI look complete. Chosen meaningful work leads to a feasible first action.
- **One coach and one learning record.** App controls, conversation, text and connected assistants share authoritative goals, reports, proposals and learning. A test retains its original prediction, versions, evidence and review timing. Agreement, actual use, reported behaviour and outcome are different facts. Review dates do not create results.
- **Simple explanation, rigorous basis.** The visible observation, behavioural interpretation and expected effect reference the same saved rationale that informed the recommendation. Detailed sources can be secondary; consequential uncertainty and the effect of accepting a change stay visible.
- **Honest quantities.** Work, outcomes and conditional projections answer different questions. Missing evidence stays unknown. More work is not automatically proportional outcome growth; an exponential “compounding” curve is not justified by the metaphor. A milestone result needs evidence separate from its contributing actions.
- **Scheduled streaks.** The user's current rule is explicit: four scheduled completions plus three unscheduled days can maintain a seven-day streak. Unscheduled days preserve the streak without adding performed actions, minutes or outcome progress. A possible label is “7 days on plan · 4 actions completed”; a distinct rest-day marker should make the accounting inspectable. This is an interface hypothesis, not a change to the rule.
- **Demonstration and delivery.** The requested seven chapters describe the intended product confidently and remove the extra illustration/enlargement framing. The interview should design that product state. A proposed connection or interaction still requires implementation verification before it can be described as operational; copy cannot establish runtime capability.

The [behavioural synthesis](behavioural-science-synthesis.md) supplies the substantive coaching evidence, not these UX essays. Relevant mappings retain its grades and limits: P9 **B/A / high** and P10 **A / high** for user-owned goals; P1 **A / high** for monitoring; P3 **A / moderate, transfer untested** and P4 **A / high** for informative review and feedback; P14 **B / moderate**, with its velocity display explicitly an open test; P29 **A within domains / C transfer** for goal-specific application. P34 records **conflicting streak evidence**; the user's scheduled-streak choice does not resolve that conflict scientifically. This brief does not re-audit those behavioural findings or transfer their effect sizes to a UI.

## Candidate flows to discuss, not accepted screen designs

Each proposal below is **H: an unvalidated product application** of the cited guidance and Adler contract. Each chapter needs an understandable resting frame; animation demonstrates a change rather than carrying the entire explanation. The seven chapters continue the same goal.

| Screen and user's question | Candidate structure and purposeful state change |
| --- | --- |
| **1 · What could this rough idea become?** | **H1:** The user requires this chapter to end with creation of the goal, milestones and actions. Keep the rough intent visible and use known context to select consequential clarifications, then animate the supported plan taking shape, the person's decision and the saved result. The example must establish the relevant starting situation, success, capacity and deadline flexibility before showing the plan ready; one answer about a target is insufficient. The real product retains unplanned drafts, while this demonstration follows a scenario through to usable work. Proposed mobile layout: a visible milestone outline with the first milestone's contributing actions expanded. The current onboarding hands off to shared Check-in; an in-place composer would be a presentation change to build. Avoid a mandatory questionnaire or unnecessary structure for other goal types. UX1, UX4, UX10; Define and Design; [runtime planning instructions](../../server/adaptive-instructions.ts). |
| **2 · What do I need to do today?** | **H2:** Show today's due actions with their meaningful amount and goal, plus clearly labelled Done / Didn't do / Discuss controls. Show all due work in a readable list rather than shrinking it to fit. Animate the selected report and its dated weekly cell, then leave the updated state and correction control visible. Partial colour requires an actual partial report; Discuss does not imply noncompletion. Rest days and unknown reports stay distinct. UX4, UX7, UX9; Observe. |
| **3 · Is the work moving me toward my goal?** | **H3:** Lead with the reported goal result and its date, then relate meaningful input and milestones to it. A supported outcome chart can extend into a labelled conditional range; an unsupported relationship shows planned work and the missing estimate honestly. Animate the arrival of a report or a changed assumption, preserving its date and meaning. UX2, UX8; Observe and Learn. |
| **4 · What change are we trying, and how will we judge it?** | **H4:** Present one goal-linked test: practical change, short observation and reason, expected observation, review point and decision controls. On acceptance, show the same record become agreed; do not manufacture exposure or success. Scientific details open from that record. This is a manageable recommendation, not an experiment-configuration dashboard. UX9, UX10; Understand, Select and Design. |
| **5 · How does earlier experience change today's advice?** | **H5:** Continue the same learning question through its dated history, current understanding and next decision. Animate an attributable report joining the existing record or a correction changing its standing. Preserve contrary evidence and pending feedback; a useful finding can remain visible after its test ends. This chapter demonstrates continuity, whereas screen 4 demonstrates choosing a change. UX9, UX10; Learn. |
| **6 · Can I continue without explaining everything again?** | **H6:** Show a short conversational exchange that visibly refers to the same goal and latest plan. Animate sending, a brief response-in-progress state, and Adler's contextual response; show any resulting saved change accurately. Keep the completed exchange readable, replayable and available without motion. UX6, UX9; shared execution path. |
| **7 · How do connected tools help with this goal?** | **H7:** Show a specific contextual benefit, such as fitting the chosen work around known calendar commitments, followed by continuing with the same goal through a compatible assistant. Animate the relevant context becoming available and the resulting preview. Distinguish context, a proposed placement and an actual booking through their states; shared context does not itself authorize an external write. UX5, UX9; shared execution path. |

**H8 · Problem → solution → demonstration:** candidate problem framing is the burden of repeatedly deciding what to do and what to change when plans meet real life. Candidate solution framing is that Adler turns the person's goal into useful actions and keeps learning from their experience. These are hypotheses about user understanding, not established prevalence claims. A generated visual could show unfinished fragments resolving into one continuing path; the path then becomes the phone story. Keep the meaning in concise copy, not only in the asset. UX2, UX3, UX6.

**H9 · Phone → mountain:** continue the same background colour and a quiet visual motif into the original mountain scene, with sufficient space for headline, explanation and phone at each viewport. Avoid requiring extra scroll solely to finish an animation. Palette, exact spacing and transition technique are aesthetic/interaction hypotheses; none of the sources proves a particular treatment. UX4, UX6, UX7.

## Observable checks before implementation is called successful

These are proposed usability checks, not validated clinical or behavioural outcomes. Compare a baseline and candidate with people matching the intended audience; record errors and explanations, not just preference. The interview can settle the candidate before recruiting or running a study. UX2, UX3, UX9, UX10.

1. **H-comprehension:** show each resting frame without narration or animation. Ask the person to describe what it helps them do and what happens next. Record correct interpretation, uncertainty and time; do not claim an arbitrary time threshold is scientific.
2. **H-first-action:** give a rough goal and observe whether the person can clarify, revise and save it, identify the first useful action, and recognise when the plan is still incomplete.
3. **H-state:** ask what Done, Didn't do and Discuss will do; then verify the person notices the saved change and can correct it. Include partial work, an unknown report and four scheduled completions across a seven-day streak.
4. **H-evidence:** ask what has been observed, what is predicted, whether an agreed test has been used, and whether a review date means it worked. Include an unsupported forecast and pending feedback; investigate each mistaken inference.
5. **H-continuity:** after leaving chat, ask the person to find the current test, earlier attempt and reason for the next change. Verify that the same records appear across demonstrated channels.
6. **H-access:** check small and short mobile viewports, larger text, keyboard/assistive navigation and reduced motion. Essential content must remain readable without enlargement, colour discrimination or waiting through an animation. A visual/browser check establishes rendering and operation; user observation establishes comprehension.

The research stage changed no runtime behaviour. The accepted direction from the subsequent grilling discussion is recorded at the end of this brief.

## Screen 5: behavioural expertise and the reason for a plan change

The user clarified that Screen 5 must show experiment-derived insights informing a visibly revised plan, with the specific behavioural science behind the recommendation. Its visible hierarchy is the goal/milestone, recommended plan with the exact edit highlighted, then the concise evidence and interpretation explaining the change. This supersedes the earlier pending-only H5 proposal. The illustrative story is still under discussion; this note changes no runtime behaviour.

The current boundary is explicit in `server/adaptive-instructions.ts:6,10,18`: Adler supports starting, sustaining, prioritizing and reviewing the person's chosen work. It does not invent domain strategy, technical prescriptions or domain milestones. `docs/method/coaching-engineering.md` sections 2.1–2.2, 4.1 and 6 distinguish the support, proposed mechanism, performed behaviour and observed goal outcome. Specific wording is compatible with behavioural coaching when its method and personal fit are supported. Choosing the exercises/repetitions needed for a physiological result, or deciding the content/quality standards needed for a successful portfolio, requires domain input. A cue for performing the person's already chosen work is a different intervention. General research does not establish that the same application transfers unchanged between goals.

**BS1 — limits of the current finish-point example.** The current story contains a reported repeated-editing problem and two attributed reports of using a chosen stopping point, finishing the selected section and leaving a next step. Neither report establishes another published case study. Its saved `claim:goal-specific-challenging` (version 2026-09-07.1) cites Locke and Latham's 2002 review. The full text, rechecked for this discussion, describes task-performance findings for specific, difficult goals, conditional on factors including ability and commitment; it explicitly says specificity alone does not necessarily improve performance (p. 706), and discusses different demands of complex/learning tasks (pp. 707–709). It does not directly test this stopping-point intervention for repeated portfolio editing. The application can remain a tentative goal-definition hypothesis informed by the person's report, not an empirically established cure for their barrier. Preserve the registry's source-not-separately-graded status and P29's domain/transfer distinction; do not borrow an efficacy grade from the wider synthesis. [Locke and Latham, full text](https://med.stanford.edu/content/dam/sm/s-spire/documents/PD.locke-and-latham-retrospective_Paper.pdf).

**BS2 — a clearer alternative to discuss.** Implementation intentions specify a situation and a concrete goal-directed response. Gollwitzer and Sheeran's meta-analysis reports an average benefit across 94 independent tests. This supports considering a cue-linked plan when a reported initiation difficulty, an available cue and user-endorsed work make it relevant. Preserve separate `claim:implementation-if-then` (technique definition, not an efficacy grade) and `claim:implementation-attainment` (empirical finding), both version 2026-09-07.1, P2's A direction / moderate magnitude, and limits on individual effect, optimal cue and transfer to Adler. A hypothetical gym example could test going directly from work on the already chosen gym days, if the person reports going home first as the relevant barrier and that alternative is feasible. The workout itself remains user/expert-selected. The trial's reports, acceptability and constraints determine whether continuing is worthwhile; attendance alone cannot establish fitness improvement or the causal mechanism. [Gollwitzer and Sheeran, publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0065260106380021).

A replacement portfolio scenario would need its own attributed barrier, chosen action, feasible cue, original prediction and later reports. The current portfolio plan already uses an after-breakfast cue; adding that same cue again would not be a real plan change. Do not silently substitute a gym goal, fabricate personal results, reduce all behavioural coaching to scheduling, or attach a research label after choosing the advice. The method and personal evidence must inform the original test; Screen 5 reveals what its results change in the next plan.

## Accepted implementation direction · September 10

The completed grilling discussion selected the implementation-intention scenario in BS2, with a specific reported portfolio barrier: opening email consumes the writing window. The test changes the response at the existing breakfast cue, retaining the person’s chosen 25-minute work. Two reported uses support a tentative recommendation to continue; Screen 5 explicitly shows that recommendation in the next plan. The source roles, original prediction and transfer limits remain separate and inspectable. This supersedes the initial pending-only H5 and earlier finish-point scenario.

The user corrected H8: the umbrella problem is having a meaningful goal and struggling to follow through, for specific obstacles. The accepted composition integrates each obstacle/help pairing with the corresponding phone screen, without the subsequently rejected standalone problem introduction. There is no duplicate tour or extra grid of screens. Native-style controls, generated paper artwork and one persistent phone support this hierarchy. The source-derived UX choices remain product hypotheses, not experimentally validated interface effects. Implementation and browser checks establish operation and accessibility, not user comprehension or effectiveness. Current details and the fictional evidence chronology are in [landing assets](../landing-assets.md).

## Rendering and copy correction · September 10

The user rejected the added ribbon section, blank interactive plan frames, stretched phone, missing projection, marketing language inside the app, paragraph-heavy layouts and the extra bottom bar. The corrected presentation uses a fixed portrait canvas. The subsequent user direction replaces its initial passive loops with discrete, reversible scroll-controlled frames and a synchronized explanation beside the phone. Functional app labels and the SMART portfolio outcome lead; milestone/action rows, the shared model’s conditional fan, a cue sequence, dated trial evidence and a before/after plan comparison replace prose-led compositions. The actual Adler coaching service and semantic reviewer reviewed the copy in an isolated fictional account. Its conflicting historical streak instruction did not override the user's rule. Normal-motion frame visibility, complete content, forward/reverse scrolling, stationary frames, explanation synchronization and phone proportions are explicit browser regression checks. The deeper iMessage conversation uses the same saved observation and P2 rationale: check available time, propose a test, show agreement and saved state, then use the actual trial reports to propose the next plan. Blue outgoing bubbles and dated exchanges follow the user’s correction. The actual coaching service and semantic reviewer also reviewed this extension; no new efficacy claim or backend capability was introduced. These establish rendering correctness, not usability or coaching effectiveness.
