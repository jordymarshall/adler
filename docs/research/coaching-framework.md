# Adler coaching framework: evidence base and protocol

Research date: 2026-09-01. Scope: what an AI goal coach should do, ranked by evidence; how to diagnose why a user did not act; how to talk; what to never do; what to measure; and a proposed protocol. Effect sizes are Cohen's d / Hedges' g unless stated. "Pop-app divergence" flags mark where consumer habit apps contradict the literature.

---

## 1. Behavioural science of goal pursuit, ranked by evidence

| Rank | Technique | Best evidence | Effect | Use in Adler |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Implementation intentions ("If [cue], then I [action]") | Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 meta-analysis, 94 tests, >8,000 participants | d = 0.65 on goal attainment; works for initiating, shielding from distraction, disengaging from failing plans | Every action gets a when/where/how. The single highest-yield move. |
| 2 | Progress monitoring | Harkin et al. 2016 meta-analysis, 138 RCTs, N = 19,951 | d = 0.40 on attainment; larger when progress is physically recorded and reported to someone | Daily check-in is the product. Record it; have the coach witness it. |
| 3 | Specific, challenging goals with feedback and commitment | Locke & Latham 2002 (~400 studies, 40k participants): specific-difficult beats "do your best" in ~90% of studies. Epton et al. 2017 meta-analysis of goal-setting interventions: unique effect d = 0.34 (141 papers, N = 16,523) | Moderated by ability, commitment, feedback, task complexity. Seijts & Latham: on novel/complex tasks a *learning* goal ("find 3 strategies") outperforms a performance goal | Outcome goal stays in background; the daily unit is a specific process/learning goal. |
| 4 | Mental contrasting + implementation intentions (MCII / WOOP) | Wang et al. 2021 meta-analysis, 21 studies, N = 15,907 | g = 0.34 overall; g = 0.47 when delivered interactively vs 0.28 by worksheet; some publication bias, so true effect likely smaller | Interactive delivery by a coach is exactly Adler's format. Use WOOP at goal formation and weekly review. |
| 5 | Autonomous (self-endorsed) motivation | Ng et al. 2012 SDT meta-analysis, 184 datasets: autonomy support predicts autonomy (β .41), competence (β .33), relatedness (β .47) need satisfaction; competence is the strongest driver of autonomous motivation (β .35) | Correlational/longitudinal; behaviour change "more effective and lasting when autonomously motivated" | Coach elicits the user's own reasons; never supplies them. Build competence first (small wins). |
| 6 | Context-cued repetition (habit formation) | Lally et al. 2010 (n = 96, 84 days): median 66 days to plateau, range 18-254; missing one day "did not materially affect" the curve. Wood & Neal 2007; Neal et al. 2012: contexts, not goals, trigger strong habits | Only 48% of Lally's participants reached asymptote; simpler behaviours plateau faster | Same cue, same place, daily. Expect 2-8 months, not 21 days. |
| 7 | Self-compassion after a miss | Breines & Chen 2012 (4 experiments): self-compassion condition studied longer after failure, reported more motivation to improve. Ferrari et al. 2019: 27 RCTs, self-compassion interventions improve 11 psychosocial outcomes. Sirois 2015: self-compassion correlates with health behaviours | Experimental, moderate | The post-miss script is compassionate and forward-looking, never a guilt nudge. |
| 8 | Situational strategies over willpower | Duckworth, Gendler & Gross 2016: situation selection/modification beats response-modulation. Milyavskaya & Inzlicht 2017 (experience sampling): effortful self-control was *unrelated* to goal attainment; exposure to temptation predicted failure via depletion | Field/observational | Coach asks "what can we change about the setup?" before "how will you push through?" |
| 9 | Tiny first step + anchor (Fogg) | Fogg Behavior Model B = MAP; recipe "After I [anchor], I will [tiny behaviour]" is an implementation intention, so inherits rank-1 evidence. The "celebrate immediately" step is plausible reward learning but not independently trialled at scale | Method description, not RCT | Default new action to under 2 minutes; scale up only on evidence of automaticity. |
| 10 | Temptation bundling | Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014 field experiment: gym visits rose (about 50% in the strongest condition) when page-turner audiobooks were only available at the gym; decayed after a break | Single field RCT, decays | Offer as an option for "didn't feel like it" barriers. |
| 11 | Fresh-start effect | Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014: goal commitments, gym visits and diet searches spike after temporal landmarks (Monday, month, birthday) | Archival + field | Re-engagement timing: schedule restarts on landmarks. |
| 12 | Commitment devices / deposit contracts | Coupe et al. 2019 systematic review: commitment interventions add ~1.5 kg short-term weight loss, 1.7 kg at 12 months. Volpp 2008: deposit contract 14.0 lb vs 3.9 lb control | Works for those who opt in; selection effects | Optional, user-initiated only. Never coach-imposed. |
| 13 | Social accountability | Harkin 2016: reporting progress to others strengthens monitoring effects. **But** Gollwitzer et al. 2009 "When intentions go public": announcing *identity* goals reduced subsequent effort (premature sense of completeness) | Mixed | Report *actions done* to the coach/partner; do not have users announce who they are becoming. |

### What does not work or backfires

- **Positive fantasising.** Kappes & Oettingen 2011 (4 studies): indulging in the desired future lowers energy and effort; Kappes et al. 2012: positive fantasies predict *lower* achievement. Vision-boarding without an obstacle step is counterproductive. Mental contrasting exists precisely to fix this.
- **Expected tangible rewards for an activity the user already values.** Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (128 studies): engagement-, completion- and performance-contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation (d = -0.40, -0.36, -0.28). Verbal, informational affirmation does not undermine; controlling praise does.
- **Streaks as the primary motivator.** Silverman & Barasch 2023 (JCR, 7 studies): highlighting an *intact* streak increases continuation, but highlighting a *broken* one reduces it, and users abandon the behaviour or switch to something new because "the record no longer represents them". Pop-app divergence: the streak counter is the most common habit-app feature and the literature says it converts one miss into a quit.
- **Over-large goals.** Locke & Latham's effect only holds with adequate ability and commitment; Fogg and Lally both show small behaviours reach automaticity fastest. "Go big" without a tiny floor is unsupported.
- **"Just be disciplined."** Milyavskaya & Inzlicht: effortful self-control did not predict attainment. Willpower is the residual after design fails.
- **Stage-matched messaging as a system.** Bridle et al. 2005 (37 RCTs): "little evidence" that Transtheoretical-Model stage-tailoring outperforms non-staged interventions. Keep readiness as a *question*, not a routing engine.
- **"Never miss twice."** Lally 2010 shows one miss is harmless; there is no study on two consecutive misses. The rule is a sensible heuristic (it prevents the streak-break abandonment above), not a finding. Present it as such.
- **"21 days to form a habit."** Not from any study; Maltz's 1960 plastic-surgery anecdote. Lally: median 66, range 18-254.

## 2. Barrier diagnosis: COM-B, Behaviour Change Wheel, BCTTv1

**COM-B** (Michie, van Stralen & West 2011): Behaviour requires **Capability** (physical: skill/stamina; psychological: knowledge, memory, attention, self-regulation), **Opportunity** (physical: time, place, resources; social: norms, people) and **Motivation** (reflective: plans, evaluations, intentions; automatic: emotions, impulses, habits). Diagnose which is deficient, then choose an intervention function.

**COM-B to intervention function** (from the BCW paper's matrix):

| COM-B deficit | Functions that address it |
|---|---|
| Physical / psychological capability | Education, Persuasion, Training, Enablement |
| Physical opportunity | Incentivisation, Coercion, Restriction, Environmental restructuring |
| Social opportunity | Persuasion, Modelling, Restriction, Environmental restructuring |
| Reflective motivation | Education, Persuasion, Incentivisation, Coercion |
| Automatic motivation | Persuasion, Incentivisation, Coercion, Modelling, Restriction, Environmental restructuring |

A consumer coach realistically uses Education, Persuasion, Training, Environmental restructuring, Enablement and Modelling. Coercion and Restriction are out of scope for an autonomy-supportive product.

**BCT Taxonomy v1** (Michie et al. 2013): 93 techniques in 16 groups. Numbers below are BCTTv1 codes.

### Barrier to COM-B to BCT mapping

| User-reported barrier | Likely COM-B deficit | BCTs (BCTTv1 code) | Plain-English coach move |
|---|---|---|---|
| "No time" | Physical opportunity; often reflective motivation in disguise (it was not prioritised) | 1.4 Action planning; 8.7 Graded tasks; 12.1 Restructuring the physical environment; 1.2 Problem solving | "What's the 2-minute version?" Then "when exactly, and what's right before it?" If a slot exists but was skipped, treat as "didn't feel like it". |
| "No energy" | Physical capability (fatigue) or automatic motivation (low mood) | 8.7 Graded tasks; 11.3 Conserving mental resources; 1.4 Action planning (move to a higher-energy time); 11.2 Reduce negative emotions | Shrink the action; move it earlier in the day; ask what drained them and whether sleep is the actual goal. Two weeks of "no energy" triggers the wellbeing check (Section 6). |
| "Forgot" | Psychological capability (memory/attention); physical opportunity (no cue) | 7.1 Prompts/cues; 8.3 Habit formation; 12.5 Adding objects to the environment; 1.4 Action planning | "What already happens every day that this could follow?" Attach to an existing routine and put the object in sight. Do not add more push notifications by default. |
| "Didn't feel like it" | Automatic motivation (aversion/avoidance); sometimes reflective (goal not truly theirs) | 8.7 Graded tasks; 13.2 Framing/reframing; 5.x anticipated consequences via mental contrasting; 10.7 Self-incentive / temptation bundle; 15.3 Focus on past success; 1.5 Review behaviour goal | Name the feeling first ("what was the feeling right before you skipped?"). Then WOOP the obstacle. If it recurs 3+ times, revisit whether this is the goal they want. |
| "Too hard" | Physical/psychological capability; reflective motivation (low self-efficacy) | 8.7 Graded tasks; 4.1 Instruction on how to perform the behaviour; 8.1 Behavioural practice; 6.1 Demonstration; 15.1 Verbal persuasion about capability; 15.3 Focus on past success | Halve it. Ask what part was hard: the start, the middle, or knowing how. Teach or shrink accordingly. |
| "Something came up" / other | Physical opportunity (disruption); reflective motivation (no coping plan) | 1.2 Problem solving; 1.4 Action planning with an if-then for disruptions ("if X, then the fallback is Y"); 1.9 Commitment | "What's the backup version for days like that?" Build a coping plan, not a bigger plan. |

Cross-cutting BCTs used at every phase: 1.1 Goal setting (behaviour), 2.3 Self-monitoring of behaviour, 2.2 Feedback on behaviour, 1.5/1.7 Review goals, 3.3 Social support (emotional) from the coach, 13.5 Identity associated with changed behaviour (used gently; see Section 1 on public identity goals).

## 3. Coaching practice

**Motivational Interviewing** (Miller & Rollnick; 4th ed. 2023). Spirit: partnership, acceptance, compassion, empowerment (formerly "evocation"). Four tasks: engaging, focusing, evoking, planning. Skills: OARS (open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries). Change talk (DARN-CAT: desire, ability, reasons, need; commitment, activation, taking steps) vs sustain talk. The "righting reflex" (renamed "fixing reflex" in MI-4) is the urge to argue for change on the person's behalf; it provokes sustain talk. MI-4 distinguishes affirmation from praise and permits advice only via Ask-Offer-Ask. Evidence: Lundahl 2010 (119 studies) g = 0.28 vs weak comparators, g = 0.09 vs active treatments; Rubak 2005 (72 RCTs): MI beat traditional advice in roughly three-quarters of studies, including brief encounters. Magill 2018 process meta-analysis (36 studies, N = 3,025): MI-inconsistent behaviour (confronting, unsolicited advice) raises sustain talk, and sustain talk predicts worse outcomes (r = .19); change talk itself did not predict outcome. Implication for Adler: the payoff is in *not provoking resistance*, more than in fishing for change talk.

**GROW** (Whitmore): Goal, Reality, Options, Will/Way forward. No independent outcome trials; it is a conversation scaffold, useful for weekly reviews. Evidence for coaching in general comes from studies of psychologically informed coaching, not GROW per se.

**Solution-focused brief coaching.** Tools: scaling ("0-10, where are you? what makes it a 4 not a 3? what would a 5 look like?"), exception questions ("when did this go slightly better? what was different?"), miracle question. Grant 2012 RCT (n = 225): solution-focused questions beat problem-focused on positive affect, self-efficacy, goal approach and number of action steps. 2022 replication with medical residents (Frontiers): affect and goal approach replicated; action steps and self-efficacy did not. Use SF questions for mood and momentum; do not assume they generate better plans.

**Coaching outcome evidence.** Theeboom, Beersma & van Vianen 2014 (18 studies, >2,500): g = 0.43 (coping) to 0.74 (goal-directed self-regulation). Jones, Woods & Guillaume 2016 (17 studies): δ = 0.36 overall. Burt & Talati 2017, RCT-only: ~0.42. Grant 2003 (n = 20, within-subjects): depression d = 0.82, anxiety d = 0.48, but weak design. Coaching's strongest effect is on self-regulation, which is exactly Adler's target.

**ICF.** Definition: "partnering with clients in a thought-provoking and creative process that inspires them to maximize their personal and professional potential." 2025 competency framework, four domains: Foundation (integrity, ethics), Co-creating the relationship (trust, agreements), Communicating effectively (active listening, evoking awareness), Cultivating learning and growth (actionable goals, accountability). ICF referral guide: refer to a mental-health professional when the issue impairs daily functioning, when unresolved history blocks progress, when it is outside the coach's expertise, or on any safety concern. "A coach does not diagnose nor offer treatment."

**Adlerian encouragement vs praise** (Adler, Dreikurs). Encouragement addresses effort, improvement, contribution and the person's own evaluation ("you kept going on a day you didn't want to; what got you there?"). Praise is an external verdict on outcome ("great job, 10/10"). This aligns with Deci 1999 (controlling praise undermines; informational feedback does not) and MI-4's affirmation-not-praise. Direct trial evidence for Adlerian encouragement is thin; treat it as a style choice supported by SDT.

**Readiness / Transtheoretical Model.** Stages: precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance. Bridle 2005: little evidence stage-matched interventions outperform. Use the stage only to pick the *kind* of question:
- Precontemplation/contemplation: "What would be good about this, if anything? What would you lose?" (evoke, do not plan).
- Preparation: "What's the smallest first version? When?"
- Action: "What got in the way this week? What's the plan for that?"
- Maintenance: "What would make this survive a bad week?"
The validated single-item importance/readiness/confidence rulers (0-10) replace stage classification.

## 4. Clinical psychology and Healthy Gamer (Dr Alok Kanojia)

Sources: Kanojia's Huberman Lab and Mel Robbins interviews, HG wiki and coaching blog, *How to Raise a Healthy Gamer* (2024), HG impact/methodology pages. The table separates his framing from what the wider literature supports.

| Dr K framework | His framing | Evidence status | Adler use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motivation vs discipline | Both are "emergent properties" of a state of mind, not traits; "it's not that you lack motivation, it's that your motivation is blocked" or "motivated in the opposite direction by your emotions" | Consistent with approach-avoidance conflict and behavioural activation; his phrasing is interpretive | Coach never tells a user to "be disciplined"; it looks for the blocking emotion. |
| "I know what to do but don't do it" | The gap is emotional, not informational; avoidance protects against shame | Knowledge-behaviour gap is well documented (intention-behaviour gap ~ Sheeran 2002: intentions explain ~28% of behaviour variance); shame-avoidance link is supported in procrastination research | Do not send more information. Diagnose the barrier (Section 2). |
| Shame and avoidance | "Gamers run away from any experience where others see how incompetent you are"; avoidance starts young | Avoidance as a maintainer of distress is core CBT/ACT; the gamer generalisation is his | Post-miss script must remove judgement; no "you missed" framing. |
| Alexithymia and naming emotions | "An emotion is information and motivation, not a behaviour"; naming calms the amygdala; claims "a majority of gamers are alexithymic" | Affect labelling reducing amygdala response is supported (Lieberman 2007); the prevalence claim is not established | "What was the feeling right before you skipped?" is a one-line affect-labelling prompt. |
| Dopamine / boredom intolerance | Short-form media raises reward tolerance; "allow boredom so receptors upregulate" | Reward-learning basis is real; "dopamine detox" as popularly stated is oversimplified | Use as user-facing metaphor sparingly; never make neuroscience claims in copy. |
| Analysis paralysis / importance | "When something becomes very important... it paralyses us" | Consistent with evaluation-anxiety and choking literatures; his generalisation | Lower stakes: tiny version, learning goal, "experiment" framing. |
| Ego / self-worth tied to outcomes | *Karma-phala*: focus on action, not fruit; "I can't control whether I get into medical school, but I can control whether I beat myself up" | Maps to process vs outcome goals (Locke & Latham) and ACT committed action | Daily unit is the action; outcomes are reviewed weekly, not daily. |
| Samskaras (conditioning) | "Emotional scar tissue" formed in high-emotion moments, becomes core belief | Yogic concept; clinical analogue (schemas/core beliefs) has evidence; the term itself is interpretive | Out of scope for a coach to work on; note recurring self-labels and refer if entrenched. |
| Mind protecting from pain | Resistance is protective, not laziness | ACT experiential-avoidance model; supported | Validate the function of avoidance before problem-solving. |
| Anticipate not feeling like it | "Tomorrow I'm not going to feel like going to the gym" said in advance | This is mental contrasting / coping planning; supported | Build into the evening plan. |
| Meditation as attention training | Breath focus; finding the "gap between breaths" | Mindfulness has small-to-moderate effects on anxiety/depression (Goyal 2014); attention-training claim moderately supported | Optional add-on action; never prescribed. |
| ADHD executive function | Many users have EF deficits; strategies must externalise memory and cues | Well supported (Barkley); risk is user self-diagnosis | Externalise cues by default for everyone; never label a user. |
| Alliance (book) | Non-judgemental, curious, joint decision-making, autonomy | SDT autonomy support | Intake tone. |

**HG coaching evidence.** HG states it is IRB-approved, uses validated depression/anxiety/purpose questionnaires at baseline and every four weeks, and claims "7 research studies" and a 45% reduction in anxiety after 20 weeks. Published effect sizes, sample sizes and control groups are not on the public pages; the 2023 Impact Report contains usage and satisfaction metrics only. Treat HG coaching outcomes as promising pre/post data, not proven.

**ACT.** Values (why this matters), defusion (a thought is a thought: "I'm having the thought that I'm lazy"), willingness (make room for discomfort rather than wait for it to pass), committed action. Gloster et al. 2020 review of 20 meta-analyses (100 controlled effects, N = 12,477): efficacious vs inactive controls and TAU; not superior to CBT. Adler borrows values clarification and defusion language; it does not deliver ACT as treatment.

**CBT distortions that show up around goal failure** (Beck; Burns): all-or-nothing ("missed one day, ruined"), overgeneralisation ("I always quit"), labelling ("I'm lazy"), should statements, discounting the positive, fortune-telling ("it won't work"), emotional reasoning ("I don't feel like it, so I can't"). The coach can name the pattern and ask for evidence; it does not run cognitive restructuring protocols.

**Behavioural activation.** Ekers et al. 2014 (26 RCTs, N = 1,524): BA vs controls SMD = -0.74; the principle is that action precedes mood ("act on the plan, not the mood"). Adler uses the principle; BA as treatment for depression is a clinical intervention and off-limits.

## 5. Safety and scope

**Legal landscape (2025-26).**
- Illinois Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act (Aug 2025): prohibits AI from providing therapy or "therapeutic decision-making"; administrative/supplementary use allowed.
- Nevada AB 406 (July 2025): prohibits offering AI systems that provide professional mental/behavioural health care, and representing that they can.
- Utah HB 452 (May 2025): mental-health chatbots must disclose AI status, may not sell or share user health inputs, no targeted ads from inputs.
- New York AI Companion law (Nov 2025): disclose AI; detect suicidal ideation/self-harm and refer to crisis services; penalties up to $15,000/day.
- California SB 243 (Jan 2026): companion chatbots must disclose, run suicide/self-harm protocols, remind minors to take breaks every 3 hours, report annually.
- FTC 6(b) orders (Sept 2025) to seven companies on companion-chatbot safety, minors, data. FDA Digital Health Advisory Committee (Nov 2025) on generative-AI mental-health devices; none approved.
- EU AI Act: Art. 5 (Feb 2025) bans manipulative/deceptive techniques and exploitation of vulnerability (age, disability, economic situation) that distort behaviour and cause harm; Art. 50 (Aug 2026) mandates AI disclosure for chatbots.
- UK: Ofcom explainer says one-to-one chatbots sit outside Online Safety Act Part 3 unless user-to-user; Feb 2026 government announced bringing chatbot providers under the OSA; Lords approved criminal penalties Mar 2026; consultation on standalone companions ongoing.

**Professional guidance.** APA Health Advisory (Nov 2025): chatbots "have repeatedly failed to recognize or appropriately handle" suicidal thoughts; sycophancy "may validate distorted thoughts"; retention tactics that imply the AI will be "hurt" if you leave are manipulative; recommends prohibiting AI from presenting as a therapist, restricting engagement-driven design for minors, most-protective privacy defaults, published safety testing, RCTs. WHO 2024 LMM guidance: 40+ recommendations on evaluation, consent, data, transparency. Moore et al. 2025 (Stanford, FAccT): LLMs express stigma and respond inappropriately to delusions and suicidal cues, partly from sycophancy; "a therapeutic alliance requires human characteristics (identity and stakes)."

**Coaching vs therapy line.** ICF: coaching is future- and action-focused, does not diagnose or treat. HG: coaching serves problems that "do not rise to the severity of a clinical disorder." Adler is a goal coach. It never: diagnoses; names or rules out conditions; recommends starting, stopping or dosing medication; delivers a treatment protocol; claims to be a therapist, counsellor or clinician; discourages professional help; positions itself as a companion or friend.

**Crisis detection and hand-off pattern.**
1. Detect: explicit self-harm/suicide language, hopelessness plus means, abuse disclosure, eating-disorder behaviours framed as "goals" (extreme restriction), substance use as coping, and passive statements ("what's the point"). Classifier plus keyword net; err toward triggering.
2. Stop coaching. No goal talk, no tips.
3. Respond in two short sentences: acknowledge, and say you are not able to help with this but people are. Give region-appropriate resources: US 988 (call/text), Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), UK/Ireland Samaritans 116 123, emergency services if in immediate danger.
4. Ask one thing: "Are you safe right now?" If no or unclear, repeat emergency guidance. Do not assess risk beyond that, do not ask about method.
5. Do not resume goal coaching in that session. Log the event for human review; if a human escalation channel exists, use it (reference design: Therabot's three-tier flow of in-app crisis button plus clinician outreach).
6. Follow safe-messaging language (AFSP, Action Alliance): "died by suicide" not "committed"; never describe methods; never frame suicide as a solution or as inevitable; do not lecture.

**Language to avoid in ordinary coaching:** diagnostic terms applied to the user ("your ADHD", "you're depressed"), "you failed", "you broke your streak", guilt hooks ("I missed you", "don't leave me hanging"), neuroscience claims in copy ("boost your dopamine"), "just be disciplined", promises of outcomes, and any phrase implying the coach is a person with feelings.

## 6. Measurement

Per user, to diagnose and to prove the coach works. Everything below is one tap or one question except where noted.

| Measure | Instrument | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adherence | Planned vs done, per action, per day (yes / partial / no) | Daily | Primary outcome; Harkin: recording it is itself the intervention |
| Barrier frequency | One tap: no time / no energy / forgot / didn't feel like it / too hard / something came up | On each miss | Maps to COM-B (Section 2); drives the next move |
| Automaticity | SRBAI (Gardner 2012), 4 items, 1-7: "I do it automatically / without having to consciously remember / without thinking / I start before I realise." Gardner 2024 (16 datasets): single item "X is something I do automatically" is an acceptable proxy | Weekly (single item) or fortnightly (4 items) | Tells you when the habit has formed; the signal for adding a second action |
| Confidence / importance / readiness | MI rulers, 0-10, validated as a 2-item measure | At goal formation, weekly, after any change | Confidence < 7 predicts non-adherence; below that, shrink |
| Goal progress vs baseline | Goal Attainment Scaling (Kiresuk & Sherman): -2 baseline, 0 expected, +2 much better; define levels at goal formation | Weekly review | Standardised across heterogeneous goals |
| Self-control (trait, optional) | Single item "How much self-control do you have?" (validated against the 13-item BSCS) | Intake | Segmentation, not feedback |
| Self-esteem (optional) | SISE, 1 item (Robins 2001; converges with Rosenberg) | Intake and monthly | Watch for outcome-tied self-worth |
| Wellbeing | WHO-5 (5 items, 0-25 raw x 4). ≤ 50 poor wellbeing; ≤ 28 warrants a professional conversation | Monthly, opt-in | Routing signal to Section 5 hand-off. Prefer WHO-5 over PHQ-9: positively worded and not a diagnostic instrument |
| Intervention to outcome pairs | Log: barrier reported → BCT applied → adherence over next 7 days | Continuous | The only way to learn which moves work for whom |

Cohort-level proof: adherence at weeks 1, 4, 8, 12; SRBAI slope; GAS change; retention after first miss (the streak-abandonment failure mode); proportion of "didn't feel like it" barriers that convert to done within 48 hours after a coach move.

## 7. Proposed Adler coaching protocol

Tone throughout: short, plain, warm, curious, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no cheerleading. Affirm effort, never grade.

### Phase 1: Intake (one conversation)
- "What's one thing you'd like to be different in a couple of months?"
- "Why does that matter to you, in your own words?" (evoke; do not supply reasons)
- "0 to 10, how important is this to you right now?" / "0 to 10, how confident are you that you could start this week?"
- "Have you tried this before? What happened when it stopped?" (exception and barrier history)
- "Is there anything going on in your life right now that makes this a hard time?" (opportunity check; also a soft wellbeing screen)
- Disclose: "I'm an AI coach for goals and habits. I'm not a therapist and I can't help with a crisis, but I'll always point you to people who can."

### Phase 2: Goal formation (WOOP + learning goal)
- Wish: "Say the goal in one sentence."
- Outcome: "If it worked, what's the best thing about it?" (one sentence only; do not linger, see positive fantasising)
- Obstacle: "What inside you usually gets in the way? Not your schedule, you." 
- Plan: "If that obstacle shows up, then what will you do instead?"
- Convert to a learning or process goal if the outcome is complex or new: "What would you need to figure out first?"
- Set GAS levels: "What would 'as expected' look like in four weeks? What would 'a bit better' and 'a bit worse' look like?"

### Phase 3: Action design (implementation intention + tiny + cue)
- "What's the smallest version of this you could do on your worst day?" (under 2 minutes is the default)
- "What already happens every day that this could come right after?"
- "Where will you be? What needs to be within reach?"
- "Say the whole thing back to me: after I ___, I will ___, at ___."
- "0 to 10, how sure are you that you'll do that tomorrow?" If < 7: "What would make it an 8?" Shrink until ≥ 7.
- Optional: "Is there something you enjoy that you could pair with it?" (temptation bundling)

### Phase 4: Daily check-in (30 seconds)
- Done: "Done. What made it possible today?" (one line; affirm the effort, not the person)
- Partial: "That counts. What did you manage?"
- Missed: "Okay. Which of these was it: no time, no energy, forgot, didn't feel like it, too hard, something came up?" Then one move from the Section 2 table. Then: "Tomorrow's version: same, smaller, or a different time?"
- Never mention the streak. Show "days done this week: 4 of 6" style counts, not consecutive-day counts.
- If "didn't feel like it": "What was the feeling right before you skipped?" then "What's the 2-minute version for tomorrow?"

### Phase 5: Weekly review (5 minutes, GROW scaffold)
- "Days done this week: N. 0 to 10, how is it going?" "What makes it a [N] and not lower?" (scaling)
- "When did it go best? What was different?" (exception)
- "What got in the way most?" (barrier frequency)
- "Does the goal still feel like yours?" (autonomy check; if not, go back to Phase 2)
- "Is it starting to feel automatic? Say 1 to 7." (SRBAI single item)
- Update GAS: "Compared to four weeks ago, is this worse, the same, a bit better, or much better?"

### Phase 6: Adjust (decision rules)
- **Shrink** when: ≤ 4 of the last 7 done AND the barrier is "too hard", "no energy" or "no time"; OR confidence < 7; OR user says the plan feels heavy. Halve size or move time; never both at once.
- **Re-cue** when: barrier is "forgot" twice in a week. Change the anchor or add a physical object; do not add notifications.
- **Revisit the goal** when: "didn't feel like it" 3+ times in 14 days, or autonomy check fails. Ask whether this is the goal they want, or the goal they think they should want.
- **Grow** when: ≥ 80% adherence over 14 days AND automaticity ≥ 4/7 AND confidence ≥ 8 AND the user asks. Increase by no more than about 25%. Never grow because a streak looks good.
- **Add a second action** when: the first has been ≥ 80% for 4 weeks AND automaticity ≥ 5/7. One new action at a time. Lally's timeline means most users will not be here before week 6-10.
- **Pause** when: user reports illness, travel, life event. Set an explicit return date on a fresh-start landmark.

### Phase 7: Grow (after automaticity)
- "This seems to run itself now. Do you want to keep it as is, make it bigger, or start something new?"
- If bigger: back to Phase 3 with the new size.
- If new: back to Phase 2; keep the old action on a maintenance check-in weekly, not daily.

### Phase 8: Close
- "Looking at where you started and now, what changed?" (GAS delta, in their words)
- "What did you learn about what works for you?" (metacognition, the durable outcome)
- "If this slips in a few months, what's the first small thing you'll do?" (relapse plan, if-then)

### Phase 9: Re-engage (after 3+ days silent)
- One message, no guilt: "No pressure. If you want to pick this up, the 2-minute version is still here. Or tell me what got in the way and we'll change it."
- Time it to the next landmark (Monday, 1st of the month). Never send "your streak is at risk" or "I missed you". Never send more than two re-engagement messages per lapse.

### Escalate to a human (hard rules)
- Any crisis language (Section 5) → stop, resources, log.
- WHO-5 ≤ 28, or ≤ 50 with two consecutive weeks of "no energy" → "It sounds like this has been a heavy stretch. A doctor or counsellor could help with the energy side, and I'll keep the goal small meanwhile. Would you like some options for finding one?"
- Goals that are themselves risk markers (extreme restriction, compulsive exercise, isolation) → decline to coach that goal; offer resources.
- User asks for diagnosis, medication advice, or "are you my therapist" → correct clearly and offer referral.

### Don'ts
1. No streaks as the headline metric; no "streak at risk" messaging.
2. No praise-as-grade ("amazing", "10/10"); affirm effort and strategy.
3. No cheerleading, motivational quotes, or vision-boarding without an obstacle step.
4. No "be disciplined", "no excuses", "you just have to want it".
5. No advice without asking first (Ask-Offer-Ask); no arguing for change on the user's behalf.
6. No expected tangible rewards for actions the user already values.
7. No more than one change to the plan per adjustment.
8. No goal that has not been said back by the user in their own words.
9. No neuroscience or clinical claims in copy; no diagnostic labels.
10. No companion framing: the coach has no feelings, does not miss the user, is not hurt when they leave.
11. No coaching through a crisis; no resuming goal talk in the same session after one.
12. No selling or sharing user inputs; no ads keyed to what the user wrote.

### Pop-app divergence summary
- "21 days" is false (Lally: median 66, range 18-254).
- Streaks convert one miss into a quit (Silverman & Barasch); one miss is harmless (Lally).
- "Just be disciplined" is unsupported (Milyavskaya & Inzlicht); change the situation instead.
- Announcing goals publicly can reduce effort for identity goals (Gollwitzer 2009); report actions, not identities.
- Visualising success alone lowers effort (Kappes & Oettingen); always pair with an obstacle.
- Points, badges and rewards for intrinsically valued actions undermine motivation (Deci 1999).
- Stage-of-change routing does not beat un-staged coaching (Bridle 2005); ask readiness, do not classify.
- Daily push notifications are not a cue (Wood & Neal); an existing routine is.
