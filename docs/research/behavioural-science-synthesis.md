# Behavioural science synthesis for the Adler Method

Consolidated from the 23 deep dives in `docs/research/deep/` and the companion files (`coaching-framework.md`, `goal-discovery-frameworks.md`, `atomic-habits-and-training-science.md`, `accreditation-frameworks.md`). Date 2026-09-02. This is the evidence base the method is written from; where it and a source file disagree, this file wins and the disagreement is in §2.

**Grades.** **A** several meta-analyses or multi-site RCTs agreeing · **B** one good RCT or field experiment, or a consistent correlational meta-analysis · **C** prospective/correlational, single lab, or cross-domain extrapolation · **D** theory, standards or arithmetic. Confidence is stated separately: a replicated finding in the wrong population is still a weak basis for a rule.

**Three facts that frame everything.** (1) **Claims ceiling:** for an individual-level, digital, self-selected behaviour-change product the defensible expectation is **d ≈ 0.1–0.2** while contact continues, mostly gone after (DellaVigna & Linos: 1.4 pp on a 17.4 pp base, 126 RCTs, 23M people, ~6× smaller than the journal literature; Maier 2022: nudging d = 0.04 after bias correction) — and larger for people with slack (digital PA: SMD 0.34 high-SES vs 0.06 low-SES, and no choice of technique moderates it). (2) **Population gap:** anchors come from clinical, older or overweight samples (mean ages 46–62 against a 16–40 audience) in a literature 96% Western. No RCT tests a goal coach for non-clinical 16–40 optimisers over months. Published effects are **priors with wide uncertainty and a defensible sign**, never predictions about a user. (3) **Idiographic ceiling:** a 7-day done-rate has SE 17 pp and a week-to-week delta ~25–30 pp with autocorrelation, so one week per arm detects only effects ~3× larger than anything below.

## 1. Principles, ranked by evidence strength

### Tier 1 — experimental, replicated, directly about what Adler does

**P1. Monitoring, performed and witnessed, is the base ingredient.** Harkin (138 RCTs, N = 19,951): attainment d = 0.40; **privately monitored but reported to someone d+ = 0.47 (k = 95) vs unreported 0.19 (k = 14)**; the *public* cell habit apps imitate is k = 3, CI [−0.16, 1.26]; objective tools g = 0.40 vs subjective logs −0.02. **Adler:** the one-tap check-in *is* the intervention and the coach is a recipient who acknowledges and notices absence; under 4 of 7 taps for two weeks, fix the tap before anything else. No audience. **A / high.**

**P2. Every action gets a when, where and what-comes-before.** Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 94 tests, d = 0.65; digital d+ = 0.25; MCII g = 0.34. Caveat: the 0.65 has **never been re-analysed with modern selection models**, unlike growth mindset, ego depletion and self-distancing, all of which shrank. **Adler:** "After I ___, I will ___, at ___", said back by the user; quote it as *the most consistently replicated move*, not as a number. **A direction / moderate magnitude.**

**P3. The structured after-action review is the largest effect available to this product.** d = 0.79 (61 studies) and 0.67; contributors are alignment to the individual, objective review media and self-led facilitation; reviewing successes **and** failures beat failures-only; the active ingredient is a prompted "why did that work" (g = 0.55), not free journaling. **Adler:** record on screen first, user reviews while Adler asks and summarises, one good day and one bad day, one change out. Tested doses were 15 min daily; Adler ships five minutes weekly. **A / moderate (transfer untested).**

**P4. Content is the moderator; evaluation is nearly inert.** Kluger & DeNisi: d = 0.41 but **38% of 607 effects negative**, harm rising as attention moves to the self. Wisniewski (435 studies): high-information feedback **d = 0.99** vs corrective 0.46 vs reinforcement/punishment **0.24**; negative feedback is not demotivating once it carries how-to. **Adler:** three slots and no fourth — record in counts with no adjectives, the condition that explains it, one how-to. **A / high.**

**P5. No person-level praise; description is the benchmark, not the floor.** With the missing control added (outcome feedback, no praise) in children *and* 114 undergraduates, person praise was worst on every post-failure measure and **process praise was no different from no praise**; what survives is informational positive feedback tied to a specific act (d = 0.33). **Adler:** "you did it on a day with two late nights" — never "crushing it", never a percentile, never a cumulative deficit. **A / high.**

**P6. Contact continues or the effect decays; maintenance is a schedule change, not a curriculum.** Effects halve by six months (0.46→0.25; +926→+413 steps). Continued contact *after* a programme is the most replicated maintenance ingredient (g = 0.385), while maintenance *content* is null across 81 trials and 69,094 people and the largest digital maintenance RCT is null against a tracker-plus-weighing control. **Adler:** at the gate (SRBAI ≥ 5/7, ≥ 80% for 8 weeks) cut the dose and change the questions; ship no content pack; any maintenance feature must beat a monitoring-only arm. **A / high.**

**P7. Willpower is not a resource.** Two registered replications killed the classic effect (d = 0.04, 23 labs; 0.06, 36 labs); what survives is a modest after-effect of genuinely long demands (d ≈ 0.31). Trait self-control works through **habits and fewer temptations** (r ≈ .30, stronger for automatic behaviour), and situation modification beat "use willpower" (d = 0.56–0.63). **Adler:** first question after any barrier is "what could change about the setup so this needs no willpower?" Never say depleted; teach no willpower belief either way. **A / high.**

**P8. Graded tasks and a floor dose; hold before you grow.** Graded tasks appear in every maintenance list and every positive digital-PA regression, and the biological floor is far below intuition — one set 2–3×/week produces real strength gains. **Adler:** new action ≤ 2 minutes; no growth for 14 days and not before ≥ 80% and SRBAI ≥ 4/7; grow frequency → volume → intensity, ≤ 25% per step (heuristic on the >30% injury signal). **A ingredient / H numbers.**

**P9. Values before goals.** Chase 2013: online goal-setting alone did nothing versus waitlist while **values plus goal setting raised next-semester GPA**; self-concordance and implementation intentions each predict progress and **interact**, so plans pay off mainly on self-endorsed goals. **Adler:** discovery produces a why-line in the user's own words before any action exists. **B/A / high.**

**P10. Autonomy support is a delivery style, and it transfers.** Slemp (754 correlations, N = 32,870): associations **not moderated by country**, confirmed experimentally in a Seoul RCT. But autonomy ≠ choice count (Asian-American children were equally motivated when a trusted other chose), and autonomous motivation is not a weekly metric. **Adler:** elicit reasons, never supply them; the user edits every plan; do not score motivation quality weekly. **A / high.**

**P11. Do not argue for change.** Magill (36 studies, N = 3,025): MI-inconsistent behaviour raises sustain talk and sustain talk predicts worse outcomes (r = .19), while change talk itself did not predict outcome. **Adler:** "yes, but" is a detection rule that the coach has been pushing. This is the depth-psychology consensus stripped of metapsychology — opposition to change is protective, so reflect the barrier and hand the decision back. **A / high.**

**P12. The coach must be able to disagree.** Cheng 2026 (*Science*; 11 models, three preregistered experiments, N = 2,405): AI affirmed users' actions **49% more often than humans**, and a single sycophantic interaction reduced willingness to take responsibility and repair conflict — while sycophantic models were rated higher and reused more. **Adler:** a review ending with no disagreement and no trade-off named is a fidelity failure; declining to coach a goal is a supported action; never optimise on satisfaction, alliance or retention. **B / high.**

**P13. Claim behaviour, never mood.** Chatbot syntheses converge on depression g ≈ 0.3 overall but **g = 0.07 in non-clinical samples** (39 RCTs), with wellbeing, stress and affect null wherever separated; behaviour change is supported (PA 0.28, sleep duration 0.44) and an AI-led diabetes-prevention programme was non-inferior to human coaching over 12 months. **Adler:** no mood, anxiety or mental-health claim anywhere — which also keeps the product outside the Illinois/Nevada/Utah definitions. **A / high.**

### Tier 2 — well supported, with a named boundary condition

**P14. Affect tracks the *rate* of progress against an expected rate, not distance to the goal**, and better-than-expected progress produces coasting. **Adler:** the review shows rate ("4 of 6 this week, 3 of 6 last"); the floor dose and ≥7 confidence gate exist so most weeks beat the expected rate slightly; one down week → one question, two → adjust; sustained coasting in a multi-goal user is the hand-off signal. **B / moderate — the velocity display is itself an open A/B against streak-break abandonment.**

**P15. Confidence is a readout of mastery, not a cause of it.** Between persons r = .38 and raising efficacy changes behaviour d = 0.47; within a person, performance → efficacy β = .205 against efficacy → performance .071. Sources rank mastery at 18.4% of explained variance and **persuasion by others at 1.0%**. **Adler:** move the ruler with the user's own record, never with encouragement; two rulers (tomorrow = adoption, bad day = maintenance); never use efficacy as a weekly outcome (ρ = −0.18 with behaviour change). **A / high.**

**P16. Recovery self-efficacy after a miss is the predictor; blame is not the enemy.** EMA overturns the affective abstinence-violation effect: efficacy drops accelerated the next lapse (HR 1.09) while **internal blame was protective** (HR 0.98), and the predictive item was *feeling like giving up*; rewarding the **return** was the best of 54 arms in a 61,293-person megastudy (+27%). **Adler:** after any "No", "Tomorrow, 0–10?" — ≥7 acknowledge, 4–6 offer the two-minute version for tomorrow only, ≤3 or "giving up" opens a same-evening conversation. Affirm the return, never the record. **C / moderate.**

**P17. One miss is harmless; phase matters more than gap length.** One miss did not affect Lally's curve, but any smoking in week two predicted 83–97% relapse and 74% of six-month failures began in weeks one or two; once a habit is predictable, incentives stop working. **Adler:** first 14 days, two misses in any 7 → shrink now; established action (SRBAI ≥ 5), three misses in 7 → ask what changed in the *context* first. "Never miss twice" stays a heuristic in the user's own words. **C / moderate.**

**P18. Satisfaction sustains what expectations started, and honest framing is free.** Efficacy predicted initiators while satisfaction predicted maintainers at 2/9/15 months; optimistic vs balanced messaging shifted cognitions and produced **no weight difference at 18 months**. **Adler:** one balanced sentence about the typical course at intake; monthly satisfaction and effort from week 8; adherence ≥ 80% with satisfaction ≤ 5 for two months is maintenance risk, never answered with a bigger dose. **B / moderate.**

**P19. Identity is built from the record, never assigned.** Habit–identity r = .55 (N = 13,340), but interventions move identity g = 0.18 while moving behaviour g = 0.61 in the same trials, half of identity interventions are null, and announcing an *identity* goal reduces subsequent effort. **Adler:** "three months of Tuesday runs", never "you're a runner now". No declarations, no badges. **A correlational / high for the rule.**

**P20. Habit is instigation automaticity in a stable context, and the timeline is per behaviour.** Median 59–66 days for simple daily acts against **122–226 days for gym attendance** (12M check-ins); routine and clock cues performed equally in the only RCT; reminders build repetition but *hinder* automaticity. **Adler:** anchor at the end of an existing sequence; reminders off by default and framed as removable; fade the daily log at SRBAI ≥ 5/7 for two weeks so the app never becomes the cue; never quote one days-to-habit number. **A/B / high.**

**P21. Immediate experiential reward predicts persistence; delayed reward predicts only intentions.** Immediate β = .24–.43 vs delayed ns; an immediate bonus raised unpaid continuation 84% vs 70%. Against it, expected contingent rewards undermine (d = −0.28 to −0.40) and social reward was a *negative* moderator of habit formation (β = −0.40). **Adler:** one line of intrinsic noticing after completion; no points, badges, social or variable rewards. **B positive half / A prohibition.**

**P22. Affect *during* the act is the leading indicator; affect afterwards is not.** During-exercise affect predicted future activity while **post-exercise affect was null**; affective judgements r = .42 with behaviour, moved by experience and not by persuasion. **Adler:** one three-face tap after every "Yes" — how it felt *while* doing it; two consecutive weeks mostly negative is a "too hard" verdict even at full adherence, answered by cutting dose or changing setting, never by motivating. **A / high.**

**P23. Notifications buy the next hour, not the next month.** OR 3.5 for opening within an hour, wording irrelevant, **no difference in time to disengagement**; suggestion effects decay to zero by ~day 28; sensor-driven timing did not beat a fixed time; reminders predicted *smaller* chatbot effects while tracking halved long-term attrition. **Adler:** one notification a day at the user's check-in time, no cue-time reminders, never add notifications to fix retention. **B / high.**

**P24. Structural constraints are not motivation problems, and the coach must be able to stop.** Schedule unpredictability predicts distress and hardship more than low wages (N = 37,263); high-agency interventions widen SES gaps; scarcity-as-bandwidth did *not* replicate. **Adler:** a constraint check fires before shrink/re-cue/revisit/re-engage when ≥60% of misses over 14 days are "no time"/"something came up" across two reviews, or a fixed external demand is named; it offers change the goal / change the setup / park with a date, and suppresses the internal-obstacle question for 28 days. There is no third shrink. **A finding / D thresholds.**

**P25. Report the record, never the person, and never compare users.** Normative feedback boomerangs on the favourably placed and does nothing in the lowest decile; competition beat social support head-to-head (35.7 vs 16.8 weekly attendances, support arm *below* control) — the lever Adler declines on values grounds, stated openly. Group *format* is a null (71 studies, g = 0.086 ns); the old gradient was contact. **Adler:** comparison is intrapersonal only. **A / high.**

**P26. Presence of meaning, not the search for it.** 91% of people across 132 nations say life has meaning; presence–wellbeing r ≈ .42 while search is small or negative in individualistic samples. Purpose tracks mortality (HR 0.76/SD) but 62% attenuates through depression, and written values exercises do not transfer (null ruling out 0.07 SD). **Adler:** no purpose module; a why-line per goal, surfaced at review and after a lapse; never a longevity or health claim. **A / high.**

**P27. "Should" goals: detect by behaviour, probe volition before shelving.** Self-critical perfectionism predicts worse progress via lower autonomous motivation, and introjection predicts effort (ρ = .25) but not continuation; the have-to signature is more obstacles, more effort, no progress. **But** socially expected obligation carries *equal* satisfaction and perceived choice among Indian adults, so the tag alone must not trigger shelving. **Adler:** shelve on the signature plus one probe — "is this one you'd still choose, or one you'd put down if you could?" Parked, visible, never deleted. **B / moderate.**

**P28. Foundations are a diagnosis before they are a goal.** Sleep→self-control is stronger *within* person (ρ = 0.35) than between; people cannot self-report their own impairment; regularity beats duration (20–48% lower mortality across quintiles); half the activity guideline already carries RR 0.82 for depression while exercise→cognition is ~0 after bias correction. **Adler:** when "no energy" dominates, shrink the existing action and ask about the night before rather than adding a foundation goal; one weekly integer (nights to bed within an hour of usual); refer for CBT-I and never ship sleep-hygiene tips, which were the control arm dCBT-I beat. **A / high.**

**P29. Health rules do not export to money, learning, career, creative or relationship goals.** Financial education explains 0.1% of behaviour variance; money moves on one structural change plus a **goal-naming** monthly reminder (+16% named, **0%** unnamed). Learning moves on retrieval (g ≈ 0.50) and spacing (g = 0.74), contradicting the four-days-a-week floor. Job-search intensity explains ~2% of offer variance and nothing of job quality; a rejection is not a lapse; completion-contingent rewards slightly *reduce* creative performance. **Adler:** route by taxonomy (habit-shaped / structural / session / campaign / dyadic) and log which rule is suspended and why. **A within domains / C transfer.**

**P30. Risk detection has a hard ceiling, and LLM detection decays with conversational depth.** Prediction sits near chance after 50 years; risk scales give PPV 5.5–16.1%. Kalinich 2026 (49 LLMs, 1.4M inferences): F1 fell with depth in every model family while clinicians stayed flat, and **restating the safety instruction recovered +0.12 F1**; models cannot grade intermediate risk, where a goal coach lives. Asking directly is safe. **Adler:** stateless classifier over the last 3 turns outside the coach's context, every turn including voice; recall-tuned stage 1; specificity from one ASQ-worded direct question; retire "are you safe right now?"; stop the goal work but stay in the conversation. **A/B / high.**

**P31. Goal text is itself a risk marker; suppress per goal, never label the user.** Restraint, negative affect and eating-as-affect-regulation expectancies spike immediately before anorexia onset — visible in goal text and taps. SCOFF is 86%/83%, so a positive is wrong four times in five at this base rate, and the male failure mode is muscularity-oriented (22% vs 5%) and invisible to it. **Adler:** checkable park rules on goal *text*; screens as openers, never scores; suppress numeric tracking **per goal** while keeping binary adherence. **A/C / high for the rule.**

### Tier 3 — directional, thin or contested; ship with a detector

**P32. Progress framing is stateful and changes the sign of the same count.** To-date motivates when commitment is uncertain, to-go when secure; progress framing licenses while commitment framing sustains; resetting helps after a weak period and hurts after a strong one — all single-lab and untested cross-culturally. **Adler:** what's done in weeks 1–2, after a lapse, or below confidence 7; what remains at automaticity ≥ 4/7 and ≥ 80% over 14 days; count a genuine baseline as progress ("3 of 4", not "0 of 4"); never fabricate progress, which is manipulation under EU AI Act Art. 5. **B / low-moderate, logged per user.**

**P33. Frame-shifting works conditionally, and only when actually executed.** Self-distancing strictly defined is **g = 0.19** with a prediction interval of −0.42 to 0.80 and GRADE low, rising to 0.44 when keyed to emotional meaning; a preregistered RCT found no benefit and worse sleep quality, and repeated third-person writing raised depressive symptoms. **Adler:** fire on a trigger, one per conversation, verify execution, close with one concrete if-then. **A of B / low-moderate.**

**P34. Streaks: unresolved, decided on judgement.** Seven consumer studies show a broken streak drives abandonment, worse when self-attributed and better when repairable; against them a ~60,000-student field RCT found streak-highlighting raised usage and achievement +0.13–0.17 SD while reporting nothing about behaviour after a break, and a direct adjudication (N = 7,004) is complete and unpublished. **Adler:** hold the no-streak default because the harm lands where the product exists to help; weekly count plus cumulative total; any opt-in goes behind an A/B with retention-after-first-miss as primary. **Conflict; revisit on publication.**

**P35. Commitment devices work for the minority who accept them.** Deposits are the only incentive surviving follow-up (RR 1.79 diet/weight, 1.39 later) and the least accepted (13.7%); externally imposed, evenly spaced deadlines outperform self-set ones. **Adler:** opt-in, user-initiated, capped, reversible, soft before hard; refusal logged as incentive preference, not low commitment; no coach-imposed penalties. **A / high.**

**P36. Fresh starts help restarts and hurt active pursuit.** Landmarks raise initiation intentions and future take-up (field RCT, N = 6,082), but making an *upcoming* landmark salient reduces effort now with no catch-up. **Adler:** time restarts and parked-goal returns to landmarks; never say "new month on Monday, good time to push" to an active user; if the user proposes "properly next month", ask what they already do daily and offer the two-minute version now. **B / moderate.**

*The psychotherapy layer is carried by P3, P5, P9–P12, P16, P19, P22, P27 and §5: alliance as task-and-goal consensus rather than bond (task and goal predict coaching outcomes, bond adds little; alliance rose without goal attainment in the generative-bot trial), ambivalence handled by not pushing, "should" goals detected by behaviour, identity reflected not assigned, scope limits from ICF/NBHWC, and the post-miss message as compassionate self-correction. Depth material contributes questions, never formulations.*

## 2. Resolved conflicts

**Self-monitoring positive vs negative.** Favour the experiment; nulls appear when both arms monitor, monitoring is subjective, or the log is offered and abandoned. The variable is *performed*, not *offered*.

**Technique count.** No dose-response (three nulls against Webb's β = 0.36); the confound is human contact. Fewer techniques, better delivered — accretion also costs time-to-use, the *first* split in van Genugten's meta-CART.

**JITAI size.** g = 1.65 vs 0.15 with PRISMA and risk-of-bias: favour 0.15. Adaptive timing is a hypothesis, not a claim.

**Notification decay.** Behaviour gone by day 28 vs app-opening stable over 30 days — not a contradiction; behaviour decays faster. Design for the behavioural curve.

**Feedback helpful or harmful.** 0.73 and 0.35 against IDEA's −2.4 kg from adding a wearable: interpretive feedback with a plan helps, ambient device feedback can displace the effort it was meant to support. No trial has manipulated tone.

**Praise.** Specific and informational is task-level and permitted; global and evaluative is meta-task and banned. The upgrade path from praise is **description**, not better-worded praise.

**Growth mindset.** d̄ = 0.05 (0.02 best-evidence) against 0.14/0.32 in targeted subgroups: ≈ 0 for unselected motivated adults. Keep the register, ship no module, cite no Dweck.

**Ego depletion.** d ≈ 0.3 with long manipulations vs ≈ 0.05 in two registered replications: no general resource, only an after-effect of long demands. Both camps imply the same product behaviour.

**Situational vs cognitive self-control.** Duckworth's hierarchy vs 14,067 ESM conflicts showing conflict-dependent efficacy: **fit over ranking** — ask situational first, then match to conflict type (initiation / persistence / inhibition). Never prescribe self-pressure, one of three strategies negatively associated with success.

**Extended care vs contact frequency.** Phase-specific: contact *during* initiation adds little (−0.3 kg ns); contact *after* it is the maintenance ingredient.

**Maintenance content vs contact.** Voils's positive trial against Cochrane's 81-trial null: attribute Voils to the telephone contact. Copy the delivery, not the curriculum.

**Coping planning.** Action-alone 0.41 vs combined 0.30 in the RCT-only pool: one coach-built if-then for the single most likely obstacle; a bank of contingencies dilutes the action plan.

**Identity direction.** Both directions have support, but intervention evidence for instilling identity is weak (g = 0.18) and the declaration hazard is real. Build from the record.

**Chronotype scheduling.** Synchrony appeared in 29 of 64 studies of 18–45s and ~50% are neutral types: cue reliability beats clock alignment; schedule to a peak only for strong types. Reverses an earlier draft.

**Implementation intentions universal vs backfiring.** 94 tests against two unreplicated studies where planning reduced progress for socially prescribed perfectionists: default *with a detector* — if planning feels like pressure, keep the cue, loosen the standard.

**Group-to-individual inference.** Fisher's "not accurate proxies" against Adolf & Fried's "sufficient, not necessary": priors with 2–4× wider uncertainty, remedied by randomisation and partial pooling.

**Repo self-contradictions, fixed here.** (a) "What's the point" is *sensitising context*, not a crisis trigger — the clinician-developed risk definition excludes exhaustion, discouragement and questioning life's meaning absent other indicators. (b) "Are you safe right now?" pulls for a reassuring answer and is a scored defect; replaced with ASQ wording. (c) "Do not resume goal coaching" is right, but refusal-and-silence adds two further defects: stop the goal work, continue the conversation. (d) The maintenance quote attributed to Kwasnicka 2016 is **not in the paper**; removed. (e) `safety-risk-detection.md` has **no IPV/abuse content** — close before any relationship goal ships. (f) D7's "project goals get a repeating work-on-it action" is right for session goals and wrong for structural money goals; replaced by the taxonomy.

## 3. The adaptive engine

**The arithmetic that governs it.** At a 70% base rate a 7-day rate has SE 17.3 pp and a weekly delta ~25–30 pp. Detecting a BCT-sized effect (+9.8 pp) within one person needs ~555 days per arm. Under the exact null P(week 2 > week 1) = 0.385, so a direction-only verdict manufactures **20 false personal rules per user-year**; "two consistent verdicts" 3.9; "same move ≥3 times, same direction" 1.0.

1. **Promotion is Bayesian, not binary.** Entries are `{move, context, P(helps me), posterior median pp, exposures, status}` with `status ∈ {observed, replicated, randomised}` — never `confirmed`. Default is a visible **"not enough data yet"**. Promote at P ≥ 0.90 AND median ≥ 5 pp AND ≥ 4 exposures per side. Retire "two consistent verdicts". Entries expire after 6 months unconfirmed.
2. **Partial pooling replaces per-user testing.** Population prior plus user-level random effect: one week of the user's own data moves an estimate ~10%, four weeks ~30%, eight weeks ~46% — correcting the maintenance file's "two slides beat the prior". Precedent: IntelligentPooling cut regret 26% by learning the *degree* of personalisation; Oralytics chose full pooling.
3. **False-discovery budget.** Σ(1 − Pᵢ) over promoted entries held below 1 per rolling 12 months, capping a playbook at five to eight entries a year — also the right number for a person to hold.
4. **Randomise what is free.** Two wordings, two cue times, review day, send-vs-hold, review opening side; record the seed. This is the only single-case design criterion Adler can fully satisfy, costs the user nothing, and is the only route to a defensible causal claim about Adler's own coaching.
5. **Pool first, personalise slowly, never on engagement.** HeartSteps' personalisation was real for time and location and **indistinguishable from noise for the engagement feature**. Personalise on barrier type, confidence, time of day, day of week, recent dosage, conflict type. One decision a day gives ~90 a quarter against HeartSteps' 450, which still pooled.
6. **Silence is an intervention and dosage is a cost.** Keep a per-user dosage score of coach-initiated messages decayed daily (λ = 0.95, from the model where larger recent dosage lowered both immediate effect and future reward). Cap coach-initiated messages at two a week beyond the check-in and review; above the user's own median, choose silence. DIAMANTE's random-message arm did **worse** than near-silence — the reason not to "keep in touch".
7. **Assume a 3–4 week half-life per message family:** ≥10 wordings per intervention type, no rationale repeated within 14 days, retire templates whose 7-day proximal effect is zero for two fortnights. LLM paraphrase as anti-habituation is untested.
8. **Hold on:** one week below the prior rate (ask, don't change), the first miss of a new action, high confidence with stable adherence, any week flagged unusual, any active user-named low window, and 28 days after a constraint check. **Change on:** two consecutive down weeks; ≤4 of 7 with a too-hard/no-energy/no-time barrier; confidence ≥2 below the user's trailing 8-week median; "forgot" twice in a week (re-cue, max twice per action per 8 weeks); the have-to signature plus a non-endorsing volition probe; two weeks of negative during-affect. One change per week, maximum.
9. **Void confounded periods.** Weekly after any change: "compared with a normal week for you, was this week unusual?" Illness, travel, a pause or a named window voids it. Weeks 1–2 of any new tap are inflated by reactivity (question-behaviour g ≈ 0.09–0.14, front-loaded) and are never a baseline.
10. **Guard against Goodhart and scale drift.** Never let the tap alone decide a change that alters the action's *size* — a shrink that raises the done-rate is uninformative by construction; pair every verdict with the GAS delta and the automaticity item plus one integrity question after any shrink. Because rulers recalibrate, use within-person relative cut-points (shrink at ≥2 below the trailing median or <5 absolute; grow at/above the median AND ≥8 AND the user asks), add a monthly fixed-anchor item with an invariant referent and a quarterly then-test; a ≥2-point divergence on two consecutive quarters means re-baseline, no trend line across the boundary, and coach the shift as information.
11. **Language.** Permitted: "both times you moved this to the morning, the week after was better — try it a third time?" Prohibited: "mornings work for you", "your data shows", "we've learned you're a morning person". Every playbook statement carries its exposure count in plain words.
12. **No per-user model before ~90 observations** — the point where a small within-person effect becomes detectable and below which idiographic networks have demonstrably low sensitivity. Before it, reason from the population prior plus stated context, and say so.

**Signals to store, all derivable from existing taps.** Per action: cue type, anchor and position, time of day, weekly enactment, SRBAI slope, during-affect trend, estimation ratio. Per user: barrier mix; `constraint_class ∈ {none, episodic, fixed}`; conflict type; planning response; evaluative sensitivity (adherence dip in the 72 h after any evaluative adjective → hard-lock to description); witness responsiveness (adherence after acknowledged vs unacknowledged check-ins); report latency; `failure_response_gain` (miss-opened vs success-opened reviews); `observance_windows` (recurring weekday clusters and multi-week regime shifts); maintenance signature (which of motives/self-regulation/resources/habit/environment moved first before a slide); lapse profile; false-positive tolerance on safety check-ins; restriction drift.

## 4. Individual-difference tailoring without diagnosing anyone

Trait tailoring buys r ≈ .07 and d ≈ .14; the one large trait-matching effect is advertising (+40% clicks), which EU AI Act Art. 5 forbids a coach; and labels are not homogeneous groups — within diagnosed ADHD, temporal, inhibitory and delay deficits form independent components whose co-occurrence is no greater than chance. **Store behavioural parameters, never categories.** If a user self-labels, accept it as their information, neither confirm nor question, and change nothing except through parameters.

1. **Externalisation need** (high "forgot") → object at the cue, one fixed slot, one visible next step. Still no cue-time push.
2. **Planning response.** If planning feels like pressure, or language carries *should / they expect / doesn't count unless*, or affect drops after a detailed plan: keep the cue, loosen the standard. Never remove the cue.
3. **Perfectionism is the highest-value adaptation available.** CBT-P gives concern-over-mistakes g = 0.89 and depression 0.60 at **mean age 23**, and modality did not moderate — online matched face-to-face. Coachable version only: define "good enough" at creation, count the minimum as done, treat a "partly" tap on a completed minimum as a standards signal, target concerns and keep strivings. Never sell "healthy perfectionism"; never deliver CBT-P as treatment.
4. **Delay sensitivity** → shorten the horizon to a daily unit with a visible immediate result; offer temptation bundling. Do not argue long-term benefits; that is the discounted quantity.
5. **Estimation ratio.** Ask for the parts and sum them before any estimate over an hour; after three dated steps with >50% underestimation, quote the user's own multiplier. Never a generic ×1.5, never "time blindness".
6. **Peak time** only for strong types; otherwise place the action where the cue is most reliable and let didn't-feel-like-it-by-hour find the window.
7. **Change tolerance.** High sensitivity → announce coach-proposed changes a day ahead, one at a time, literal wording, no novelty gamification.
8. **Load signature.** Adherence collapsing across *all* goals plus reported exhaustion is load, never willpower: shrink everything, propose rest, hold every build.
9. **Pessimism flag** (more con than pro reasons at goal formation; confidence <5 on every goal; goals that neither move nor get parked for three weeks) → WHO-5 and the referral script, while continuing to coach the smallest action.
10. **Self-criticism marker** from post-miss language ("I'm useless at this") → flip the template from problem-solving to self-compassion first; self-compassion mediated the procrastination–stress link in all four samples.
11. **User-named recurring low window** ("heavy week", "custody weekend", "night-shift block"). Inside it, low-energy and didn't-feel-like-it taps do not increment the shrink or revisit counters; the pre-agreed minimum is offered and the plan holds. Caps: 7 days per occurrence, one per 21 days; above ~a third of days it is a load signature. Store nothing physiological, infer nothing, predict nothing.
12. **Do not accept self-report as the outcome.** The strongest trial in exactly Adler's population moved self-reported study skills, time management and wellbeing and left GPA and credits flat. Confidence rising while completion does not is a warning, not a success.

## 5. The wisdom layer

Wise reasoning is a **state, not a trait**: within-person SD exceeds between-person SD on every facet, no individual-difference measure predicts it, and global wisdom self-reports load on socially desirable responding. Never assess it, never profile a "wise user", and fire these on triggers only — high distress or emotion-laden language; the same obstacle logged ≥3 times; an explicit quit question. One move per conversation, never stacked. **Verify execution** (32% executed the distanced condition wrongly in the one preregistered trial) and always close with one concrete if-then, because distancing raises construal level and advice runs idealistic.

- **Friend question** (drop/quit decisions): "Someone you respect has your exact situation, same week, same constraints. What do you tell them?" → "What part of that are you not doing?" → mandatory third turn: "Do you want that as this week's action? When and where?"
- **Year question** (after a bad week only): "A year from now, looking back at this week — what will you say about it?" then "What of this actually changes the thing you're building?" Never as "it doesn't matter"; the mechanism is impermanence, and the benefit appeared only for those who did badly.
- **Name/third person** (high-arousal rumination only): low-effort and physiologically replicated, unlike detached reappraisal. Cap written third-person reflection at ~1/week; never a daily default for a low-mood user.
- **Pre-mortem** (dated project ≥4 weeks out): "It's [deadline]. It didn't happen. Three reasons why" → convert one into an if-then. Never within 7 days of a lapse, never for low-confidence users or flagged self-critical perfectionists. Every pre-mortem study measures confidence or risk count, never completion.
- **Backward plan and unpack** for any time estimate — the rare debiasing move that raised motivation and performance as well as accuracy.
- **Personal reference class**, Adler's structural advantage: "Last three weeks you planned N and did M. Plan against that number?" The outside view with the user's own base rate, which sidesteps every criticism about thin or mismatched reference classes. Requires ≥3 comparable pairs.
- **Depth-borrowed, used once and sparingly:** "If this goal quietly disappeared, what would get easier?" (secondary gain, after three weeks of the same barrier and failed practical fixes); "If nobody ever knew you'd done it, would you still want it?" (should audit); "What's the version you'd be slightly embarrassed by but would actually do?" (how you shrink an action sized for the idealised self); "Part of you wants X, part wants Y — what does each need?" (two parts, one sentence, no origins, dropped the moment it produces distress rather than relief).

Prohibited: a cold "why do you feel that way" (immersed why produces rumination); "Will I?" interrogative self-talk (failed replication); detached-observer reappraisal in place of name/you framing; any distancing move in a neutral or positive state.

## 6. Never do

**Tone and feedback.** No person-level praise or evaluation ("amazing", "crushing it", "proud of you", "you're the kind of person who…"). No praise-as-grade. No percentiles, leaderboards, feeds, follower counts, shared streaks or comparison with other users. No cumulative deficit counts. No inflated modifier before a small number. No cheerleading, exclamation marks, emoji, motivational quotes, or vision-boarding without an obstacle step. No unsolicited mid-week summaries. No feedback surface whose passive consumption could substitute for the behaviour.

**Attribution and blame.** No "no excuses", "you just have to want it", "everyone has the same 24 hours", "make time", "if it mattered enough", "discipline", "commitment issue", "how badly do you want it", "unlock your potential", "transform". No reframing a named external demand as a limiting belief; no mindset intervention on a scheduling fact. No willpower or depletion explanation for a lapse. No "you broke the chain", guilt hooks, or "I missed you". No prescribing grit.

**Framing and claims.** No mood, anxiety, wellbeing, stress or mental-health claim. No longevity or gene-expression claims from purpose. No neuroscience in copy. No "21 days", no single days-to-habit number, no 1%/37× compounding, no 4%/85% flow figures. No "change your identity first"; no "forget goals, focus on systems". No promise that maintenance becomes automatic — it becomes cheaper. No diagnostic labels, including the user's own repeated back as fact. No "burnout" as a state the user is in. No inferred emotional state displayed. No prevalence figures in copy. No effect size attributed to a specific user.

**Mechanics.** No streak counters or "streak at risk" messaging. No points, badges, variable rewards, social rewards or coach-issued rewards; no expected tangible reward for an action the user already values. No coach-imposed penalties or deposits. No fabricated or endowed progress; no progress bars toward distant known-distance outcomes. No push notifications as cues; no sensor or calendar "smart timing". No extra messages to stay top of mind; no third re-engagement message per lapse; no fourth re-engagement on the same goal. No more than one plan change per adjustment; no third shrink of the same action. No buddy matching or assigned partners. No goal Adler generated; no goal the user has not said back in their own words. No stage-of-change routing; no personality quiz as a routing engine. No regulation-fit score, self-compassion score, positivity ratio, wheel-of-life or balance score. No gratitude journals, affirmation cards or one-off wellbeing exercises — nothing shorter than four weeks.

**Scope and safety.** Never diagnose, name or rule out a condition, or present a screen as a score. Never advise on medication, supplements, meal plans or exercise prescription. Never deliver a treatment protocol. Never claim or imply to be a therapist. Never coach a risk-marker goal. Never keep numeric tracking on a goal showing eating-disorder or compulsive-exercise signals. After a risk disclosure, never ask about method, plan or timing; never extract a promise not to self-harm; never terminate the conversation; never resume goal work in that session. Never present the coach's presence as body doubling or companionship. Never optimise on satisfaction, alliance or retention. Never store or infer cycle, hormonal or reproductive data. Never ask about culture, ethnicity, religion or values orientation. Never sell, share or target ads on user inputs.

## 7. What Adler should test with its own data

**n-of-1, randomised where possible.** Review opening side (success-first vs miss-first, alternating weeks ≥6) → `failure_response_gain`. To-date vs to-go over 14 days. One extra mid-week summary, once per user. Send vs hold on re-engagement and mid-week nudges. Two wordings of the same move. Cue change (enactment 7 days before vs after). Observance-aware schedule change vs the shrink rule. Whether the pre-agreed minimum inside a named low window protects the following week. Whether completion drops the day after drinking, and whether the exercise action predicts *other* goals' adherence next day — the per-user test of the keystone-habit claim the literature does not support.

**Cohort.** (1) **Is the tap or the coach the intervention?** Randomise a subset to tap-only for four weeks with the weekly review held constant — the most informative experiment in the product, because monitoring's d = 0.40 and the question-behaviour effect may exceed the adaptive value of g = 0.15. (2) **Does an AI witness reproduce the human-witness effect?** No trial isolates reported-to-a-machine vs to-a-person vs recorded-privately; acknowledged vs unacknowledged check-ins is the cheap version. (3) **Tone A/B:** description vs specific informational affirmation, 7-day adherence primary — no trial has ever manipulated feedback tone. (4) **Streaks:** consecutive-day opt-in vs weekly count plus cumulative total, primary outcome **retention after the first miss**. (5) **Shelve vs endorse** for obligation-tagged goals — decides whether want-to/have-to is a universal or a US undergraduate finding. (6) **Relational opener** (record-first vs one non-evaluative relational line) — the coldness test; a null retires a real worry cheaply. (7) Does the how-to slot beat record-plus-process alone over the next seven days, and does reviewing a success raise next week's adherence or only satisfaction? (8) Cost of a false-positive safety check-in on adherence and reply latency — unmeasured anywhere, and it sets the stage-1 threshold. (9) Retention after the first down week when rate is displayed vs a plain count. (10) Session count shown vs hidden for creative goals, self-rated quality at 8 weeks. (11) **Estimate the two variance parameters** (between-person SD of the done-rate, and of a move's true effect) from the first few hundred users — every promotion threshold in §3 depends on them and both are currently assumptions.

**Report everything split by `constraint_class` and by whether the constraint check ever fired.** Publishing only the average is how an intervention-generated inequality stays invisible. Expect ~20–25% dropout by week 8 and 3–5% 30-day retention at consumer baseline; a usage half-life of 5–10 days is the number to beat, not a failure.
