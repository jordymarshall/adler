# Individual learning: hypotheses, comparisons and decisions

Research date: 7 September 2026. Engineering recommendations, not a claim that these capabilities are implemented or that Adler has demonstrated efficacy. The [current Adler Method](../method/adler-method.md) governs; this note extends the reasoning behind its Define → Observe → Understand → Select → Design → Learn gates.

Read alongside the existing [idiographic inference](deep/idiographic-inference.md), [adaptive interventions](deep/adaptive-interventions.md), [self-regulation](deep/self-regulation-theory.md) and [maintenance](deep/maintenance-long-term.md) research. Their statistical proposals and historical numerical defaults are not runtime requirements. In particular, neither two favourable weeks, three repetitions, a universal washout nor an assumed autocorrelation establishes a personal causal effect.

## Main recommendation

Engineer **prospective, mechanism-informed learning with explicit decision rules** as the default. Reserve formal individual experiments for questions and circumstances that support their design. Keep three things separate:

- **What the person chooses:** keep, change, stop or defer an action or support.
- **What the coach thinks may explain the difficulty or improvement:** a scoped hypothesis informed by research and personal reports.
- **What the evidence actually establishes:** observations, an inconclusive comparison, a repeated association or a defensible experimental estimate.

A person can reasonably keep a useful, acceptable change without proving why it helped. Conversely, producing a hypothesis, accepting a proposal or completing a plan does not establish a mechanism or an effect on the ultimate goal. This separation is an Adler design recommendation derived from the methods below.

## 1. Start with the decision, then articulate the mechanism

The coach should first establish what decision better information would change. A reported preference can directly inform a plan; it need not become an experiment. A projection can remain arithmetic conditional on a chosen input; it need not become a causal hypothesis.

For a hypothesis worth pursuing, preserve this path:

**Dated observation → possible constraint → relevant mechanism → feasible change → expected near-term signal → behavioural outcome → ultimate goal.**

Each arrow has its own uncertainty. For example: the person says a book left near lunch helped them remember → a cue/setup explanation is plausible → leaving the book there is a feasible adjustment → notice whether starting is easier → record actual reading → separately record books finished. Improvement in pages read does not by itself prove cue salience caused it, or that books finished will rise proportionately.

This is a lightweight product translation of SOBC and CLIMBR, not an attempt to turn every check-in into a laboratory protocol. Capture a useful mechanism signal if it changes the decision and is practical to report. Otherwise mark the mechanism as unmeasured and keep the explanation tentative. Do not manufacture numerical psychological scales or call ordinary chat a validated assay.

In the existing method, Understand and Select should retain source IDs, synthesis principles, population and transfer limits, reported versus inferred barrier, and plausible alternatives. Design should state the specific change and prediction before feedback arrives. Learn should consider evidence against the explanation as well as evidence for it.

## 2. Choose a learning design that fits the question

These are proposed product modes, not evidence grades or a mandatory progression toward experiments.

| Mode | Appropriate use | What the result can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation or clarification | Unclear constraint, new goal, preference, sparse reports, an action not yet tried. | A dated report or better question. No intervention effect. |
| Prospective change with follow-up | A reasonable adjustment is useful now; there is no practical comparison or reversibility. | Whether the adjustment was used, what happened afterward and whether the person wants to continue. Causal attribution remains unresolved. |
| Planned repeated comparison | Comparable recurring opportunities and acceptable alternatives exist, but assignment is not randomized or important confounds remain. | A descriptive comparison scoped to its conditions. Repetition improves the record without automatically removing bias. |
| Designed single-case experiment | Repeatable outcomes, meaningful alternatives, acceptable assignment, sufficient opportunities and a defensible treatment of carryover/dependence are available. | An inference bounded by the actual design, measurement and analysis. Requires implemented analytical support before causal language. |

A repeated crossover can be inappropriate when learning persists, a setup change cannot sensibly be reversed, or withdrawing useful support would be unacceptable. Other single-case designs, such as staggered introduction across suitable independent streams, can address some irreversible changes; they need deliberate replication and analysis. [Single-case design guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6955662/). Several unrelated goals do not automatically constitute independent baselines. Do not force a crossover to make the product look rigorous.

Randomization describes assignment, not evidential strength by itself. Its proposed timing, alternatives and practical implications must be intelligible and acceptable to the person. A formal experiment needs its own design and inference support; casual observation must not be silently relabelled after favourable results. For future repeated-crossover protocols, [SPENT 2019, published 2020](https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.m122), supplies the prospective reporting counterpart to CENT. It is protocol guidance, not an efficacy grade or a required questionnaire for ordinary coaching.

## 3. Route by goal type and by the particular intervention

This mapping applies the current method's P29 route. It is a design heuristic grounded in the methodological conditions above, not a validated taxonomy of experimental efficacy. A campaign can contain repeatable sessions; choose at the level being changed.

| Goal route | Controllable measure and learning question | Appropriate timing and design boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habit-shaped | Cue opportunity, whether action started, actual amount, practical effort; does this setup fit recurring situations? | Review after relevant opportunities and feedback. Repetition is useful; acquired cue learning can carry over and defeat an assumed reversal. No universal days-to-habit or weekly verdict. |
| Structural | Whether the chosen setup was installed and remains available; did it remove the reported obstacle? | Verify installation, then observe the first relevant use and material context changes. Do not invent daily completion for a persistent change. |
| Session / skill | Sessions actually attempted, meaningful output and task-appropriate quality or learning feedback. | Allow practice and feedback latency; improving skill and varying task difficulty complicate comparisons. Compare reversible session supports only when tasks and carryover permit. Time spent is not skill mastery. |
| Campaign | User-chosen controllable steps, exposure dates and later responses/outcomes. | Keep execution feedback separate from lagged external results. Applications can be completed without offers; focused work can occur without revenue. A same-week input/outcome ratio is not a causal conversion rate. |
| Dyadic / uncertain | The user's action and boundary; separately reported interaction or response. | Learn around relevant interactions. Another person's response is neither adherence nor an input the user controls. Changes may affect both parties and future interactions, so independence and reversibility cannot be assumed. |

For money or complex professional goals, behavioural evidence may inform starting, attention, planning or recovery from setbacks. It does not select technical business strategy or supply an optimal hours quota. Delayed outcomes and external events require domain assumptions to stay visible; a sparse association cannot become a forecast of causal revenue returns.

## 4. Give each question its own clocks and review rule

An action opportunity, a report, an intervention decision, a review and an outcome observation are different events. The goal deadline is another constraint. This operational distinction follows the JITAI separation of proximal/distal outcomes and decision timing, combined with the method's flexible cycles.

Before starting a change, record:

- **Opportunity and exposure:** when the relevant situation occurs; what counts as actually trying the change.
- **Earliest useful feedback:** when the near-term signal and later goal outcome could reasonably appear; unknown delays remain explicit.
- **Review trigger:** useful exposures plus available feedback, a chosen date, a changed constraint or a user-requested review. The date prompts a review of evidence; it does not force a verdict.
- **Practical decision rule:** what would justify continuing, adjusting, asking one consequential question, pausing or ending; what burden or problem warrants stopping early.

Keep four temporal issues separate: **feedback delay** means the result is not yet observable; **carryover** means an earlier intervention still affects later periods; **dependence** means successive measurements are related; **context drift** means the situation or task has changed. Dropping two days or setting a default correlation does not solve all four.

For example, a person can assess whether a calendar adjustment made the next work session feasible before a client responds to earlier work. Adler should update the feasibility question while the outcome question remains pending. It must not treat unreturned client responses as zeros or punish execution because a delayed result has not arrived.

## 5. Make the record comparable without increasing reporting burden

The following are engineering recommendations, not a prescribed user questionnaire:

- Preserve the action definition, amount/unit, relevant context, plan version and outcome measurement definition in force when an observation occurred.
- Distinguish proposed, scheduled, attempted and reported exposure. Calendar presence supports context; it does not establish completion. Repeated reports through different channels are one observation, with provenance and corrections.
- Keep explicit misses separate from missing reports and unavailable opportunities. Retain denominators and exclusions; do not silently remove difficult periods to improve a result.
- Preserve measurement changes. A five-page fallback can improve action completion while reducing total pages; report both rather than conclude that the old and new completion rates measure the same work.
- Record concurrent changes and cross-goal demands when relevant. Several simultaneous changes may be necessary for a usable plan; then evaluate the bundle and avoid attributing the result to one component.
- Preserve original predictions and later revisions. New hypotheses generated from results are exploratory, not predictions successfully tested by the same results.

## 6. Review in a fixed reasoning order, with flexible timing

This proposed sequence makes Learn auditable without introducing fixed thresholds:

1. **Was the change actually tried?** If not, learn about feasibility or preference; do not reject its behavioural mechanism.
2. **Is the expected feedback available and interpretable?** If missing, immature or measured differently, state the limitation and resolve only what is answerable.
3. **What happened to the proposed mechanism, the action and the goal outcome?** Separate these levels; leave unmeasured links unresolved.
4. **What else changed?** Consider context, task difficulty, other interventions, reporting and plausible competing explanations.
5. **What should happen next?** Keep, adjust, clarify, pause or close, with a practical reason and evidence links. An acceptable change can be kept with uncertain attribution.
6. **What remains worth learning?** Narrow the next question if it matters; otherwise stop collecting. Do not produce a new hypothesis merely to keep the loop animated.

Suggested evidence descriptions include insufficient feedback, consistent with the prediction, mixed, or inconsistent with the prediction under these conditions. Keep these separate from workflow status and the decision to continue. A null-looking sparse comparison is not proof of no effect; favourable reports are not confirmation of a universal personal rule.

Stopping because of burden, lack of usefulness, a changed goal or an unsuitable intervention is a legitimate decision. Preserve the reason and available results. Formal statistical stopping would additionally require a specified analysis; repeated informal peeking must not generate significance or probability claims.

## 7. Prioritize learning by its usefulness to the person

Before asking for extra information, the coach should be able to identify which plausible answer would change its recommendation, why that choice matters, whether the answer can arrive soon enough, and whether the burden is justified. Use existing context to make a question easier, while keeping its source and uncertainties clear. If the answer would not change the decision, do not collect it just to complete a framework field.

This is a **qualitative value-of-information heuristic**, informed by JITAI burden considerations and MRC's focus on key uncertainties and resource consequences. It is not a computed optimum, expected utility or information-gain score. No source here establishes an ideal maximum number of active hypotheses. Prioritize within the person's shared capacity, allow questions to remain parked, and prefer fewer simultaneous changes when that preserves both usefulness and interpretability.

As a plan becomes sustainable, review whether effort, satisfaction, context and support needs have changed. Maintenance is a different question from initial uptake. Do not require perpetual measurement to keep a successful plan, declare automaticity from adherence, or expire every personal inference after an arbitrary interval.

## 8. Bound cumulative learning and verify the implementation

A useful accumulated insight should retain **what was learned, where it applied, the contributing reports, competing explanations, the practical planning implication and what would cause reconsideration**. Reuse across goals requires a plausible shared mechanism and relevant context; it remains a transfer hypothesis. A travel-related observation about reading is not a diagnosis of how the person works everywhere.

The following scenarios should be explicit acceptance cases for the engineering design:

| Scenario | Required behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Two favourable sessions after a change | Record the result; keep personal attribution tentative. |
| Same report arrives through web and text | Preserve one exposure and its provenance, not two replications. |
| User changes action size midway | Preserve both versions and actual quantities; qualify comparisons. |
| Structural adjustment is installed once | Verify use/persistence; do not create recurring fabricated adherence. |
| Action executed, outcome still delayed | Credit the action and leave outcome assessment pending. |
| Multiple changes and a better result | Evaluate the bundle; do not choose a component as the proven cause. |
| User declines or stops a test | Preserve choice and reason; no inferred low motivation or failed mechanism. |
| A previously helpful setup stops fitting | Revisit context and scope; retain history without asserting a permanent trait. |
| Research source is real but applies to a different mechanism/population | Keep the transfer limitation; reject unsupported rationale despite valid IDs. |

Engineering evaluation should distinguish faithful record handling, sound methodological decisions, user comprehension and actual behavioural benefit. Passing schema validation or producing readable explanations establishes neither valid personal causality nor coaching efficacy. Bayesian pooling, calibrated uncertainty, automated causal discovery and formal sequential testing remain separate research and validation work, not implied capabilities of this design.
