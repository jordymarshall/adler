# Landing headline copy

Researched 7 September 2026. This note recommends copy; it does not record an A/B test or a measured conversion result.

## Recommendation

Use the user's proposed headline:

> Reach your goals with a system that understands you.

Keep the existing subheadline:

> Adler turns your goals into manageable actions, then uses behavioural science and your check-ins to adapt the system as you go.

This is an editorial recommendation informed by the guidance below. The headline leads with the desired outcome, then makes personal fit the reason to consider Adler. The subheadline explains the actual work: actions, check-ins and adaptation. Together they carry both the benefit and the mechanism without asking the headline to explain the whole product.

“Understands you” is a warmer and clearer expression of personal relevance than the current “learns with you,” which leaves the object of learning ambiguous. It is still broad: the surrounding demonstration must make that understanding concrete through reported circumstances and changes to the plan. Under the [Adler Method](../method/adler-method.md), it means learning from the user's reports and revising tentative explanations. It does not mean already knowing the person, inferring a diagnosis or proving a personality trait.

## Guidance behind the choice

- **Explain the offer and its value immediately.** Nielsen Norman Group recommends a concise, descriptive homepage tagline that communicates what the service does, what users can accomplish and why its offering is relevant. Its guidance also calls for representative examples. For Adler, the headline/subheadline pair and real app demonstration should be assessed together. [Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/)
- **Put useful information first.** NN/g's later eye-tracking research continues to find substantial scanning behavior. It recommends front-loading information, clear headings and plain language. Keeping “Reach your goals” first gives the reader an immediate purpose; the second clause supplies the distinction. This application is our interpretation, not a comparison the researchers tested. [How People Read Online: New and Old Findings](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-people-read-online/)
- **Prefer concrete, restrained claims.** Morkes and Nielsen's original controlled writing study compared concise, scannable and objective prose with promotional writing. Its usability findings support avoiding inflated language; they do not establish that one Adler headline will attract more customers. [Concise, Scannable, and Objective](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/)
- **Use familiar words and active phrasing.** GOV.UK's writing guidance recommends the user's language, specific explanations, short sentences and active voice. It is government service guidance, so we transfer its clarity principles rather than treating its tone as a marketing template. [Use clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)

## Two alternatives

| Headline | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- |
| Reach your goals with a system built around you. | Plain and direct about personal fit. Less expressive about continuing to learn, which the existing subheadline explains. |
| Reach your goals with a system that adapts to your life. | Most explicit about observable product behavior. Less personal than “understands you,” but directly supported by changing actions and schedules. |

The user's proposal is the preferred brand expression. The second alternative is the clearest functional expression. No source reviewed establishes a winning phrase or an optimal word count for this product.

## Comprehension check

Before interpreting a signup-rate difference, ask prospective users what Adler does, how the system becomes personal, and what they would need to contribute. They should describe goals becoming actions and check-ins informing changes. If “understands you” creates an expectation of instant knowledge or general emotional companionship, the headline needs the more explicit adaptation wording. This is a proposed check, not research already conducted with Adler users.
