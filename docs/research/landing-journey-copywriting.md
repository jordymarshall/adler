# Landing journey copywriting

Researched 9 September 2026. This note informed the portfolio story, which the user subsequently chose to keep inside the phone walkthrough. It records writing guidance and editorial applications, not an Adler comprehension study, conversion result or change to product behavior.

## The writing decision

The user's accepted promise is the organizing idea: “You bring what you want to achieve. Adler figures out how to help you get there.” Their stated unmet need is expert judgment: knowing what to do, receiving useful help without first diagnosing the problem, and having that help carry through into everyday use. The copy should make that experience concrete. These are direct inputs from this conversation, not evidence that every prospective customer shares the same motivation.

## Guidance and its application

### 1. Start with the customer's actual alternative

Ash Maurya advises positioning against what the customer currently uses to get the job done. His pitch sequence identifies that existing approach, exposes its relevant limitation, then demonstrates the proposed change. This is practitioner guidance, illustrated with product pitches; it is not a controlled comparison of landing pages. [Source](https://ashmaurya.com/blog/position-against-your-true-competition-to-win-the-customer)

**Application:** The user liked their completion grid. Preserve that recognizable benefit while making its unanswered question visible: what should happen next? Do not caricature tracking as worthless or replace the specific unmet need with a broad accusation that people lack discipline.

### 2. Demonstrate the smallest transformation that explains the value

Maurya's demo guidance starts with the customer's situation, desired outcome and current workaround. He recommends showing the minimum needed to explain how the new approach closes the gap; breadth can obscure the value proposition. His account is advisory and includes a founder anecdote, not general proof that a particular demo format converts best. [Source](https://ashmaurya.com/blog/the-art-of-the-demo)

**Application:** Carry one recognizable situation through the section. Make the revealing moment Adler bringing a useful recommendation the person would not have known to request. Goals, habits, memory, learning, texting and calendar connections can support that experience without becoming six independent sales pitches.

### 3. Make the reader able to picture the claim

Joanna Wiebe's specificity tutorial recommends concentrating on one main idea, replacing compressed abstractions with details that readers can imagine in their own lives, and checking whether the reader must infer the point. She also places visuals where they explain the message. This is practitioner guidance with a reported client example; the example does not isolate specificity from the other edits or establish an effect for Adler. [Source](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-be-specific/)

**Application:** Terms such as intelligent, personal and adaptive need observable support. Pair each important claim with something the coach notices, asks, recommends or follows up on. A visualization should answer what changed and why that helps. It should do more than decorate an abstract promise.

### 4. Make the story easy to scan without stripping out its meaning

Morkes and Nielsen compared writing styles on a tourism website in 1997. Concise and scannable versions improved several task measures; the combined concise, scannable and objective version performed better than the promotional control on the reported measures. Outcomes included task performance, recall and satisfaction. The study did not measure purchases or establish an optimal length for contemporary animated landing pages. [Source](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/)

**Application:** Let the headings communicate the basic argument, with one short paragraph per scene. Cut repetition and inflated claims. Retain enough concrete detail to explain the promise. This reconciles scan-friendly writing with Wiebe's warning that making every message shorter can erase its meaning.

### 5. Treat customer language as input, then check the interpretation

Wiebe recommends using customer language to develop copy and checking how readers interpret the resulting messages. She distinguishes hearing useful material from interpreting it correctly. Her validation tutorial is a practical process with acknowledged limitations, not a guarantee of unbiased research or improved conversion. [Source](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-validate-your-copy/)

**Application:** Preserve the force of the user's statement that Adler should supply the expertise. Do not soften it into another general coaching slogan. The draft remains an editorial proposal; later reader feedback could reveal whether it communicates useful initiative or accidentally promises omniscience.

## 10 September: YC guidance and the connections chapter

Michael Seibel advises describing what the product does in simple language and, where helpful, walking through the user’s path. He recommends removing jargon, acronyms and ambiguous marketing terms and checking whether a reader can explain the description back. This is founder pitch guidance, not a controlled landing-page study. [How to Pitch Your Company, Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/) (19 July 2016; accessed 10 September 2026).

Paul Graham describes using ordinary words and simple sentences, then editing to remove unnecessary complexity. His essay is writing advice, not evidence about Adler’s conversion rate. [Write Simply](https://www.paulgraham.com/simply.html) (March 2021; accessed 10 September 2026).

**Editorial application:** Preserve the user’s approved story. Make the extra chapter name a recognizable action and its benefit:

> **Bring Adler into the apps you already use.**
>
> Connect your calendar so Adler can plan around your day. Work on the same goals from compatible assistants, with your plan and check-ins already there.

The headline avoids making MCP the value proposition. The phone names the actual calendar and MCP setup paths; ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Apple Health appear under planned connections. Availability comes from `src/integration-catalog.ts` and the personal-token runtime, not the writing sources. This is an editorial choice informed by the guidance; it is not a claim that the authors endorse this wording or that the copy has passed a comprehension study.

## Review criteria for the proposed section

These are editorial checks derived from the guidance and this conversation, not externally validated rules:

- The headings alone express one coherent customer experience.
- The decisive scene shows useful coaching judgment, rather than asking the person to diagnose themselves or administer an experiment.
- The visual makes the claim more specific and understandable.
- Texting has a meaningful place in that experience, because the user explicitly identified it as important.
- Product claims and illustrative interactions are checked against current behavior separately; the writing sources cannot establish Adler's capabilities.
- No source above establishes a winning headline, a required number of scenes or a conversion improvement for this draft.
