# YC and Paul Graham principles for the Adler landing page

Researched 10 September 2026 against the authors' published pages. These are practitioner judgments, not controlled evidence about Adler's conversion rate or endorsements of Adler. The applications below are editorial hypotheses for the landing-page audit; they do not establish product capabilities or customer demand.

## 1. Name a problem someone urgently wants solved

**Source:** Graham distinguishes a small group with strong present demand from a broad audience with mild hypothetical interest. He recommends investigating problems users actually have. [Paul Graham, *How to Get Startup Ideas*](https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html), November 2012, especially “Problems” and “Well.”

**Adler application:** Start with the user who has a specific goal and repeatedly fails to carry out the plan. Describe a recognizable interruption or failure. General aspirations about a better life do not establish a reason to buy now. This proposed audience still needs validation with prospective customers.

## 2. Say what the product does before asking visitors to decode it

**Source:** Seibel recommends a simple company description, an ordinary-language walkthrough when helpful, and removing ambiguous marketing terms. His email exercise checks whether another person can explain the description back unaided. [Michael Seibel, *How to Pitch Your Company*](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/), 19 July 2016, questions 1 and “Improving Your Answers.”

**Adler application:** A visitor should be able to say what Adler is, what they give it, and what it does for them from the opening alone. Test comprehension before debating whether a headline sounds distinctive. This source concerns investor pitches; its application to customer copy is an inference.

## 3. Put customer value before the machinery delivering it

**Source:** Blackappl separates customer value from enabling technology and proposes a landing hierarchy covering value, delivery, workflow, and the action available now. [Dominika Blackappl, *Practical Design: Messaging*](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/practical-design-messaging), 8 August 2016.

**Adler application:** Explain the practical improvement first: a workable next action and a plan that changes when attempts fail. Agent, harness, memory, and methodology explanations earn space only when they clarify that improvement or resolve a credibility question. Keep scientific depth available without making it a prerequisite to understanding the offer.

## 4. Make the difference concrete enough to demonstrate

**Source:** Seibel's differentiation question asks for a specific insight about the problem, derived from customer understanding or experience, rather than a vague assertion that an existing category needs improvement. [Michael Seibel, *How to Pitch Your Company*](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/), 19 July 2016, question 4.

**Adler application:** Demonstrate what happens after a missed action: context is clarified, a specific change is proposed, its use is reviewed, and the plan is updated from that evidence. This is a candidate explanation of Adler's value, not proof that competitors lack it. Verify every depicted operation against the current product.

## 5. Show the strongest product evidence early

**Source:** Graham advises designing for casual visitors, explaining the product concisely, putting impressive material on the front page, and combining explanation with demonstration. [Paul Graham, *The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn*](https://www.paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html), April 2006, section 3.

**Adler application:** Let a short, legible product sequence establish the value before requiring a long tour. Additional screens should answer new purchase questions. A polished fictional example can explain behavior; it cannot substitute for customer results or prove effectiveness.

## 6. Keep only information needed to take the next step

**Source:** Hale uses a hypothetical single-button page to ask what information helps or prevents action. He places the call to action near the experience that makes the product's value clear. [Kevin Hale, YC conversion and pricing lecture recap](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-7-recap-kevin-hale-on-conversion-rates-and-pricing/), 16 October 2019, 03:57–07:51.

**Adler application:** Give each section one identifiable job in the purchase decision. Remove duplicated benefits, parallel stories, and optional detail that interrupts the decision. Bring the primary CTA back when the demonstration has made the offer understandable. The source does not prescribe an exact page length or number of sections.

## 7. Build visual character from a few deliberate elements

**Source:** Graham argues that strong design depends on carefully chosen structure, solves the right human problem, and can use ornament without hiding weak substance. [Paul Graham, *Taste for Makers*](https://www.paulgraham.com/taste.html), February 2002.

**Adler application:** Use consistent typography, spacing, color roles, and product framing. Keep distinctive imagery or motion when it directs attention or makes a change understandable. Do not require visitors to traverse decoration to reach the product or price. Simplicity does not require an anonymous visual identity.

## 8. Resolve the actual objections to buying

**Source:** Hale checks product identity, audience fit, credibility, other users, price and conditions, and access to help. He specifically criticizes withholding cost or an explanation of a free offer. [Kevin Hale, YC conversion and pricing lecture recap](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-7-recap-kevin-hale-on-conversion-rates-and-pricing/), 16 October 2019, 07:51–11:14.

**Adler application:** State the real offer, price, billing terms, access conditions, and what happens after clicking. Use only genuine customer evidence and available capabilities. If users cannot purchase today, the CTA must describe the actual available action. Better wording alone cannot establish willingness to pay.

## How to use this evidence

For each existing line, visual, and interaction, identify the purchase question it answers and the evidence supporting it. Keep it, rewrite it, move it to optional detail, or remove it. Treat a shorter coherent story as a testable recommendation: check unaided comprehension and observe qualified visitors moving through signup and payment. None of these sources establishes Adler's winning copy, an obligatory visual style, a target conversion rate, or a guaranteed sales improvement.
