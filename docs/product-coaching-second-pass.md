# Product and behavioural-science review of Adler's next iteration

8 September 2026 · Accepted product direction. Implementation and effectiveness require separate verification.

The user accepted the six changes in the next-iteration proposal for All Goals A and Goal C, then accepted this further joint product and behavioural-science pass. Those choices remain the foundation: compact goal rows, a current plan with visible action lanes, the existing progress/projection graphs, contextual controls, an inspectable evolving plan and consistent mobile hierarchy. An integrated disposable A/C prototype now illustrates the next experience; its checks and remaining limits are recorded in the [prototype archive](https://github.com/jordymarshall/adler/tree/prototype/goal-views-integrated/docs/prototypes/goal-views-integrated). The [structure status](product-structure-status.md) records subsequent decisions. This document defines accepted direction, not delivered production capability or demonstrated efficacy.

Read with the [governing principles](product-principles.md), [current method](method/adler-method.md), [engineering design](method/coaching-engineering.md) and [evidence review](research/coaching-product-evidence-review.md). The evidence review distinguishes primary findings, transfer limits, existing policy and new design judgments. The recommendations below are design hypotheses, even where informed by strong research.

## 1. The product must answer two different learning questions

Adler helps the person find a feasible way of doing meaningful work toward a goal, and learns whether that work is producing the intended result. This sharpens the existing direction rather than adding a second coach or product.

| Learning question | What the coach needs to understand | What the user should experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What helps me do useful work in my actual life? | Opportunity, chosen work, starting/execution, useful quantity or quality, reported effort, competing demands and preferences | A manageable plan, a relevant adjustment when needed, and a clear way to report or correct experience |
| Is that work moving me toward my goal? | Separately reported or observed results, plausible delay, measurement meaning and the uncertain input–outcome relationship | Progress with a useful implication, an honest conditional outlook where supported, and a plan to learn when the relationship is unclear |

If the behaviour is itself the goal, these questions can largely coincide. If the goal is revenue, mastery or another person's response, they can diverge substantially. Minutes worked do not establish mastery; adherence does not establish revenue. A smaller action can improve completion while reducing useful work. Preserve the action's changing quantity/criterion and the actual outcome through every revision.

Do not define success as a composite score of adherence, streaks, output and burden. Choose meaningful goal-specific measures with the person, then use burden and other priorities as constraints and context. An acceptable steady routine, changing the timetable, and reconsidering a goal can each be appropriate. P9–P12, P24 and P29 and the method's Define/Design/Learn gates inform this position; they do not establish one universal optimum.

## 2. Make the next useful decision the coach's organizing task

The existing method already requires Define → Observe → Understand → Select → Design → Learn. Make its practical discrimination an explicit evaluation requirement. The following are internal reasoning distinctions, not five reporting fields or five new UI boxes.

| Reported situation | First useful question or response | Misleading shortcut to prevent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An expected report is missing | Establish what happened, if knowing would change the next decision | Calling it a missed action, assuming low motivation, or fabricating zero input |
| The work window was unavailable | Examine the actual constraint and a feasible change to opportunity or priorities | Adding reminders for an opportunity that does not exist |
| The opportunity existed but work did not start or continue | Use the person's account and relevant research to distinguish a useful mechanism to explore | Diagnosing a barrier from the completion graph alone |
| Work was completed but output/result did not improve | Check whether useful work, measurement and feedback timing fit the chosen goal; clarify the user's strategy where needed | Increasing work automatically or inventing domain expertise |
| Work is useful and acceptable to maintain | Understand relevant successful conditions, preserve the setup and consider lighter support | Raising difficulty or generating another experiment just to show activity |

This is an application of the current framework, including COM-B's capability/opportunity/motivation concepts. [The original COM-B paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42) supplies a design framework, not proof of any particular personal explanation. Several conditions may coexist. Ask the smallest consequential question; do not require the user to complete the taxonomy.

Use the whole person's goals and commitments when choosing a change. Calendar gaps are context, not proof of usable capacity or performed work. Show the actual tradeoff when moving work between goals; do not silently solve one goal by overloading another. Preserve user priorities and make a deadline's flexibility explicit.

## 3. Treat experimentation as disciplined learning with a practical purpose

The central learning question is: **Which uncertainty is worth resolving because another answer could change this person's plan?**

Use the least burdensome adequate response. A confirmed preference or a factual clarification may settle the decision. A prospective adjustment may be worthwhile with a tentative explanation. A repeated comparison needs comparable opportunities and tolerable alternatives. Formal causal experimentation needs a suitable implemented design and analysis; ordinary before/after coaching does not acquire those properties by being called a test.

For a substantive adjustment, the saved decision should establish the following before collecting confirming experience:

- The attributable observation and plausible alternatives.
- The particular research claim or concept that informs the interpretation, including its role and limits.
- The exact change, useful work it preserves, and why it fits the person's circumstances.
- What experience would inform the practical keep/change decision, what remains unmeasured, and when feedback could be useful.
- When to stop or revise because the change is impractical, unwanted or burdensome.

These largely extend the existing decision/test records. Do not build a parallel experimental engine simply to name the same concepts again. Preserve original predictions, accepted versus pending revisions, and contrary or corrected evidence. New observations that generate an explanation cannot also be presented as independent prospective confirmation of that explanation.

At review, separate three judgments: whether the arrangement is worth continuing; what the evidence suggests about its proposed mechanism; and whether the goal outcome changed. A helpful arrangement can be kept while its mechanism remains uncertain. If several changes were needed together, evaluate the bundle rather than attributing its result to one component.

Prioritize learning across goals using the decision's importance, likely practical benefit, feasibility and reporting burden. This is a qualitative product heuristic, not a computed information-gain optimum. Do not impose one active test per goal, one global maximum or a universal trial duration. The [individual-learning methodology](research/individual-learning-methodology.md) and [JITAI design principles](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364076/) support attention to feedback timing and assessment burden; they do not establish those numerical policies.

## 4. Separate action timing, review timing and outcome timing

A goal can take a day or years. Plan near-term work at the granularity needed to act, retain meaningful later milestones, and keep uncertain distant work provisional. The next review is chosen for the available opportunities, expected feedback and decision at stake. An unexpected constraint or user request can justify an earlier review.

For example, whether a writing window remains available may be learned before a manuscript's quality can be assessed. Whether business work was carried out may be known before customer responses or revenue arrive. A date passing makes a review due; it does not make the evidence conclusive.

In the existing timeline, distinguish an action occurrence, milestone target/scenario, change introduced and review marker by their role and label. Use spatial position and date selection to connect them. Do not make the user operate separate cycle settings to obtain sensible defaults. Choosing or editing those defaults remains possible through the same goal and Coach.

## 5. Let the evidence determine how much authority a projection gets

Keep the user's approved graphs and full forward scenario range. The underlying deterministic tool and every surface must agree on the meaning of the estimate.

| Situation | Appropriate presentation |
| --- | --- |
| Direct relationship and an assumed future plan | Conditional arithmetic: “If you read this amount…”; show the chosen input and conversion assumptions |
| Comparable reported work with sufficient coverage for the stated purpose | A recent-pace scenario with visible period/coverage, remaining assumptions and a range |
| Sparse, delayed or confounded outcome association | Attributable input and result observations; an exploratory scenario only when defensible and clearly subordinate to its limitations |
| No defensible relationship yet | Useful work, actual results and the next informative result check; no invented finish date |

Neither a fixed number of intervals nor a wide coloured fan establishes predictive validity. For a consequential revenue forecast, assess comparability, lag, missingness, changes in the work and other plausible contributions before giving a central date prominence. If those are unresolved, a descriptive relationship can remain inspectable without becoming the headline finish estimate. Do not infer causation from co-trending cumulative hours and revenue.

The current code already has safeguards: explicit unknowns, excluded-period reasons, incompatible-measure checks, separate direct/learned models and heuristic range labels. Concrete review findings in [goal-projection.ts](../shared/goal-projection.ts) are narrower: pace uses complete seven-day blocks within up to 56 days; fewer than two complete weeks uses the future plan with a ±50% sensitivity range; one eligible positive learned interval can generate an early central date. Those are operational heuristics, not established adequacy rules for every goal. Dropping incomplete weeks may make the remaining record unrepresentative even when the omission is disclosed. [projection-model.ts](../shared/projection-model.ts) also selects one driver action; it does not represent a general multi-input outcome model.

The next engineering pass should make these limits affect eligibility and visual prominence, and choose aggregation appropriate to the relevant decision. Do not replace them with another universal sample threshold or immediately commission an opaque prediction model. A future calibrated forecasting track needs goal-family-specific prospective validation against simple baselines, including missingness and context change.

When previewing a plan change, show its actual schedule, quantity, capacity and any supported conditional implication in the existing view. Accepting a timing suggestion does not demonstrate improved adherence or update observed results. Keep historical reports intact; distinguish a changed future-work scenario from a changed estimate based on new observed evidence.

## 6. Make learning visible through changes to the person's plan

All Goals remains a stable overview of both useful progress and decisions worth attention. Goal C remains the place to understand and act on a goal. Insights remains the existing cross-goal view of what Adler is learning, with the same underlying records and links.

In Goal C, lead with current milestone, current work and the relevant outlook. Annotate the existing dated evidence with meaningful changes. Selecting a change reveals the short explanation and its saved research basis. Earlier revisions show their own actions and reports; the current plan is always easy to return to.

Useful learning wording answers: **what changed, what happened when it was tried, and what Adler recommends now.** Scientific depth opens from that explanation. A tentative inference remains tentative; a pending review does not display a completed result. Cross-goal reuse names the original context and why the suggestion may transfer. It never creates a broad personal trait from one goal's experience.

One concrete production wording gap: [learningStatus](../shared/learning.ts) can return “Trying now” from agreement and dates without establishing reported use. The review schema separately retains exposure, but the headline can imply more than the record supports. Prefer a planned/ready-to-try description until use is established, and then show actual reported experience. Exact labels should be comprehension-tested. Keep workflow, evidence standing and practical decision distinct without exposing three administrative status systems to the user.

The prototype's selected-state and hierarchy repairs remain first: reveal other current actions, fix historical/current control context, then reduce repeated copy. [Linear's milestone interaction](https://linear.app/docs/project-milestones) illustrates contextual filtering into the same project; it is a useful design precedent, not evidence of behavioural efficacy. [Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) supports deferring secondary detail, while current work, consequential uncertainty and acceptance effects remain visible.

## 7. Make starting, reporting and returning feel worthwhile

Onboarding already starts with one goal description. Preserve it. The first useful response should provide a provisional action/plan when the available context supports one, selected tracking, a meaningful next checkpoint and any consequential missing question. It must not fabricate personal knowledge or require a baseline waiting period before useful work can begin. A user-owned strategy may need clarification for a complex goal.

The first interaction after reporting should establish what was saved and what it affects. Exact controls use the existing deterministic command path; substantive interpretation uses the shared reviewed coach. Do not block a simple structured report on optional scientific exposition. If interpretation is still processing or fails, make the saved-report state truthful. Measure latency and unnecessary questions rather than adding speculative processing infrastructure.

When something goes wrong, preserve accumulated work, make the next feasible opportunity clear and allow an uncomplicated correction or return. Keep the agreed inline flame, but do not make a reset erase the visible journey or prescribe effort from a target streak rate. Planned rest, unknown reports and missed work retain different meanings. Evaluate the actual streak policy rather than asserting either universal benefit or universal harm.

When work is going well, review relevant successes as well as difficulties, and keep useful conditions in memory. Success can justify maintaining or lightening support. The research on structured reviews motivates this direction, with task/population transfer limits documented in the evidence memo. It does not mandate a weekly interview or promise a particular effect in Adler.

The landing journey should eventually demonstrate the same real product states: a useful first plan, actual reported friction, a sourced tentative adjustment, subsequent experience and an honest next decision. Use approved actual-app captures and the existing visual continuity. Avoid a montage in which every intervention succeeds or a graph that equates an accepted suggestion with improvement. Landing changes remain secondary to making that journey work in the product.

## 8. Verify judgment and user benefit, as well as record correctness

The repository already implements a shared service, research gates, claim grounding, semantic review and versioned learning. The [release verification](evaluation/shared-coaching-v2.md) reports deterministic/browser checks and limited live-provider examples. It explicitly does not establish independent scientific adjudication, general model/reviewer reliability or coaching effectiveness. This review does not rerun or extend those tests.

The next evaluation should use multi-turn trajectories where an initially plausible explanation is contradicted, where continuing is appropriate, and where action execution and ultimate outcomes diverge. Measure unnecessary questions, unsupported advice, unjustified adaptations, failure to adapt when warranted, reporting burden and latency. Model agreement is not an independent expert reference.

Keep actual product effectiveness separate. A prospective comparison against a credible feasible-plan-and-monitoring experience can test whether adaptive coaching adds value. Select meaningful work/outcomes and an appropriate follow-up for the goal family, alongside burden and return after disruption. Plan allocation, analysis, power and missing-data handling before claiming effects. App opens, experiment counts and user satisfaction are not substitutes for the intended outcome. The evidence review discusses why access to an individual experiment alone cannot establish benefit.

### Acceptance scenarios for the next implementation

| Scenario | Observable requirement |
| --- | --- |
| A first goal with enough context to act | Useful provisional work and selected tracking; no fabricated personal insight or compulsory research interview |
| A first complex goal without a chosen approach | A consequential clarification or user-owned learning objective; no invented commercial strategy |
| Report missing, or changed quantity not supplied | Unknown retained; no inferred failure, false streak certainty or silently imputed input |
| Busy calendar or displaced opportunity | Relevant source stays contextual; no fabricated exposure; feasible tradeoffs consider other goals |
| An agreed timing change with no reported use | Current agreement visible; no demonstrated benefit, completed attempt or automatically improved observed pace |
| Repeated useful success | A defensible hold/maintain response is eligible; no automatic difficulty increase |
| A smaller action produces a higher completion rate | Old/new quantities and criterion remain visible; the coach considers meaningful work and result, not just the rate |
| Completed work with delayed or absent outcome | Keep the outcome pending when its expected feedback window has not arrived; investigate work quality, measurement or strategy when available evidence makes that useful; no forced outcome gain or personal dollars-per-hour cause |
| A review date arrives without use or informative feedback | Feasibility/unknown state or useful question; no manufactured experimental verdict |
| Context changes or a premise is corrected | Dependent learning is reconsidered; historical rationale remains attributable; the current plan is explicit |
| A second action or earlier revision is selected | Units, milestone context, reports and controls correspond to that selection on desktop and mobile |
| Same experience reported through chat and another channel | One authoritative occurrence, retained source provenance and consistent state; no duplicate experimental exposure |
| One useful insight considered for another goal | Visible original scope and a tentative transfer rationale; no universal identity label |
| A user pauses, resumes or completes a goal | A clear path with truthful retained evidence; no retrospective fabricated completions or compulsory further experimentation |

## 9. Implementation priorities

1. **Reliable meaning:** repair prototype action/history selection; define and verify what reported work, plan revision, test use, review and conditional outlook mean across the shared system and A/C.
2. **Reliable coaching judgment:** evaluate the existing gates on the decision cases above. Deepen records or tools only where failures show information is missing or inaccessible. Preserve inexpensive deterministic actions and the single coaching service.
3. **Evidence-appropriate outlook:** address cadence, coverage, sparse association and single-driver applicability in the existing deterministic projection path, with truthful display states and no fabricated probability.
4. **The agreed interface iteration:** compose current milestone/action lanes/graphs; compress All Goals; connect relevant changes and Coach context; retain scientific depth on demand.
5. **Comprehension and benefit:** test whether users can identify current work, interpret the implication, understand a proposed change and correct a record; then evaluate whether the coaching improves meaningful outcomes with acceptable burden.

Treat this as one coherent improvement to the chosen product, with these checks integrated into its implementation slices. It does not require new top-level pages, a new framework selector, an experiment-administration dashboard or replacing the existing shared coach.
